# 22. Relaxation, entertainment and stress relief

The first half concerns costs and safety at entertainment venues, measured in money and personal freedom. The second gives more specific ways to manage stress and preserve energy. Some of its studies measured mood or anxiety; others measured all-cause mortality. These outcomes cannot be converted into one another. [Chapter 3](03_use_your_energy_well.md) covers the first things to do when you feel low; this chapter gives more specific steps.

The introduction and all 11 entries are translated here. The [Chinese text](../../book/22-怎么放松.md) remains available.

### 1. Find the exit when you enter a karaoke venue, bar or escape room; leave if it is locked or obstructed
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: $0 or €0; take about 10 seconds on arrival to locate exit signs and a route out.
- In plain language: Find the exits before your evening begins. If a door on the escape route will not open, objects block the passage, or the exit sign is hidden, choose another venue and tell the staff. In a basement or crowded room, make especially sure you know how to leave: smoke can rapidly impair visibility in a fire, and a narrow passage can become dangerous.
- Benefit: In China, the manager of a licensed entertainment venue is responsible for fire safety, equipment checks and an evacuation plan under Article 20 of the entertainment-venue regulation. While it is open, escape routes and exits must remain clear and unlocked, without barriers or covered signs, under Article 21. A separate rule for Chinese internet cafés, Article 24 of their regulation, forbids blocking or locking doors, windows and escape routes during business hours, as well as using open flames for lighting or allowing smoking. People may report violations at entertainment venues to the responsible authorities; under Article 30, signs must display police and cultural-authority reporting numbers. Russian emergency officials likewise warn against doors that need a key to open from inside or obstructed routes. London Fire Brigade gives the same practical advice for bars and clubs. These rules and recommendations do not quantify how much a ten-second check reduces mortality.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: State Council of China (2006), Regulations on the Administration of Entertainment Venues, Decree 458, Articles 20–21. <http://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2008-03/28/content_6297.htm>; current 2020 text, Articles 20, 21, 30 and 37. <https://xzfg.moj.gov.cn/front/law/detail?LawID=1536>. State Council of China (2002), Regulations on the Administration of Commercial Internet Access Service Venues, Decree 363. <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2002/content_61788.htm>; current text, Article 24. <https://xzfg.moj.gov.cn/front/law/detail?LawID=944>. Russian Emergency Ministry, guidance on keeping emergency exits clear. <https://43.mchs.gov.ru/deyatelnost/press-centr/garnizonnye-novosti/5647515>. London Fire Brigade, fire safety for nightclubs, bars and pubs. <https://www.london-fire.gov.uk/safety/the-workplace/nightclubs-bars-and-pubs-fire-safety/>. US Fire Administration, review of nightclub fires. <https://apps.usfa.fema.gov/downloads/pdf/statistics/v3i7.pdf>.
- Notes: A basement room, one exit, a cluttered corridor and a failed exit sign are different warning signs. A locked exit or blocked route calls for immediate action; a basement location or a single exit **does not by itself prove** a legal violation. Capacity, design and local rules matter. If a venue feels cramped or overcrowded, or you can find only one unclear way out, choose another. Do not judge an escape room by whether you can solve its puzzle: ask how its door opens in an emergency. China's Decree 458 covers certain licensed song, dance and amusement venues, so it is not automatically the law for every bar or escape room; Russia, Belarus, the US and European countries have their own rules. There is no universal number of seconds available to escape a fire; crowds and alcohol can make evacuation harder.

### 2. Ask for a price list before ordering; do not accept “enjoy yourself now, we'll work out the bill later”
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Asking for the menu and noting prices costs $0 or €0; it takes a short conversation with staff.
- In plain language: Before ordering a drink, a “special” cocktail, a fruit platter or a paid service, ask its price and about any mandatory extras. Keep the menu or photograph the relevant page if permitted. Ask for a confirmed itemised list after ordering. If the bill differs, request a detailed breakdown and an explanation on the spot; keep the receipt and any messages.
- Benefit: Article 29 of China's entertainment-venue regulation requires venues to display the prices of services and goods, show consumers a price list and refrain from forcing or deceiving them into purchases. Price-law breaches are addressed under Article **54** of the current regulation. Russia requires food-service menus or price lists to make product, portion and price information available; Belarus has its own retail and catering rules. In the EU, traders must give clear information about the total price before a purchase. These provisions protect access to prices. There are no data showing that “almost all” expensive-drink disputes start without a price list.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: State Council of China (2006), Regulations on the Administration of Entertainment Venues, Decree 458. <http://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2008-03/28/content_6297.htm>; current text, Articles 29 and 54. <https://xzfg.moj.gov.cn/front/law/detail?LawID=1536>. Russian consumer protection authority, menu and drinks-list requirements for food services. <https://zpp.rospotrebnadzor.ru/news/federal/565057>. Belarusian Tax Ministry, official list of current regulations including Council of Ministers Decree 703 on retail and catering, updated in 2026. <https://nalog.gov.by/tax_control/control_of_goods/tobacco/>. European Union, information about the total price before purchase. <https://europa.eu/youreurope/citizens/consumers/shopping/contract-information/index_en.htm>. Beijing government, consumer complaints through the 12315 platform. <https://www.beijing.gov.cn/fuwu/bmfw/bmjy/202504/t20250409_4060597.html>.
- Notes: In China you can raise a billing dispute through 12315; it is a Chinese service, not a hotline for other countries. In Russia, complaints about consumer rights can go to Rospotrebnadzor; in Belarus, use the competent local consumer authority or MART. First keep the bill and ask the venue to resolve the difference. In the US, exact menu rules and complaint routes vary by state and locality; the Chinese regulation does not apply. In the EU, the European Consumer Centre can help with a cross-border dispute. Paying a bill does not automatically waive your right to dispute an incorrect charge, but without the menu and confirmed order it can be harder to establish what was agreed.

### 3. Leave if someone offers an unknown substance; do not let others use your room to take it
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: $0 or €0; you need to end the outing and leave.
- In plain language: If someone produces an unknown powder, tablet or vape cartridge and asks you to “try it”, decline and leave. Do not stay to watch or agree to let the room be used for taking it. If others may be in danger, alert staff or the police from a safe place.
- Benefit: In China, Article 14 of the entertainment-venue regulation prohibits a licensed venue and its staff from selling or supplying drugs, organising, forcing, encouraging or deceiving people into using them, allowing people to use them, or facilitating those acts. Under Article 43, a venue that breaks this rule can have unlawful proceeds and property confiscated and be ordered to close for **3–6 months** to put matters right; serious cases can cost it its licence and lead to fines for responsible staff. Article 30 requires prominent warnings about drugs, gambling and prostitution, with numbers for reporting violations. For an individual in China, taking a drug, supplying it to someone else and knowingly allowing its use in a place under their control have different consequences: Articles 84–85 of the 2025 public-security law, in force since 2026, provide for administrative penalties, while Criminal Code Article 354 and the Supreme People's Court's Article 12 interpretation set the conditions for a crime of providing a place for drug use. **Booking the room in your name does not automatically make every single incident a crime.**
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: State Council of China (2006), Regulations on the Administration of Entertainment Venues, Decree 458. <http://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2008-03/28/content_6297.htm>; current text, Articles 14, 30 and 43. <https://xzfg.moj.gov.cn/front/law/detail?LawID=1536>. National People's Congress of China (2025), Public Security Administration Punishments Law, Articles 84–85. <https://www.npc.gov.cn/npc/c2/c30834/202506/t20250627_446254.html>. Supreme People's Court of China (2016), interpretation on drug offences, Article 12. <https://gongbao.court.gov.cn/Details/53c344a8e4b67174a8747677d23060.html>. Criminal Code of China, Article 354. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/201802/t20180206_364975.shtml>.
- Notes: The Chinese rules for licensed karaoke and amusement venues are not law in the US or European countries. Apply local law to questions about supply or allowing drug use on premises. Under the court interpretation, the criminal threshold for providing a place depends, for example, on how many people and occasions were involved, whether a minor was involved, profit or serious harm; control of the premises also matters. Leaving is a practical way to avoid danger, but these laws do not measure how much the advice protects personal freedom.

### 4. Decline food, drinks or vapes of uncertain origin; replace a drink left unattended
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: Declining a doubtful offer costs $0 or €0. Replacing a drink left unattended costs whatever the venue charges; that step is not necessarily free.
- In plain language: Do not try a sweet, drink or vape cartridge of unknown contents offered by a stranger. If you left your drink unattended and are unsure about it, do not finish it; get a fresh one. Spiking is the perpetrator's fault, never the victim's. If you suddenly feel confused or sick, lose coordination or cannot remember what happened, get somewhere safe, ask someone you trust or venue staff for help, and seek medical care promptly. Report suspected spiking to the police: some substances become difficult to detect quite quickly.
- Benefit: China brought synthetic cannabinoids under class-wide control on **1 July 2021**. In cases investigated in Wujiang, liquids containing them could be coloured and flavoured, and the devices looked much like ordinary vapes; sellers offered them in bars, karaoke venues and hotels. Minors accounted for nearly **40%** of the local cases involving these vapes that officials handled in **2021**. This is a figure for those local cases, not a national estimate. On **1 October 2023**, China placed etomidate in its category-II psychotropic-substance list, with an exception for approved medicinal products. Under China's 2025 public-security law, effective **1 January 2026**, inducing, deceiving or forcing another person to take drugs can bring **10–15 days** of administrative detention and a fine of **1,000–5,000 yuan**. Where it is a crime, Criminal Code Article 353 provides up to **3 years'** imprisonment or another stated criminal penalty, **3–7 years** in serious cases, and **3–10 years** for forcing someone to take drugs; offences against minors are punished more severely. For *voluntary unlawful use*, Article 84 provides **10–15 days** of detention and a possible fine of up to **3,000 yuan**; less serious cases can bring up to **5 days** or a fine of up to **1,000 yuan**. A ban on entering entertainment venues for **6 months to 1 year** may also be imposed. A person found to have a drug dependence may be ordered to undergo **3 years** of community rehabilitation; under statutory conditions, compulsory isolation normally lasts **2 years** and may be extended by no more than **1 year**. **None of these measures follows automatically when someone is drugged without their knowledge**: the facts and legal grounds must be established.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Supreme People's Procuratorate of China (2021), report on Wujiang cases. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/zdgz/202110/t20211011_531831.shtml>. National Medical Products Administration, Ministry of Public Security and National Health Commission of China (2023), 2023 Notice 120, hosted by the Shanghai drug regulator. <https://yjj.sh.gov.cn/qtgzwj/20230911/3b612c94e35b4b63abba314c972a2593.html>. Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2025), 2025 Public Security Administration Punishments Law, Articles 20, 84–85 and 113. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202506/t20250627_699863.shtml>. National People's Congress (1997), Criminal Code, Article 353. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/201802/t20180206_364975.shtml>. Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2007), Anti-Drug Law, Guangdong government copy, Articles 33, 38 and 47. <http://www.gd.gov.cn/zwgk/wjk/zcfgk/content/post_2520706.html>. Supreme People's Court of China, official Anti-Drug Law text. <https://gongbao.court.gov.cn/Details/6dcc499b2e87f0d38f63d1011abdee.html>. UK government, advice and support after spiking. <https://www.gov.uk/guidance/spiking-advice-and-support>. US Drug Enforcement Administration, advice on suspected drug-facilitated assault. <https://www.dea.gov/sites/default/files/2022-01/Drug%20Facilitated%20Sexual%20Assault.pdf>. European Commission, EU emergency number 112. <https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/112>.
- Notes: The same precaution applies at a friend's gathering, near a school or on a long journey, not only in a club. Ordinary-looking sweets, drinks and vapes do not reveal their contents; appearance alone proves nothing. In the US, call **911** if you are in immediate danger; in the EU, call **112**. China's fines, detention rules and rehabilitation periods are not US or European law. Spiking can be followed by theft, sexual assault or extortion, but their frequency cannot be inferred from the cited reports. Seek help and report what happened even if you cannot recall every detail. See entry 3 above for unknown substances offered at a venue and [chapter 8, entry 27](08_protect_your_rights_and_property.md) for requests to carry someone else's belongings.

### 5. Use your own ID at an internet café; do not lend it or use someone else's
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: Presenting your own ID instead of another person's costs $0 or €0. Time at a computer is charged at the venue's rate.
- In plain language: Where an internet café checks identity for entry or a session, register under your own name. Do not lend your ID to a friend or use theirs. A Chinese internet café must keep its session records for at least 60 days. A false name in the log can complicate finding out who used the computer; it does not prove that the ID holder carried out everything done on it.
- Benefit: Article 23 of China's regulation on commercial internet-access venues requires an operator to check and record a visitor's valid identity document and information about internet use. Registration details and backup records must be kept for **at least 60 days**, may not be changed or deleted during that period, and must be provided to the cultural authority or police when lawfully requested. The record can assist an investigation but does not, by itself, establish who committed an act. Article 21 bars minors from such venues and requires a no-minors sign at the entrance; Article 31 provides penalties for the operator that admits a minor. A separate Chinese entertainment-venue regulation, Article 23, also bars minors from song-and-dance venues.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: State Council of China (2002), Regulations on the Administration of Commercial Internet Access Service Venues, Decree 363, Article 23. <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2002/content_61788.htm>. State Administration for Market Regulation of China, current regulation as amended through **6 December 2024**, Articles 21, 23 and 31. <https://www.samr.gov.cn/zw/zfxxgk/fdzdgknr/bgt/art/2023/art_2e700be8d38f4c1f96ac7b44189084be.html>. State Council of China, Regulations on the Administration of Entertainment Venues, Article 23. <https://xzfg.moj.gov.cn/front/law/detail?LawID=1536>.
- Notes: The Chinese regulation covers commercial internet cafés and computer rooms, not every public Wi-Fi hotspot or library. Authorities receive the records **through a lawful request**, rather than whenever they wish. In the US and European countries, identity checks and retention requirements depend on local law and the service; China's 60-day minimum is not a local rule. Lending an ID can create a misleading record, but it does not automatically make its holder liable for someone else's acts. For other risks of lending identity documents, see [chapter 9, entry 16](09_legal_pitfalls.md). Do not help a minor evade the Chinese internet-café entry rule; distinguish the operator's penalties from any consequences for a visitor.

### 6. Check an escape room's address before booking; avoid residential buildings and second basement levels or lower
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Checking the address and asking which floor the game uses costs $0 or €0; it takes a brief question before booking.
- In plain language: Find out which floor the game is on and how to get out in a fire without solving a puzzle. If the venue hides its address, cannot explain how doors release in an emergency, or the route out looks unsafe, choose another. In China, escape rooms and live role-play venues must not operate in residential buildings or on the second basement level or lower.
- Benefit: A **2022** notice from five Chinese government departments requires live role-play venues to meet workplace and fire-safety rules, routinely identify and correct fire hazards, and prepare staff for evacuation and the early stages of firefighting. It excludes residential buildings and levels **below the first basement**: this means the second basement and lower, not the first basement on that condition alone. The notice also requires age guidance for scripts. Minors cannot enter a setting unsuitable for them, and outside national holidays, rest days and school vacations venues cannot offer these activities to them. These requirements address safety and admission but do not quantify how much checking an address reduces mortality.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Ministry of Culture and Tourism, Ministry of Public Security, Ministry of Housing and Urban-Rural Development, Ministry of Emergency Management and State Administration for Market Regulation of China (2022), notice on the management of live role-play venues. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2022-06/27/content_5698021.htm>; official Ministry of Culture and Tourism text. <https://zwgk.mct.gov.cn/zfxxgkml/zcfg/gfxwj/202206/t20220627_934100.html>. Russian Emergency Ministry, fire safety in crowded venues. <https://40.mchs.gov.ru/deyatelnost/poleznaya-informaciya/rekomendacii-naseleniyu/pamyatki/pravila-pozharnoy-bezopasnosti/pravila-povedeniya-pri-pozhare-v-mestah-massovogo-skopleniya-lyudey>. UK government, fire-safety assessment for small and medium places of assembly. <https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/fire-safety-risk-assessment-small-and-medium-places-of-assembly/fire-safety-risk-assessment-small-and-medium-places-of-assembly-accessible>.
- Notes: Darkness, partitions and props can make it harder to find a way out; combustible decoration adds risk. These are possible features, not a description of every escape room. Check the floor and address on the booking page, then ask staff where the exit is and how the door opens during an alarm without completing a game task. In the US and European countries, the Chinese ban on residential buildings and the second basement **is not local law**: legality depends on local rules and the building. An allowed floor does not guarantee a safe exit; an unsafe evacuation route is reason enough to leave. For checking exits on arrival, see entry 1 above.

### 7. If your mood stays low, choose walking, running or another form of exercise you can manage
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Walking outside generally costs $0 or €0; around half an hour per session can be a practical starting point. The review did not establish that duration as a required dose, and gym classes may cost money.
- In plain language: If you can exercise, try walking regularly or choose another activity that suits you. Walking or jogging, yoga, strength work, mixed aerobic exercise, and tai chi or qigong have all been studied in people with depression. A finding about prescribed intensity is no reason to increase yours abruptly. Match activity to your health and discuss care with a clinician when symptoms interfere with daily life.
- Benefit: A review included **218 randomized trials**, **495 arms** and **14,170 participants** meeting clinical thresholds for major depression. Compared with active controls such as usual care or a placebo tablet, standardized differences in depressive symptoms (Hedges' g) were **−0.62** for walking or jogging (95% credible interval **−0.80 to −0.45**), **−0.55** for yoga (**−0.73 to −0.36**), **−0.49** for strength training (**−0.69 to −0.29**), **−0.43** for mixed aerobic exercise (**−0.61 to −0.24**), and **−0.42** for tai chi or qigong (**−0.65 to −0.21**). Greater *prescribed* intensity was associated with a larger effect in this analysis; strength training and yoga appeared most acceptable to participants. The measured outcome was **depression symptoms, not mortality**.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Noetel M, Sanders T, Gallardo-Gómez D, et al. (2024), review of exercise for depression, BMJ, 384, e075847. <https://doi.org/10.1136/bmj-2023-075847>; abstract and correction record. <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38355154/>. BMJ correction (2024): an erroneous methods sentence was removed without a material change in the findings. <https://www.bmj.com/content/385/bmj.q1024>.
- Notes: Only **1** of the 218 trials met Cochrane criteria for low risk of bias. CINeMA confidence was low for walking or jogging and very low for the other exercise types. Participants could not be fully blinded, so expectations may have affected reports. Similar estimates do not mean every activity will help every person equally. Thirty minutes is a practical suggestion, not a universally proven prescription from this review. Exercise can form part of care for diagnosed depression; do not stop other treatment on your own. For first steps when energy is low, see [chapter 3](03_use_your_energy_well.md).

### 8. For an anxiety disorder, discuss an eight-week mindfulness-based stress reduction course
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=多 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: **Eight weeks**, with weekly sessions and daily practice at home. Course prices vary across the US and Europe; there is no verified comparable amount in USD or EUR here, so check the full fee before enrolling.
- In plain language: If anxiety disrupts your life, seek an assessment and discuss treatment options. In one trial, a structured mindfulness-based stress reduction (MBSR) course met a predefined standard for being no less effective than escitalopram for adults with diagnosed anxiety disorders. That result does not make any meditation app a substitute for care, or mean you should stop prescribed medication without medical advice.
- Benefit: At **3** US medical centers, **276 adults** with diagnosed anxiety disorders were randomly assigned **1:1** to an **8-week** MBSR course or escitalopram at **10–20 mg**; **208** completed the trial. At week eight, Clinical Global Impression of Severity scores fell by **1.35** and **1.43** points, respectively. The between-group difference was **−0.07** points (95% confidence interval **−0.38 to 0.23**; **P = 0.65**), meeting the prespecified noninferiority margin of **−0.495**. Treatment-related adverse events were reported by **15.4%** of the MBSR group and **78.6%** of the medication group; **0** and **10** participants, respectively, withdrew because of them. The trial measured anxiety and tolerability, **not mortality**.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Hoge EA, Bui E, Mete M, Dutton MA, Baker AW, Simon NM (2023), randomized trial of MBSR versus escitalopram for anxiety disorders, JAMA Psychiatry, 80(1), 13–21. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2022.3679>; full study. <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9647561/>.
- Notes: Escitalopram is an SSRI antidepressant also used to treat anxiety disorders; the study did not compare MBSR with every medication. Its noninferiority finding concerns **this 8-week** standardized course and outcome, not every self-guided meditation exercise. The adverse-event rates are from this trial, not a forecast for an individual. Courses can cost money and take considerable time; they are not available everywhere. Discuss starting or changing treatment with a clinician.

### 9. To settle yourself a little, try five minutes of cyclic sighing
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: **Five minutes a day** for about a month; without a paid app it costs $0 or €0.
- In plain language: Inhale through your nose, take a second short top-up inhalation, then exhale slowly and for longer through your mouth. Repeat at a comfortable pace for up to five minutes. If you feel dizzy or uncomfortable, return to ordinary breathing. This may help ease tension; it does not treat the cause of anxiety.
- Benefit: In a remote randomized trial, participants practiced one of three breathing methods for **five minutes daily** over **1 month**, or spent **5 minutes** in mindfulness meditation. The methods were cyclic sighing with a longer exhale, box breathing with equal phases, and cyclic hyperventilation with retention. In the analysis, breathing practice considered together—and cyclic sighing in particular—produced greater improvement in self-reported mood and a greater reduction in respiratory rate than meditation (**p < 0.05** for mood; **p < 0.05** for respiratory rate). This does **not** show that each of the three methods separately outperformed meditation on every measure. Mortality was not studied.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Balban MY, Neri E, Kogon MM, et al. (2023), Brief structured respiration practices enhance mood and reduce physiological arousal, Cell Reports Medicine, 4(1), 100895. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.xcrm.2022.100895>; full study. <https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9873947/>.
- Notes: This was one relatively small remote trial. Participants reported their own mood; respiratory rate was a physiological measure. It would be wrong to call **all** the results self-report. Both breathing and meditation improved some outcomes from baseline, so a comparative advantage for breathing does not make meditation useless. The trial does not prove that one 5-minute session will immediately solve a problem in any setting or that 5 minutes of breathing reduces mortality. Breathe without strain; there is no need to force your lungs to their limit.

### 10. Keep seeing people whose company does you good
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: It takes time and may involve travel or a paid activity. A free nearby walk can cost $0 or €0; transport, cafés and other plans have local prices.
- In plain language: Arrange to see someone you enjoy and trust from time to time, rather than reaching out only on a bad day. If meeting is impossible, a call may help you stay in touch, though the research below did not test the best frequency of meetings or calls. Living alone, feeling lonely and having few social contacts are distinct conditions.
- Benefit: A review of **70 prospective studies** covered **3,407,134 people** (about **3.4 million**), followed for an average of about **7 years**. After statistical adjustment for some confounders, the weighted **odds ratios** for death were **1.29** for social isolation, **1.26** for loneliness and **1.32** for living alone. These are relative odds, **not increases of 29, 26 or 32 percentage points** in the probability of death. The association was stronger in samples whose **mean age was under 65**; it cannot simply be assigned to every individual under 65.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Holt-Lunstad J, Smith TB, Baker M, Harris T, Stephenson D (2015), Loneliness and social isolation as risk factors for mortality: a meta-analytic review, Perspectives on Psychological Science, 10(2), 227–237. <https://doi.org/10.1177/1745691614568352>; study abstract. <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25910392/>.
- Notes: All the included data were observational. Illness may both reduce social contact and increase the chance of death; statistical adjustment cannot rule out every such explanation. The review **did not test** a prescription to meet more often and does not prove that meetings themselves lower mortality. Choose supportive relationships: [chapter 3](03_use_your_energy_well.md) also advises limiting relationships that drain you. The numerical association cannot be assigned to one particular visit.

### 11. Use nearby green space if you have it; consider greenery when choosing where to live
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=小 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Sitting or walking in a free nearby green space can cost $0 or €0, plus time. Housing in a greener area may cost more; choosing it is not necessarily free.
- In plain language: If a park or quiet green area is nearby, you can visit for a walk, an errand or a rest. When choosing housing, look at the surrounding greenery too. Do not take this as proof that a park visit adds years to your life: the cited study measured **greenness around the home**, not visits.
- Benefit: A pooled analysis of **9 cohort studies** from **7 countries** included **8,324,652 people** (about **8.32 million**). For each **0.1** higher normalized difference vegetation index (NDVI) within **500 m or less** of a home, the pooled **hazard ratio for all-cause mortality** was **0.96** (95% confidence interval **0.94–0.97**). **7 of 9** studies found a statistically significant inverse association; **2** did not. A hazard ratio of 0.96 is roughly a **4% lower relative hazard over time**, not a four-percentage-point reduction in the absolute probability of death.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Rojas-Rueda D, Nieuwenhuijsen MJ, Gascon M, Perez-Leon D, Mudu P (2019), Green spaces and mortality: a systematic review and meta-analysis of cohort studies, The Lancet Planetary Health, 3(11), e469–e477. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S2542-5196(19)30215-3>; study abstract. <https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31777338/>.
- Notes: Results varied greatly between studies (**I² = 95%**). Greener neighbourhoods may differ in income, air quality and opportunities for exercise; observational data cannot fully remove those differences or prove that greenery itself caused the association. The review did not count park visits, so it cannot promise a mortality benefit from walking there or sitting on a bench. Visiting an accessible green space is a separate practical suggestion; considering greenery when choosing housing is closer to what was measured.
