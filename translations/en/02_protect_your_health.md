# 2. Don’t damage your health over time

This chapter focuses on chronic risk factors with substantial effects on all-cause mortality and strong supporting evidence, ordered by benefit relative to cost. Except for the randomized trials identified below, the figures come from observational research. Confounding and reverse causation can affect the hazard ratios (HRs) and risk ratios (RRs). Read them as estimates of the direction and size of an association, not as a promised reduction in your own risk.

### 1. Quit smoking—the sooner, the better
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Getting through withdrawal takes effort over weeks or months. You stop spending money on cigarettes: if you smoke a pack a day, your monthly saving before treatment costs is roughly 30 times the price you actually pay per pack, in USD or EUR. Medication or counseling may have a separate cost; quitting does not require doing it without support.
- In plain language: In a large US study, people who smoked had a life expectancy more than ten years shorter than those who had never smoked. Quitting before 40 avoided about 90% of the extra risk associated with continuing to smoke. That is a reduction in the excess risk from smoking, not a 90% reduction in every cause of death. Quitting earlier was associated with more years of life regained.
- Benefit: In an observational US cohort, current smokers had a life expectancy more than 10 years shorter than never-smokers. Quitting before age 40 avoided about 90% of the excess mortality risk associated with continued smoking. People who quit at ages 25–34, 35–44 and 45–54 gained about 10, 9 and 6 years of life, respectively, compared with those who continued. In observational Chinese cohorts, the study centered around 2010 found an all-cause mortality RR of 1.65 for urban male smokers and 1.22 for rural male smokers. Among people who stopped by choice, rather than because of illness, little excess risk remained after 10 years of cessation.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Jha P et al. (2013). 21st-century hazards of smoking and benefits of cessation in the United States. NEJM. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMsa1211128> ; Chen Z et al. (2015). Contrasting male and female trends in tobacco-attributed mortality in China: evidence from successive nationwide prospective cohort studies. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(15)00340-2>
- Notes: Secondhand smoke also kills. An analysis of 192 countries estimated that exposure caused about 603,000 deaths worldwide in 2004, approximately 1% of all deaths that year (Öberg M et al., 2011. Worldwide burden of disease from exposure to second-hand smoke: a retrospective analysis of data from 192 countries. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(10)61388-8>). Avoid other people’s smoke even if you do not smoke yourself, and keep children away from it.

> Editorial clarification: These are population estimates, not a deadline before which smoking is safe. The 2004 secondhand-smoke estimate is historical, not a current annual count. “Little excess risk after ten years” describes the Chinese study group; it does not mean that every disease risk returns to a never-smoker’s level on that schedule.

### 2. Keep your home and car smoke-free, including when guests visit
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No spending. The difficult part can be asking relatives and guests not to smoke indoors.
- In plain language: Secondhand smoke was estimated to cause about 600,000 deaths worldwide in 2004; nearly three in ten were children. A meta-analysis found higher odds of high blood pressure, heart disease, heart attack and stroke among people exposed to it, with estimates roughly 30%–50% higher depending on the outcome. The association with cardiovascular disease was stronger for exposure at home than elsewhere.
- Benefit: In 2004, an estimated 603,000 deaths worldwide were attributable to secondhand smoke, about 1.0% of all deaths; 28% were children. A meta-analysis of 57 studies reported odds ratios of 1.28 for hypertension, 1.39 for heart disease, 1.50 for myocardial infarction and 1.36 for stroke, with a stronger association for exposure at home than outside the home.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Öberg M, Jaakkola MS, Woodward A, Peruga A, Prüss-Ustün A (2011). Worldwide burden of disease from exposure to second-hand smoke: a retrospective analysis of data from 192 countries. Lancet. The estimated 603,000 deaths in 2004 represented 1.0% of global mortality; 47% were women, 28% children and 26% men, with rounding. Children accounted for 61% of disability-adjusted life years lost. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(10)61388-8> ; Feng S et al. (2026). The Associations Between Secondhand Smoke Exposure and Various Cardiovascular Diseases: A Meta-Analysis. Nicotine & Tobacco Research. Across 57 studies: hypertension OR 1.28 (95% CI 1.15–1.40), heart disease 1.39 (95% CI 1.28–1.50), myocardial infarction 1.50 (95% CI 1.17–1.84), stroke 1.36 (95% CI 1.18–1.54); the association with cardiovascular disease was stronger for home exposure. <https://doi.org/10.1093/ntr/ntaf111>
- Notes: Home is an important place to prevent exposure, both because of the time spent there and because the meta-analysis found a stronger association there. Children bore 61% of the estimated loss of healthy life measured in disability-adjusted life years. See recommendation 1 for quitting smoking yourself.

> Editorial clarification: Odds are not the same as probabilities. The home-versus-other-setting comparison does not prove that an equal dose of smoke is intrinsically more toxic at home; exposure duration and other differences may contribute. The mortality estimate refers to 2004.

### 3. Skip sugary drinks
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No additional spending; switching to water or unsweetened tea can save money.
- In plain language: In two US cohorts, drinking at least two servings of sugary drinks a day was associated with an all-cause mortality hazard about 21% higher than drinking less than one serving a month. At one to fewer than two servings daily, it was about 14% higher. These are associations over follow-up, not proof that the drinks alone caused the difference.
- Benefit: Two observational US cohorts, with approximately 118,000 participants and 36,000 deaths, found an all-cause mortality HR of 1.21 for at least 2 servings a day versus less than 1 a month, and 1.14 for 1 to fewer than 2 servings a day. In the observational European EPIC cohort of about 450,000 people, at least 2 glasses a day versus less than 1 a month was associated with an HR of 1.08 for sugar-sweetened soft drinks and 1.17 for soft drinks overall.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Malik VS et al. (2019). Long-Term Consumption of Sugar-Sweetened and Artificially Sweetened Beverages and Risk of Mortality in US Adults. Circulation. <https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCULATIONAHA.118.037401> ; Mullee A et al. (2019). Association Between Soft Drink Consumption and Mortality in 10 European Countries. JAMA Internal Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jamainternmed.2019.2478>
- Notes: These are observational data. People who drink a lot of sugary drinks can differ in other aspects of their lifestyle, which may inflate the estimated association. In EPIC, at least 2 glasses of artificially sweetened drinks a day was associated with an HR of 1.26, higher than for sugary drinks. Reverse causation is one possible explanation: people with metabolic health problems may switch to diet drinks. This is not proof that diet drinks are more harmful. Do not treat them as a reason to drink unlimited amounts; make water your usual choice.

> Editorial clarification: A study serving is not necessarily a bottle. EPIC used a glass of approximately 250 mL. The US category associated with HR 1.14 was 1 to fewer than 2 servings daily, rather than an overlapping “1–2.” Neither study randomly assigned drinks.

### 4. Don’t chew areca nut or betel quid
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No additional spending; you save what you would have spent on the product.
- In plain language: Studies linked chewing betel quid to about 21% higher all-cause mortality and roughly 50% higher risks of diabetes and metabolic syndrome. Those findings are separate from the established cancer concerns, including oral and esophageal cancer. Products without added tobacco are not harmless.
- Benefit: A meta-analysis of 17 Asian studies involving about 388,000 people found an all-cause mortality RR of 1.21 for betel-quid chewers compared with non-chewers (P = 0.02; about 179,600 participants in the mortality analysis), an RR of 1.47 for diabetes and 1.51 for metabolic syndrome. Areca nut/betel-quid chewing also carries established cancer risks, including oral cancer; the mixture, including whether tobacco is added, matters when interpreting the evidence for different cancer sites.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Yamada T, Hara K, Kadowaki T (2013). Chewing betel quid and the risk of metabolic disease, cardiovascular disease, and all-cause mortality: a meta-analysis. PLoS One, 8(8), e70679. <https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0070679>
- Notes: Oral-cancer risk increases with longer use and greater daily consumption; it decreases over time after stopping. Adding tobacco adds further harm. The metabolic and mortality estimates are associations, not guaranteed reductions that an individual will obtain after quitting.

> Editorial clarification: IARC distinguishes the evidence by product: betel quid without tobacco causes oral cancer; betel quid with tobacco also causes cancers of the pharynx and esophagus. Stopping areca products with or without tobacco reduces oral-cancer risk. [IARC assessment](https://publications.iarc.who.int/_publications/media/download/2691/8fbbe22749ff05e99bd470de1feffd227f02a890.pdf) and [cessation evidence](https://www.iarc.who.int/news-events/launch-of-iarc-handbooks-of-cancer-prevention-volume-19-oral-cancer-prevention/).

### 5. Consider replacing table salt with a lower-sodium potassium-containing salt, if it is suitable for you
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: About 3.20 USD for an 11 oz container in one US example or 7.75 EUR for 350 g in Germany. These are full package prices, not the extra cost over regular salt. Once you have changed the product, using it takes little extra effort; taste varies by formulation. [Products, prices and limitations](cost_examples.md).
- In plain language: In a randomized trial of about 20,000 high-risk adults, replacing ordinary household salt with a lower-sodium substitute reduced the death rate by about 12% and the stroke rate by about 14% over roughly five years. Random assignment makes this stronger evidence than an ordinary observational association. It does not mean that everyone should switch without checking whether extra potassium is safe for them.
- Benefit: In rural China, 20,995 people with a history of stroke or aged at least 60 with high blood pressure were followed for a mean of 4.74 years. Compared with regular salt, the substitute produced rate ratios of 0.88 for all-cause death, 0.86 for stroke and 0.87 for major cardiovascular events. Serious adverse events attributed to hyperkalemia did not differ significantly between groups.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Neal B et al. (2021). Effect of Salt Substitution on Cardiovascular Events and Death. NEJM. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa2105675>
- Notes: Disputed. In the observational PURE study (O’Donnell M et al., 2014. Urinary sodium and potassium excretion, mortality, and cardiovascular events. NEJM. <https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1311889>), estimated sodium excretion below 3 g/day was associated with an OR of 1.27 for death or a cardiovascular event; at least 7 g/day with an OR of 1.15, compared with the reference range. This suggested a J-shaped association. SSaSS, however, was randomized and used partial sodium replacement, not an attempt to reduce sodium to the very low levels suggested by that observational comparison. Its participants were at high cardiovascular risk; younger, healthy people would generally have less absolute benefit. Ask a clinician before switching if you have impaired kidney function or take medicines that retain potassium.

> Editorial clarification: SSaSS used 75% sodium chloride and 25% potassium chloride; commercial products can have different proportions. The ratios concern event rates, not a fixed five-year probability. No significant difference in serious hyperkalemia does not establish safety for people excluded because of kidney disease or potassium-related risks. WHO’s 2025 recommendation is conditional and applies to adults, excluding pregnant women and people with kidney impairment or other conditions that compromise potassium excretion; it does not cover children. Do not increase the amount of salt you use. [WHO guideline](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240105591).

### 6. Brush carefully, clean between your teeth daily, and see a dentist about missing teeth
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: A 50 m floss pack is about 8.20 USD in one US listing or 2.45 EUR in Germany. Annual spending depends on the product and how much you use; allow another 2–3 minutes daily. Professional cleaning and tooth replacement cost extra and need a separate quote. [Price examples](cost_examples.md).
- In plain language: In a Japanese study of older adults, using floss or another interdental cleaner was associated with an all-cause mortality hazard about 11% lower. A separate review found higher odds of death among older adults with no teeth or fewer than 20 teeth. That does not show that flossing or replacing teeth will produce the same change in your chance of dying. Dental care matters for comfort, eating and oral health in its own right.
- Benefit: A 6-year Japanese cohort of 9,676 people found an all-cause mortality HR of 0.89 among people using interdental cleaning tools and 0.77 among those using tongue cleaners. In a meta-analysis of community-dwelling older adults, the odds ratio for death was 1.87 for having no teeth (95% CI 1.35–2.59) and 2.04 for having fewer than 20 teeth (1.67–2.49).
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Wang K, Matsuyama Y, Kiuchi S, et al. (2026). Routine oral health practices and all-cause mortality. Journal of Dentistry. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jdent.2026.106789> ; Ko MJ, Seo S, So JS, et al. (2026). Deteriorated oral health and function as risk factors for physical disability and mortality in community-dwelling older adults: a systematic review and meta-analysis. European Geriatric Medicine. <https://doi.org/10.1007/s41999-025-01319-4>
- Notes: Disputed. These are observational data: poor oral health can accompany worse general health and financial circumstances, and the direction of causation is uncertain. Basic interdental care is inexpensive, while periodontal disease and missing teeth can themselves make eating difficult. The cost and appropriate way to replace a tooth require an individual dental assessment.

> Editorial clarification: The Japanese cohort consisted of independently living older adults with natural teeth; it excluded people with no teeth. HR 0.89 describes about an 11% lower hazard, while OR 1.87 and 2.04 describe odds, not 87% and 104% higher probabilities. These studies do not establish that tooth replacement reduces mortality.
