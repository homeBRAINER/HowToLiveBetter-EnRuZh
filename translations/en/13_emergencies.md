# 13. Emergencies: what to do first

> Partial translation: the introduction and entries 1–11 of 42. Emergency numbers below are local; the BASIC-OHCA figures and Civil Code rule concern China.

This chapter answers one question: what should you do first in an emergency, what comes next, and what should you avoid? First-aid and wilderness entries use survival or cause-specific mortality as outcomes; threats and scams use money and personal freedom. Those measures cannot be converted into one another. The order gives priority to situations that are common and where doing the right thing quickly makes the greatest difference to life or death. For first aid, the main people who may benefit are you, your spouse and direct relatives: 79.2% of cardiac arrests in a Chinese emergency-service registry happened at home. Friends and colleagues come next. Helping a stranger still matters, but ranks last for your own expected benefit here; it also brings the risk of a false accusation or being drawn into a legal case. That is why self-protection steps and the applicable legal protections are addressed early in these entries.

### 1. Someone collapses and is not breathing normally: start chest compressions, call emergency services and get an AED
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->

- Cost: Starting compressions costs $0 or €0. A Red Cross or hospital CPR course takes about half a day to a day, and the skill can serve you for years; local course fees vary and have not been verified here. Even without a course, you can place one hand over the other in the centre of an adult's chest and press hard at 100–120 compressions a minute, allowing the chest to rise fully between presses.
- In plain language: A family member may be the person who needs you: in a Chinese registry of 38,227 cardiac arrests attended by emergency services, 79.2% happened at home. If an adult collapses, does not respond and is not breathing normally—or only gasps—call emergency services and start compressions in the centre of the chest. Ask someone else to fetch an AED and make the call if they are there; if you are alone, call first and follow the dispatcher's directions. In a large review, survival to hospital discharge was 16.1% in groups receiving bystander CPR and 3.9% in groups without it, an association of more than fourfold. Use the AED as soon as it arrives and follow its voice prompts.
- Benefit: A meta-analysis of 79 studies and 142,740 adults with out-of-hospital cardiac arrest found 7.6% overall survival to hospital discharge. In groups with bystander CPR, discharge survival was 16.1%, versus 3.9% without it; 53% of arrests were witnessed, but only 32% received bystander CPR. These are observed group rates, not a guaranteed fourfold improvement for an individual. In China's BASIC-OHCA registry, 30,282 of 38,227 emergency-service-attended arrests (79.2%) occurred at home; 7,121 of 35,090 eligible cases (20.3%) received bystander CPR, and 441 of 38,227 (1.2%) survived to discharge or 30 days. The two studies describe different populations and endpoints; their survival figures should not be compared directly. Start compressions, have someone call and find an AED, then follow its prompts.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Sasson C et al. (2010). Predictors of survival from out-of-hospital cardiac arrest: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Circulation: Cardiovascular Quality and Outcomes. <https://doi.org/10.1161/CIRCOUTCOMES.109.889576>; Zheng J et al. (2023). Incidence, process of care, and outcomes of out-of-hospital cardiac arrest in China: a prospective study of the BASIC-OHCA registry. The Lancet Public Health. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S2468-2667(23)00173-1>; National People's Congress (2020), Civil Code, Article 184. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202006/t20200602_463888.shtml>. Current technique: [American Heart Association, emergency treatment](https://www.heart.org/en/health-topics/cardiac-arrest/emergency-treatment-of-cardiac-arrest) and [European Resuscitation Council, 2025 adult basic life support](https://www.erc.edu/media/wrhj5sye/gl2025-04-bls-e.pdf). Emergency numbers: [US 911](https://www.911.gov/calling-911/) and [EU 112](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/112).
- Notes: Do not first press below the nose, search for family members or film the person. Do not hold back because compressions might fracture a rib. For an adult, press the lower half of the breastbone about 5–6 cm deep at 100–120 a minute, let the chest recoil and minimise pauses; stop briefly when the AED tells you to. Continue until the person moves or breathes normally, emergency professionals take over, or the dispatcher directs you otherwise. In the US call 911; throughout the EU call 112. In China, the ambulance emergency number is 120. China's Civil Code Article 184 says a person who voluntarily provides emergency aid is not civilly liable for harm caused to the recipient by that aid. That Chinese rule does not define a rescuer's legal protection in the US or Europe.

### 2. An older person has fallen: check for a response and breathing, call for help, and do not rush to lift them
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->

- Cost: Checking on someone and calling emergency services costs $0 or €0 and takes a few minutes. If the person is a stranger, the dispatcher may keep your number, call back or ask what you saw. In a dispute, defending a claim can take months; in China, lawyers' fees are not automatically paid by the losing party. These are possible time and legal costs, not a measured average.
- In plain language: Start with an older relative or someone you know. Check whether they respond and breathe normally. If not, follow entry 1 and start chest compressions. If they are breathing, do not pull them up immediately: a stroke or fainting spell may have caused the fall, and an injury may have followed it. Ask whether they remember falling. A memory gap can point to fainting or a problem with blood flow to the brain. Severe headache, a drooping face, slurred speech or weakness in an arm or leg are reasons to call emergency services without lifting them. The same applies to back pain, loss of bladder or bowel control, or signs of a fracture: do not move them without the necessary skills. If they are alert, have none of those warning signs and try to get up themselves, help them rise slowly, sit and rest, and watch how they feel before leaving. Chinese law distinguishes two accusations: harm caused during a voluntary emergency rescue is covered by Civil Code Article 184; an allegation that you caused the fall must be proved by the person making it. In a published Chinese case, a rescuer fractured a person's ribs during CPR; the court rejected a claim for more than 9,000 CNY. Those legal rules and that decision do not apply automatically in the US or Europe.
- Benefit: China's 2011 technical guide calls falls the leading cause of injury-related death among adults aged 65 and over in China and says not to lift an older person immediately without assessing them. If they are unconscious, call emergency services; if they vomit, protect the airway by turning the head to one side and clearing the mouth and nose as needed; if they become unresponsive and stop breathing normally, begin chest compressions. If movement is unavoidable, keep it steady and the person as level as possible. If conscious, ask whether they remember the fall; no recollection may indicate syncope or a cerebrovascular event. A severe headache, facial droop, impaired speech or weakness can mean that lifting them worsens a brain injury; apparent fracture, back pain or loss of bladder or bowel control are also reasons not to move them without training. Call emergency services in all these situations. If they can rise on their own, assist them slowly, let them sit or lie down and observe them before leaving. Under China's Civil Procedure interpretation, Article 90, a party making a factual claim generally has to prove it; inadequate proof has adverse consequences for that party. Civil Code Article 1198 places a duty to provide reasonable safety measures on operators or managers of certain public venues and organisers of public events; Article 184 removes civil liability for harm caused to a recipient during voluntary emergency aid. In a Supreme People's Court example, a pharmacy operator's CPR caused rib fractures and lung injury; after an 18-day hospital stay the patient sought more than 9,000 CNY, but the court found the rescuer not liable.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: China Ministry of Health (2011), Technical Guide to Fall Interventions for Older People, full text via Guangdong CDC <http://cdcp.gd.gov.cn/gcdcdfiles/editor/uploadfile/20111027094832117.pdf>, field-care section reproduced by Guinan County Government <https://www.guinan.gov.cn/lnb/zc1__zwgk/zc/aztfl/qt/content_27609778>; National People's Congress (2020), Civil Code, Articles 183 and 184 <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202006/t20200602_463888.shtml>; Supreme People's Court (2023), Typical Cases on Core Socialist Values, case 13, Qi v Sun <https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/408162.html>; Supreme People's Court (second amendment 2022, effective 10 April 2022), Interpretation of the Civil Procedure Law, Article 90 <https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/353651.html>; Civil Code Article 1198 in the same Civil Code text above; Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2023 amendment, effective 1 January 2024), Civil Procedure Law, Articles 152 and 164, text reproduced by Shanghai Development and Reform Commission <https://fgw.sh.gov.cn/ys-syjf-zc-2.4.1-h5/20240408/073febdb2b04464390546232d4089f51.html>; State Council (2006), Measures for the Payment of Litigation Costs, Order 481, Articles 6 and 29 <https://www.gov.cn/ziliao/flfg/2006-12/29/content_483682.htm>; Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2025 revision, effective 1 January 2026), Public Security Administration Punishments Law, Articles 26(1) and 50(5) <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202506/t20250627_699863.shtml>. Additional first-aid guidance: [American Red Cross](https://www.redcross.org/take-a-class/first-aid/performing-first-aid/first-aid-steps).
- Notes: Grade B reflects practical guidance without a measured reduction in deaths from following this sequence. China's Article 184 concerns harm caused during voluntary emergency aid; it does not make every intervention appropriate. Do not put a possibly injured person into a private car on your own initiative when an ambulance can assess and transport them. Stay on the line and follow the dispatcher's instructions. In China, an ordinary passerby generally has no duty to stop and help and may walk on, but there are exceptions, including responsibility for the person or for creating the danger; you cannot abandon someone in your care. Calling about a stranger can mean giving a phone number, receiving a follow-up call, being asked for a statement, or facing an unfounded allegation. A dispute may lead to a lawsuit, complaints to your employer, being filmed or having the footage posted online. A detailed discussion is in [Should you stop for a stranger in distress?](../../docs/遇到陌生人出事该不该停.md). If you decide to help, do not drag the person to their feet: the medical risk includes worsening a head or spinal injury. Venue operators, managers and event organisers in China may have a separate safety duty under Article 1198. In the US call 911; throughout the EU call 112. Rescue duties and liability protections vary by state and country; the Chinese rules here do not decide them. For fall prevention see chapter 1, entry 13; for later symptoms after a head injury see entry 9 here; for a rescuer's costs see entry 40; for preserving evidence after online harassment see chapter 8, entry 14.

### 3. Sudden facial droop, weakness in one arm or slurred speech: call emergency services now; do not wait or drive yourself
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->

- Cost: Remembering three checks costs $0 or €0. Ask the person to smile, raise both arms and say a complete sentence. This is a way to recognise warning signs, not an estimate of ambulance or hospital charges.
- In plain language: If one side of a person's face suddenly droops, an arm will not stay raised, or speech becomes slurred, call emergency services immediately: 911 in the US or 112 across the EU. Note when symptoms began; if nobody knows, tell the crew when the person was last known to be well. Do not give food, water or medicine before clinical assessment. Do not wait to see whether rest helps, and do not drive the person yourself. Earlier treatment of an eligible ischaemic stroke improves the chance of recovery without major disability. In an alteplase study, the odds ratio for a good outcome was 1.75 when treatment began within 3 hours and 1.26 from 3 to 4.5 hours, compared with control. An odds ratio is not a multiplier of one person's probability.
- Benefit: A meta-analysis of individual data from 6,756 patients in 9 randomised alteplase trials for ischaemic stroke measured good functional outcome, a modified Rankin score of 0–1 at 3–6 months. Treatment within 3 hours yielded an odds ratio of 1.75 (95% CI 1.35–2.27); after 3 but within 4.5 hours, 1.26 (1.05–1.51); beyond 4.5 hours, 1.15 (0.95–1.40), which was not statistically conclusive in that group. These figures concern alteplase and functional recovery, not a direct reduction in mortality. Rapid assessment gives clinicians more options to preserve function.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Emberson J, Lees KR, Lyden P, et al. (2014). Effect of treatment delay, age, and stroke severity on the effects of intravenous thrombolysis with alteplase for acute ischaemic stroke: a meta-analysis of individual patient data from randomised trials. Lancet, 384(9958), 1929–1935. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(14)60584-5>. [Published full text](https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/107143/1/107143.pdf); [American Heart Association/American Stroke Association, 2026 guideline summary](https://professional.heart.org/en/science-news/2026-guideline-for-the-early-management-of-patients-with-acute-ischemic-stroke/top-things-to-know); [American Stroke Association, stroke warning signs](https://www.stroke.org/en/about-stroke/stroke-symptoms); [European emergency number 112](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/112).
- Notes: Do not try to distinguish an ischaemic from a haemorrhagic stroke at home; clinicians choose treatment after assessment and imaging. Thrombolysis increases bleeding risk and requires clinical selection. Call 911 in the US or 112 in the EU: an ambulance can alert a receiving hospital and route the patient according to the local stroke system. The onset time, or the last time the person was known to be well, helps clinicians decide which treatments are possible. Do not give water or medicine, wait to see whether symptoms pass, or drive yourself. The 2014 study's group result after 4.5 hours does not mean all later treatment is futile: the US 2026 guideline supports thrombolysis for selected patients with unknown onset or 4.5–9 hours from onset based on advanced imaging; thrombectomy may also help selected patients. This entry's source metadata labels the outcome as mortality, but the reported 1.75, 1.26 and 1.15 are odds ratios for functional outcome, not mortality.

### 4. Sudden vertigo, trouble standing, double vision or loss of sight can also mean stroke: call emergency services
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->

- Cost: Remembering two more warning signs costs $0 or €0. Alongside the smile, arm and speech checks, notice a sudden loss of balance and ask about double vision, loss of sight or a missing part of the visual field. Do not make a dizzy person stand with eyes closed to test balance: they may fall. You can notice whether they can touch their nose with a finger while seated, but do not delay the emergency call for this. This is not an estimate of medical charges.
- In plain language: In a study of 736 people with ischaemic stroke, 14.1% had no facial droop, arm weakness or speech difficulty when they first presented. Adding gait imbalance or leg weakness and visual symptoms reduced the proportion missed in that same group to 4.4%. If severe spinning dizziness and loss of balance, double vision, loss of sight in one eye or an inability to touch the nose accurately starts suddenly, call now: 911 in the US or 112 across the EU. Note when symptoms began or when the person was last known to be well. A normal FAST check, or symptoms that have passed, does not remove the need for urgent assessment.
- Benefit: A retrospective review of 736 patients admitted to the University of Kentucky Stroke Center with acute ischaemic stroke in 2014 found that 14.1% had no FAST symptoms at presentation. Including gait imbalance or leg weakness and visual symptoms reduced the proportion of confirmed strokes missed from 14.1% to 4.4%. These are missed cases among patients already known to have had a stroke at one centre; they are not a measured mortality reduction or the accuracy of a home test in everyone with dizziness.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Aroor S, Singh R, Goldstein LB (2017). BE-FAST (Balance, Eyes, Face, Arm, Speech, Time): Reducing the Proportion of Strokes Missed Using the FAST Mnemonic. Stroke, 48(2), 479–481. <https://doi.org/10.1161/STROKEAHA.116.015169>. [University of Kentucky study abstract](https://scholars.uky.edu/es/publications/be-fast-balance-eyes-face-arm-speech-time-reducing-the-proportion/); [American Stroke Association, B.E. F.A.S.T. signs](https://www.stroke.org/en/about-stroke/stroke-symptoms); [EU emergency number 112](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/112).
- Notes: A stroke affecting the cerebellum or brainstem in the posterior circulation may occur without facial droop or arm weakness; spinning vertigo, veering while walking, double vision or choking when swallowing may be more noticeable. These signs can be mistaken for a neck problem, low blood sugar or benign positional vertigo. Missing the nose on a finger-to-nose check or swaying with eyes closed can suggest impaired coordination, but no single check rules stroke out. Do not ask someone who may fall to stand for a test: call first when symptoms appear suddenly, and record the onset time. In the US call 911; throughout the EU call 112. Seek emergency assessment even if symptoms resolve, because a transient ischaemic attack is possible. This was a single-centre retrospective study, and its authors called for prospective validation. The entry's source metadata says “mortality”, but 14.1% and 4.4% measure recognition of stroke, not mortality.

### 5. A sudden curtain over one eye needs emergency assessment, even if sight returns within minutes
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->

- Cost: Allow half a day to a day for emergency assessment and possible vascular tests. Deciding to seek help requires no purchase; the cost of care depends on the country, insurance and tests, so it is not presented as $0 or €0 or given an invented estimate.
- In plain language: If sight suddenly goes dark in one eye as if a curtain has fallen, blood flow to the retina may be interrupted, although other serious causes are possible. If a retinal artery is blocked, delay may leave less vision to save. Even if sight returns after a few minutes, the episode may warn of a vascular problem and a later stroke. In a study of people with transient ischaemic attack (TIA) or minor stroke, rapid assessment and treatment were associated with a fall in recurrent strokes within 90 days from 10.3% to 2.1%. Those figures describe the entire study group, not specifically people with visual loss. Call now: 911 in the US or 112 throughout the EU. Get emergency assessment where clinicians can investigate both the eye and stroke risk; do not wait for tomorrow's routine eye appointment.
- Benefit: The American Heart Association's 2021 scientific statement calls central retinal artery occlusion a medical emergency and asks care systems to recognise and triage it rapidly; time matters when the retina is ischaemic. In the British EXPRESS study, among patients with TIA or minor stroke referred to a specialist clinic, recurrent stroke within 90 days occurred in 32 of 310 people (10.3%) in the first period and 6 of 281 (2.1%) after rapid assessment and treatment were introduced. This was not a separate trial of treatment for sudden monocular visual loss.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Mac Grory B, Schrag M, Biousse V, et al. (2021). Management of Central Retinal Artery Occlusion: A Scientific Statement From the American Heart Association. Stroke, 52(6), e282–e294. <https://doi.org/10.1161/STR.0000000000000366>; Rothwell PM, Giles MF, Chandratheva A, et al. (2007). Effect of urgent treatment of transient ischaemic attack and minor stroke on early recurrent stroke (EXPRESS study): a prospective population-based sequential comparison. Lancet, 370(9596), 1432–1442. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(07)61448-2>. [EXPRESS abstract in PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17928046/); [American Heart Association statement](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/pdf/10.1161/STR.0000000000000366); [EU emergency number 112](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/112).
- Notes: The B grade reflects that EXPRESS compared successive periods in one population; it did not randomly assign people to early or later care, so other changes over time may have contributed to the difference. Do not dismiss temporary blindness lasting a few minutes as fatigue or low blood sugar: transient retinal ischaemia can be a TIA, with particular concern about stroke in the following days. Painless loss of sight in one eye without redness or tearing deserves special attention, although clinicians must assess other presentations too. Do not wait for a routine ophthalmology visit the next day; seek an emergency department able to evaluate vascular causes and arrange stroke care. The 10.3% and 2.1% figures concern recurrent stroke after TIA or minor stroke in the EXPRESS cohort; they do not give your personal risk after one episode of visual loss. The entry's source metadata says “mortality”, but these percentages measure recurrent stroke, not mortality.

### 6. Chest pressure or tightness can be a heart attack: call emergency services now, not after 15 minutes, and do not drive yourself
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->

- Cost: Recognising the warning signs and making the decision to call requires no purchase: $0 or €0. This is not an estimate of ambulance or hospital charges.
- In plain language: Pressure, squeezing or tightness in the chest that lasts more than a few minutes, or goes away and returns, can signal a heart attack. Do not wait for a 15-minute threshold: call emergency services as soon as you suspect one — 911 in the US or 112 across the EU. Do not try to endure it or drive yourself. In one study of patients with ST-elevation myocardial infarction who underwent angioplasty after arrival, 2.8% died within 30 days when the interval from hospital arrival to opening the artery was no more than 55 minutes, versus 9.7% when it was longer. This comparison starts at the hospital door. It does not set a safe delay before calling or prove that timing alone caused the entire difference in mortality.
- Benefit: An analysis of an Israeli national acute coronary syndrome survey of patients with ST-elevation myocardial infarction reported 30-day mortality of 5/178 (2.8%) when the hospital door-to-balloon interval was at most 55 minutes and 15/155 (9.7%) when it exceeded 55 minutes. The interval runs from arrival to reopening the blocked artery. These observational data do not compare early with late calls for an ambulance.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Karkabi B, Meir G, Zafrir B, et al. (2021). Door-to-balloon time and mortality in patients with ST-elevation myocardial infarction undergoing primary angioplasty. European Heart Journal — Quality of Care and Clinical Outcomes, 7(4), 422–426. <https://doi.org/10.1093/ehjqcco/qcaa037>. [PubMed abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32374838/); [American Heart Association, heart attack warning signs](https://www.heart.org/en/health-topics/heart-attack/warning-signs-of-a-heart-attack); [American Heart Association, first aid for chest pain](https://cpr.heart.org/en/resuscitation-science/first-aid-guidelines/first-aid); [EU emergency number 112](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/112).
- Notes: The study measured delay inside the hospital, not the time from symptom onset to arrival, and did not establish a safe 15-minute waiting period before calling. Early medical assessment gives clinicians more chances to treat a heart attack. Pain or discomfort may be felt in the upper abdomen, back, jaw or arm; shortness of breath, nausea, a cold sweat or sudden weakness may stand out instead. Older adults and people with diabetes may have little or no typical chest pain. Call when you suspect a heart attack, even without classic pain. Do not drive yourself: losing consciousness at the wheel could cause another emergency.

### 7. Sudden, severe chest or back pain that spreads toward the waist: call emergency services and describe how it began
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->

- Cost: Recognising the warning signs and making the call requires no purchase: $0 or €0. This is not an estimate of ambulance, imaging or hospital charges.
- In plain language: If severe pain begins suddenly in your chest or back, especially if it feels tearing or moves toward your lower back or abdomen, call emergency services immediately: 911 in the US or 112 across the EU. Tell the dispatcher where it started and how it spread. A large difference in blood pressure between the arms, a weak or missing pulse on one side, fainting or new weakness are also worth reporting if you already know about them; do not delay the call to test yourself. One possible cause is a tear in the wall of the aorta. In a registry of 464 patients with acute aortic dissection, 27.4% died in hospital. Among patients with the most dangerous type, type A, who did not undergo surgery, 58% died in hospital. These figures describe patients who already had a diagnosed dissection; they do not tell you the chance that your pain is a dissection. Do not drive yourself or wait until tomorrow.
- Benefit: The International Registry of Acute Aortic Dissection recorded 464 patients at 12 referral centres. Sudden, severe, sharp pain was the most common first complaint, though presentations varied. Overall in-hospital mortality was 27.4%. Among patients with type A dissection, hospital mortality was 58% for those managed without surgery and 26% for those treated surgically. People who did not receive surgery were often older or had other serious conditions, so this comparison does not measure the effect of surgery alone. Prompt recognition matters because type A dissection requires immediate specialist assessment and usually emergency surgery.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Hagan PG, Nienaber CA, Isselbacher EM, et al. (2000). The International Registry of Acute Aortic Dissection (IRAD): new insights into an old disease. JAMA, 283(7), 897–903. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.283.7.897>. [Registry abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10685714/); Isselbacher EM, Preventza O, Hamilton Black J III, et al. (2022). 2022 ACC/AHA Guideline for the Diagnosis and Management of Aortic Disease. Circulation. <https://doi.org/10.1161/CIR.0000000000001106>. [Guideline text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9876736/); [EU emergency number 112](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/112).
- Notes: A dissection can resemble a heart attack, but the choice of tests and treatment can differ sharply. Tell the ambulance team and hospital clinicians about the abrupt onset, any change in the pain's location, and any known unequal arm pressures or pulse difference before treatment decisions are made. They decide which imaging and medicines are appropriate; contrast-enhanced CT angiography is commonly used when acute aortic syndrome is suspected. A tearing description or migrating pain is not required for dissection, and a normal-feeling pulse does not rule it out. Poorly controlled high blood pressure, Marfan syndrome, a known aortic aneurysm and a family history of aneurysm or dissection raise concern. Do not take blood thinners on your own or insist on a particular scan. Call 911 in the US or 112 in the EU, and follow the dispatcher's instructions.

### 8. A sudden, unusually severe headache that peaks within an hour needs emergency assessment
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->

- Cost: Calling for help requires no purchase: $0 or €0. Assessment and imaging take time; costs depend on where you live and your coverage. For scale only, the US Medicare-approved 2026 total for an outpatient noncontrast head CT is $212 in a hospital outpatient department, while a Bulgarian hospital lists a self-pay noncontrast head CT at €120. Neither is a quote for emergency care, and neither should delay the call.
- In plain language: A new, severe headache that reaches its worst point within an hour deserves urgent assessment, especially if it explodes to full intensity in seconds. Call 911 in the US or 112 in the EU, and say when the pain began and how quickly it peaked. One dangerous cause is bleeding around the brain, called a subarachnoid haemorrhage. In a Canadian emergency-department study of 2,131 adults with a new nontraumatic headache peaking within an hour and a normal neurological examination, 132 (6.2%) had this bleeding. That percentage describes this selected group, not every headache. Do not take a painkiller and go back to sleep instead of seeking care.
- Benefit: In the prospective study at 10 Canadian emergency departments, 132 of 2,131 eligible patients (6.2%) had subarachnoid haemorrhage. The Ottawa Subarachnoid Haemorrhage Rule identified all 132: sensitivity 100% (95% confidence interval 97.2–100.0%), specificity 15.3%. It calls for investigation when any one of six features is present: age 40 or older; neck pain or stiffness; witnessed loss of consciousness; onset during exertion; a thunderclap headache that peaks instantly; or limited neck flexion on examination. Low specificity means many people flagged for tests will not have a haemorrhage. The rule is a tool for clinicians assessing alert patients with a new severe nontraumatic headache that peaks within an hour and a normal neurological examination; it is not a home checklist and does not cover every dangerous headache.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Perry JJ, Stiell IG, Sivilotti MLA, et al. (2013). Clinical decision rules to rule out subarachnoid hemorrhage for acute headache. JAMA, 310(12), 1248–1255. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.2013.278018>. [Study abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/24065011/); [American College of Emergency Physicians, clinical policy for acute headache](https://www.acep.org/siteassets/sites/acep/media/clinical-policies/cp-headache.pdf); [American Heart Association/American Stroke Association, 2023 guideline](https://www.ahajournals.org/doi/10.1161/STR.0000000000000436); [US emergency number 911](https://www.911.gov/calling-911/) and [EU emergency number 112](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/112). Price examples: [Medicare head CT lookup](https://www.medicare.gov/procedure-price-lookup/cost/70450/) and [St Naum Hospital, Bulgaria](https://svnaum.com/bg/cenorazpis/tzenorazpis/computed-tomography-and-mrt-bg-euro).
- Notes: Speed of onset matters: pain that reaches maximum intensity in seconds is especially alarming, but the studied group also included headaches that peaked later within one hour. Vomiting, a stiff neck, sensitivity to light, fainting or new neurological symptoms add urgency. The study's rule does not apply to someone with a new neurological deficit, and its reported 100% sensitivity in the cohort is not a guarantee for every patient. Its specificity of 15.3% means that a positive rule is a reason to investigate, not a diagnosis. Clinicians usually begin assessment for suspected bleeding with a noncontrast head CT; depending on when symptoms began and what the scan shows, they may need further testing. A negative early check by yourself cannot rule bleeding out. Do not wait for a routine appointment or assume that a slower-onset headache must be harmless. Rebleeding can be fatal, so get evaluated promptly.

### 9. An older person develops confusion or trouble walking weeks after a head injury: seek urgent assessment; a head CT may be needed
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->

- Cost: Medical assessment and a head CT take time. For scale, the 2026 US Medicare-approved total for a noncontrast head CT in a hospital outpatient department is $212, while a Bulgarian hospital lists a self-pay noncontrast head CT at €120. Neither is a quote for emergency care. Do not delay urgent assessment over these examples.
- In plain language: If an older person becomes confused, unsteady, unusually sleepy or weak on one side weeks or months after a fall, seek urgent medical assessment. Ask whether they hit their head in the previous two or three months, even if they seemed fine at the time or cannot remember the fall. Blood may gradually collect beneath one of the brain's coverings, causing a chronic subdural haematoma. A clinician can decide whether a head CT is needed. If weakness is new, drowsiness is marked or symptoms are worsening quickly, call 911 in the US or 112 in the EU. Do not automatically attribute the change to dementia or an old stroke.
- Benefit: A Chinese neurosurgical expert consensus describes chronic subdural haematoma as blood collecting between the dura and arachnoid membranes, usually becoming established in the third week after head injury. It is commoner in older adults. In selected groups cited there, recurrence after surgery reached 33%, mortality reached 32%, and a good overall outcome was reported for 24% of patients older than 90. These are upper estimates and outcomes in particular groups, not an individual's prognosis. The abstract of a systematic review of 18 studies reported annual incidence estimates ranging from 3.39 per 100,000 people in Brazil to 39.1 in the US. In its discussion, the authors compare, for example, 8.2 per 100,000 in one Finnish region with 39.1 in the US. The review says incidence may be up to three times higher among people over 80, but this is not a universal multiplier. Countries, study periods and whether only operated cases were counted differed, so the extremes are not a rate for any particular country or person. Head trauma and falls were common antecedents; heavy long-term alcohol use, anticoagulants and violent injury also appeared. A geriatric review describes confusion, cognitive decline, gait disturbance and sleepiness, which can resemble other conditions of older age.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Zhang J et al. (2021). Expert consensus on drug treatment of chronic subdural hematoma. Chinese Neurosurgical Journal, 7(1), 47. <https://doi.org/10.1186/s41016-021-00263-z>; Dziho A et al. (2025). Global prevalence and incidence of chronic subdural hematoma: A systematic review. Brain and Spine, 5, 105893. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bas.2025.105893>; Mathon B, Shotar E (2026). Diagnostic and therapeutic management of chronic subdural hematoma in elderly patients. Gériatrie et Psychologie Neuropsychiatrie du Vieillissement, 24(1), 30–41. <https://doi.org/10.1684/pnv.2026.1274>. Price examples: [US Medicare outpatient head CT](https://www.medicare.gov/procedure-price-lookup/cost/70450/) and [St Naum Hospital, Bulgaria](https://svnaum.com/bg/cenorazpis/tzenorazpis/computed-tomography-and-mrt-bg-euro).
- Notes: A person may appear well immediately after an injury and later forget that it happened. New problems with walking, awareness or movement need prompt assessment rather than a home diagnosis: a stroke and other illnesses are possible too. Aspirin, clopidogrel, warfarin and other medicines affecting clotting increase concern; tell the clinician about them, but do not stop prescribed treatment on your own. Heavy long-term drinking is another risk factor. For fall prevention, see [chapter 1, entry 13](01_avoid_dying_early.md).

### 10. One leg suddenly swells or hurts: seek medical care; add breathlessness or chest pain and call emergency services
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->

- Cost: A clinician may order a venous duplex ultrasound. For scale, a Pennsylvania practice lists a $150 self-pay fee for a unilateral or limited venous duplex, and a vascular clinic in Lyon lists an indicative €75.60 fee for a lower-limb venous Doppler study. These are scheduled outpatient prices, not emergency-care quotes. Sudden breathlessness calls for emergency help first.
- In plain language: If one leg suddenly becomes noticeably larger than the other, tight, painful or tender, get medical assessment as soon as possible. A clot in a deep vein is one possibility; a clinician may use ultrasound to check. If you also become suddenly short of breath, have chest pain that worsens when you breathe in, a racing heart, faintness or fainting, call 911 in the US or 112 in the EU immediately. A clot may have travelled to the lungs. In a multinational registry of people with acute pulmonary embolism, 17.4% died within three months. That figure applies to patients with the disease, not to everyone with a swollen leg. Travel lasting at least four hours was associated with roughly twice the risk of a venous clot, although the absolute risk remained low.
- Benefit: The ICOPER registry enrolled 2,454 patients with acute pulmonary embolism at 52 hospitals in 7 European and North American countries. Reported all-cause mortality at 3 months was 17.4% (426 of 2,454). The diagnosis had objective confirmation in 2,110 patients (86%); follow-up was completed for 98% at 3 months, with 52 people lost to follow-up. This is neither the risk associated with any suspected leg clot nor an individual's chance of dying. The WHO WRIGHT project found that journeys of 4 hours or longer, during which passengers remain seated and immobile, approximately doubled the risk of venous thromboembolism, which includes deep vein thrombosis and pulmonary embolism. The absolute risk for people sitting immobile on such journeys was about one in 6,000. That estimate concerns long journeys, not every 4 hours spent at a desk.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Goldhaber SZ, Visani L, De Rosa M (1999). Acute pulmonary embolism: clinical outcomes in the International Cooperative Pulmonary Embolism Registry (ICOPER). Lancet, 353(9162), 1386–1389. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(98)07534-5>; WHO (2007). Study results released on travel and blood clots. <https://www.who.int/news/item/29-06-2007-study-results-released-on-travel-and-blood-clots>. [ICOPER study abstract](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/10227218/); [CDC signs of DVT and pulmonary embolism](https://www.cdc.gov/blood-clots/about/). Price examples: [Cardiovascular Experts of Pennsylvania](https://cardiovascularexperts.com/pricing/) and [Médecine Vasculaire Lyon Villeurbanne](https://www.mvml.fr/honoraires).
- Notes: Swelling in both legs may have a cause involving the heart, kidneys, liver or impaired venous drainage; swelling in one leg does not prove a clot either. Risk rises after surgery or a fracture with prolonged bed rest, with a cast, during pregnancy or after birth, with cancer, oral contraceptives and long periods of immobility during travel. A clot that lodges in a pulmonary artery can cause sudden breathlessness, pain on breathing in, a rapid heartbeat, lightheadedness, fainting or coughing up blood. Pulmonary embolism can occur without noticeable leg symptoms. Call 911 in the US or 112 in the EU immediately if these symptoms appear. The 17.4% figure comes from a registry of people with acute pulmonary embolism; it is not a mortality risk for all swollen legs.

### 11. For severe bleeding, press firmly on the wound; use a tourniquet for uncontrolled limb bleeding and call emergency services
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->

- Cost: You can apply direct pressure without buying anything. A home or car first-aid kit can include a reliable windlass tourniquet, provided you learn to use it. As September 2026 price examples, the American Red Cross lists a SOF Tactical Tourniquet for $30.99, while a European seller lists a C-A-T Gen 7 for €38. These are different devices, not an effectiveness comparison. It takes minutes to learn where to place a tourniquet, how to tighten it until bleeding stops and how to record the time; hands-on practice is better than reading alone.
- In plain language: If blood is flowing heavily or spurting, immediately press hard on the wound with your hand, using a cloth if one is available. Ask someone else to call emergency services. If alone, call on speakerphone while maintaining pressure. If life-threatening bleeding from an arm or leg does not stop, place a tourniquet above the wound, tighten it until bleeding stops and note the time. Do not loosen it yourself. Never put a tourniquet around the neck or torso; keep pressing directly on those wounds until help arrives. In a study at a military hospital, 90% survived when the tourniquet was applied before signs of shock and 10% when shock was already present. These were different observed groups, not an individual forecast. Do not substitute a thin cord or shoelace for a purpose-made tourniquet.
- Benefit: An observational study at 11 Texas trauma centres followed 1,026 people with limb vascular injuries over 6 years; 181 (17.6%) received a tourniquet before hospital arrival. Unadjusted mortality was 3.9% with a tourniquet and 5.2% without one, a difference that was not statistically conclusive on its own. After adjustment for demographic, physiological and injury-related differences, tourniquet use was associated with survival: adjusted odds ratio 5.86, 95% confidence interval 1.41–24.47. In a Baghdad hospital study, 232 (8%) of 2,838 casualties with major limb trauma received 428 tourniquets on 309 limbs. Survival was 90% when no shock was present at application, against 10% after shock had developed. All 5 casualties who needed a tourniquet but did not receive one died; 87% of those receiving one survived. Those groups were small or differed in clinical condition, so the percentages are not a precise treatment effect for every casualty. Act in this order: apply direct pressure; use a tourniquet if life-threatening limb bleeding cannot be controlled; get emergency help at the same time; do not loosen the tourniquet.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Teixeira PGR et al. (2018). Civilian Prehospital Tourniquet Use Is Associated with Improved Survival in Patients with Peripheral Vascular Injury. Journal of the American College of Surgeons, 226(5), 769–776. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jamcollsurg.2018.01.047>; Kragh JF et al. (2009). Survival with emergency tourniquet use to stop bleeding in major limb trauma. Annals of Surgery, 249(1), 1–7. <https://doi.org/10.1097/SLA.0b013e31818842ba>. First-aid technique: [American Heart Association and American Red Cross, 2024 first-aid guidelines](https://cpr.heart.org/en/resuscitation-science/2024-first-aid-guidelines) and [American Red Cross tourniquet instructions](https://www.redcross.org/take-a-class/resources/articles/how-to-apply-a-tourniquet). Price examples: [American Red Cross Store](https://www.redcross.org/store/american-red-cross-tactical-tourniquet-by-tactical-medical-solutions/SOFTT-W-RC.html?cgid=shop-by-brand) and [European C-A-T seller](https://wescue.com/en-eu/products/combat-application-tourniquet-cat-gen-7).
- Notes: Place the tourniquet about 5–8 cm (2–3 inches) above the wound, between the wound and the heart, never over a joint; tighten it until bleeding stops. If the first one is fully tightened and bleeding continues, a second can go above it. Tell the emergency crew when it was applied. Do not loosen it periodically: a medical professional should remove it. The Baghdad study found no amputations solely caused by tourniquet use and reported 4 temporary nerve palsies. A thin cord or shoelace is not a substitute for a purpose-made device. A neck or torso wound cannot be treated with a limb tourniquet: keep applying direct pressure; pack a deep wound only if trained. The cited Chinese official teaching materials on bleeding control have not yet been checked here. Call 911 in the US or 112 in the EU.
