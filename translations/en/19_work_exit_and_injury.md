# 19. Working, leaving a job, and workplace injuries

This chapter is about calculating pay, checking what you sign, and watching deadlines. The first three entries concern pay and conditions while you are working, the next six concern leaving a job, and the final seven concern occupational disease and workplace injury. Injury benefits can be much larger than redundancy pay, while the effects of an occupational disease may be impossible to undo. The Chinese rules below apply in China; United States and European Union rules are identified separately.

### 1. Keep your time records and claim the overtime pay you are owed
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. Keep your own copies of time records, shift rosters, overtime approvals, and work assignments sent in workplace chats.
- In plain language: In China, work beyond normal hours must be paid at no less than 150% of the ordinary wage rate. Work on a rest day with no replacement rest day is paid at no less than 200%. Work on a statutory public holiday is paid at no less than 300%, and time off cannot replace that holiday payment. If the employer does not pay, complain to the labour authority. If it still misses a payment deadline set by that authority, it can be ordered to pay you another 50% to 100% of the amount owed. In the US and the EU, check the applicable local rules before calculating a claim: the Chinese three-tier scale does not carry over.
- Benefit: China's Labour Law article 36 still says no more than 8 hours a day and an average of no more than 44 hours a week, but the **current standard schedule** under the State Council's amended working-hours regulation is 8 hours a day and **40 hours a week**. Article 41 ordinarily limits an extension to 1 hour a day, or in exceptional circumstances to 3 hours a day and 36 hours a month. Article 44 sets minimum pay of 150% for extended daily hours, 200% for a rest day when replacement rest is not arranged, and 300% for a statutory holiday. Under Labour Contract Law article 85(3), failure to pay overdue overtime after an official order can result in an additional payment to the worker of 50–100% of the arrears.
- Evidence grade: A

- Notes: In China, a weekly rest day is different from a statutory holiday: moving a weekend around does not automatically make it a 300% holiday. A company approval rule or the claim that you “volunteered” does not automatically erase an obligation to pay for assigned work, but the instructions and approvals will matter as evidence. Special comprehensive or irregular working-hours systems need lawful administrative approval; a manager cannot establish them by assertion alone. See [chapter 8, entry 17](08_protect_your_rights_and_property.md) for the employment-dispute time limit. **United States:** under the federal Fair Labor Standards Act, covered nonexempt workers generally get at least 1.5 times their regular rate for hours over 40 in a workweek. The Act does not itself require 2 times pay or a premium simply because work falls on a weekend or public holiday; state law and agreements can add rights. **European Union:** the Working Time Directive generally caps average weekly work, including overtime, at 48 hours over an applicable reference period, subject to its conditions and exceptions. It does not establish a single EU-wide 150% / 200% / 300% pay scale; check your country's law and agreement.

### 2. Check your total service and the payment for unused annual leave
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. Gather records of work for all employers and social-insurance contributions if they help establish your service history.
- In plain language: In China, you become entitled to paid annual leave after 1 year of continuous work. Its length depends on your **total** service, not just time with your current employer: from 1 year to under 10 years brings 5 days; from 10 to under 20 years, 10 days; and 20 years or more, 15 days. If the employer cannot arrange your leave and you agree not to take it, the statutory payment is 300% of daily pay **in total**. Ordinary wages already paid count toward that amount, so the extra payment is 200%. Read a waiver before signing it.
- Benefit: Entitlement follows 1 year (12 months) of continuous work. Annual leave is 5 days at 1 to under 10 years of cumulative service, 10 days at 10 to under 20 years, and 15 days at 20 years or more. Statutory holidays and weekly rest days are not counted as annual-leave days. When business needs prevent the employer from arranging leave and the worker agrees not to take it, the pay for the unused days is 300% of the daily wage, including the ordinary 100% already paid; the additional amount is 200%. That 300% is the day's total, not a supplement on top of wages already paid. Daily pay is the average monthly wage over the preceding 12 months, excluding overtime, divided by 21.75. Leave is prorated when you join or leave during the year; leave already taken in excess of the prorated amount is not clawed back at departure. If the employer neither gives leave nor pays and then fails to comply with a correction order, an additional compensation amount equal to the unpaid annual-leave remuneration may be due. The State Council regulation took effect on 1 January 2008.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: State Council (2007). Regulation on Paid Annual Leave for Employees, Order No. 514, articles 2–5 and 7. <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2008/content_859865.htm>; Ministry of Human Resources and Social Security (2008). Implementation Measures for Paid Annual Leave of Enterprise Employees, Order No. 1, articles 3–5, 10–12 and 15. <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2009/content_1265995.htm>; the Ministry's official text of the measures. <https://www.mohrss.gov.cn/xxgk2020/gzk/gz/202112/P020211229590352867273.pdf>; United States: Department of Labor. Vacation leave. <https://www.dol.gov/index.php/general/topic/benefits-leave/vacation_leave>; European Commission. Working Time Directive. <https://employment-social-affairs.ec.europa.eu/policies-and-activities/rights-work/labour-law/working-conditions/working-time-directive_en>
- Notes: China's 5 / 10 / 15 days are based on service accumulated across employers. Only when a worker **personally asks in writing** not to take leave can the employer limit payment for those days to ordinary wages. Do not sign that sort of waiver if you want your leave or the corresponding payment. A worker may lose the year's entitlement after at least 20 days of paid personal leave, or sick leave above the applicable threshold: 2 months at under 10 years of service, 3 months at 10 to under 20 years, or 4 months at 20 years or more. Home-visit leave, marriage or bereavement leave, maternity leave, and the period of paid recovery from a workplace injury do not use up annual leave. **United States:** the federal Fair Labor Standards Act does not generally require paid vacation; state law, a contract or an employer policy may give you rights. **European Union:** the Working Time Directive requires at least 4 weeks of paid annual leave. The Chinese 300% unused-leave formula is not a general US or EU formula; check the rules where you work.

### 3. Check the probation period and pay before signing
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. Compare the contract length with the probation period and pay terms before signing.
- In plain language: In China, a contract lasting from 3 months to under 1 year allows no more than 1 month of probation; from 1 year to under 3 years, no more than 2 months; and a contract lasting at least 3 years or an open-ended contract, no more than 6 months. The same employer can agree to probation with the same worker only once. Moving you to another role after confirmation does not make another probation period lawful. Pay during probation must meet the statutory floor, including the local minimum wage. If you have already worked through an unlawfully long probation period, the employer owes compensation for the excess period based on your post-probation monthly pay.
- Benefit: Labour Contract Law article 19 allows at most 1 month of probation for a contract lasting 3 months to under 1 year, 2 months for 1 to under 3 years, and 6 months for a contract lasting at least 3 years or without a fixed end date. There can be no probation for a contract to complete a particular task or lasting under 3 months. If a contract specifies only a probation period, that period is treated as the employment contract term and the probation clause is invalid. Under article 20, probation pay cannot be below the lowest pay for the same post at that employer **or** 80% of the pay agreed in the contract, and it cannot be below the local minimum wage. Under article 83, an unlawful excess probation period already served triggers compensation calculated using monthly pay after probation. An employer ending the contract during probation must have a statutory ground and explain the reason.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2007). Labour Contract Law, articles 19–21 and 83. <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2007/content_711013.htm>; Shanghai Human Resources and Social Security Bureau. Official reproduction of the provisions. <https://rsj.sh.gov.cn/trlzyhshbzbgz_17256/20200617/t0035_1388322.html>; United States: Department of Labor. Fair Labor Standards Act. <https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/flsa>; European Commission. Directive on Transparent and Predictable Working Conditions. <https://employment-social-affairs.ec.europa.eu/policies-and-activities/rights-work/labour-law/working-conditions/transparent-and-predictable-working-conditions_en>
- Notes: Common Chinese violations are 3 months of probation in a contract lasting exactly 1 year, repeating probation after a transfer, and refusing to register social insurance during probation. Insurance obligations begin when employment begins; see [chapter 12, entry 12](12_starting_a_business.md). Dismissal during probation does not automatically mean zero compensation: entry 6 of this chapter deals with unlawful termination and the 2N rule. **United States:** China's 80% pay rule and once-only probation rule are not US federal rules; applicable minimum-wage and overtime protections still apply to covered work, while state law and contract terms may add protections. **European Union:** Directive 2019/1152 generally limits probation to 6 months, subject to justified exceptions and national implementation. Check the member state's rules; do not assume the Chinese 80% floor applies there.

### 4. Check the N calculation when your employment ends
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money; allow about 10 minutes to check the calculation. Have your payslips and the stated reason for termination ready.
- In plain language: In China, if the reason for ending your employment qualifies for statutory economic compensation, each full year with that employer brings one month's average pay. Six months to under a year counts as a year; less than six months counts as half a year. Bonuses, allowances and subsidies are part of the pay calculation, not just basic salary. Check the figure before signing your final settlement: N applies to specified legal grounds, including a lawful economic layoff, rather than every departure.
- Benefit: Labour Contract Law article 47 sets N at one month's pay for each full year of service with that employer. Service of 6 to under 12 months counts as a year, and less than 6 months as half a year. Monthly pay is the average over the 12 months before termination. Bonuses, allowances and subsidies are included; if employment lasted under 12 months, use the months actually worked. If the calculated average is below the local minimum wage, use the local minimum. **Only when a worker's monthly wage is higher than three times the previous year's local average monthly employee wage** is the compensation wage capped at that threefold local average and the years counted capped at 12. Article 46 lists the qualifying termination grounds; the formula does not make every dismissal eligible.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2007). Labour Contract Law, effective 1 January 2008, articles 46–47. <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2007/content_711013.htm>; official publication by the Shanghai Human Resources and Social Security Bureau. <https://rsj.sh.gov.cn/tgjfl_17254/20200617/t0035_1388217.html>; State Council (2008). Implementing Regulation of the Labour Contract Law, article 27. <https://xzfg.moj.gov.cn/front/law/detail?LawID=284>; United States: Department of Labor. FLSA severance-pay guidance. <https://webapps.dol.gov/elaws/faq/esa/flsa/007.htm>; European Commission. Collective Redundancies Directive. <https://employment-social-affairs.ec.europa.eu/policies-and-activities/rights-work/labour-law/employee-involvement/collective-redundancies_en>
- Notes: Check the 12-month average against all monetary pay, including bonuses, allowances and subsidies; basic salary alone is insufficient. The 12-year cap accompanies the high-earner wage cap and does not apply to everyone. **United States:** the federal Fair Labor Standards Act does not require severance pay; a contract, policy or state law may provide it. **European Union:** EU collective-redundancy rules require information and consultation but do not impose a single N-per-year severance formula across member states. Check the law and any agreement where you work.

### 5. Check whether pay in place of notice is due
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money. Compare the reason stated for termination with the date and form of the notice you received.
- In plain language: In China, for one of the termination grounds in article 40, the employer may either give you 30 days' individual written notice or pay one additional month's wage instead. Where the ordinary N compensation is also due, this is commonly called N+1: calculate the additional month separately. Collective economic layoffs under article 41 follow a different procedure; lack of individual 30-day notice does not, by itself, create an automatic “+1”.
- Benefit: Labour Contract Law article 40 lets an employer choose between 30 days' written individual notice and one month's additional wage for three specified grounds. They are: inability to do the original or reassigned job after the statutory medical period; continued inability to do the job after training or reassignment; and a major change in the circumstances underlying the contract when the parties cannot agree new terms. Article 46 makes N compensation due for such a termination, and the payment in place of notice is additional. The implementing rule bases that additional month on **last month's wage**, whereas N uses the average over the preceding 12 months. Article 41 collective layoffs instead require a separate information, consultation and authority-reporting process; it does not create a universal N+1.
- Evidence grade: A

If a termination is presented as an article 41 collective layoff, check its real legal basis and that procedure instead. **United States:** the FLSA itself does not require a discharge notice. The federal WARN Act may require 60 calendar days' notice for a qualifying plant closing or mass layoff by a covered employer; violations may bring back pay and benefits for the period of violation, up to 60 days, subject to its rules. It is not a general one-month payment. **European Union:** Directive 98/59/EC requires consultation and information for qualifying collective redundancies; individual notice and payments depend on member-state law and agreements, not the Chinese 30-day or N+1 formula.

### 6. After an unlawful dismissal, check the right to reinstatement or 2N compensation
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Filing a labour-dispute arbitration claim in China is free; a dispute can take several months. Legal advice or a later court case may cost money.
- In plain language: If your employer in China unlawfully ends your employment contract, you can seek continuation of the contract. If you do not want that or continuation is impossible, the law provides **2N** damages: twice the ordinary economic-compensation amount. This replaces ordinary N; it is not added to it. Review the stated reason and your evidence with legal aid or a lawyer, then decide whether to negotiate or bring an arbitration claim.
- Benefit: Under Labour Contract Law article 48, a worker may request continued performance after an unlawful termination. If they do not request it, or performance has become impossible, article 87 requires damages equal to twice the article 47 compensation standard, or 2N. Article 25 of the implementing regulation makes clear that 2N damages **do not stack** with ordinary N. Labour-dispute arbitration carries no filing charge under article 53 of the Labour Dispute Mediation and Arbitration Law.

### 7. Do not sign a “personal reasons” resignation if your employer is dismissing you
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money; you may need to withstand pressure to sign on the spot and read the document carefully.
- In plain language: If your employer in China is ending your job, do not sign a statement that falsely says you are leaving for personal reasons. An ordinary voluntary resignation normally brings no statutory N economic compensation and can also defeat an unemployment-benefit claim. Leaving because the employer has breached the law, such as by withholding wages or required social-insurance contributions, is different: document the true reason. If you are told, “Sign now and we will pay compensation separately,” get the amount and payment date into a written agreement first. Do not sign an inaccurate statement under pressure; check any actual deadlines and consequences that apply to your case.
- Benefit: Articles 38 and 46 of China's Labour Contract Law distinguish a worker leaving because of an employer's statutory breach from an ordinary worker-initiated resignation. Economic compensation can be due for the former; it is not due for the latter merely because employment has ended. Article 14 of the unemployment-insurance rules makes loss of work against the worker's will one of the conditions for benefits; the responsible authority must assess the real circumstances. An inaccurate “personal reasons” record makes it harder to secure compensation and benefits, though signing does not by itself remove every way to contest what happened.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2007). Labour Contract Law, effective 1 January 2008, articles 38 and 46. <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2007/content_711013.htm>; State Council. Unemployment Insurance Regulation, article 14. <https://xzfg.moj.gov.cn/front/law/detail?LawID=517>; Shenzhen Social Insurance Fund Administration. Explanation of leaving because the employer failed to pay social-insurance contributions. <https://hrss.sz.gov.cn/szsi/gzfw/ywzsk/sbgx/qycbywbslc/content/post_10499294.html>; United States: Department of Labor. State unemployment eligibility and voluntary leaving. <https://oui.doleta.gov/unemploy/content/denialinformation.asp>; European Union: Your Europe. Unemployment benefits and national rules. <https://europa.eu/youreurope/citizens/work/social-security-and-benefits/unemployment/index_en.htm>
- Notes: “It is only for the paperwork” is no substitute for a signed payment agreement. Ask for time to read and retain copies. It would be unsafe to promise that refusing to sign today can never have legal consequences; the contract and circumstances matter. **United States:** there is no nationwide Chinese N formula. A voluntary quit without good cause often disqualifies a worker from state unemployment benefits, but good-cause rules and exceptions vary by state. Record the real reason and ask the state agency. **European Union:** severance and unemployment-benefit eligibility depend on the member state; a resignation does not have the same legal effect everywhere.

### 8. Save your own payslips, time records, contract and work messages before leaving
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money; allow about half an hour while you still have lawful access to your employment records.
- In plain language: Once you return your work computer and lose access to company accounts, getting your payslips, schedules, contract, social-insurance records and work messages can become harder. Keep records that show your pay, hours and assignments. Specific documents help you prove a wage or overtime claim, even where the employer must produce some evidence. Save your own employment records lawfully; the company's source code, customer lists and other people's personal data are different matters.
- Benefit: If your access is cut off, you will still have evidence of contract terms, pay, working time, contributions and instructions. That can support a payment claim or challenge an incorrect reason for dismissal. Without your own copies, preparing a dispute becomes harder, although the documents may still be obtainable later.
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources: Practical advice, with no direct study; see [chapter 7](07_when_money_is_tight.md) for routes to enforce employment rights.
- Notes: Keep only material relevant to your own employment and follow applicable privacy and confidentiality rules. Do not copy employer code, customer databases or technical documents while collecting records; [chapter 11](11_legal_risks_for_tech_workers.md) explains the separate risks. In the United States and the European Union, access and evidence rules depend on the applicable jurisdiction and circumstances.

### 9. After leaving, check unemployment benefits and any noncompete agreement
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money; review your documents and contact the relevant employment service promptly.
- In plain language: After a job ends, do two things: check whether you can register as unemployed and claim benefits, and reread any agreement that restricts work for a competitor. In China, unemployment benefits generally require at least one year of contributions, a loss of work not initiated by you, unemployment registration and a willingness to seek work. An ordinary voluntary resignation can prevent a claim, which is another reason to keep the stated reason truthful as described in entry 7. It is sensible to apply soon, but China currently has no general application deadline for this benefit. Check the noncompete's scope, duration, monthly compensation and release terms; delay alone does not automatically cancel it.
- Benefit: [Chapter 7](07_when_money_is_tight.md) explains how to seek unemployment benefits and other support. [Chapter 11, entry 10](11_legal_risks_for_tech_workers.md) explains Chinese noncompete compensation and ways to challenge a restriction. Acting early helps you avoid delaying payments and gives you time to understand the agreement. Benefit and dispute deadlines vary by country and legal ground; there is no single rule under which both rights simply expire together.
- Evidence grade: C
