# 18. Raising children: costs and benefits

Count both money and time, as in [Chapter 10](10_dating_and_marriage.md), and make the decision for yourself. If you are already pregnant and need a sequence of medical and administrative steps, see [Chapter 27](../../book/27-怀孕和生产.md). The Chinese benefits and employment protections below apply in China; the US and European examples are separate. Check current eligibility and amounts where you live before adding a benefit to your budget.

### 1. Find out which child benefits you can actually claim
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: There is no fee to apply for the Chinese benefit, but the application takes time. In China, apply in the child's place of household registration. In the US or Europe, check the tax authority or benefit office that covers your family.
- In plain language: China's national child benefit pays **CNY 3,600 per year** for each eligible child under three. A child born on or after **1 January 2025** can receive **CNY 10,800** over three full years. A child born earlier who was still under three on that date can receive a prorated amount for the remaining months. One parent or another guardian applies. If you live in the US or Europe, look up your own tax credits and family benefits; do not treat the Chinese amount as money available to you.
- Benefit: China's national baseline is **CNY 3,600 per eligible child per year** until age **3**. For a child born from **1 January 2025**, that can total **CNY 10,800**. A child born before then who had not yet turned three qualifies for the remaining eligible months. The child must meet the scheme's eligibility requirements, including Chinese nationality. Under the Chinese scheme, the benefit is exempt from individual income tax and is excluded from household income when assessing specified means-tested assistance. Some local governments provide additional support.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: [Communist Party Central Committee and State Council offices (2025): child-benefit implementation plan](https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2025/issue_12206/202508/content_7035435.html); [National Health Commission of China (2025): programme questions, including children born before 2025](https://www.nhc.gov.cn/rkjcyjtfzs/c100147/202507/06cb12b180904128ae7e55620b713ac0.shtml); [US Internal Revenue Service: Child Tax Credit](https://www.irs.gov/credits-deductions/individuals/child-tax-credit); [European Union: family benefits and cross-border eligibility](https://europa.eu/youreurope/citizens/work/social-security-and-benefits/family-benefits/).
- Notes: **US:** for the **2025 tax year**, the federal Child Tax Credit is worth up to **$2,200 per qualifying child**. It is a tax credit, not an automatic annual cash payment; filing status, income, the child's details and Social Security number requirements matter. A refundable portion has separate conditions. Check state programmes too. **Europe:** family allowances vary by country and family circumstances; there is no single EU-wide amount in euros. Put only benefits you are likely to receive into a household budget in **$** or **€**. Even the full **CNY 10,800** for three years covers only a small part of child-rearing costs, so do not base the decision to have a child on that payment alone.

### 2. Check the length of maternity leave and who pays for it
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Checking your leave and insurance rights costs nothing. Ask your employer or insurer what documents you need, who pays during leave and which earnings are used in the calculation.
- In plain language: In China, statutory maternity leave is **98 days**, up to **15** of them before birth. Add **15 days** for a difficult birth and **15 days for each additional baby** in a multiple birth. Where the worker is covered by maternity insurance, the fund pays the maternity allowance using the employer's average monthly employee wage in the preceding year. If she is not covered, the employer pays based on her wage before leave. Provincial rules may add leave. Do not apply these Chinese durations or payment rules to a US or European job.
- Benefit: Articles **7** and **8** of China's regulation establish the **98-day** baseline, the possible **15 prenatal days**, **15 more days** for a difficult birth and **15 days for each additional baby** and the insured-versus-uninsured payment rule. Some Chinese provinces add **60 days**, giving **158 days** in those provinces; **158 days is not one nationwide entitlement**. The payer and rate for additional leave also depend on local rules.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: [State Council of China (2012): Decree No. 619, Special Rules on the Labor Protection of Female Employees, Articles 7–8](https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2012-05/07/content_6584.htm); [Chinese Ministry of Justice: example of a local 60-day extension](https://www.moj.gov.cn/pub/sfbgw/flfggz/flfggzdfzwgz/201609/t20160921_141453.html); [US Department of Labor: Family and Medical Leave Act](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fmla/faq); [European Union: Directive 92/85/EEC, Articles 8 and 11](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A31992L0085).
- Notes: **US:** eligible workers at covered employers can take up to **12 workweeks** of job-protected leave under the federal FMLA for birth and bonding, but the FMLA itself requires **unpaid** leave. State rules, employer plans and some federal employment may provide paid leave. **EU:** Directive 92/85/EEC requires at least **14 continuous weeks** of maternity leave, including at least **two compulsory weeks**; national law determines the detailed duration and how pay or an adequate allowance is provided. Check the country where you work. China's national **98-day** floor plus a **60-day** provincial extension equals **158 days** only where that extension applies. Leave length, income during leave and job protection are separate questions.

### 3. Keep a record if pregnancy or breastfeeding affects your pay or job
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Save pay slips, written reassignment or dismissal notices and messages at no charge. Getting legal help or pursuing a complaint can take time.
- In plain language: In China, an employer may not reduce a worker's pay or dismiss her because of pregnancy, childbirth or breastfeeding. While she breastfeeds a child under one year old, she has **one hour** for breastfeeding during the working day, and the employer may not assign overtime or night work. Keep documents showing what changed and when. US and European workers also have pregnancy-related protections, but the exact rights and ways to enforce them differ.
- Benefit: Article **5** of China's regulation prohibits pay cuts and dismissal for those reasons. Article **9** establishes the nursing hour and the overtime and night-work restrictions for a worker breastfeeding a child under **1 year**. Article **13** sets a **CNY 1,000–5,000 fine per affected worker** for violations of **Article 6(2), Article 7 or Article 9(1)**—the specified late-pregnancy work, maternity leave, and overtime/night-work rules. That particular fine does **not automatically apply to every Article 5 pay cut or dismissal**. Other employment remedies and liability rules must be assessed separately.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: [State Council of China (2012): Decree No. 619, Special Rules on the Labor Protection of Female Employees, Articles 5, 9 and 13](https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2012-05/07/content_6584.htm); [US Equal Employment Opportunity Commission: pregnancy discrimination](https://www.eeoc.gov/pregnancy-discrimination); [US Equal Employment Opportunity Commission and Department of Labor: time and space to pump at work](https://www.eeoc.gov/time-and-place-pump-work-your-rights); [European Union: Directive 92/85/EEC, Article 10 on dismissal](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=CELEX%3A31992L0085).
- Notes: **US:** federal law prohibits covered employers from discriminating because of pregnancy. Under the FLSA as amended by the PUMP Act, most nursing employees can take reasonable breaks and use a private space, other than a bathroom, to express milk for up to **one year** after birth; that is not China's fixed one-hour rule. **EU:** Directive 92/85/EEC requires protection against dismissal from the beginning of pregnancy to the end of maternity leave, subject to exceptional grounds unrelated to pregnancy that national law permits. If you face a pay cut, reassignment or dismissal, keep dates and records and check the procedure and time limit where you work; [Chapter 19](../../book/19-在职离职和工伤.md) discusses employment disputes more broadly.

### 4. Count the care time spent by each person
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=多 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: Care during the first three years often needs someone available throughout the day and at night. That is not the same as **24 hours of active work every day**. Record hands-on care, travel, illness and interrupted sleep for each adult separately.
- In plain language: Before a child is born, put the practical shifts on a shared calendar: who stays home, wakes at night, takes time off for illness, travels with the child and covers when the other adult cannot. If someone reduces paid work, multiply the hours of work actually lost by their net hourly earnings. Do not treat every minute at home as foregone wages. The cost of food and nappies or diapers is visible on a receipt; time and fatigue can be easier to miss.
- Benefit: A time ledger can show who carries the unpaid work and what paid opportunities are affected. Use the same approach to unpaid labour as in [Chapter 10](10_dating_and_marriage.md). In some households, time costs may exceed spending on formula and nappies or diapers, but that is **not a proven universal ratio**: it depends on earnings, available help and the care arrangement.
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources: The author's practical budgeting approach; no direct study is cited for a universal ratio. [Chapter 10](10_dating_and_marriage.md) uses a comparable unpaid-work calculation.
- Notes: The aim is to agree on the division of work before a child is born, not to calculate a precise “price of a child”. Record opportunities to sleep, get relief and return to work. Compare care by parents, help from relatives and paid care. Explain your assumptions before adding time and purchased services to the same total.

### 5. Budget separately for early care, school years and education afterward
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=多 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: The amount depends strongly on your area, housing and care choices. Build the US budget in **$** and a European-country budget in **€** using actual local prices; making the budget takes time.
- In plain language: In the first three years, a major choice is who provides care: a parent, relatives or a paid carer. During compulsory schooling, housing, transport and activities may weigh more heavily; later, the education path can change the numbers again. One large lifetime total hides those choices. First include benefits, maternity payments and medical reimbursements you have actually confirmed, then calculate the gap in each period.
- Benefit: The **0–3**, **compulsory-schooling** and **post-compulsory-education** periods have different kinds of costs. Separating them shows which choices can change and when cash will be needed. This is a planning method, not a published average cost of raising a child or a claim that housing and lessons dominate in every family.
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources: The author's practical budgeting approach; no direct study is cited to establish average shares for these periods.
- Notes: Do not book a benefit before confirming eligibility, or count a medical bill as reimbursed without checking your coverage. The age at which compulsory education ends and how families pay for later education vary by country. An online figure for “the cost of raising a child” is not decision-ready without its place, year and included expenses. Use separate local **$** and **€** scenarios rather than converting a Chinese price list into another currency.

### 6. Put relatives' wishes, your relationship and hoped-for help in old age on separate lines
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: A serious conversation itself costs **$0 or €0**, but it takes time and a willingness to discuss preferences honestly.
- In plain language: Your parents may want a grandchild; you may hope a child will strengthen a relationship or help you when you are old. Those wishes can matter to you, but you and your partner will bear most of the immediate care and expense. In **20 years**, an adult child might lack either the means or the wish to support you. Write down what changes if you have a child and, alongside it, what changes if you do not.
- Benefit: Comparing both futures keeps other people's expectations from hiding your own costs. Support in old age depends on another person's future circumstances; it is not guaranteed income. As in [Chapter 10](10_dating_and_marriage.md), this comparison can clarify a choice without proving that one life is objectively more worthwhile.
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources: The author's practical approach; no direct study establishes the right personal decision. [Chapter 10](10_dating_and_marriage.md) uses a similar two-sided comparison.
- Notes: Listing only the costs of having a child—or only fears about not having one—is not a balanced calculation. Discuss duties, any voluntary help from relatives and the lives you could accept in either case. A personal decision need not reduce to financial return.
