# 11. Legal risks for programmers and technical workers

> Complete translation: the introduction and all 15 entries. The laws, deadlines, monetary thresholds and court cases below concern China. They are not statements of US or European law.

This chapter concerns your freedom and your money. Programmers and other technical workers can mistake these situations for “just a technical issue,” even when they involve a specific criminal offense or civil liability. Each entry gives statutory figures and examples published by the Supreme People's Procuratorate or Supreme People's Court. The Criminal Law citations link to the consolidated text incorporating Amendment XI, republished by the Beijing traffic police. The entries run roughly from situations a technical worker might most readily underestimate, combined with the severity of the consequences; this is a practical ordering, not a measured risk score. Entry 1 sets out the general approach.

> Legal clarification, 21 September 2026: the relevant Criminal Law provisions have also been checked against the [consolidated text incorporating Amendment XII](https://fgk.chinatax.gov.cn/zcfgk/c100009/c5212248/content.html). A complaint, a technical capability or a monetary threshold alone does not establish a crime: the other elements of the offense still have to be proved. Chinese fines and amounts in reported cases stay in CNY because they are legal or historical figures, not local shopping prices.

### 1. Before you start, consider whose interests you could harm, their ability to pursue a claim, and the evidence your actions leave; get a criminal defense lawyer promptly if you face criminal proceedings
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: 0 to assess the risk. Ask whether the vendor, platform or company could report your conduct, alongside whether it is technically possible. Assume server logs, payment records and group chats could become evidence. Hiring a private lawyer is a separate expense.
- In plain language: Before acting, consider who could complain and why. Server logs, payments and chats can document what happened. In the Chinese police detention procedure described below, the decision on approving arrest may take up to 37 days in the specified circumstances. You can retain a lawyer from the first questioning or the first compulsory measure; detention facilities generally have to arrange a properly requested lawyer meeting within 48 hours. Get help early rather than waiting for that window to end. You can still obtain a defense after prosecution begins.
- Benefit: Any organization or individual who discovers facts suggesting a crime has the right to report them. A case must be opened where the authorities find facts indicating a crime that requires criminal responsibility. After criminal detention, police ordinarily have 3 days to request approval of arrest; special circumstances permit an extension of 1–4 days, and the period can reach 30 days for major suspects in itinerant, repeated or group offending. The procuratorate then has 7 days to decide: 37 days in that extended route. A suspect may retain a defender from the first questioning or compulsory measure; during investigation, that defender must be a lawyer. A detention facility generally must arrange a properly requested lawyer meeting within 48 hours. Conditional release pending trial, known as *qubao houshen*, can be available in circumstances including a possible sentence of public surveillance, criminal detention or a separately imposed additional punishment; the measure may last no more than 12 months. These are national Chinese rules.
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources: National People's Congress (2018), Criminal Procedure Law, as amended in 2018, Articles 34, 39, 67, 79, 91, 110 and 112. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/zdgz/201810/t20181027_396818.shtml> Full text also available from the [State Administration for Market Regulation](https://www.samr.gov.cn/zw/zfxxgk/fdzdgknr/bgt/art/2025/art_48b3b0c3f954448e996602a71fc6f878.html).
- Notes: The 37 days concern detention through the arrest-approval decision in the extended procedure, not a cap on all custody. Investigative custody can follow an approved arrest. Assistance during those first 37 days can include a meeting, arguments to the procuratorate against approving arrest, and an application for conditional release. That does not make representation after prosecution “too late”: Article 34 expressly preserves a defendant's right to retain a defender at any time. The 48-hour meeting rule has documentary requirements; during investigation, national-security and terrorism cases require the investigating authority's permission for a meeting. Conditional release can require a guarantor or financial security and is not identical to bail in every other country. The remaining entries explain possible grounds for a complaint. Assessing evidence means understanding exposure and preserving records, not concealing them; legality does not depend on how powerful a potential complainant is.

### 2. Don't develop or sell game cheats; “it only reads the screen” does not settle whether an AI tool is lawful
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: 0; turn down these contracts and the income from these “little tools.”
- In plain language: For the prohibited tools covered by Chinese law, 20 instances of supply to people or 5,000 CNY in illegal gains can meet a “serious circumstances” threshold. The usual sentencing tier allows up to 3 years; particularly serious circumstances carry 3–7 years. Those thresholds can be reached quickly. A cheat that read and altered game memory to provide wallhacks and automatic aiming generated over 80,000 CNY in sales in 3 months, with Wang personally gaining over 30,000 CNY. He received 1 year in prison, suspended for 1 year, and a 5,000 CNY fine. In a separate unauthorized private-server case, modified game-server code brought in over 20 million CNY in player top-ups; sentences ranged from 2 years 6 months to 4 years, and the largest fine was 10 million CNY.
- Benefit: Article 285(3) covers supplying programs or tools specially designed for intrusion into or unlawful control of computer systems, as well as knowingly supplying tools for others to commit those acts. Serious cases carry up to 3 years' imprisonment or criminal detention, with a fine or a fine alone; particularly serious cases carry 3–7 years plus a fine. For the relevant category of tools, serious circumstances include supply on at least 20 person-occasions or illegal gains of at least 5,000 CNY. Deliberately circumventing or disabling a rights holder's copyright-protection measures can also fall under Article 217(6), subject to its profit-purpose and seriousness conditions: up to 3 years' imprisonment or criminal detention with a fine or a fine alone, or 3–10 years plus a fine at the higher tier. Reported Chinese cases include Wang's memory-reading and memory-modifying cheat: over 80,000 CNY in sales and over 30,000 CNY in personal gains in 3 months; 1 year suspended for 1 year and a 5,000 CNY fine. Sales of the “TZ” cheat series exceeded 200,000 CNY; Xiang received 3 years suspended for 3 years and a 20,000 CNY fine. Liu and others modified game-server programs to operate unauthorized private servers, taking over 20 million CNY in top-ups; prison terms ranged from 2 years 6 months to 4 years and fines from 1.3 million to 10 million CNY.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: National People's Congress (2020), Criminal Law incorporating Amendment XI, Articles 285(3) and 217(6). <https://jtgl.beijing.gov.cn/jgj/jgxx/flfg/fl/11033925/index.html> Supreme People's Court and Supreme People's Procuratorate (2011), interpretation on criminal cases endangering computer-system security, Article 3; republished by Shenzhen police. <https://ga.sz.gov.cn/ZWGK/ZCFG/ZCJD/content/post_1304363.html> Supreme People's Procuratorate (2026), Shanghai Jing'an game-cheat prosecutions. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/zdgz/202608/t20260825_735480.shtml> The [procuratorial newspaper's full report](https://www.jcrb.com/livenews/2026/202608/t20260825_998170.html) confirms the sales, gains and sentences. Supreme People's Procuratorate (2021), representative intellectual-property cases from 2020, private-server case 7. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/xwfbh/wsfbt/202104/t20210425_516525.shtml> [Individual case text](https://www.spp.gov.cn/xwfbh/dxal/202104/t20210425_517110.shtml).
- Notes: The cheat cases cited here involved reading or modifying memory and bypassing protection. No official judgment about an otherwise comparable screen-capture-and-simulated-keypress-only tool was identified in this review. The ticket case in entry 3 shows that an offense can arise without memory modification or breaking the underlying system. It does not prove that every screen-reading AI tool, accessibility aid or authorized automation is criminal. Authorities assess the evidence and legal elements; a vendor's complaint does not determine guilt by itself, nor does the developer's own label determine legality. The 20-person-occasion threshold counts occasions of supply to people, not necessarily 20 different buyers. It is not universal: Article 3 sets a lower threshold for certain financial-system tools and also includes losses and other serious circumstances. Being below 20 does not establish legality. The private-server reference is case 7 in the linked collection.

### 3. Don't build or sell scripts for unauthorized ticket grabbing, flash-sale abuse, fake orders or promotional fraud—even if they just automate clicks
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: 0.
- In plain language: Avoiding memory modification or a break-in does not make an unauthorized script lawful. In a Chinese ticket case, the operator imported purchased 12306 accounts and used multiple threads to submit requests at high speed. He obtained over 200 tickets and more than 20,000 CNY in illegal gains, receiving 1 year 6 months in prison, suspended for 1 year 6 months, and a 5,000 CNY fine. The serious-circumstances thresholds relevant to illegal acquisition of system data include 5,000 CNY in illegal gains or 10,000 CNY in loss. Separately, an online seller of ticket-grabbing software was ordered to pay 20,000 CNY in an unfair-competition case.
- Benefit: Under Article 285(2), illegally obtaining data from computer systems through intrusion or other technical means, in violation of national provisions and in serious circumstances, carries up to 3 years' imprisonment or criminal detention, with a fine or a fine alone; particularly serious cases carry 3–7 years plus a fine. The interpretation includes illegal gains of at least 5,000 CNY or losses of at least 10,000 CNY among the serious-circumstances grounds. In the reported criminal case, purchased 12306 accounts were imported in bulk and multiple threads sent dense bursts of ticket requests. The operator obtained over 200 tickets for customers and more than 20,000 CNY in illegal gains: 1 year 6 months suspended for 1 year 6 months, plus a 5,000 CNY fine. In a separate civil case, Zheng sold software targeting a ticketing app through an online shop; the court found unfair competition and awarded 20,000 CNY, covering economic loss and reasonable enforcement expenses. Both examples concern China.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: National People's Congress (2020), Criminal Law incorporating Amendment XI, Article 285(2). <https://jtgl.beijing.gov.cn/jgj/jgxx/flfg/fl/11033925/index.html> Supreme People's Court and Supreme People's Procuratorate (2011), interpretation on computer-system-security offenses, Article 1. <https://ga.sz.gov.cn/ZWGK/ZCFG/ZCJD/content/post_1304363.html> Supreme People's Procuratorate (2026), Shanghai Jing'an prosecution for bulk ticket purchases using unauthorized software. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/dfjcdt/202607/t20260707_731426.shtml> Supreme People's Court (2025), representative intellectual-property cases from 2024, case 7: ticket-grabbing software and unfair competition. <https://enipc.court.gov.cn/zh-cn/news/view-4205.html> [Court publication of the cases](https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/462881.html).
- Notes: In the criminal case, the software did not break or damage the platform's underlying system. The case involved unauthorized bulk requests using purchased accounts, not speed alone. Article 285 can apply without memory modification; receiving fees to obtain tickets and selling abusive scripts can both involve profit-making. The cited materials do not establish the outcome for a single personal use, and that absence is not a legal exemption. These cases do not make all automated clicking, legitimate flash-sale purchases or authorized API use criminal. The offense's other elements and the actual conduct still matter; the monetary thresholds are not the only grounds in the interpretation.

### 4. Keep scraping within your authorization: prefer public pages, don't bypass protection, and don't collect personal data or sell scraped datasets without a lawful basis
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: 0 to check robots.txt and API permissions. If data requires a login, token or signature check, establish your right to access it before proceeding; do not obtain it by defeating access controls.
- In plain language: Unauthorized acquisition of system data can carry up to 3 years in the ordinary serious tier or 3–7 years in particularly serious cases. Personal data adds another risk. Under China's 2017 interpretation, relevant serious-circumstances thresholds include 50 records of location, communications content, credit or property information; 500 records of accommodation, communications logs, health or transaction information; 5,000 other personal-information records; or 5,000 CNY in illegal gains. A developer disguised photo theft as a “beauty score” app and took 1,751 photos. His case also involved distributing a leaked personal-data database; he received 3 years in prison, suspended for 3 years. The official account does not describe all 1,751 photos as facial images, or the database distribution as a sale.
- Benefit: Unlawful system-data acquisition can attract the penalties in entry 3: up to 3 years or, in particularly serious cases, 3–7 years. Under Article 253-1 and the 2017 interpretation, unlawful acquisition, sale or provision of personal information can reach the serious tier at 50 location/communications-content/credit/property records; 500 accommodation/communications-log/health/transaction records; 5,000 other personal-information records; or 5,000 CNY in illegal gains. The lower tier permits up to 3 years' imprisonment or criminal detention, with a fine or a fine alone; the particularly serious tier carries 3–7 years plus a fine. In Li's case, a fake “beauty score” app stole 1,751 photos, some containing over 100 personal-information records. He also obtained and distributed a leaked database containing over 81 million records; the reported distribution was free. His sentence was 3 years suspended for 3 years. In another Chinese case, technology company Z scraped a delivery platform's shop and order data, causing over 40,000 CNY in losses. It was investigated for illegal acquisition of computer-system data; after remediation and compensation, prosecutors decided in May 2022 not to prosecute the company and 14 individuals.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: National People's Congress (2020), Criminal Law incorporating Amendment XI, Articles 285(2) and 253-1. <https://jtgl.beijing.gov.cn/jgj/jgxx/flfg/fl/11033925/index.html> Supreme People's Court and Supreme People's Procuratorate (2017), interpretation on personal-information offenses, Articles 3–5. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/xwfbh/wsfbt/201705/t20170509_190088.shtml> [Text in the Supreme People's Court Gazette](https://gongbao.court.gov.cn/Details/f9bbdd79e39de33c7b3417fa4061ff.html). Supreme People's Procuratorate (2022), representative personal-information cases, case 2. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/xwfbh/wsfbt/202212/t20221207_594915.shtml> [Detailed 2021 account of Li's case](https://www.spp.gov.cn/zdgz/202109/t20210913_529323.shtml). Supreme People's Procuratorate (2025), enterprise-data-security cases, company Z example. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/xwfbh/wsfbh/202501/t20250124_680670.shtml> [Procuratorial account of the May 2022 non-prosecution decision](https://jinyun.lsjc.gov.cn/jcyoutWeb_jy/site/waiwang/ygjw-ywzn/info/2022/6505.html).
- Notes: Article 3 of the 2017 interpretation includes providing lawfully collected personal information to someone else without the data subject's consent within “providing personal information,” with an exception for processing that makes people unidentifiable and cannot be reversed. Exporting a customer table to an outside partner therefore needs scrutiny too: 5,000 records can fit in a small table. This does not dispense with the other legal conditions for a crime. The listed thresholds are not exhaustive; lower quantities can matter under other provisions. **Clarifications:** A login or token can be part of authorized access and is not itself evidence of circumventing protection. Conversely, a public page or a permissive robots.txt file does not settle all privacy, copyright or competition questions. Avoiding collection and resale is a cautious practical boundary, not a claim that every lawful dataset sale is prohibited. The Li case distinguishes photos from personal-information records and free distribution from selling. The company Z case ended in non-prosecution after the authorities considered the circumstances, compensation and independently assessed remediation; it must not be presented as a conviction or a guarantee that remediation always prevents prosecution.

### 5. Don't take jobs developing illegal gambling, fraud or pornography apps, or set up payment channels or unblock accounts for these “clients”
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: 0. Turn down this kind of high-paying outsourcing work with vague requirements.
- In plain language: A lucrative but evasively described job can make you an accomplice to a gambling operation. The 3-year maximum for helping online criminal activity is not a universal cap. A turnkey gambling website business involved nearly 7 billion CNY; 79 people in the wider series of cases received final sentences ranging from 6 months to 9 years and 9 months. They were not all developers. An esports betting app case involved a Hainan group of 28 suspects, including technical, sales and customer support staff; the report gives prison terms of 1 year to 3 years and 6 months for Chen Dong, Yu Ming and others, rather than identifying all 28 as convicted programmers. A man who unblocked QQ accounts for fraudsters received 1 year and 6 months and a 20,000 CNY fine.
- Benefit: Under Chinese Criminal Law Article 287-2, knowingly providing technical support, payment settlement or other assistance for online crimes, in serious circumstances, carries up to 3 years' imprisonment or criminal detention, with a fine imposed either in addition or alone. If the conduct also constitutes another crime, the provision carrying the heavier punishment applies. Running a casino under Article 303(2) carries up to 5 years' imprisonment, criminal detention or public surveillance, plus a fine; serious cases carry 5–10 years plus a fine. In the Zhang Sheng cases, the group developed gambling games and sold turnkey websites, charging setup and reporting fees; the amount involved was nearly 7 billion CNY. Zhang Sheng received 9 years and 9 months and a 50 million CNY fine. Wang Qiang, who headed outsourced development, was among those implicated; the report's total of 79 people and 6-month to 9-year-and-9-month range covers the wider series of convictions for several offenses. Chen Dong and others developed and ran the esports betting app, with technical work based in Hainan; the group of 28 suspects included several different roles, and the reported prison terms were 1 year to 3 years and 6 months, plus fines. In the QQ case, Zhang ran a studio that unblocked accounts for a fraud group and received 1 year and 6 months and a 20,000 CNY fine. These are Chinese laws and cases.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: National People's Congress (2020), Criminal Law incorporating Amendment XI, Articles 287-2 and 303(2): <https://jtgl.beijing.gov.cn/jgj/jgxx/flfg/fl/11033925/index.html>; Supreme People's Procuratorate (2023), turnkey gambling websites involving nearly 7 billion CNY: <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/zdgz/202307/t20230711_621248.shtml>; Supreme People's Procuratorate (2022), the Minhang esports betting case: <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/zdgz/202208/t20220805_569952.shtml>; Supreme People's Procuratorate (2025), typical cases on helping online criminal activity and related crimes, case 1: <https://www.spp.gov.cn/xwfbh/wsfbt/202507/t20250728_702377.shtml>. For assessing knowledge, see the [2019 judicial interpretation, Article 11](https://www.cac.gov.cn/2019-10/25/c_1573534999086260.htm) and the [2025 joint guidance](https://gongbao.court.gov.cn/Details/88ebf70a60488ce712b53141665b5e.html).
- Notes: A client does not have to confess their intended crime for knowledge to be established. Abnormally high fees, deposit and withdrawal functions combined with betting odds, and evasiveness about the purpose can be relevant evidence. Assess the whole picture; a high price alone does not automatically prove knowledge, and contrary evidence matters. Developers who knowingly participate in running a casino can be treated as accomplices, outside the 3-year ceiling for the separate assistance offense. The examples here concern gambling and fraud; they do not establish that every app involving gambling or adult content is illegal in every country.

### 6. Hand over accounts and access when you leave; don't delete databases, leave backdoors or lock the company out, even if it owes you money
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: 0. Pursue unpaid wages through the applicable labor dispute process, including labor arbitration in China; see chapter 7.
- In plain language: A password supplied by the company does not give you permission to use its systems after you leave. Unauthorized control in serious circumstances can carry up to 3 years; particularly serious cases carry 3–7 years. Merely logging in is not, by itself, proof of every element of that offense. Deliberately damaging systems or data can carry up to 5 years, or at least 5 years where the consequences are particularly serious. After leaving his employer, senior engineer Lü used an old administrator account to change a shared server's password and delete data and logs. Losses exceeded 120,000 CNY; he received a 3-year sentence suspended for 5 years and a 30,000 CNY fine. Necessary recovery costs can count toward the loss, so tens of thousands of CNY can matter, but a repair bill alone does not establish a crime.
- Benefit: Chinese Criminal Law Article 285(2) punishes unlawful control of a computer information system in serious circumstances with up to 3 years' imprisonment or criminal detention and a fine, either additional or standalone; particularly serious cases carry 3–7 years plus a fine. Article 286 covers interference with system functions that prevents normal operation and has serious consequences, as well as specified destructive interference with stored, processed or transmitted data or programs that has serious consequences. The ordinary penalty is up to 5 years' imprisonment or criminal detention; particularly serious consequences carry at least 5 years' imprisonment. Lü's unauthorized access, password change and deletion caused losses exceeding 120,000 CNY. The court found that the system's functions had not been damaged and convicted him of unlawful control, rather than system destruction: 3 years suspended for 5 years, plus 30,000 CNY. In another example, software employee Bai deliberately inserted faulty code into an upgrade to pressure a customer over payment. The customer could no longer log in and lost more than 40,000 CNY. The procuratorate discusses this as a case involving damage to a computer information system; the cited account does not state a final verdict.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: National People's Congress (2020), Criminal Law incorporating Amendment XI, Articles 285(2) and 286: <https://jtgl.beijing.gov.cn/jgj/jgxx/flfg/fl/11033925/index.html>; Supreme People's Procuratorate (2024), typical cases on prosecuting cybercrime and improving cyberspace governance, case 6: <https://www.spp.gov.cn/xwfbh/wsfbh/202402/t20240223_644505.shtml>; Supreme People's Procuratorate (2025), protecting enterprise data security and innovation, the Bai example: <https://www.spp.gov.cn/xwfbh/wsfbh/202501/t20250124_680670.shtml>. The [2011 judicial interpretation, Article 11](https://ga.sz.gov.cn/ZWGK/ZCFG/ZCJD/content/post_1304363.html) includes direct losses and necessary costs of restoring data and functions in economic loss.
- Notes: Possessing old credentials and having current authorization are different things. Whether access amounts to unlawful control depends on the actual conduct and statutory conditions, not simply a successful login. Recovery costs can contribute to the relevant loss threshold; the amount, causation and other elements still need proof. Lü's case describes his departure, without establishing that he was dismissed. The 2025 press release presents Bai as an example rather than a numbered “case 7,” and does not confirm the procedural outcome.

### 7. Don't take company source code, client lists or technical documents when you leave, upload them to personal cloud storage, or reuse them at your next job without permission
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: 0. Complete the handover, then remove company copies from personal devices and cloud accounts under an authorized process. Preserve records that must be retained; don't unilaterally destroy company data or evidence.
- In plain language: Writing code yourself does not necessarily mean you own the rights or may take it to another employer. Equally, a client list is not automatically a trade secret. In one case, former employees used security inspection system source code, core algorithms and business information at a new company. Four defendants received terms ranging from 10 months to 5 years and fines of 60,000–500,000 CNY; the company was fined 4 million CNY. The published account does not assign the maximum 5-year sentence and 500,000 CNY fine specifically to Sun. In another case, Tian exploited a system flaw to download more than 33,000 design files he had not created and received 1 year and 10 months plus a 100,000 CNY fine. A company may also seek civil compensation alongside criminal proceedings.
- Benefit: Under Chinese Criminal Law Article 219, disclosing or using trade secrets in breach of a confidentiality obligation can, in serious circumstances, carry up to 3 years' imprisonment or criminal detention, with a fine imposed additionally or alone. Particularly serious cases carry 3–10 years plus a fine. Article 10 of the 2025 Anti-Unfair Competition Law also covers acquiring secrets through electronic intrusion and inducing or helping others breach confidentiality obligations. The protected information must be unknown to the public, have commercial value, and be subject to confidentiality measures by the rights holder. In the security inspection case involving Sun and other former employees, technical secrets and business secrets were used for a competing business. The published sentencing range for four defendants is 10 months to 5 years and 60,000–500,000 CNY in fines; the new company received a 4 million CNY fine. Tian's unauthorized downloads included more than 33,000 design files he had not designed; he received 1 year and 10 months and a 100,000 CNY fine. Engineer Yang stored app module source code on a personal computer and in the cloud, then used it for a similar app after changing jobs. The procuratorate describes a trade secret infringement case, but the cited press release gives no final verdict. These examples concern China.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: National People's Congress (2020), Criminal Law incorporating Amendment XI, Article 219: <https://jtgl.beijing.gov.cn/jgj/jgxx/flfg/fl/11033925/index.html>; NPC Standing Committee (2025), revised Anti-Unfair Competition Law, Article 10: <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202506/t20250627_699862.shtml>; Supreme People's Procuratorate (2025), typical intellectual property protection cases, case 7: <https://www.spp.gov.cn/xwfbh/wsfbh/202504/t20250423_693691.shtml>; Supreme People's Procuratorate (2020), intellectual property protection cases for 2019, case 16: <https://www.spp.gov.cn/xwfbh/wsfbh/202004/t20200425_459623.shtml>; Supreme People's Procuratorate (2025), protecting enterprise data security and innovation, the Yang example: <https://www.spp.gov.cn/xwfbh/wsfbh/202501/t20250124_680670.shtml>. See also the [NPC's full 2025 Anti-Unfair Competition Law](https://www.npc.gov.cn/npc/c2/c30834/202506/t20250627_446247.html) and [Copyright Law, Article 18](https://www.ncac.gov.cn/xxfb/flfg/flfg_532/202103/t20210309_50530.html).
- Notes: See entry 11 for work-related software rights. Ownership depends on the applicable law, the circumstances of development and any valid agreement; “I wrote it” is not enough, but “all code always belongs to the company” is also too broad. Client lists can contain protected business information only if the requirements for a trade secret are met. Civil damages are possible, not an automatic additional judgment. Sun handled international business, while other defendants were involved in technical secrets; the group sentencing range should not be attributed to him individually. The 2025 press release's Yang example is not numbered “case 5.”

### 8. Don't run your programs on other people's computers, servers or cameras without permission, or use company machines for unauthorized mining
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: 0.
- In plain language: In China, unlawfully controlling 20 or more computer systems is one way to meet the “serious circumstances” threshold: up to 3 years, or 3–7 years for particularly serious cases. Fewer than 20 is not a safe harbor; other criteria can apply. Conduct that does not constitute a crime can still lead to detention of up to 5 days, with a possible 50,000–500,000 CNY fine; more serious administrative cases can bring 5–15 days and a possible 100,000–1 million CNY fine. There is also a career consequence: the specified public security punishment for violating Cybersecurity Law Article 29 bars a person from cybersecurity management and key network operations roles for 5 years; criminal punishment for such a violation brings a lifetime bar. This is not a ban triggered by every unrelated administrative or criminal penalty.
- Benefit: Under Criminal Law Article 285(2), unlawful control in serious circumstances carries up to 3 years' imprisonment or criminal detention, with a fine imposed additionally or alone; particularly serious cases carry 3–7 years plus a fine. The 2011 judicial interpretation includes unlawful control of at least 20 systems among the serious-circumstances criteria. Cybersecurity Law Article 66 provides, for the covered conduct that falls short of a crime, confiscation of illegal proceeds and detention of up to 5 days, with an optional fine of 50,000–500,000 CNY; more serious circumstances bring 5–15 days, with an optional 100,000–1 million CNY fine. Its third paragraph sets the 5-year or lifetime occupational ban for the Article 29 violations described above. In a published case, Li bought network camera IP addresses and used software to control 105 cameras and invade people's privacy: 3 years' imprisonment suspended for 5 years, plus a 20,000 CNY fine. In another, Wu and others installed malware in phone motherboards and controlled more than 3.3 million phones to collect verification codes. Five defendants received 2 years to 4 years and 6 months for unlawful control. These are Chinese laws and cases.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: National People's Congress (2020), Criminal Law incorporating Amendment XI, Article 285(2): <https://jtgl.beijing.gov.cn/jgj/jgxx/flfg/fl/11033925/index.html>; Supreme People's Court and Supreme People's Procuratorate (2011), interpretation on crimes against computer information system security, Article 1: <https://ga.sz.gov.cn/ZWGK/ZCFG/ZCJD/content/post_1304363.html>; NPC Standing Committee (2025), Cybersecurity Law adopted in 2016 and amended in 2025, effective 1 January 2026, Articles 29 and 66, including the occupational ban in Article 66(3): <https://www.cac.gov.cn/2025-12/29/c_1768735112911946.htm>; Supreme People's Procuratorate (2022), the Binzhou camera control case: <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/dfjcdt/202205/t20220518_557113.shtml>; Supreme People's Procuratorate (2021), typical cyberspace governance cases, case 6: <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/xwfbh/wsfbh/202101/t20210125_507452.shtml>. The [amendment decision](https://www.cac.gov.cn/2025-10/29/c_1763461514768457.htm) confirms the 2026 effective date and renumbering.
- Notes: The cited Supreme People's Court and Supreme People's Procuratorate materials do not supply a specific company-server mining case. The mining warning rests on the rules governing unauthorized control, not on a claim that every instance of mining necessarily constitutes this crime. The occupational restriction can matter more to a technical worker than a fine. The ban appeared in Article 63 of the 2016 law; the provision verified in the amended text is Article 66(3). Administrative fines in this provision are discretionary additions to detention, not invariably mandatory.

### 9. Don't sell tools or VPN accounts, or set up nodes, for unauthorized cross-border internet access in China
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: 0.
- In plain language: Chinese rules on international internet connections allow police to order a connection stopped, issue a warning, impose a fine of up to 15,000 CNY and confiscate illegal proceeds where the specified requirements are breached. Commercial operation can raise the separate issue of illegal business activity: serious cases carry up to 5 years and a fine of 1–5 times illegal proceeds, subject to the statutory alternatives below; particularly serious cases carry at least 5 years. This entry concerns selling access and setting up nodes. The penalties in Article 14 do not establish a universal ceiling for everything an individual might do online: Article 15 preserves liability under other laws. Enforcement and the legal characterization of particular services can differ; no specific Supreme People's Court or Supreme People's Procuratorate judgment on circumvention is verified here.
- Benefit: Article 6 of China's Interim Provisions on International Networking requires direct international connections to use the international gateways of the national public telecommunications network and prohibits organizations and individuals from independently establishing or using other channels. Article 14 provides for an order to stop the connection and a warning, with a possible fine of up to 15,000 CNY and confiscation of illegal proceeds. Where a commercial operation meets the elements of illegal business activity under Criminal Law Article 225, serious cases carry up to 5 years' imprisonment or criminal detention, with a fine of 1–5 times illegal proceeds imposed additionally or alone. Particularly serious cases carry at least 5 years plus either a fine of 1–5 times illegal proceeds or confiscation of property. Applying that offense requires proof of its elements; using VPN technology does not by itself establish them.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: State Council (1997), Interim Provisions on International Networking, Articles 6 and 14, republished by the Chongqing Agriculture and Rural Affairs Commission: <https://nyncw.cq.gov.cn/wsdw/zz/zhfb/wlyxxaq/202406/t20240619_13306645.html>; National People's Congress (2020), Criminal Law incorporating Amendment XI, Article 225(4): <https://jtgl.beijing.gov.cn/jgj/jgxx/flfg/fl/11033925/index.html>. The [Ministry of Justice's consolidated text, revised in 2024](https://xzfg.moj.gov.cn/mobile/law/detail?LawID=1713) also includes Article 15. See the [Ministry of Industry and Information Technology's explanation of cross-border business rules](https://www.cac.gov.cn/2017-01/26/c_1120381529.htm).
- Notes: Grade B reflects the limits of the case evidence and differences in enforcement, not an assurance that selling access is permitted. Article 14's warning and fine provisions concern the listed connection violations; they also include stopping the connection and confiscating illegal proceeds. They do not exclude liability for other conduct under Article 15. The ministry's explanation distinguishes unauthorized cross-border telecommunications businesses from enterprises obtaining lawful connections for their own office use. This is not a worldwide ban on VPNs or a claim that all corporate VPN use is illegal.

### 10. If your former employer stops paying noncompete compensation, demand payment in writing; after 3 months of employer-caused nonpayment, seek release, and challenge a restriction unrelated to secrets you knew
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: 0 to keep the original agreement and evidence of nonpayment when you leave. Legal representation, if needed, is a separate expense.
- In plain language: These rules protect employees in China. A post-employment noncompete can last no more than 2 years and requires monthly compensation. If compensation was not agreed and you have complied with the restriction, you can claim 30% of your average monthly pay over the 12 months before departure, or the local minimum wage at the place of contract performance if that is higher. If the employer is responsible for 3 months of nonpayment, you can seek termination of the restriction. Since 1 September 2025, the judicial interpretation expressly supports a request to declare the clause ineffective if you never knew or had access to the employer's trade secrets or confidential intellectual property information. In a pharmaceutical company's claim against its chief technology officer Zheng for 7.1 million CNY in contractual penalties, the court limited the restriction to the secrets he actually knew and dismissed the company's claims.
- Benefit: Under China's Labor Contract Law, noncompetes are limited to senior managers, senior technical staff and other employees with confidentiality duties. Post-employment restrictions last at most 2 years, with monthly compensation during that period. Where compensation was not agreed and the employee has complied, Interpretation I provides for 30% of average monthly pay over the preceding 12 months, no lower than the applicable local minimum wage. If agreed compensation goes unpaid for 3 months for reasons attributable to the employer, the employee may seek termination. If the employer seeks early termination, the employee may claim an additional 3 months' compensation. Under the 2025 Interpretation II, employees who did not know or access relevant secrets may seek a declaration that the clause never took effect; restrictions disproportionate in scope, territory or duration to the secrets involved can be invalid to that extent. In Zheng's case, the employer demanded 7.1 million CNY in penalties. The court found that the new employer's products were not substitutes for the relevant products and that Zheng's restriction had to match the confidential information he knew. All the company's claims were dismissed.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: NPC Standing Committee (2012), Labor Contract Law as revised in 2012, Articles 23–24, republished by the Fushun Emergency Management Bureau: <https://yjglj.fushun.gov.cn/008/20260622/d846d3c2-df3d-4c4c-af16-898ede43e1ee.html>; Supreme People's Court (2020), Interpretation I on labor disputes, Articles 36–40: <https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/282121.html>; Supreme People's Court (2025), Interpretation II, Judicial Interpretation No. 12 of 2025, Articles 13–15, effective 1 September 2025: <https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/472691.html>; Supreme People's Court (2025), typical labor dispute cases, case 4: <https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/472681.html>. The [Quanzhou government republishes the full 2025 interpretation](https://www.quanzhou.gov.cn/zfb/xxgk/zfxxgkzl/fgwj/sfjs/202512/t20251225_3245895.htm); [Articles 23–24 of the Labor Contract Law are also reproduced by the Nansha People's Congress](https://www.gzns.gov.cn/rd/flfg/gjflfg/201705/t20170505_345140.html).
- Notes: Keep a written record and formally assert your rights; do not assume the agreement disappears automatically on the first missed payment or after 3 months. If a valid restriction applies and the employer pays, breaching it can require repayment of compensation and payment of agreed penalties. Paying a penalty may not release you from continued performance. A valid noncompete during employment does not depend on separate compensation. Zheng had accessed confidential information: his case concerns the proper scope of a restriction, not an employee who knew no secrets at all.

### 11. Keep personal open-source work separate from your job: use your own time and equipment, and don't mix in company code
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: 0. Read the intellectual property clauses in your employment contract before signing.
- In plain language: In China, software is expressly included among works whose economic copyright can belong to the employer when the statutory conditions are met. For example, the special rule covers software created mainly with the employer's material and technical resources and for which the employer assumes responsibility; the author retains attribution rights under that rule. A valid legal or contractual allocation can also determine ownership. Service inventions can likewise belong to the employer for patent purposes. Working after hours does not settle the issue if you use company equipment, internal resources or confidential business data. Nor does incidental use of a company computer automatically prove that the statutory conditions are met.
- Benefit: Copyright Law Article 18 defines a work made to fulfill an employer's tasks as a work created in employment. Under the general rule, copyright belongs to the author, the employer has priority use within its business, and for 2 years after completion the author cannot authorize a third party to use it in the same way without the employer's consent. A special rule covers engineering designs, software and other specified works created mainly using the employer's material and technical resources, with the employer bearing responsibility, as well as works whose ownership is assigned to the employer by law or contract: the author retains the right of attribution, while the other copyright rights belong to the employer. Software Protection Regulations Article 13 additionally covers software developed for expressly assigned work objectives, software that is a foreseeable or natural result of the employee's duties, and software mainly using specified employer resources for which the employer assumes responsibility. Under Patent Law Article 6, inventions made in performing the employer's tasks or mainly using its material and technical resources are service inventions: the employer normally owns the right to apply and, if granted, the patent. For inventions made using employer resources, an ownership agreement between the parties governs where Article 6 permits it. These are Chinese rules.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: NPC Standing Committee (2020), Copyright Law as revised in 2020, Article 18: <http://www.npc.gov.cn/npc/c2/c30834/202011/t20201119_308796.html>; NPC Standing Committee (2020), Patent Law as revised in 2020, Article 6: <http://www.npc.gov.cn/npc/c2/c30834/202011/t20201119_308800.html>. See also the [Software Protection Regulations, Article 13](https://www.cac.gov.cn/2013-02/08/c_126468744.htm), the [Copyright Administration's consolidated law](https://www.ncac.gov.cn/xxfb/flfg/flfg_532/202103/t20210309_50530.html) and the [Patent Administration's Article 6](https://www.cnipa.gov.cn/art/2020/11/23/art_97_155167.html).
- Notes: Software has specific employment rules, but that does not make every program written by an employee company property. Company computers, an internal network or business data can matter even after hours; the role of those resources, the assigned work and the applicable agreement must be assessed. For a personal project, use your own equipment and time, keep company repositories and confidential data out, and clarify any overlap with your work before publication. Separation reduces disputes; it does not override statutory rights or a valid agreement.

### 12. Follow the license when using GPL or other open-source code in a product; provide source when required, obtain another license, or use a different dependency
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: 0 to check a dependency's license before adopting it. If its terms conflict with your planned closed-source distribution, choose a compatible alternative or obtain another license from the rights holder. You cannot simply relabel someone else's code; a separate license may cost money.
- In plain language: Open-source code still has copyright conditions. In China, compensation for infringement is based on the rights holder's loss or the infringer's illegal gains, with licensing fees used as a reference when those are hard to calculate. Intentional, serious infringement can bring compensation of 1–5 times the amount determined under those methods. In one gateway software case, a company hired its competitor's former employees through an affiliate and produced substantially similar software. It argued that the claimant's code was based on GPLv2 software and should have been open. The Supreme People's Court treated the claimant's possible license breach and its copyright as separate questions and upheld an order to stop infringement and pay a total of 500,000 CNY, including reasonable enforcement expenses.
- Benefit: Chinese Copyright Law Articles 53–54 provide compensation based on actual loss or illegal gains, with licensing fees as a fallback reference, and 1–5 times the assessed amount for intentional infringement in serious circumstances. Where the covered infringement also harms the public interest, authorities can confiscate illegal proceeds and impose administrative fines. In case (2021) Supreme People's Court IP Civil Final No. 51, gateway software developed after hiring the claimant's former employees was found substantially similar to the claimant's software. The GPLv2 argument did not defeat the copyright claim: whether a developer has itself breached GPLv2 is distinct from whether it owns copyright in its contributions. The appeal was dismissed and the injunction and 500,000 CNY award were upheld.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: NPC Standing Committee (2020), Copyright Law as revised in 2020, Articles 53–54: <http://www.npc.gov.cn/npc/c2/c30834/202011/t20201119_308796.html>; Supreme People's Court Intellectual Property Court, “Open-source software copyright infringement,” case (2021) No. 51: <https://ipc.court.gov.cn/zh-cn/news/view-3042.html>. The linked case summary was published on 15 May 2024, rather than 2022. For license conditions, see the [GNU licensing FAQ](https://www.gnu.org/licenses/gpl-faq.html.en).
- Notes: Someone else's use of GPL code does not give you unrestricted permission to copy their work. GPL permits commercial use; source obligations depend on the license version, the covered work and how you distribute it. Private use does not ordinarily require publication under GPL, and providing required source to recipients is not always the same as posting it for everyone. AGPL has additional network-use conditions. For separate Chinese claims directly enforcing GPL against distributors that withheld source, no Supreme People's Court judgment text is verified here; do not treat that as proof that no such cases exist. Grade B reflects reliance on license terms, general copyright law and the cited decision, rather than a dedicated GPL statute.

### 13. Collect only the personal information your app needs, obtain parental or guardian consent for children under 14, and write a clear privacy notice
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: 0 for reviewing permissions and the privacy notice before release. Remove permissions the service does not need; implementing the necessary changes can take staff time.
- In plain language: Collect only information genuinely needed for the stated purpose. For children under 14, Article 31 requires consent from a parent or other guardian. Violations can bring an order to correct the problem, a warning, confiscation of illegal gains and suspension or termination of an app's services. Refusing to comply can bring a fine of up to 1 million CNY; directly responsible managers and other directly responsible people face 10,000–100,000 CNY. Serious violations can bring a fine of up to 50 million CNY or up to 5% of the previous year's turnover, with 100,000–1 million CNY for the responsible individuals. Developers and product staff should understand their own responsibilities; a job title alone does not establish personal liability.
- Benefit: China's Personal Information Protection Law requires a clear, reasonable purpose, processing directly related to that purpose, and the method with the least impact on individuals' rights. Collect only the minimum necessary information. Processing needs consent or another statutory basis under Article 13. Article 31 requires parental or guardian consent and specific processing rules for information about children under 14. Under Article 66, regulators can order correction, issue a warning, confiscate illegal gains and order apps that process information unlawfully to suspend or end their services. Refusal to correct brings an additional fine of up to 1 million CNY, with 10,000–100,000 CNY for directly responsible managers and other directly responsible personnel. For serious violations, the fine can be up to 50 million CNY or up to 5% of the previous year's turnover; responsible individuals face 100,000–1 million CNY and a possible temporary ban on serving as directors, supervisors, senior managers or personal information protection officers in relevant businesses. Business suspension and action to revoke licenses are also possible. For the criminal offense of selling or supplying personal information, see entry 4. These are national Chinese rules.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2021), Personal Information Protection Law, Articles 6, 13, 31 and 66. <http://www.npc.gov.cn/npc/c2/c30834/202108/t20210820_313088.html> The full law is also published by the [Cyberspace Administration of China](https://www.cac.gov.cn/2021-08/20/c_1631050028355286.htm); Articles 7 and 17 cover transparency and information to provide to individuals.
- Notes: Personal responsibility can extend beyond management, but the evidence checked here does not establish how often programmers or product staff are penalized. MIIT publishes enforcement notices about noncompliant apps; no specific removal notice has been verified for this entry. **Legal clarification, 21 September 2026:** explain what information you collect, why, how you process it, how long you retain it and how people can exercise their rights. A privacy notice or a consent checkbox does not authorize unnecessary collection. Distinguish the consent route from the other statutory grounds for processing; do not assume every employee who touched the code is a directly responsible person.

### 14. Arrange the required Chinese website and app filings or licenses before launch, and retain relevant network logs for at least 6 months
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: China's noncommercial internet information service filing has no administrative fee. Allow several weeks for preparation and processing through your hosting provider; the authority's deadline for a complete filing is 20 working days, not a promise about the whole process. Hosting, optional filing assistance and any required security assessment are separate expenses. Assessment requirements depend on the system's security classification; a small website is not automatically exempt.
- In plain language: A free ICP filing does not authorize every business model. Operating without the required noncommercial filing leads to an order to correct the problem; refusal can lead to closure. Failing to display the website's license or filing number carries a 5,000–50,000 CNY fine. Providing a service that requires a commercial license with only a noncommercial filing can trigger confiscation and fines: 3–5 times illegal gains, or 100,000–1 million CNY where there are no illegal gains or they are below 50,000 CNY. Keep the required network logs for at least 6 months. Under the Cybersecurity Law effective from 1 January 2026, failure to meet the relevant security duties can bring a warning, an order to correct and a possible 10,000–50,000 CNY fine; refusal or harmful consequences raises this to 50,000–500,000 CNY, with 10,000–100,000 CNY for directly responsible personnel. More serious consequences have higher penalties.
- Benefit: China requires a license for commercial internet information services and a filing for noncommercial services; neither may operate without the required authorization. Under Article 19 of the internet information service rules, an unfiled noncommercial service must correct the violation and can be closed if it refuses. Unlicensed commercial operations face confiscation of illegal gains and a fine of 3–5 times those gains; with no gains or gains below 50,000 CNY, the fine is 100,000–1 million CNY. Serious cases can lead to website closure. Article 22 sets a 5,000–50,000 CNY fine for not displaying the license or filing number on the homepage. Article 23 of the amended Cybersecurity Law requires internal security rules, protection against attacks, retention of relevant network logs for at least 6 months, data classification, and measures such as backups and encryption of important data. Article 61 provides the current 10,000–50,000 CNY optional initial fine and the 50,000–500,000 CNY tier for refusal or harmful consequences, with 10,000–100,000 CNY for directly responsible managers and other directly responsible personnel. For serious consequences, such as large data leaks, operator fines rise to 500,000–2 million CNY and personal fines to 50,000–200,000 CNY; particularly serious consequences carry 2–10 million CNY and 200,000–1 million CNY respectively. These are national Chinese rules; critical information infrastructure also has separate requirements and penalty provisions.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: State Council (2011), Measures for the Administration of Internet Information Services, 2011 revision, Articles 4, 19 and 22. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2011/content_1860864.htm> Full text also available from [SAMR](https://www.samr.gov.cn/zw/zfxxgk/fdzdgknr/bgt/art/2023/art_483f0dd8eb1b4dc5961e4e008bd4a083.html). Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2025), Cybersecurity Law, amended in 2025 and effective from 1 January 2026, Articles 23 and 61; these were Articles 21 and 59 in the 2016 text. <https://www.cac.gov.cn/2025-12/29/c_1768735112911946.htm> Additional official guidance: [MIIT's app-filing notice, No. 105 of 2023](https://www.miit.gov.cn/jgsj/xgj/wjfb/art/2023/art_dd783a581c9644a4aee10afa582811db.html) and the [Hunan Communications Administration filing guide](https://hunca.miit.gov.cn/bsfw/bszn/art/2024/art_7ef0d8bd3b0d433ba4b9b277f883f74d.html).
- Notes: Watch for a personal noncommercial filing that no longer matches a paid service, or a change of domain, operator or service without updating the filing. App filing is a separate requirement; a website filing does not automatically cover the app. Retaining logs for at least 6 months can help reconstruct an incident, but cannot by itself prove that you were uninvolved. **Legal and technical clarification, 21 September 2026:** the former Cybersecurity Law penalty ranges of 10,000–100,000 CNY for operators and 5,000–50,000 CNY for responsible managers are not the current Article 61 ranges. ICP filing concerns permission to provide the service, not whether DNS can technically resolve a name. For example, [Alibaba Cloud explains](https://help.aliyun.com/zh/icp-filing/basic-icp-service/support/for-the-record-domain-faq) that pointing a domain at its mainland servers without the required filing can cause website access to be blocked. Do not treat successful DNS resolution as permission to launch. Nor does calling a website “small” settle its security assessment obligations; confirm its classification and applicable requirements.

### 15. Check security assessment and algorithm-filing requirements before offering a public generative AI service, and label generated content
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: 0 for checking the published requirements yourself. Prepare the filings with the competent cyberspace authorities where required; assessment, technical changes and outside assistance can cost money, so this is not a zero-cost compliance estimate. A personal tool that is not offered to the public in China falls outside the 2023 interim measures' public-service scope.
- In plain language: If you offer a generative AI service to the public in China and it can influence public opinion or mobilize people, the rules require a security assessment and algorithm filing, including updates or cancellation when appropriate. Plan these requirements before launch and apply their respective deadlines. Label generated images, videos and other covered content. A private tool is outside these particular public-service measures, but still has to respect applicable law. Violations can be punished under the Cybersecurity Law, Data Security Law, Personal Information Protection Law and other applicable laws. The measures themselves also provide warnings, public criticism, correction orders and, for refusal or serious violations, suspension of the relevant service.
- Benefit: Article 17 requires providers of generative AI services with public-opinion influence or social-mobilization capability to conduct security assessments under national requirements and complete algorithm filings, amendments and cancellations under the algorithm recommendation rules. Article 12 requires labeling of generated images, videos and other content under the deep-synthesis rules. The interim measures took effect on 15 August 2023 and apply to services offered to the public in China. Their scope does not include the development or use of generative AI without offering such a public service.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Cyberspace Administration of China and six other departments (2023), Interim Measures for the Management of Generative AI Services, Articles 12 and 17. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202307/content_6891752.htm> [CAC's full text](https://www.cac.gov.cn/2023-07/13/c_1690898327029107.htm) also sets out scope in Article 2 and liability in Article 21. Further rules: [2018 security assessment requirements, Article 7](https://www.cac.gov.cn/2018-11/15/c_1123716072.htm), [algorithm recommendation rules, Article 24](https://www.cac.gov.cn/2022-01/04/c_1642894606364259.htm), and the [2025 Measures for Labeling AI-Generated and Synthetic Content](https://www.cac.gov.cn/2025-03/14/c_1743654684782215.htm).
- Notes: The B grade reflects a regulatory requirement and cross-references to other laws, not a finding that violations have no consequences. No Supreme People's Court source text for a civil case specifically involving AI-generated copyright or likeness infringement has been verified for this entry. **Legal clarification, 21 September 2026:** Article 21 expressly includes its own fallback measures where laws and administrative regulations do not provide a penalty; it also refers to the Law on Progress of Science and Technology. Assessment and filing are distinct procedures: the 2018 rules require submission of an assessment report before launching the specified new service or feature, while Article 24 of the algorithm rules sets 10 working days from providing the service for algorithm filing, 10 working days after a change for an update, and 20 working days after termination for cancellation. These deadlines do not dispense with other launch requirements. From 1 September 2025, the labeling rules address text, audio, images, video and virtual scenes, with visible labels in the specified cases and embedded metadata labels. They also allow an output without a visible label under Article 9's conditions: the agreement must specify the user's labeling duties and responsibilities, and the provider must retain relevant logs for at least 6 months. This is not permission to remove embedded labels maliciously. For ongoing emotional interaction that simulates a person, also check the [separate rules effective from 15 July 2026](https://www.cac.gov.cn/2026-04/10/c_1777558395078289.htm); ordinary assistants without that ongoing emotional interaction are outside those particular rules.
