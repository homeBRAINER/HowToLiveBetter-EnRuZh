# 5. Don’t waste money

> Complete chapter translation: the introduction and all 31 recommendations. Editorial clarifications and regional notes are labeled separately.

This chapter measures money: savings percentages, differences in annualised returns and expected values. It does not convert them into lifespan or time saved. Investment entries explain calculations and checked historical data; they are not investment advice.

### 1. Turn off automatic renewals and renew manually when a subscription expires
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No money; about 10–20 minutes to check the automatic-payment settings in Alipay, WeChat and your Apple or Android account; each later manual renewal takes another click.
- In plain language: The saving comes from subscriptions you stopped using but still pay for every month. Checking the automatic-payment lists in Alipay, WeChat, Apple and Android takes less than 20 minutes. Chinese rules require a prominent reminder before a renewal charge and a simple way to cancel at any time.
- Benefit: You save the total cost of subscriptions that keep charging you after you stop using them; the amount varies. Chinese rules in force since 2021 require prominent reminders before renewal and an easy way to cancel at any time, without unreasonable charges.
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources: State Administration for Market Regulation (2021, amended 2025). Measures for the Supervision and Administration of Online Transactions, Order 37, Article 18. <https://www.samr.gov.cn/zw/zfxxgk/fdzdgknr/fgs/art/2025/art_4b47c79b8d994a42bba4835997688faa.html>
- Notes: For a service you use frequently, an annual subscription is often cheaper than paying monthly, and keeping automatic renewal may make sense. The target here is the spending you have forgotten about.

### 2. If you pay income tax in China, review your annual return between March and June and claim eligible deductions
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No filing fee; roughly a quarter of an hour in China's Individual Income Tax app.
- In plain language: If you worked for only part of the year, changed jobs, or missed deductions for children's education, a mortgage, rent or elderly dependants, the annual reconciliation may produce a refund. You can handle it on your phone. Certain taxpayers with annual comprehensive income below CNY 120,000 or additional tax below CNY 400 do not need to file; see the conditions below.
- Benefit: The reconciliation combines wages and salaries, remuneration for services, manuscript income and royalties, refunding overpayments or collecting underpayments. The 2023 policy extends to December 31, 2027: annual comprehensive income of no more than CNY 120,000, or additional tax due of no more than CNY 400, can qualify for exemption from reconciliation. Working for only part of the year, changing jobs or adding previously unclaimed deductions can lead to a refund.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: State Taxation Administration (2025). Administrative Measures for Annual Reconciliation of Comprehensive Individual Income Tax, Article 6, and explanatory guidance. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/202502/content_7006465.htm> Ministry of Finance and State Taxation Administration (2023). Announcement extending the annual reconciliation policy. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202308/content_6900594.htm>
- Notes: If you are required to pay additional tax, you must file; failing to do so breaches the law. Special additional deductions cover children's education, continuing education, major medical expenses, housing-loan interest, housing rent, support for elderly dependants and care of children under 3. Mortgage-interest and rent deductions cannot both be claimed for the same permitted period. **Clarification:** the low-income/small-balance exemption concerns taxpayers who otherwise owe additional tax and whose withholding obligations were fulfilled as required. It does not prevent claiming a refund, and a job change alone does not guarantee one. These are Chinese rules, not US or European filing deadlines or allowances.

### 3. In China, check whether you can withdraw housing provident-fund savings for rent, renovation or property-management fees
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: One withdrawal application.
- In plain language: Buying a home is not the only permitted use of a housing provident fund. The amended rules, effective September 20, 2026, list nine grounds for withdrawal, including rent, renovation of an owner-occupied home and its property-management fees. The fund centre must decide within 3 days of accepting an application.
- Benefit: The amended regulation lists nine withdrawal grounds, including rent, renovation of an owner-occupied home and property-management fees for that home. The management centre must approve or refuse a withdrawal within 3 days of accepting the application.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: State Council (2026). Decision Amending the Regulations on the Administration of Housing Provident Funds, Decree 844, adopted at the State Council's 93rd executive meeting on July 31, 2026, effective September 20, 2026. Amended Article 24 permits withdrawal for: (1) rent; (2) buying, building, rebuilding or carrying out major repairs to an owner-occupied home; (3) repaying principal and interest on a home-purchase loan; (4) renovating an owner-occupied home; (5) paying its property-management fees; (6) retirement; (7) complete loss of working capacity combined with termination of employment; (8) settling abroad; (9) other housing-consumption circumstances approved by the State Council. Article 25 requires a decision within 3 days of acceptance. Article 18 sets both the employee's and employer's contribution at no less than 5% of the employee's average monthly salary in the previous year. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2026/issue_12946/202608/content_7079363.html>
- Notes: The balance consists of your wage contributions plus the employer's contribution; leaving it untouched generally earns little interest. The regulation also allows heirs or legatees to withdraw the balance after an employee dies or is declared dead. Documents and procedures depend on the local centre: read its application guide before visiting. **Regional note:** this is China's housing provident-fund scheme, not a US or European pension or housing account.

### 4. Review your mobile and broadband plans every year and downgrade what you do not use
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No purchase needed; roughly a quarter of an hour to inspect your bills. Check contract charges before switching.
- In plain language: An old plan may cost more than a suitable plan currently on sale. Multiply the monthly difference in USD or EUR by the number of months you will keep it, then subtract switching costs. In China, operators may not unreasonably refuse or delay number portability or create special tariffs just for people porting their numbers; complain if they obstruct the process.
- Benefit: China's rules prohibit refusing, obstructing or delaying number-porting services without a legitimate reason; using interception, restrictions or other technical measures to impair service for customers who port; and creating dedicated tariff or marketing plans for those customers. Even a modest monthly overpayment on an old plan accumulates over a year.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Ministry of Industry and Information Technology (2019). Regulations on the Administration of Number Portability Services, document 242 of 2019. <http://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2019-11/13/content_5451483.htm>
- Notes: Check your actual usage over the previous six months in the operator's app, then choose a matching plan. If support says a downgrade requires a shop visit or is prohibited during the contract term, ask for the written basis. In China, unresolved telecom complaints can be taken to 12300. **Clarification:** the cited rules concern number portability; they do not establish an unconditional right to downgrade any contract without charges. US and European procedures and cancellation rights depend on the jurisdiction and contract; compare actual local offers rather than a converted Chinese price estimate.

### 5. Do not buy lottery tickets as an investment
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Nothing.
- In plain language: China's rules require at least 50% of sales to go into prizes for lotto and number games. Double Color Ball allocates 51%: each CNY 1 spent corresponds to CNY 0.51 in long-run expected prizes, a 49% expected loss. In 2024, national lottery sales reached CNY 623.486 billion, including CNY 161.031 billion allocated to public-welfare funds rather than prizes.
- Benefit: China's minimum prize allocations are 50% for lotto/number games, 65% for instant games and 73% for prediction-based betting games. Double Color Ball allocates 51% of sales to prizes: CNY 1 spent has an expected return of CNY 0.51 and an expected loss of 49%. National lottery sales in 2024 were CNY 623.486 billion, of which CNY 161.031 billion went to public-welfare funds rather than back to ticket buyers (China, 2024).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Ministry of Finance (2015). Notice on Standardising and Strengthening the Allocation of Lottery Funds, document 94 of 2015. <http://m.mof.gov.cn/czxw/201511/t20151113_1560814.htm> Ministry of Finance (2025). Announcement 8 of 2025 on the collection and allocation of lottery public-welfare funds in 2024. <http://zhs.mof.gov.cn/zhengcefabu/202508/t20250828_3970977.htm> China Welfare Lottery, Double Color Ball game rules, Article 14. <https://mzj.nc.gov.cn/ncsmzj/flcp/202111/5dccc1a5da89434e94a62f8ea0e5887e.shtml>
- Notes: Negative expected value comes from the lottery's design, not a personal run of bad luck. A CNY 2 Double Color Ball ticket is an entertainment purchase, not a financial plan. These are Chinese game prices and historical accounts, not local ticket-price estimates. Actual winnings vary; the expected loss is not a promise that each player will lose exactly that percentage.

### 6. Walk away from promises of high returns, guaranteed principal and certain profit
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Nothing; pause when you hear those promises.
- In plain language: In a 2018 warning, China's banking and insurance regulator said returns above 6% should prompt questions, above 8% were dangerous, and above 10% could mean losing all your principal. Chinese asset-management products may not promise guaranteed principal and returns. Participants bear losses from illegal fundraising themselves; a rescue is not guaranteed.
- Benefit: At the 2018 Lujiazui Forum, the regulator's chairman used 6%, 8% and 10% as escalating warning thresholds. China's asset-management rules prohibit financial institutions from promising guaranteed principal and returns or advancing their own funds to meet payments when a product cannot pay. Under the rules against illegal fundraising, participants bear their own losses (China, 2018 and 2021).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Guo Shuqing's address to the 10th Lujiazui Forum, June 2018, republished by the Tianjin local financial regulator. <https://jrgz.tj.gov.cn/ztlm/ztzl/jrjfwphjr/202006/t20200625_2744709.html> People's Bank of China and other authorities (2018). Guiding Opinions on Regulating the Asset Management Business of Financial Institutions, document 106 of 2018, Articles 2 and 6. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2018/content_5323101.htm> State Council (2021). Regulations on Preventing and Handling Illegal Fundraising, Decree 737, Article 25. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2021/content_5588815.htm>
- Notes: The 6%/8%/10% figures are the regulator's historical rules of thumb, not statutory limits. ### 7. Avoid credit-card minimum payments and borrowing for discretionary spending
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Nothing; keep enough cash to pay the full statement balance by its due date.
- In plain language: A minimum payment is a loan, not a discount. Daily interest can add up to roughly 18% a year in the historical Chinese example below, and carrying a balance can end your interest-free period. Check the annual rate: China's central bank requires issuers to display it prominently rather than showing only a daily rate or daily repayment.
- Benefit: Before the reform effective in 2021, China's credit-card overdraft rate was capped at 0.05% per day, with a floor of 0.7 times that rate. The ceiling corresponds to 18.25% simple annual interest. Since 2021, issuers and cardholders negotiate the rate. Paying only the minimum can leave the remaining principal accruing daily interest and remove the grace period. The central bank's 2020 notice requires a prominently displayed annual rate; showing only a daily rate or daily repayment is insufficient.
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources: People's Bank of China (2020). Notice on Promoting Market-Based Reform of Credit-Card Overdraft Interest Rates, document 327 of 2020. <https://xining.pbc.gov.cn/zhengwugongkai/4081330/4406346/4693549/4159909/index.html>
- Notes: The 18.25% figure is 0.05% × 365, a simple-interest calculation; monthly compounding would increase it. Some banks offer lower rates: use the annual rate on your own statement. If you cannot meet a payment, investigate a legitimate, lower-cost option rather than letting minimum payments accumulate interest. **Clarification:** the amount on which interest is charged and the treatment of past purchases depend on the card's terms. The historical ceiling applied until the end of 2020, not merely before 2020; 18.25% is not a current universal rate.

### 8. Avoid impulsive livestream tips, game purchases and shopping orders
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Nothing; spend a few minutes disabling payments without confirmation, setting per-transaction and daily limits, and removing saved payment methods.
- In plain language: China's gaming market generated CNY 325.783 billion from 674 million users in 2024—roughly CNY 480 per user. Once you pay, recovering the money can be difficult. Chinese law provides a route to refunds for certain unauthorised purchases by minors; adults cannot rely on that same protection. Disable frictionless payments, set limits and unlink payment methods.
- Benefit: China's gaming-market revenue in 2024 was CNY 325.783 billion, with 674 million users: dividing those figures gives about CNY 480 per user. Chinese courts are instructed to support a guardian's refund claim for qualifying tips and game payments made without consent by a minor with limited civil capacity. Adults do not have that particular protection. Under China's 2021 rules, online gaming services may provide minors with 1 hour of play, from 20:00 to 21:00, on Fridays, Saturdays, Sundays and statutory holidays (China, 2020 and 2021).
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: National Press and Publication Administration (2024). China's gaming-market revenue exceeds CNY 325.7 billion, based on the China Game Industry Report 2024. <https://www.nppa.gov.cn/xxfb/ywdt/202412/t20241216_877445.html> Supreme People's Court (2020). Guiding Opinions on Properly Hearing Civil Cases Involving the COVID-19 Epidemic (II), document 17 of 2020, Article 9. <https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/230181.html> National Press and Publication Administration (2021). Notice on Further Strict Management to Prevent Minors' Addiction to Online Games, document 14 of 2021. <https://www.nppa.gov.cn/xxfb/tzgs/202108/t20210830_666285.html>
- Notes: Grade B reflects aggregate spending figures, not a direct measurement of how much avoiding tips saves. Disabling frictionless payments and setting limits are practical suggestions, grade C. Articles 19 and 145 of China's Civil Code are reproduced in the next entry. **Clarification:** average market revenue is not a typical person's spending, and adults may have other refund grounds, including fraud or breach of contract. This is not investment advice.

### 9. In China, challenge a child's large unauthorised payments when they exceed the child's legal capacity
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No application fee, but time to collect evidence and negotiate. A platform may initially refuse and later refund; litigation can take months and involve legal fees. Prepare the evidence and try the platform and China's 12315 consumer-complaint service first.
- In plain language: From age 8, a minor's transaction that clearly exceeds what is appropriate for their age and understanding needs a legal representative's consent or ratification. Without it, you can seek a refund. Children under 8 lack independent civil capacity. Silence counts as refusal in the statutory ratification procedure; it is not an automatic rule for every unanswered purchase.
- Benefit: Under China's Civil Code, minors aged 8 and over have limited civil capacity. Transactions beyond their age and understanding require a legal representative's consent or later ratification. Children under 8 lack civil capacity and act through their legal representative. Failure to respond within the statutory ratification procedure is treated as refusal.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: National People's Congress (2020). Civil Code, full text republished by the Supreme People's Procuratorate. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202006/t20200602_463888.shtml> Article 19: a minor aged 8 or over has limited civil capacity and acts through a legal representative or with that representative's consent or ratification, but may independently carry out transactions that confer only a benefit or suit their age and understanding. Article 20: a minor under 8 lacks civil capacity and acts through a legal representative. Article 145: transactions by a person with limited civil capacity that confer only a benefit or suit their age, understanding and mental health are valid; other transactions become valid with the representative's consent or ratification. Failure by the representative to express a position is treated as refusal of ratification.
- Notes: Evidence matters: establish that the child, rather than you, made the payment. Relevant material includes the timing of purchases, whether you were at work or the child was at school, device-usage records, chats, game records and the child's account. None of those timing details proves identity by itself. There is no single monetary threshold for an age-appropriate transaction: a game skin costing tens of CNY and tips worth tens of thousands of CNY are different cases. Prevention is easier: cap payments without confirmation, enable youth mode and keep payment passwords private. For your own impulsive spending, see entry 8. **Legal clarification:** Article 145 gives the representative 30 days after the other party requests ratification; silence then counts as refusal. Under Article 18, a person aged 16 or over who mainly supports themselves through their own work is treated as having full civil capacity.

### 10. Teach children to refuse requests for a parent's phone, transfers or verification codes
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Nothing; explain the six scams below and promise, “Tell me straight away if you've been scammed. I won't shout at you.” Cap payments without confirmation, enable youth mode and do not share payment passwords.
- In plain language: These scams use different stories but converge on the same demand: get a parent's phone and transfer money or read out a verification code. Teach your child to stop and tell you. Make it safe to speak up immediately; speed can matter when trying to recover money.
- Benefit: Quanzhou's anti-fraud centre and education bureau described six scams targeting young people. (1) Cheap private sales of game accounts or equipment: after paying CNY 260, a child was told the transfer was unlawful because they were a minor and the account would be frozen; a “verification” link opened on their father's phone led to theft of more than CNY 30,000. (2) Supposed removal of gaming time limits: threats that a parent would go to prison unless a payment was cancelled led to a CNY 7,000 transfer from the mother's phone. There is no private channel to unlock official anti-addiction restrictions. (3) Fake refunds: someone posing as support said a minor's tips could be refunded only after the parent sent gifts to a specified livestream. CNY 73,000 was spent from the mother's account before the scammer blocked contact. The warning describes demands to send gifts for a refund as “100%” fraudulent; this is not a refund procedure. (4) Impersonating classmates or teachers: a stolen account sends a voice message naming a classmate and asking for money, or a fake teacher enters a class group, copies a name and avatar, requests CNY 500 in school fees and plants accomplices who say they have paid. (5) Fan groups: a promised CNY 888 gift required a CNY 48.88 “verification” payment, followed by threats of a CNY 80,000 freeze and a parent's imprisonment; the loss reached CNY 16,000. (6) Cheap second-hand goods lure buyers off-platform for direct transfers. The same October 2025 guidance warns against three further traps: lending, selling or renting bank cards or SIM cards that may be used to move criminal proceeds, with possible criminal records affecting postgraduate study or public-service recruitment; “phone-bridge” jobs that connect calls for fraudsters; and overseas “high-paying jobs” that may end in confinement after arrival.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Quanzhou Anti-Fraud Centre (2025). First lesson of the school year: six fraud traps young people must avoid, republished by the Fujian Provincial Public Security Department. <http://gat.fujian.gov.cn/ztzl/fjjffpzxrx/spjq/202510/t20251028_7026836.htm>
- Notes: Grade B reflects official descriptions and individual cases, not statistics on incidence or prevention effectiveness. Distinguish this from entry 9: a child's own game payments may be challenged under the Civil Code; money sent to scammers calls for a police report and urgent payment interception, as in chapter 8, entry 2. Recovery depends partly on whether funds remain accessible. Explain in advance that threats of “you broke the rules, your parents will go to prison” are a scam warning sign. Keep the promise not to scold: fear and concealment allowed losses to grow in these cases. Adult impersonation and AI face-swap scams are covered in chapter 8, entries 3 and 4. **Clarification:** the warning that genuine police or lawyers would never contact a minor online is too absolute. The decisive danger here is a demand for codes, access to a parent's payment account or a transfer to avert punishment. The source calls “send gifts for a refund” 100% fraudulent; this describes that tactic, not a measured detection rate.

### 11. Decline extended warranties on electronics when you can afford a repair
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Nothing upfront; accept the possibility of paying for a repair after the standard warranty expires.
- In plain language: Extended warranties commonly cost 10–50% of the product's original price and have much higher gross margins than ordinary merchandise. Industry estimates for Best Buy in 2003 attributed more than half its profit to warranties that accounted for only 3–4% of revenue. That markup is part of what you pay.
- Benefit: Extended warranties were priced at 10–50% of the product price. Industry estimates cited in the research put their average gross margin at 50–60%, about 18 times the margin on ordinary goods; in 2003, they accounted for 3–4% of Best Buy revenue but more than 50% of profit (US retail data from the 2000s).
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Chen T, Kalra A, Sun B (2009). Why Do Consumers Buy Extended Service Contracts? Journal of Consumer Research 36(4):611–623. <https://doi.org/10.1086/605298>
- Notes: The margin figures are industry estimates cited from Business Week (2004), not the paper's own measurements. This advice concerns ordinary purchases where you can afford one repair; a device you depend on for work without a backup is a different case. Evaluate phone screen insurance separately using your own breakage history. A retailer's gross margin is not identical to your personal expected loss.

### 12. Ask about approved generic medicines and, in China, centrally procured alternatives
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No additional purchase; ask the prescriber or pharmacist whether an appropriate generic or centrally procured medicine is available.
- In plain language: China's second national procurement round cut prices by an average of 53%, with a maximum reduction of 93%. By April 2026, 11 rounds had covered 490 medicines. Asking about an approved equivalent with the same active ingredient may substantially reduce your bill.
- Benefit: China's second national centralised procurement round reduced prices by an average of 53%, and by as much as 93%. By April 2026, there had been 11 rounds covering 490 medicines; 1,695 products had passed, or were deemed to have passed, consistency evaluation, about 2/3 of commonly used chemical medicines (official Chinese data, 2020–2026).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: National Healthcare Security Administration (2020). Questions and answers on the second round of national centralised medicine procurement. <https://www.nhsa.gov.cn/art/2020/1/17/art_38_2264.html> National Healthcare Security Administration (2026). State Council policy briefing on medicine-price formation throughout the product lifecycle and across sales channels. <http://www.nhsa.gov.cn/art/2026/4/15/art_14_20221.html>
- Notes: There is debate: consistency evaluation is a quality requirement for procurement, and the official position is therapeutic equivalence. Some clinicians have questioned the performance of individual products, but no systematic evidence supporting those concerns is cited here. Ask your doctor before changing medicines, especially long-term treatment for a chronic condition. **Regional note:** Chinese procurement price reductions do not predict savings in the US or Europe. Use locally authorised products and professional substitution advice; FDA-approved generics must meet requirements for equivalence, quality and clinical benefit. [FDA: generic drug facts](https://www.fda.gov/drugs/generic-drugs/generic-drug-facts).

### 13. If you are insured in China, activate eligible family use of your employee medical-insurance account
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No fee; complete the family-sharing registration in the local insurance app or at the relevant office.
- In plain language: Linking eligible relatives lets money in your employee medical-insurance personal account pay qualifying medical and pharmacy bills for a spouse, parents or children. Without the required arrangement, unused money may remain in your account while relatives pay out of pocket.
- Benefit: The 2021 Chinese guidance allows the account to pay the insured person's, spouse's, parents' and children's out-of-pocket medical costs at designated providers, and their eligible spending on medicines, medical devices and medical consumables at designated pharmacies. It also calls for exploring use of the account for relatives' individual contributions to resident medical insurance.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: General Office of the State Council (2021). Guidance on Improving Outpatient Mutual-Aid Coverage in Basic Medical Insurance for Employees, document 14 of 2021, section 5. Personal accounts primarily pay eligible out-of-pocket expenses at designated medical providers or retail pharmacies. They may pay the insured person's, spouse's, parents' and children's personal medical costs and spending on medicines, medical devices and consumables at those providers. The guidance proposes exploring their use for those relatives' resident-insurance contributions. It excludes public-health spending, fitness, wellness and other expenses outside basic medical-insurance coverage. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2021-04/22/content_5601280.htm>
- Notes: Some insured people have balances of several thousand CNY while elderly relatives pay for medicines themselves. A family-sharing link can remain in place for later use; look for the local “family mutual aid” service in the insurance app. It is not permission to pay for fitness or wellness, or to cash out an account through sham pharmacy purchases. **Regional note:** this is a Chinese insurance-account arrangement, not a general right to transfer US or European health benefits. Eligible relationships and procedures must be checked locally; the 2021 list is not a complete statement of later extensions. **Update:** the family-sharing scope began expanding to insured close relatives beyond the spouse/parents/children list in 2024; the recipient must also participate in basic medical insurance. Confirm implementation with the local insurer. [NHSA, August 2024](https://www.nhsa.gov.cn/art/2024/8/26/art_14_13655.html); [NHSA: eligibility conditions](https://www.nhsa.gov.cn/art/2024/5/8/art_14_12622.html).

### 14. Use safe tap water instead of buying bottled water all year
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: A kettle, time and a little electricity or gas where boiling is advised. Water and energy tariffs vary locally; use the USD or EUR amounts on your own bills.
- In plain language: Safe tap water can cost far less than bottled water. Follow your water supplier's quality reports and health advice; routine boiling is not required everywhere. If water develops an unusual smell, colour or cloudiness, stop using it for drinking and report it. Extra boiling does not make chemical contamination safe.
- Benefit: A historical Beijing example used the 2015 first-tier household tariff of CNY 5/m³, or CNY 0.005/L. At an explicitly hypothetical bottled-water price of CNY 1 per 500 mL, or CNY 2/L, bottled water would cost 400 times as much. China's mandatory drinking-water standard GB 5749-2022 was published on March 15, 2022 and took effect on April 1, 2023; its responsible authority is the National Disease Control and Prevention Administration (Chinese tariff and standard, not local US/European prices).
- Evidence grade: C (disputed)
- Sources: Beijing Municipal Development and Reform Commission. Household water tariff table. <https://fgw.beijing.gov.cn/bmcx/djcx/jzldj/202003/t20200331_1752796.htm> State Administration for Market Regulation, national standards portal. GB 5749-2022, Standards for Drinking Water Quality. <https://openstd.samr.gov.cn/bzgk/std/newGbInfo?hcno=99E9C17E3547A3C0CE2FD1FFD9F2F7BE> Hangzhou joint investigation team (2025). Report of July 23, 2025 on abnormal water supply in parts of Yuhang District, republished by Xinhua. <http://www.news.cn/20250723/1be62dac3b0b4461a643791ff552e93d/c.html> Yuhang District Government. ### 15. Avoid frequent stock trading
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Nothing; resist the urge to trade on every piece of news.
- In plain language: In data on roughly 66,000 US household accounts, the most active traders earned 11.4% a year while the market returned 17.9%. More than six percentage points separated them. Gross returns were fairly similar across trading groups; trading costs accounted for much of the gap in net returns.
- Benefit: A study of 66,465 US households with discount-brokerage accounts from 1991 to 1996 found annualised returns of 11.4% for the most active traders, compared with 17.9% for the market and 16.4% for the average household. Average annual turnover was 75%. Gross returns differed little between frequent and infrequent traders; trading costs explained much of the net-return gap (US, 1991–1996).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Barber BM, Odean T (2000). Trading Is Hazardous to Your Wealth: The Common Stock Investment Performance of Individual Investors. The Journal of Finance 55(2):773–806. <https://doi.org/10.1111/0022-1082.00226>
- Notes: These are US data from the 1990s. Mainland China's A-share commission and stamp-duty structure differs, but the mechanism by which turnover adds costs remains relevant. The historical gap is not a forecast for today's market. This is not investment advice.

### 16. Do not borrow to invest, use leverage or buy products you do not understand

### 18. Compare fees and favour lower costs among otherwise comparable funds
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Nothing; before buying, check management, custody and distribution/service fees.
- In plain language: A recurring fee difference compounds. In a simplified illustration where the more expensive option leaves you with 1% less wealth each year, the final amount is about 18% lower after 20 years and 26% lower after 30. You cannot know future returns, but you can compare the fees disclosed today.
- Benefit: In that simplified illustration, the reduction is 1 − 0.99^20 ≈ 18.2% after 20 years and 1 − 0.99^30 ≈ 26% after 30 years. China began reforming mutual-fund fees in 2023, lowering caps on active-equity management and custody fees; the specific caps remain to be verified here.
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources: China Securities Regulatory Commission (2023). Questions and answers on mutual-fund fee reform. <http://www.csrc.gov.cn/csrc/c100028/c7418692/content.shtml> Fama EF, French KR (2010), cited in entry 17. <https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2010.01598.x>
- Notes: This is compound-interest arithmetic, assuming identical returns before costs. Include purchase charges, redemption fees and the distribution/service fees attached to China's Class C units. **Calculation clarification:** 0.99^20 is the amount remaining, not the amount lost; the loss requires subtracting it from 1. ### 19. Do not put everything into one stock, one platform or one property
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Nothing; accept that you will not have placed every dollar or euro in the single best performer.
- In plain language: Assets that do not move perfectly together can be combined to reduce portfolio volatility for a given expected return. If all your wealth depends on one stock, one platform or one property, trouble there can affect everything you own.
- Benefit: Markowitz's 1952 portfolio-selection theory shows how imperfectly correlated assets can be combined to improve the risk–return trade-off. With suitable portfolio choices, the same expected return can be achieved at lower volatility. Concentrating everything in one asset leaves the whole portfolio exposed to that asset's variance and specific problems.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Markowitz H (1952). Portfolio Selection. The Journal of Finance 7(1):77–91. <https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1952.tb01525.x>
- Notes: Grade B reflects a theoretical derivation rather than an empirical estimate of a fixed amount saved by diversification. Diversification can reduce volatility; it does not guarantee against losses. A broad index fund, discussed in entry 17, is a convenient way to spread exposure within its market. **Clarification:** simply adding any asset does not automatically preserve returns and reduce risk. Diversifying investments and reducing dependence on a single intermediary address different risks. This is not investment advice.

### 20. In China, compare the tax benefit and withdrawal restrictions before funding a personal pension

If you pay no income tax, you gain no contribution deduction but may still pay withdrawal tax. Do not lock away emergency savings for the tax break.
- Benefit: Eligible contributions are deducted from comprehensive or business income up to CNY 12,000 a year. Applying (marginal tax rate − 3%) to the contribution gives the simplified annual figures CNY 840 at 10%, CNY 2,040 at 20% and CNY 3,240 at 30%. Someone below the taxable-income threshold gets no contribution-stage tax saving but still faces withdrawal tax. The contribution limit resets each calendar year and unused capacity does not carry forward. Nationwide participation began on December 15, 2024.

### 21. Pay per visit or for a short gym membership until you have a year's consistent attendance
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->

### 24. Do not stockpile goods because of crossed-out prices or sales events
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: You may miss some genuine discounts; remember the normal price of items you buy often.
- In plain language: The first number you see can anchor your idea of value, and later adjustments may not fully undo it. “Was 999, now 499” can make the bargain look bigger than it is, whatever the currency. A crossed-out number is not proof that the product actually sold at that price.

### 28. Calculate before making an early mortgage repayment
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: About half an hour; make an honest estimate of the stable after-tax return available elsewhere.

### 30. After a disputed livestream purchase, request the seller's and marketer's details from the platform

- Cost: No fee; submit a request to the platform.
- In plain language: A common obstacle in livestream disputes is not knowing whom to pursue. In China, the platform must provide necessary information about the livestream operator and marketer, together with relevant business records, when the consumer requests it in a dispute.
- Benefit: China's implementing regulation requires a livestream-marketing platform, on a consumer's request during a dispute, to provide relevant information about the livestream operator and marketing personnel and records of the related business activity. This helps identify the responsible party.
