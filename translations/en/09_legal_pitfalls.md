# 9. Laws you can easily break without realizing it

> Complete translation: the introduction and all 20 entries. The [Chinese chapter](../../book/09-普通人容易踩的法律红线.md) is available alongside it. The laws, penalties, and cases concern China; regional notes are separate. Legal clarifications are labeled. Reference checks: September 21, 2026.

This chapter measures consequences in personal freedom and money. Conduct that may seem trivial can lead to detention, fines, or a criminal sentence. Entries give the figures in the legislation and, where verified, cases published through official channels. References to the Public Security Administration Punishments Law use the 2025 revision, effective 2026-01-01; its article numbers differ from the earlier law. Criminal Law references use the text consolidated through Amendment XI reproduced by Beijing traffic police, with the relevant amendments identified. This is a stated source edition, not a claim that there have been no later amendments. The order combines how easily a person might cross a legal boundary unintentionally with how serious the consequences can be.

### 1. Check emergency reports before sharing them; do not fabricate scenes with editing tools or AI
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=自由 -->

- Cost: No purchase is needed. Before forwarding a report about a disaster, epidemic, danger, or police incident, check whether an official notice supports it. If you cannot verify it, do not forward it, alter an image, or add “apparently” as a substitute for checking.
- In plain language: In China, knowingly spreading a false emergency report can lead to 5–10 days of administrative detention and an additional fine of up to CNY 1,000, even if someone else invented it. Where the criminal offense applies, seriously disrupting social order carries up to 3 years in prison; serious consequences raise the range to 3–7 years. Fabricated “scene photos,” including edited or AI-generated images, can be part of the false report. **Legal clarification:** failing to verify a message is not automatically the same as knowingly spreading false information. Intent, the kind of information, and the required disruption or consequences matter; using AI is not itself the offense.
- Benefit: Article 29 of China's Public Security Administration Punishments Law provides 5–10 days of administrative detention, with an optional additional fine of up to CNY 1,000, for intentionally spreading rumors, falsely reporting danger, epidemics, disasters, or police incidents, or otherwise intentionally disturbing public order. In less serious cases, the penalty is up to 5 days' detention or a fine of up to CNY 1,000. Under Criminal Law Article 291-1(2), fabricating such emergency information and spreading it through information networks or other media—or knowingly and intentionally spreading such false information—must seriously disrupt social order to meet this offense. The penalty is up to 3 years' imprisonment, criminal detention, or noncustodial public surveillance; serious consequences carry 3–7 years' imprisonment. These are Chinese national provisions.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2025). Public Security Administration Punishments Law, 2025 revision, Article 29. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202506/t20250627_699863.shtml>; Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2015). Criminal Law Amendment IX, provision 32, adding Criminal Law Article 291-1(2). <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/201802/t20180205_364562.shtml>; National People's Congress (2020). Criminal Law consolidated through Amendment XI, Article 291-1; reproduced by Beijing traffic police. <https://jtgl.beijing.gov.cn/jgj/jgxx/flfg/fl/11033925/index.html>
- Notes: Knowingly forwarding the specified false information and inventing it are covered by the same criminal provision; “I only forwarded it” is not an automatic defense. Edited or AI-generated images presented as real scenes can amount to fabrication when the legal conditions are met. The Ministry of Public Security published several collections of emergency-related online-rumor cases in 2025, but its website was unavailable for the case verification underlying this entry, so no individual example from those collections is included. The [2025 administrative law](https://www.samr.gov.cn/zw/zfxxgk/fdzdgknr/bgt/art/2025/art_8a1c722747214869b28c0ad7a0f8bb7a.html) and [Amendment IX](https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/15293.html) preserve the intent and disruption requirements. **US/Europe:** verify local emergency information, but do not transfer Chinese rumor offenses, detention periods, or fines to another jurisdiction. Freedom-of-expression protections and liability for false reports depend on the applicable law.

### 2. In China, do not post insulting images or comments about legally protected heroes and martyrs or desecrate national symbols
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=自由 -->

- Cost: No purchase is needed. A cautious approach in China is not to comment on, edit into memes, or forward material about protected heroes and martyrs, the national flag, national emblem, or national anthem if you are unsure of the legal boundary. This is a precaution, not a statement that every comment or joke is prohibited.
- In plain language: Chinese law penalizes specified insults to protected heroes and martyrs and specified acts against national symbols. For the hero-and-martyr administrative offense, the usual range is 5–10 days' detention or a CNY 1,000–3,000 fine; more serious cases carry 10–15 days' detention. Criminal offenses in this area can carry up to 3 years in prison. In one case, two posts insulting Yuan Longping in a 499-member village WeChat group led to six months of noncustodial public surveillance. In another, two Weibo posts about border troops from an account with more than 2.5 million followers led to eight months in prison and a public apology in national media. **Legal clarification:** these offenses have specific elements; the penalties do not automatically apply to any joke or criticism.
- Benefit: Under Article 35 of China's 2025 Public Security Administration Punishments Law, infringing protected heroes' and martyrs' names, images, reputation, or honor through insult, defamation, or other means, harming the public interest, can bring 5–10 days' detention or a CNY 1,000–3,000 fine. The article also covers desecrating or denying their deeds and spirit, or producing or spreading material glorifying wars or acts of aggression, where public order is disturbed. More serious cases carry 10–15 days' detention, with an optional additional fine of up to CNY 5,000. A serious criminal infringement of heroes' and martyrs' reputation or honor under Article 299-1 carries up to 3 years' imprisonment, criminal detention, noncustodial public surveillance, or deprivation of political rights. Article 299 separately covers intentionally insulting the national flag or emblem in public, and specified public insults to the national anthem—including deliberate alteration of its words or score, or distorted or derogatory performance—in serious circumstances for the anthem offense; the maximum imprisonment term is 3 years, with other listed penalties available. Official cases: Xiao received six months of noncustodial public surveillance for 2 posts insulting Yuan Longping in a 499-member WeChat group; Qiu received eight months in prison and had to apologize publicly in national media for 2 posts distorting the deeds of border troops, published to more than 2.5 million Weibo followers. These are Chinese cases and penalties.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2025). Public Security Administration Punishments Law, 2025 revision, Article 35. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202506/t20250627_699863.shtml>; Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2018). Law on the Protection of Heroes and Martyrs, Articles 22 and 26. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/gyssshmhsh/201912/t20191202_440079.shtml>; Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2020). Criminal Law Amendment XI, provision 35, adding Article 299-1. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/zdgz/202012/t20201227_503682.shtml>; National People's Congress (2020). Criminal Law consolidated through Amendment XI, Articles 299 and 299-1. <https://jtgl.beijing.gov.cn/jgj/jgxx/flfg/fl/11033925/index.html>; Supreme People's Court (2022). Ten representative cases concerning heroes' and martyrs' rights, cases 2 and 3. <https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/382301.html>
- Notes: The dedicated Article 35 was added in the 2025 administrative-law revision. **Historical clarification:** administrative liability was not wholly absent before then: Article 26 of the 2018 Heroes and Martyrs Protection Law already referred qualifying conduct to public-security penalties, alongside civil and criminal liability. It is therefore inaccurate to describe criminal proceedings and civil public-interest suits as the only earlier routes. A large WeChat group is not necessarily private for purposes of assessing dissemination; the 499-member case demonstrates that posting there can meet the elements of a public-interest offense. It does not establish that every group chat is a public place for every legal provision. “Public surveillance” is a Chinese noncustodial sentence, not six months in prison. **US/Europe:** protections for expression, national symbols, and historical figures differ substantially. These Chinese offenses are not general international restrictions on discussion or satire.

### 3. Check the legal risks before sharing overseas political content in China; do not assume saving every kind of material is lawful
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=自由 -->

- Cost: No purchase is needed. For material that may fall within China's national-security or prohibited-content rules, a cautious course is to close the page without archiving screenshots, forwarding it, or copying it onto a domestic platform. Avoid becoming involved in repeated political posting under an identifiable account, or accepting overseas article fees or funding, without understanding the applicable law. **Legal clarification:** an overseas source, political subject, identifiable account, or foreign payment is not by itself proof of any offense listed here; an anonymous account does not remove liability either.
- In plain language: The cited Chinese offenses concern particular forms of incitement, dissemination, and provision of protected information, rather than merely reading a page. One express possession offense must not be overlooked: knowingly and unlawfully possessing books, recordings, or other items promoting terrorism or extremism, in serious circumstances, can carry up to 3 years in prison even without further distribution. Sharing screenshots, forwarding material to groups, or adding comments can be dissemination; a screenshot kept privately is not automatically dissemination. Inciting subversion or separatism can carry up to 5 years; ringleaders or people whose offenses are grave face 5 years or more. Deprivation of political rights is an additional penalty, ordinarily for 1–5 years, subject to the exceptions below; confiscation of property is also possible. Stealing, gathering, buying, or unlawfully supplying state secrets or intelligence for overseas entities or persons generally carries 5–10 years. Assess the possible consequences before sharing; a casual repost can expose you to a dispute with serious stakes.
- Benefit: Chinese Criminal Law Article 105(2) punishes inciting subversion of state power or overthrow of the socialist system through rumors, defamation, or other means with up to 5 years' imprisonment, criminal detention, noncustodial public surveillance, or deprivation of political rights. Ringleaders or grave offenders face at least 5 years' imprisonment; the ordinary maximum for a single fixed-term sentence under Article 45 is 15 years. Inciting separatism or undermining national unity under Article 103(2) has the same ranges. Colluding with overseas institutions, organizations, or individuals to commit the relevant offenses brings heavier punishment under Article 106. For domestic or overseas institutions, organizations, or individuals funding the specified national-security crimes, Article 107 provides up to 5 years or the listed alternative penalties for directly responsible people, and at least 5 years in serious cases. Under Article 111, stealing, gathering, buying, or unlawfully providing state secrets or intelligence for overseas institutions, organizations, or persons carries 5–10 years; especially serious cases carry 10 years or more or life, and less serious cases up to 5 years or alternative penalties. National-security convictions require additional deprivation of political rights: the usual term under Article 55 is 1–5 years, subject to statutory exceptions. Property confiscation may also be imposed under Article 113. At the administrative level, Cybersecurity Law Article 13(2) prohibits using networks for the specified activities against national security, honor, or interests, including inciting subversion or separatism; Article 71(1) refers publication or transmission of prohibited information to the applicable laws and administrative regulations. A separate possession offense, Article 120-6, added by Amendment IX, covers knowingly and unlawfully possessing material promoting terrorism or extremism in serious circumstances: up to 3 years' imprisonment, criminal detention, or noncustodial public surveillance, with a fine in addition or on its own. All these provisions concern China.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: National People's Congress (1997). Criminal Law, Articles 45, 55, 56, 103, 105, 106, 107, 111, and 113, with later amendments to be taken into account; see the correction on Article 107 below. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/201802/t20180206_364975.shtml>; Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2025). Cybersecurity Law, 2025 amendment effective 2026-01-01, Articles 13(2) and 71(1). <https://www.cac.gov.cn/2025-12/29/c_1768735112911946.htm>; Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2015). Criminal Law Amendment IX, adding Article 120-6; full text reproduced by the National Public Complaints and Proposals Administration. <https://www.gjxfj.gov.cn/gjxfj/fgwj/flfg/webinfo/2017/03/1601761496614423.htm>
- Notes: The cited incitement and disclosure provisions do not create a general offense of reading overseas websites. Nor do they prove that possessing every type of content is lawful: Article 120-6 expressly covers knowing, unlawful possession of terrorism or extremism material in serious circumstances. For content that may be prohibited, the precaution is not to archive it, circulate it, add to its dissemination through comments, or organize others to share it. Reposting is not an automatic defense; uncertainty about a claim's truth is another reason to pause, as in entry 1. **Legal clarification:** overseas hosting alone is not the aggravating factor in Article 106; the provision requires collusion with an overseas institution, organization, or person to commit the specified crimes. Mere private screenshots are not equivalent to public circulation. The 1–5-year political-rights range has exceptions: under Articles 55 and 57, a public-surveillance sentence can have a matching deprivation term, while death or life sentences require lifelong deprivation. Article 113 also permits the death penalty for certain national-security offenses, including Article 111, where harm is especially serious and circumstances especially grave; the ranges above are not an exhaustive sentencing opinion. Article 107 did change: [Amendment VIII in 2011](https://www.npc.gov.cn/zgrdw/npc/xinwen/lfgz/lfdt/2011-05/03/content_1654462.htm) broadened the funding provision. See chapter 11, entry 9 for the separate issue of tools used to bypass internet restrictions. Sharing this kind of material should be weighed against possible imprisonment, loss of political rights, and confiscation, rather than treated as a harmless click. A reported comparable case involved a blogger sentenced to 7 years in 2023 after long-term political posting on an overseas platform; the available account was limited to overseas media and documents released by relatives, without an independently checked official judgment, so it is not used as a verified case study. **US/Europe:** Chinese national-security and content rules do not define the legality of political discussion elsewhere. Check the law actually applicable to you. 

### 4. Do not circulate, sell, or run groups for material classified as obscene under Chinese law
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=自由 -->

- Cost: No purchase is needed. Do not forward such material, put it in shared group files, or become the owner or administrator of a group established for distributing it.
- In plain language: The Chinese distribution provisions cited here distinguish private viewing from sending material to others. Posting it even once to a group makes it available to that group; it does not automatically establish every element of a criminal offense. Administrative distribution offenses can carry 10–15 days' detention, with an additional fine of up to CNY 5,000; serious criminal distribution can carry up to 2 years in prison. Selling or distributing it for profit can carry up to 3 years, 3–10 years in serious cases, or 10 years or more up to life imprisonment in especially serious cases. For an online group mainly used to distribute obscene electronic material, having at least 30 members or causing serious consequences can bring criminal liability for its creator, administrators, and principal distributors.
- Benefit: Article 80 of China's 2025 Public Security Administration Punishments Law provides 10–15 days' detention, with an optional additional fine of up to CNY 5,000, for disseminating obscene information through networks or other communication tools. Less serious cases carry up to 5 days' detention or a CNY 1,000–3,000 fine. Material involving minors attracts heavier punishment. Criminal Law Article 364(1) provides up to 2 years' imprisonment, criminal detention, or noncustodial public surveillance for serious distribution offenses. Under Article 363, selling or distributing obscene material for profit carries up to 3 years or the listed alternative penalties, plus a fine; serious cases carry 3–10 years plus a fine; especially serious cases carry 10 years or more or life, plus a fine or confiscation of property. Article 3 of the 2010 judicial interpretation covers online groups mainly used for distributing obscene electronic information: if membership reaches 30 or serious consequences result, the creator, administrators, and principal distributors are convicted and punished under Article 364(1). These are Chinese national rules.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2025). Public Security Administration Punishments Law, 2025 revision, Article 80. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202506/t20250627_699863.shtml>; National People's Congress (2020). Criminal Law consolidated through Amendment XI, Articles 363 and 364. <https://jtgl.beijing.gov.cn/jgj/jgxx/flfg/fl/11033925/index.html>; Supreme People's Court and Supreme People's Procuratorate (2010). Interpretation on criminal cases involving the production, copying, publication, sale, and dissemination of obscene electronic information through the internet, mobile communication terminals, and telephone services, Part II, Article 3. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/zdgz/201002/t20100204_25001.shtml>
- Notes: Private viewing is not the distribution conduct described here, whereas posting into a group makes material available to others. **Legal clarification:** the cited laws cover more than distribution—Article 80 also lists production, transport, copying, sale, and rental, and Article 363 covers several profit-making activities. Therefore, “only distribution is punishable” is too broad. This is also not a blanket assurance that possessing or viewing any sexual material is lawful, especially where children or abuse are involved. The [2010 interpretation](https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/302.html) confirms the 30-member rule and its alternative serious-consequences test; neither is a safe allowance for smaller groups. Other quantity thresholds and definitions of serious circumstances depend on the applicable judicial interpretations and are not exhaustively verified here. **US/Europe:** rules on consensual adult sexual material differ from those on child sexual abuse material, nonconsensual intimate images, and other illegal content. Chinese “obscenity” classifications and thresholds must not be treated as local law or a universal permission to view or keep material.

### 5. Reject “part-time jobs” that require your bank card to receive, withdraw, or transfer money, whatever the fee
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=自由 -->

- Cost: No purchase is needed. Turn down offers of daily cash that require you to bring your card to a bank.
- In plain language: A few hundred yuan earned this way can lead to detention or a criminal conviction in China. Wang brought ID and 1 bank card to a specified location and helped transfer and withdraw money, earning over CNY 500. Prosecutors declined to prosecute, but police imposed 10 days of administrative detention and a CNY 1,000 fine. Man supplied a group with his own and other people's bank cards and SIM cards for collecting and withdrawing money; he received 2 years and 3 months in prison and a CNY 22,000 fine. **Legal clarification:** administrative detention is not a criminal conviction, and Wang's punishment is a case outcome, not a statutory minimum for everyone.
- Benefit: Under China's Criminal Law Article 312, knowingly moving, acquiring, selling on someone else's behalf, or otherwise concealing criminal proceeds or their returns can bring up to 3 years' imprisonment, criminal detention, or noncustodial public surveillance, with a fine additionally or on its own. Official cases: Wang followed instructions to bring ID and 1 bank card to a designated location and assist with transfers and withdrawals. He earned over CNY 500; after a decision not to prosecute, police imposed 10 days' detention and a CNY 1,000 fine under the Anti-Telecom and Online Fraud Law. Man provided his own and other people's bank cards and SIM cards to a group for receiving, transferring, and withdrawing funds; he was sentenced to 2 years and 3 months in prison and fined CNY 22,000. These are Chinese national provisions and cases. **Legal clarification:** Article 312 also provides 3–7 years' imprisonment plus a fine in serious cases; 3 years is not the overall maximum.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: National People's Congress (2020). Criminal Law consolidated through Amendment XI, Article 312. <https://jtgl.beijing.gov.cn/jgj/jgxx/flfg/fl/11033925/index.html>; Supreme People's Procuratorate (2023). Typical cases involving telecom and online fraud and associated offenses, case 9. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/xwfbh/wsfbt/202311/t20231130_635188.shtml>; Supreme People's Court and Supreme People's Procuratorate (2025). Typical cases involving concealment of criminal proceeds and their returns, case 5. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/xwfbh/wsfbt/202508/t20250825_704514.shtml>
- Notes: Lending cards and moving money through accounts are covered in chapter 8, entry 7; this entry concerns making withdrawals and transfers yourself. Claiming that you did not know where the money came from does not settle the question: unusually high pay, requests for face verification, and transactions that make no ordinary sense can be evidence of knowledge. **Legal clarification:** knowledge must be assessed from the evidence as a whole; none of those signs automatically proves it. The [official Man case](https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/474151.html) distinguishes knowing that funds are criminal proceeds from the different knowledge requirement for assisting online crime. Wang's 10 days of administrative detention cannot be described as a universal “lightest outcome” or as a criminal conviction. The full penalty range is in [Article 312](https://www.samr.gov.cn/zw/zfxxgk/fdzdgknr/bgt/art/2025/art_890f1333b6284c3cbb225c3cd2647c4b.html).

### 6. Do not take a cut of a loan in exchange for helping someone falsify the application
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=自由 -->

- Cost: No purchase is needed. Refuse; do not lend your ID or credit report, sign an unread loan agreement, or attend a bank's in-person loan signing on someone else's behalf.
- In plain language: Knowingly helping to obtain a loan with fabricated documents in return for a share can amount to loan fraud in China. A group of 126 people presented unemployed applicants as highly creditworthy customers and obtained over CNY 30 million from more than 80 bank branches. The applicants who signed for the loans were promised 30% of the amount borrowed. Of the first 80 defendants sentenced, 76 received 1 year and 4 months to 6 years and 6 months. Their claim that they had intended to repay was rejected after investigators established that they had no jobs or assets and could not repay. Signing in your own name also puts your finances and credit record at risk. **Legal clarification:** the offense depends on intent and the other legal elements; inability to repay alone does not establish fraud. Civil liability also depends on the agreement and the facts.
- Benefit: China's Criminal Law Article 175-1 covers obtaining loans, bill acceptances, letters of credit, guarantees, and similar facilities from banks or other financial institutions by deception, causing substantial loss. The penalty is up to 3 years' imprisonment or criminal detention, with a fine additionally or on its own; particularly substantial loss or other particularly serious circumstances carry 3–7 years plus a fine. The 2022 filing standard specifies direct economic loss of at least CNY 500,000. Article 193 concerns loan fraud with the purpose of unlawful appropriation: for example, inventing financing or project arrangements, using false economic contracts or supporting documents, or using false ownership documents as security. The filing threshold is CNY 50,000. A “relatively large” amount carries up to 5 years' imprisonment or criminal detention and a CNY 20,000–200,000 fine; a “huge” amount or other serious circumstances carries 5–10 years; an “especially huge” amount or other especially serious circumstances carries 10 years or more, up to life. Forging, altering, buying, or selling official state-agency documents, certificates, or seals under Article 280 carries up to 3 years, with listed alternative penalties; serious cases carry 3–10 years. Official case: a 126-person group operated in 16 provinces and provincial-level cities, presenting unemployed people with clean credit histories as highly creditworthy borrowers. It obtained over CNY 30 million at more than 80 bank branches, promising the front borrowers 30% of each loan. Prosecutors pursued 105 such borrowers. In the first-instance judgments reported in 2026, 76 of the 80 defendants sentenced received 1 year and 4 months to 6 years and 6 months for loan fraud, plus fines. Another 4, who also withdrew money for the group, received combined sentences for loan fraud and money laundering of 2 years and 4 months to 7 years and 6 months, plus fines. These are Chinese provisions; the Article 175-1 text follows Amendment XI, effective 2021-03-01. **Legal clarification:** Article 193 additionally prescribes fines of CNY 50,000–500,000 for the 5–10-year tier; for the highest tier, that fine or confiscation of property applies. The amended Article 280 also requires a fine; its 1997 wording should not be used as the current text.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2020). Criminal Law Amendment XI, provision 11 amending Article 175-1. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202012/t20201227_503700.shtml>; National People's Congress (1997). Criminal Law, Articles 88, 193, and 280; historical text, with the Article 280 update noted here. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/201802/t20180206_364975.shtml>; Supreme People's Procuratorate and Ministry of Public Security (2022). Standards for filing and prosecuting criminal cases under police jurisdiction (II), provisions 22 and 45. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/xwfbh/wsfbt/202204/t20220429_555906.shtml>; Supreme People's Procuratorate (2026). “Money laundering behind an assembly line of fraudulent loans,” Procuratorate Daily. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/zdgz/202604/t20260428_726494.shtml>; Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2012). Exit and Entry Administration Law, Article 12; National Immigration Administration publication. <https://www.nia.gov.cn/n741440/n741547/c1013311/content.html>; Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2023 revision). - Notes: In this case, prosecutors rejected “I intended to repay” after finding that the applicants had no jobs or fixed assets, could not repay, and had spent their shares; those circumstances supported a finding of unlawful appropriation. Knowingly withdrawing the group's funds to conceal their origin can also amount to money laundering when that offense's elements are met. Borrowing and signing in your own name can leave you with a debt and damage your credit record even without a criminal conviction. **Legal clarification:** this is not an automatic rule for forged signatures, identity theft, or every invalid agreement. Leaving China does not erase the case: under Article 88, evading investigation or trial after the competent authorities have opened an investigation or a court has accepted the case can remove the limitation period for prosecution. Article 12 bars Chinese citizens from leaving while a criminal sentence remains uncompleted or while they are criminal suspects or defendants; a court may also bar departure over an unresolved civil case. Under Article 266, a court may restrict departure, or ask the relevant authorities to assist, when a judgment debtor fails to perform the obligations in an enforceable legal document. The criminal provisions can be checked in the [consolidated Criminal Law](https://www.samr.gov.cn/zw/zfxxgk/fdzdgknr/bgt/art/2025/art_890f1333b6284c3cbb225c3cd2647c4b.html), and the civil enforcement provision in the [2023 NPC bulletin](https://www.npc.gov.cn/npc/c2/c30834/202401/P020240108541839745616.pdf). The April 2026 case report described first-instance judgments; some defendants had appealed and the appeals were still pending when it was published. For withdrawals and transfers, see this chapter's entry 5; for spotting loans taken out using your identity, see chapter 8, entry 8.

### 7. Do not withdraw money with a bank card you find; return a found phone or wallet to its owner or the police
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=自由 -->

- Cost: No purchase is needed. Contact the owner or take the item to a police station.
- In plain language: In China, unlawfully keeping someone else's forgotten property of sufficient value and refusing to hand it over can bring up to 2 years in prison; a huge value can raise the range to 2–5 years. Withdrawing money at an ATM using a card you found can instead amount to credit-card fraud: up to 5 years and a CNY 20,000–200,000 fine for a relatively large amount, or 5–10 years and a CNY 50,000–500,000 fine for a huge amount. This is a publicly prosecuted offense: the owner need not bring a private criminal prosecution. **Legal clarification:** a duty to return lost property does not mean that every instance of keeping it automatically meets the criminal offense; its elements and the legal classification of the property still matter.
- Benefit: Article 314 of China's Civil Code requires found lost property to be returned to the person entitled to it: promptly notify them to collect it or hand it to the police or another relevant authority. Under Criminal Law Article 270, unlawfully appropriating another person's forgotten property of a relatively large value and refusing to hand it over can bring up to 2 years' imprisonment, criminal detention, or a fine. A huge value or other serious circumstances carries 2–5 years' imprisonment plus a fine. Using a found credit card at an ATM counts as using another person's card without authorization; where the elements of an offense are met, it is treated as credit-card fraud under Article 196. A relatively large amount carries up to 5 years' imprisonment or criminal detention and a CNY 20,000–200,000 fine; a huge amount or other serious circumstances carries 5–10 years and a CNY 50,000–500,000 fine. These are Chinese national provisions. **Legal clarification:** Article 196 also has a highest tier: an especially huge amount or other especially serious circumstances can bring 10 years or more, up to life, plus a CNY 50,000–500,000 fine or confiscation of property.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: National People's Congress (2020). Civil Code, Article 314. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202006/t20200602_463888.shtml>; National People's Congress (2020). Criminal Law consolidated through Amendment XI, Articles 196 and 270. <https://jtgl.beijing.gov.cn/jgj/jgxx/flfg/fl/11033925/index.html>; Supreme People's Procuratorate (2008). Reply on the legal classification of using another person's found credit card at an ATM. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/flfg/sfjs/201208/t20120830_47871.shtml>
- Notes: Article 270 normally requires a private complaint brought by the injured party in court. ### 8. Claim discounts using your real identity; do not buy virtual numbers for fake accounts or repeatedly exploit a bug

- Cost: No purchase is needed. Skip “coupon task” groups, leave platform loopholes alone, and do not treat promotions as income you can multiply through fake accounts.
- In plain language: Using your own account to claim a legitimate coupon within the promotion's terms is ordinary shopping. Creating fake new users or pretending to be both sides of a transaction to obtain money can amount to fraud in China. Under the general fraud rules, the first monetary threshold is set locally within CNY 3,000–10,000 and the next within CNY 30,000–100,000; the corresponding prison ranges are up to 3 years and 3–10 years. CNY 500,000 reaches the highest amount category, carrying 10 years or more, up to life. **Legal clarification:** those are the general fraud thresholds. For conduct classified as telecom or online fraud, the 2016 special rules instead set nationwide thresholds of CNY 3,000, CNY 30,000, and CNY 500,000. The applicable classification matters. A loss of “CNY 400,000–500,000” cannot all be assigned to the 3–10-year band: CNY 500,000 reaches the higher category. Other legal conditions and sentencing rules also matter. In one case, delivery riders bought virtual numbers for CNY 0.1 each, used fake new accounts to claim CNY 15 off purchases of CNY 29 or more, and resold the goods. The 2011 interpretation sets threshold bands at CNY 3,000–10,000 for “relatively large,” CNY 30,000–100,000 for “huge,” and a threshold of CNY 500,000 for “especially huge”; provincial authorities choose the first two local thresholds within those bands. Teaching criminal methods, including selling instruction that meets that offense, carries up to 5 years under Article 295, with listed alternatives; serious cases carry 5–10 years, and especially serious cases 10 years or more, up to life. ### 9. Never throw anything from a balcony or window—not even cigarette butts, trash bags, or bottles

- Cost: No purchase is needed.
- In plain language: In China, throwing something from a building can bring up to 5 days of administrative detention or a fine of up to CNY 1,000 even if it hits nobody, provided it endangers people, property, or public safety. Serious circumstances bring 10–15 days of detention. Conduct that meets the criminal offense of throwing objects from a height can bring up to 1 year in prison. ### 10. Do not buy replica guns, BB guns, or air guns online, or “toy guns” of uncertain origin

- In plain language: In China, a nonstandard gun that cannot fire standard ammunition meets the firearm classification threshold if its muzzle energy per unit projectile cross-sectional area reaches 1.8 joules per square centimeter. ### 14. Do not eat wild animals or buy, sell, or keep protected wildlife as pets without a lawful basis

Wang's own sale involved 30 birds for no more than CNY 400. In November 2021, prosecutors announced decisions not to prosecute all 3 people. From 2022, the joint judicial interpretation specifies that cases involving captive-bred animals whose breeding methods are established and used at scale, and which are traded or transported as pets, are generally not treated as crimes. These are Chinese rules and historical case amounts.

### 15. Do not detain, lock up, or trap someone in their home to collect a debt
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=自由 -->

- Cost: Refusing to use coercion costs nothing. If someone will not repay, pursue a civil claim; see chapter 8, entry 16 on documenting a loan. **Practical clarification:** going to court takes time and can involve filing fees and legal fees. Budget for preparing evidence, hearings, and possible enforcement; a judgment does not guarantee that the debtor has money to pay. The standard first-instance time limits in China are 6 months under the ordinary procedure and 3 months under the simplified procedure, with extensions possible; these are statutory limits, not promised recovery dates. See [Civil Procedure Law Articles 152 and 164](https://www.gzhzcourt.gov.cn/upload/editor/file/2025/08/04/6388989731436328121054632.pdf) and the [court fee rules](https://gongbao.court.gov.cn/Details/d0faf211b7a8b0c57e4fc3daa78c36.html). If you hire a lawyer, plan to pay them yourself; do not assume every legal expense will be reimbursed.
- In plain language: Even a genuine debt does not entitle you to detain someone. China's Criminal Law expressly includes detention “to collect a debt.” Administrative penalties can be 10–15 days of detention plus a CNY 1,000–2,000 fine. Unlawful detention that meets the criminal offense can bring up to 3 years in prison; causing serious injury raises the range to 3–10 years, and causing death to 10 years or more. Use the courts to pursue repayment.
- Benefit: Under Article 47 of China's Public Security Administration Punishments Law, unlawfully restricting someone's personal freedom, entering their home, or searching their body carries 10–15 days of administrative detention plus a CNY 1,000–2,000 fine. Less serious circumstances carry 5–10 days plus a fine of up to CNY 1,000. Criminal Law Article 238 prescribes up to 3 years' imprisonment, criminal detention, noncustodial public surveillance, or deprivation of political rights for unlawfully detaining someone or otherwise depriving them of freedom. Causing serious injury carries 3–10 years; causing death carries 10 years or more. Unlawfully seizing or detaining a person to collect a debt is punished under the same provisions, even where the debt is genuine. These are Chinese national rules. **Legal clarification:** beating or humiliating the victim aggravates the punishment. Using violence that causes injury or death can instead trigger the intentional-injury or intentional-homicide provisions specified in Article 238.

### 16. Do not lend your ID or pass off someone else's documents as your own to register, open accounts, or buy tickets

- Cost: No purchase is needed. Refuse requests to borrow your ID; when you must provide a copy, mark its intended purpose.
- In plain language: In China, renting out, lending, or transferring your resident identity card brings a warning and a fine of up to CNY 200. Impersonating someone with their card, or using a fraudulently obtained card, brings a CNY 200–1,000 fine or up to 10 days of administrative detention. Using a forged or altered identity card, passport, social security card, or driver's license—or misappropriating someone else's—in an activity where national rules require proof of identity can be a criminal offense in serious circumstances. The penalty is criminal detention or noncustodial public surveillance, with a fine additionally or on its own. Using someone else's identity to travel by train or check into a hotel is the kind of identity-required situation at issue.
- Benefit: Under China's Resident Identity Card Law, renting out, lending, or transferring a card brings a warning and a fine of up to CNY 200. Impersonating another cardholder, using a card obtained by deception, or buying, selling, or using a forged or altered card brings a CNY 200–1,000 fine or up to 10 days of administrative detention. Under Criminal Law Article 280-1, using forged or altered identity cards, passports, social security cards, driver's licenses, or other lawful identity documents—or misappropriating another person's—in an activity where national rules require proof of identity carries criminal detention or noncustodial public surveillance, with a fine additionally or on its own, when the circumstances are serious. These are Chinese national provisions.

### 17. Cooperate when police stop or take you into custody: do not shove, hit, or grab a body camera; be especially careful if you have been drinking
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=自由 -->

- Cost: No purchase is needed. If you dispute an officer's actions, use a complaint, administrative review, or court proceedings afterward; do not resort to violence at the scene.
- In plain language: In China, obstructing lawful official duties can bring a warning or a fine of up to CNY 500; serious cases bring 5–10 days of administrative detention. Assaulting an officer is a separate offense. Biting, slapping, kicking, throwing an officer to the ground, or throwing objects at them counts as a violent attack if it causes at least the Chinese forensic category of minor injury, 轻微伤. The ordinary prison range is up to 3 years; using a firearm, a controlled knife, or a vehicle in a way that seriously endangers the officer can bring 3–7 years. Ordinary resistance, such as pulling an arm away or struggling free, does not meet this offense's violent-attack requirement when its harmfulness is limited. In the first month after the separate offense took effect, prosecutors approved the arrest of 405 suspects nationwide. Nearly half the cases in the reported Jiangsu sample involved drunken disturbances or disputes followed by violence against officers.
- Benefit: Obstructing state personnel in the lawful performance of their duties carries a warning or a fine of up to CNY 500; serious circumstances carry 5–10 days of administrative detention, with an additional fine of up to CNY 1,000 possible. Obstructing People's Police officers brings heavier punishment within the applicable rules. A violent attack on a People's Police officer lawfully performing official duties carries up to 3 years' imprisonment, criminal detention, or noncustodial public surveillance. An attack involving a firearm, controlled knife, vehicle collision, or similar conduct that seriously endangers the officer's safety carries 3–7 years. Biting, slapping, kicking, throwing an officer down, or throwing objects that cause at least 轻微伤 meets the violent-attack definition. Ordinary, low-harm resistance, including pulling an arm away, struggling free, or kicking one's legs, does not. These are Chinese national provisions.
- Evidence grade: A

### 18. Do not have sexual contact with a child under 14; “she agreed” is not a defense
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=自由 -->

- Cost: No purchase is needed. If you are uncertain about age, do not proceed.
- In plain language: In China, sexual intercourse with a girl under 14 is treated as rape, with heavier punishment, regardless of purported consent. The basic prison range is 3–10 years, with higher penalties in aggravated cases. For a child under 12, the perpetrator is deemed to know that she is under 14. For a girl aged 12 to under 14, awareness can be established when her physical development, speech, behavior, clothing, or daily routine indicates that she may be under 14 and the perpetrator nevertheless proceeds. A claim of genuinely not knowing her age is assessed strictly; it is not a general escape from liability.
- Benefit: Under Chinese Criminal Law Article 236, intercourse with a girl under 14 constitutes rape and is punished more severely; the basic range is 3–10 years. The Supreme People's Court's explanation distinguishes coercive conduct from conduct without coercion. With violence, threats, or other coercion, lack of knowledge that the victim was under 14 does not prevent a rape conviction. Without coercion, knowing or being expected to know her age satisfies the knowledge requirement: the 2015 reply described a person dealing with a child under 12 as someone who should have known; the current rule expressly treats the perpetrator as knowing. For ages 12 to under 14, observable development, speech, behavior, clothing, and related circumstances can establish that knowledge. The claimed exception for a genuine mistake is interpreted narrowly. These rules concern China.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: National People's Congress (2020). - Notes: **Source limitation and update:** the earlier joint guidance and the Court's 2003 reply were not accessible in the source review, so the 2015 Court reply supplied the explanation of knowledge. The [2023 joint opinion, Article 17](https://www.court.gov.cn/shenpan/xiangqing/401012.html), has now been checked: it expressly deems knowledge for victims under 12 and sets out the assessment for ages 12 to under 14. It took effect on 2023-06-01 and replaced the 2013 opinion. **Legal clarification:** 3–10 years is not the maximum for every case. Article 236 provides 10 years or more, life imprisonment, or the death penalty in specified aggravated cases, including intercourse with a girl under 10 or causing injury to a girl under 14. Turning 14 is not blanket permission: Article 236-1 separately covers sexual relations by people with specified care or supervisory duties with girls aged 14 to under 16, and Article 237 covers other sexual offenses against children, including boys. The statutory term here refers specifically to a girl under 14; the safety instruction addresses every reader regardless of gender, while the legal charge depends on the conduct and statutory elements. ### 19. Before you hit someone, consider the consequences: detention, compensation, and a criminal sentence
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=自由 -->

- Cost: No purchase is needed. Hold back for those few seconds.
- In plain language: In China, assault can bring 5–10 days of administrative detention plus a CNY 500–1,000 fine. Group assaults, attacks on specified vulnerable people, repeated assaults, or attacks on several people can bring 10–15 days plus CNY 1,000–2,000. **Legal clarification:** the specified groups are people with disabilities, pregnant people, children under 14, and adults aged 70 or over; less serious cases have a lower penalty range. An intentional assault causing injury in the Chinese forensic category 轻伤 can lead to a criminal case and up to 3 years in prison; serious injury can bring 3–10 years. Compensation is a separate issue. In one case, a man who broke another person's 3 ribs paid CNY 113,000 and reached a settlement before prosecutors decided not to prosecute. In another, a man who caused a fractured radius and refused compensation received 10 months for intentional injury. Payment or nonpayment alone does not determine the result.
- Benefit: Under China's administrative law, beating someone or intentionally injuring them carries 5–10 days of detention plus a CNY 500–1,000 fine. Aggravated cases carry 10–15 days plus CNY 1,000–2,000: group assaults; attacks on a person with a disability, a pregnant person, someone under 14 or aged 70 or over; repeated assaults; or injuring several people in one incident. Less serious cases carry up to 5 days or a fine of up to CNY 1,000. Intentional injury meeting the criminal threshold of 轻伤 carries up to 3 years' imprisonment, criminal detention, or noncustodial public surveillance; serious injury carries 3–10 years. Civil compensation can include medical expenses, lost earnings, care, transport, hospital meal allowances, and nutritional support. Where the general civil disability-compensation rules apply, the calculation uses the previous year's urban per-capita disposable income at the court's location over 20 years from the disability assessment, adjusted for the degree of lost work capacity or disability. From age 60, subtract a year for each additional year of age; from 75, use 5 years. In 2022, prosecutors nationwide received more than 70,000 cases involving 轻伤, often arising from marital, family, or neighbor disputes, or chance encounters. The prosecution service's representative cases include Lu, who fractured 3 of the victim's ribs, an injury assessed as 轻伤 level 2, paid CNY 113,000, and reached a settlement before a non-prosecution decision. Shi caused a fractured radius, also 轻伤 level 2, refused compensation, and received 10 months for intentional injury. ### 20. If you cannot care for your newborn, contact social services; formal adoption needs registration, selling a child can be trafficking, and abandonment can be a crime
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=自由 -->

- Cost: China's adoption registration itself has no fee; this is not a promise that documents, travel, or other arrangements cost nothing. Contact the civil-affairs department at county level or above, submit the required documents, and allow time for review and adoption assessment. The Chinese procedure and fee do not describe adoption costs in the US or Europe.
- In plain language: If you cannot raise your child, seek help. In China, an adoption relationship is established by registration with the civil-affairs department. People and organizations allowed to place a child for adoption include an orphan's guardian, a child-welfare institution, and birth parents whose exceptional difficulties make them unable to raise the child. Privately handing a child over, especially in return for money, can cross from an adoption arrangement into child trafficking, with a basic prison range starting at 5 years and the death penalty possible in especially serious cases. The key distinction is an intent to make an unlawful profit. Examples include having a baby to sell immediately, handing a child over for money despite knowing or not considering whether the recipient intends to raise the child, and accepting a large sum clearly beyond a modest contribution toward nutrition or a thank-you payment. A private placement driven by genuine hardship, without a profit-making purpose and with only a small such contribution, is distinguished from trafficking. **Legal clarification:** this does not make the placement a legally registered adoption or rule out other liability. Refusing a duty to care for a child in egregious circumstances can constitute abandonment, punishable by up to 5 years. Deliberately killing a newborn, including by strangling or drowning, is intentional homicide; a child being newly born does not make that lawful or automatically reduce the offense.
- Benefit: Chinese Criminal Law Article 240 provides 5–10 years' imprisonment plus a fine for trafficking in women or children. Listed aggravated circumstances bring 10 years or more or life imprisonment, plus a fine or confiscation of property; especially serious circumstances can bring the death penalty and confiscation. Paragraph 17 of the joint anti-trafficking opinion distinguishes selling one's own child from a private adoption arrangement primarily by whether the person intends to make an unlawful profit. Its examples include: treating childbirth as a means of profit and selling the baby immediately; handing a child over for money while knowing that the recipient does not intend to raise the child, or disregarding that question; and handing a child over for a large payment that is plainly not a modest nutrition contribution or thank-you payment. The paragraph also covers other circumstances demonstrating an unlawful profit-making purpose. A private placement due to hardship, without that purpose and with only a small nutrition or thank-you contribution, is not treated as trafficking on that basis. Article 261 punishes a person who owes support to someone unable to live independently because of old age, youth, illness, or another reason, but refuses that support in egregious circumstances: up to 5 years' imprisonment, criminal detention, or noncustodial public surveillance. Civil Code Article 1094 lists eligible people or organizations placing children for adoption: guardians of orphans, child-welfare institutions, and birth parents with exceptional difficulties who cannot raise their children. Article 1105 requires registration with a civil-affairs department at county level or above; the adoption relationship begins on the registration date.
