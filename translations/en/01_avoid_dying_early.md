# 1. Avoid dying early

This chapter covers deaths from external causes, a few vaccines and screening tests with particularly strong evidence, and symptoms that warrant medical attention. Figures under “Benefit” come from randomized trials, large cohort studies or official statistics; individual entries explain the study design. Costs are rough comparisons. Prices vary by model, location and purchase conditions.

### 1. Wear your seat belt in the front and back seats
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money; about 2 seconds each time you get in the car.
- In plain language: Buckle up: a seat belt roughly halves your risk of being killed in a crash when riding in the front seat of a passenger car, and reduces it by about 60% in a light truck. Among US vehicle occupants killed in 2022 whose belt use was known, half were unrestrained.
- Benefit: NHTSA estimates that seat belts reduce fatal injury risk by 45% for front-seat occupants of passenger cars and 60% for front-seat occupants of light trucks. In 2022, 50% of US passenger-vehicle occupants killed whose restraint use was known were unrestrained. WHO states that seat belts can reduce vehicle occupants’ risk of death by up to 50%. WHO estimated 248,099 road traffic deaths in China in 2021, or 17.4 per 100,000 people.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: NHTSA (2024). Occupant Protection in Passenger Vehicles: 2022 Data (DOT HS 813 573). <https://crashstats.nhtsa.dot.gov/Api/Public/ViewPublication/813573> ; WHO (2025). Road traffic injuries fact sheet. <https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/road-traffic-injuries> ; WHO GHO RS_196/RS_198 (China, 2021). <https://ghoapi.azureedge.net/api/RS_196?$filter=SpatialDim%20eq%20%27CHN%27>
- Notes: NHTSA’s 45% and 60% figures are official estimates based on US crash data, citing Kahane (2015), not a randomized trial. Back-seat occupants need belts too: in the same report, 60% of second-row occupants killed whose belt use was known were unrestrained. WHO’s figure for China is a modeled estimate, several times higher than recorded traffic-police deaths; the definitions and methods differ. This entry uses WHO’s estimate.

> Editorial note: The percentages cited from NHTSA concern fatal injuries; they do not establish the same effect for serious nonfatal injuries. The WHO page was updated on 20 July 2026; the bibliography retains its earlier 2025 date.

### 2. Wear a helmet on a motorcycle or e-bike, and fasten the chin strap
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Around 110 USD in the United States or 100 EUR in the European example for a basic motorcycle helmet; about 5 seconds to put it on before each ride. [Price examples](cost_examples.md).
- In plain language: Wear your helmet and fasten the chin strap. Motorcycle riders wearing helmets are less likely to die or sustain a head injury in a crash. A loose helmet can come off when you need it most.
- Benefit: In the Cochrane meta-analysis, helmet use among motorcycle riders was associated with lower odds of death: odds ratio (OR) 0.58, with a 95% confidence interval (CI) of 0.50–0.68. This corresponds to 42% lower odds. For head injury, the OR was 0.31 (95% CI 0.25–0.38), corresponding to 69% lower odds.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Liu BC et al. (2008). Helmets for preventing injury in motorcycle riders. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD004333.pub3>
- Notes: The research concerns motorcycle riders; the advice for e-bikes is extrapolated from a similar mechanism of injury. A helmet hanging from the handlebars will not protect your head: wear it and fasten the strap.

> Editorial note: Odds and probability are different measures. The 42% and 69% figures describe lower odds; they are not guaranteed reductions in an individual’s probability of death or injury. The estimates come from motorcycle data and cannot be applied directly to e-bikes.

### 3. Install smoke alarms, and add a carbon monoxide alarm if you heat your home with coal or gas
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: About 25 USD for a battery-powered smoke alarm and 45 USD for a carbon monoxide alarm in the US examples. German examples are about 9–20 EUR for a smoke alarm and 25–50 EUR for a CO alarm. Check the battery replacement schedule: some alarms need a new battery every year; others have a sealed battery. [Price examples](cost_examples.md).
- In plain language: In a study of house fires, a working smoke alarm was associated with about 60% lower odds of death. In about four in ten fatal residential fires, occupants are asleep when the fire starts; do not rely on the smell waking you. If you burn coal indoors or use gas heating in winter, add a carbon monoxide alarm: China recorded about 11,500 carbon monoxide poisoning deaths in 2018 alone, with roughly seven in ten deaths during the three winter months occurring at home.
- Benefit: A North Carolina case-control study found an association between a working smoke alarm and lower odds of death: OR 0.39 (95% CI 0.18–0.83). In US fatal fires in occupied residential buildings in 2018–2020, smoke alarms were absent in 24% and occupants were asleep when the fire started in 41%. China recorded 11,523 carbon monoxide poisoning deaths in 2018. The shares occurring at home in December, January and February were 72.59%, 67.42% and 66.48%, respectively.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Marshall SW et al. (1998). Fatal residential fires: who dies and who survives? JAMA. <https://doi.org/10.1001/jama.279.20.1633> ; USFA (2022). Fatal Fires in Residential Buildings (2018–2020), Topical Fire Report Series 22(2). <https://www.usfa.fema.gov/downloads/pdf/statistics/v22i2.pdf> ; You J et al. (2020). Number of Deaths due to Carbon Monoxide Poisoning by Month and by Place of Death — China, 2018. China CDC Weekly. <https://doi.org/10.46234/ccdcw2020.008> ; Editorial addition on maintenance: USFA. Smoke Alarms. <https://www.usfa.fema.gov/prevention/home-fires/prepare-for-fire/smoke-alarms/>
- Notes: The evidence on smoke alarms and mortality comes from one case-control study, not a randomized trial, hence grade B. The benefit of carbon monoxide alarms is inferred from the concentration of poisoning deaths at home in winter; a study measuring the alarms’ own effect on mortality is not cited here.

> Editorial note: An OR of 0.39 means approximately 60% lower odds of death in this study, not a 60% reduction in the probability of death for every household. Maintain alarms according to their instructions: USFA distinguishes models with replaceable batteries from those with sealed batteries. [USFA: Smoke Alarms](https://www.usfa.fema.gov/prevention/home-fires/prepare-for-fire/smoke-alarms/).

### 4. Replace gas hoses and stoves when their service life ends, do not alter gas pipes yourself, and feel free to refuse door-to-door sales from the gas company
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: The connector alone costs roughly 55 USD in the US example or 35–45 EUR in the German examples; professional installation costs extra. Budget for replacing the stove every few years, according to its specified service life, and check service-life dates when replacing equipment. [Price examples](cost_examples.md).
- In plain language: A compliant gas hose is inexpensive compared with the consequences of a leak. Replacing it when its service life ends is a duty explicitly set out in China’s regulations. Altering pipes or removing a gas meter yourself is expressly prohibited. The same regulations prohibit gas suppliers from requiring you to buy their designated products or services, so you can refuse a door-to-door sales pitch claiming that you “must replace” something.
- Benefit: The regulations require users to promptly replace gas appliances and connecting hoses that the state has ordered phased out or whose service life has expired. They prohibit unauthorized installation, alteration or removal of indoor gas facilities and meters. They also expressly prohibit suppliers from requiring users to buy designated products or accept services provided by the supplier.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: State Council of China (2010). Regulations on the Administration of Urban Gas, State Council Order No. 583. Article 27: “Gas users shall comply with rules for the safe use of gas, use compliant gas appliances and cylinders, and promptly replace gas appliances, connecting hoses and similar items that the state has explicitly ordered phased out or whose service life has expired.” Article 28 prohibits “(1) unauthorized operation of public gas valves”; “(2) using gas pipes as load-bearing supports or grounding conductors”; and “(4) unauthorized installation, alteration or removal of indoor gas facilities and gas meters.” Article 20 prohibits gas suppliers from “(6) requiring gas users to purchase products they designate or accept services they provide.” Article 29 allows users to complain to the gas regulator and other relevant authorities about charges and services: “The relevant authorities shall handle the complaint within 15 working days of receiving it.” Quotations translated from Chinese. <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2010/content_1758214.htm>
- Notes: Article 20(6) provides grounds for refusing and complaining about sales of self-closing valves and alarms offered immediately after a visit presented as a “safety inspection.” Still to be verified: the standard response to smelling gas—open windows, close the valve, do not operate electrical switches or appliances, and make phone calls outside. An official text for a word-for-word check of these steps has not yet been obtained. Carbon monoxide poisoning is a separate issue; see chapter 13.

> Editorial note: This entry concerns Chinese law. Refusing a supplier’s sales offer does not remove the duty to replace expired equipment. The emergency steps awaiting verification above are not a verified procedure.

### 5. Do not pick, buy or eat wild mushrooms, and do not rely on folk tests to identify poisonous ones
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money; the trade-off is giving up a seasonal wild food.
- In plain language: In 2025 alone, China recorded 828 mushroom poisoning incidents, involving 2,165 people and 13 deaths. That year, 34 species were newly recorded as causing poisoning. Testing with a silver needle, assuming mushrooms eaten by insects are safe, or believing that only brightly colored mushrooms are poisonous are all unreliable.
- Benefit: In 2025, China CDC investigated 828 mushroom poisoning incidents involving 2,165 people, with 13 deaths: a case fatality rate of 0.6%. From 2019 to 2024, there were 276–676 incidents each year, with case fatality rates of 0.87%–2.86%. In 2025 alone, 138 poisonous species were identified, including 34 newly recorded as causing poisoning in China.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Mushroom Poisoning Outbreaks — China, 2025. China CDC Weekly (2026): “In 2025, China CDC investigated 828 mushroom poisoning incidents across 27 provincial-level administrative divisions (PLADs), affecting 2,165 individuals and causing 13 deaths - a case fatality rate of 0.6%, the lowest of the past six years. In total, 138 poisonous mushroom species were identified, including 34 newly recorded in poisoning incidents in China.” “From 2019 to 2024, the annual number of incidents ranged from 276 to 676, and the case fatality rate ranged from 0.87% to 2.86%.” <https://doi.org/10.46234/ccdcw2026.120> ; The same journal’s report for 2024: <https://doi.org/10.46234/ccdcw2025.106>
- Notes: New species are recorded as causing poisoning each year, undermining the assumption that “local people know which ones are safe.” Silver-needle tests, cooking mushrooms with garlic, relying on evidence that insects have eaten them, and judging toxicity by bright colors do not work. After accidental ingestion, get urgent medical advice and do not induce vomiting. Take the remaining mushrooms or photographs to the hospital: poisonous mushrooms cause several different clinical syndromes, and identifying the species helps determine treatment.

> Editorial note: Do not induce vomiting after swallowing a poison. Get urgent advice from a poison center or medical service. [Poison Control: First aid guidelines](https://www.poison.org/first-aid-for-poisonings).

### 6. Keep e-bikes out of shared corridors and elevators, and do not charge them at home
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Keeping an e-bike out of your home costs nothing in itself; use the shared charging area downstairs and walk a few dozen extra meters. Charging fees depend on the facility.
- In plain language: Keep your escape route clear: an e-bike fire in a shared corridor can block your only way out. Chinese regulations prohibit parking and charging e-bikes in specified common areas of high-rise buildings. Failure to correct a violation can bring fines of 500–1,000 yuan for noncommercial activity and 2,000–10,000 yuan for commercial activity. Fines are secondary to the danger of being trapped by fire.
- Benefit: China’s Ministry of Emergency Management regulations expressly state: “Parking or charging electric bicycles is prohibited in the shared entrance halls, evacuation corridors, stairwells and emergency exits of high-rise residential and public buildings.” For failure to correct the violation, the stated fines are 500–1,000 yuan for noncommercial organizations and individuals, and 2,000–10,000 yuan for commercial ones. Quotation translated from Chinese.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Ministry of Emergency Management of China (2021). Provisions on Fire Safety Management of High-Rise Civil Buildings, Ministry Order No. 5, Articles 37 and 47. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2021-08/01/content_5628815.htm>
- Notes: A lithium battery fire can reach flashover within tens of seconds and produce highly toxic smoke; in a corridor, that means setting the only escape route on fire. Do not buy modified batteries or unbranded chargers, and do not charge overnight. Still to be verified: the official annual national numbers of e-bike fires and deaths.

> Editorial note: The cited Chinese provision covers the specified common areas of high-rise buildings. It does not, by itself, establish a blanket prohibition on elevators and charging inside every home. Article 47 distinguishes commercial from noncommercial activity for both organizations and individuals. [Official text, Articles 37 and 47](https://www.mem.gov.cn/gk/zfxxgkpt/fdzdgknr/gz11/202106/t20210621_405573.shtml).

### 7. Check your blood pressure, and if it is high, use medication to bring it to your target
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Around 60 USD for an upper-arm automatic monitor in the US example, or roughly 30–45 EUR in the German examples; one minute per measurement. Medication is an ongoing expense: the monthly amount depends on the medicine, dose and coverage, so there is no single reliable price. [Price examples](cost_examples.md).
- In plain language: For every 10 mmHg reduction in blood pressure, the risk of a major cardiovascular event falls by about 20%, stroke by 27%, heart failure by 28%, and death by 13%. In the cited Chinese screening study of people aged 35–75, 44.7% had high blood pressure; only 7.2% of those with high blood pressure had it under control.
- Benefit: A meta-analysis of 123 trials involving more than 610,000 people found that each 10 mmHg reduction in systolic blood pressure reduced major cardiovascular events: risk ratio (RR) 0.80, 95% confidence interval (CI) 0.77–0.83. The RR was 0.73 for stroke and 0.72 for heart failure. All-cause mortality fell by 13%: RR 0.87, 95% CI 0.84–0.91. Screening of 1.7 million people aged 35–75 in China found that 44.7% had hypertension. Of those with hypertension, 44.7% knew about it, 30.1% were receiving treatment, and 7.2% had it controlled.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Ettehad D et al. (2016). Blood pressure lowering for prevention of cardiovascular disease and death: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(15)01225-8> ; Lu J et al. (2017). Prevalence, awareness, treatment, and control of hypertension in China (China PEACE Million Persons Project). Lancet. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(17)32478-9>
- Notes: The target—130 or 140 mmHg—is still debated, but the value of knowing that you have high blood pressure and lowering it is not in dispute. With fewer than one in ten people with hypertension in China having it controlled in the cited study, checking your blood pressure is one of the best-value actions in this book.

> Editorial clarification: The figures describe study populations, not a guaranteed reduction in your personal risk. Agree on a treatment plan and target with a clinician; the heading is not an instruction to choose or adjust medication yourself.

### 8. If you are overweight, get a fasting blood glucose test after age 35, and repeat it every three years even if it is normal
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: For self-pay testing in the US example, allow roughly 41–45 USD for HbA1c including the listed service fee, or about 81 USD for a combined glucose/HbA1c panel including that fee. German laboratory examples list glucose at about 3 EUR and HbA1c at about 16 EUR including VAT, plus 5 EUR for blood collection. One blood draw. These are prices for alternative tests, not an instruction to order all of them. [Laboratories and price conditions](cost_examples.md).
- In plain language: A blood test can detect prediabetes before you have any symptoms. The US screening recommendation covers people aged 35–70 who are overweight or have obesity, with repeat screening every three years if results are normal.
- Benefit: The US Preventive Services Task Force recommends screening asymptomatic adults aged 35–70 who are overweight or have obesity—body mass index of at least 25 and 30, respectively—for prediabetes and type 2 diabetes. The recommendation is grade B. For adults with normal blood glucose levels, screening every three years is considered a reasonable approach.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: US Preventive Services Task Force (2021). Screening for Prediabetes and Type 2 Diabetes. <https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/screening-for-prediabetes-and-type-2-diabetes>
- Notes: A diagnosis of prediabetes does not automatically mean you need medication: lifestyle changes can reverse it in a substantial proportion of people. Awareness of diabetes among adults in China has long been low, and many people discover it only after complications develop.

> Editorial note: This entry’s evidence grade A and the USPSTF recommendation’s grade B are different assessments. The USPSTF recommendation covers nonpregnant adults aged 35–70 with overweight or obesity; it describes screening every three years as reasonable, not as an established optimal interval for everyone. [USPSTF recommendation and screening interval](https://www.uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/screening-for-prediabetes-and-type-2-diabetes).

### 9. Do not speed or drive after drinking
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: No money; allow a few more minutes for the drive and skip alcohol at social meals if you will be driving.
- In plain language: A 1% increase in average speed raises the risk of a fatal crash by about 4%. There is no safe “a little is fine” threshold for alcohol: risk starts rising at very low blood alcohol concentrations.
- Benefit: WHO reports that every 1% increase in average speed is associated with a 4% increase in fatal crash risk. The risk associated with driving after drinking begins to rise at very low blood alcohol concentrations.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: WHO (2025). Road traffic injuries fact sheet. <https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/road-traffic-injuries>
- Notes: The 1%/4% speed–fatality relationship is an estimate from crash models, not a randomized trial. For alcohol-impaired driving, only WHO’s qualitative conclusion is given here; the original research on the precise dose–response relationship has not been checked for this entry.

> Editorial note: The WHO page is now dated 20 July 2026; the bibliography retains its earlier 2025 date.

### 10. Use a child car seat for children under four; do not hold them in your arms
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=多 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: About 190 USD in the US convertible-seat example or 120 EUR in the German example for a seat covering the early years; these examples are not a minimum safe spending requirement. A seat can last several years; allow an extra minute on each trip. [Price examples](cost_examples.md).
- In plain language: A car seat lowers the risk of fatal injury in a crash by about 70% for infants under one and by about half for children aged 1–4. Holding a child in your arms does not offer that protection: you cannot keep hold of them during sudden braking.
- Benefit: NHTSA estimates that child car seats reduce fatal injury risk in passenger cars by 71% for infants under one and by 54% for children aged 1–4. The WHO estimate cited here is a 71% reduction in infant deaths.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: NHTSA (2024). Occupant Protection in Passenger Vehicles: 2022 Data (DOT HS 813 573). <https://crashstats.nhtsa.dot.gov/Api/Public/ViewPublication/813573> ; WHO (2025). Road traffic injuries fact sheet. <https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/road-traffic-injuries>
- Notes: The figures are official US estimates based on crash data. Incorrect installation can defeat the purpose of a car seat. Secure it according to the instructions, and use a rear-facing seat for a child under one.

> Editorial note: The ages above are not cutoffs for ending protection. NHTSA advises keeping a child rear-facing until they reach the seat manufacturer’s height or weight limit, then using the appropriate next restraint for their size. [NHTSA: Car seats and booster seats](https://www.nhtsa.gov/vehicle-safety/car-seats-and-booster-seats). WHO’s live fact sheet has been updated; the historical 71% figure has not been independently confirmed from the current page.

### 11. If children live with you, fit window and balcony opening restrictors; insect screens are not fall protection
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Roughly 25–30 USD for the US window-opening-control examples or about 8–10 EUR for the German child-lock examples, before installation and shipping. A simple installation takes about ten minutes. [Price examples](cost_examples.md).
- In plain language: Window locks are a relatively inexpensive safety measure. After New York City provided free window guards and home visits to explain their use to high-risk households, reported child falls in one borough fell by half over three years. The city later required landlords to install guards in homes where children lived. An insect screen can be pushed out; it is not a safety barrier.
- Benefit: New York City’s “Children Can’t Fly” program, launched in 1972, distributed free window guards and provided home education in high-risk areas. Reported falls in the Bronx fell by 50% from 1973 to 1975. The city subsequently amended its Health Code to require landlords to install window guards in apartments occupied by children aged ten or younger.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Spiegel CN, Lindaman FC (1977). Children can't fly: a program to prevent childhood morbidity and mortality from window falls. American Journal of Public Health: “Significant reduction in falls resulted, particularly in the Bronx, where reported falls declined 50 percent from 1973 to 1975.” The 1976 New York City Health Code amendment was “to require that landlords provide window guards in apartments where children ten years old and younger reside.” <https://doi.org/10.2105/AJPH.67.12.1143> ; (2021). Unintentional Window Falls in Children and Adolescents. Academic Pediatrics: 38,840 emergency department visits in the National Electronic Injury Surveillance System from January 2007 to August 2017. “The majority of falls occurred in children under the age of 6 and were related to falls from a second story or below.” <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.acap.2020.07.008>
- Notes: Guards are not just for high floors: most falls in the cited US data were from the second story or below. Insect screens keep mosquitoes out; they can give way when pushed, so do not treat them as guards. Keep beds, sofas, boxes and other climbable objects away from windows and balcony edges.

### 12. Keep children in sight near water, and wear a life jacket when boating or swimming in open water
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: About 17 USD for the basic US adult life-jacket example, or about 25–40 EUR for the German examples. Choose the right size and intended use; supervising a child still takes your attention. [Price examples](cost_examples.md).
- In plain language: In a study of recreational boaters, those wearing a life jacket had about half the risk of drowning compared with those without one. Drowning remains the leading cause of death among children aged 1–14 in China, and the rural drowning death rate is roughly twice the urban rate.
- Benefit: In a matched cohort study using US Coast Guard data, recreational boaters wearing a personal flotation device had a lower risk of drowning: adjusted RR 0.51, 95% CI 0.35–0.74. In China, drowning mortality among people under 20 fell from 6.60 per 100,000 in 2013 to 3.28 per 100,000 in 2021, with the rural rate roughly twice the urban rate. Drowning remained the leading cause of death among children aged 1–14. In 2021, drowning accounted for 31.1% of injury deaths among those aged 0–19, and road traffic injuries for 27.9%.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Cummings P et al. (2011). Association between wearing a personal floatation device and death by drowning among recreational boaters. Injury Prevention. <https://doi.org/10.1136/ip.2010.028688> ; Li Z et al. (2023). Unintentional Drowning Mortality Among Individuals Under Age 20 — China, 2013–2021. China CDC Weekly. <https://doi.org/10.46234/ccdcw2023.198> ; Zhou J et al. (2024). Injury Mortality of Children and Adolescents Aged 0–19 Years — China, 2010–2021. China CDC Weekly. <https://doi.org/10.46234/ccdcw2024.057>
- Notes: Grade A applies only to life jackets. “Keep children in sight” is consensus advice, grade C; there is no randomized trial and one would not be conducted. Drowning deaths in China are concentrated in rural areas, in summer, and among males aged 15–19.

> Editorial clarification: The life-jacket effect estimate comes from recreational boating data. It is not a separately measured estimate for open-water swimming. The Chinese statistics refer to the years cited, rather than a new measurement for the publication date of this translation.

### 13. After age 60, train your balance and leg strength, and make your bathroom and stairs safer
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=多 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Two or three 30-minute tai chi or balance-training sessions a week. A nonslip bath mat costs about 22 USD in the US example; nonslip shower strips start at about 4 EUR in the German example. Mats, handrails, nightlights and installation have separate costs; the total depends on your home. [Price examples](cost_examples.md).
- In plain language: Exercise reduces the rate of falls in older adults by about 23% and the proportion of people who fall by 15%. Practice balance exercises, such as tai chi, two or three times a week; these percentages describe exercise overall, not tai chi alone. Home safety improvements, such as nonslip surfaces and handrails, also help: studies report a reduction of roughly 20%. Falls are the leading cause of injury death among people over 65 in China, and more than half of falls happen at home.
- Benefit: A Cochrane meta-analysis of 108 trials involving 23,407 people found that exercise reduced the rate of falls in older people by 23%: rate ratio (RaR) 0.77, 95% CI 0.71–0.83. The number of people who fell declined by 15%: RR 0.85. Home safety assessment and modifications reduced the rate of falls: RaR 0.81, 95% CI 0.68–0.97. For tai chi, the reported fall-risk RR was 0.71. Falls are the leading cause of injury death among people over 65 in China; 55.97% of falls among older people recorded by surveillance in 2018 occurred at home.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Sherrington C et al. (2019). Exercise for preventing falls in older people living in the community. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD012424.pub2> ; Gillespie LD et al. (2012). Interventions for preventing falls in older people living in the community. Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews. <https://doi.org/10.1002/14651858.CD007146.pub3> ; Lu Z et al. (2021). Characteristics of Falls Among Older People — China, 2018. China CDC Weekly. <https://doi.org/10.46234/ccdcw2021.013>
- Notes: The outcome is falls, not deaths. In older people, the sequence from a fall to a hip fracture and then death can be short. The population covered is people over 60 living in the community.

> Editorial note: These estimates concern different interventions and outcomes. They do not establish that adding home modifications after exercise reduces an individual’s risk by another 20%, and the percentages should not be added. A fall rate and the proportion of people who fall are different measures; RR and RaR are not interchangeable. The home-safety estimate has been corrected to RaR 0.81, as reported in the [2012 Cochrane review](https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/CD007146_interventions-preventing-falls-older-people-living-community).

### 14. Check your hepatitis B status and vaccination record; get vaccinated if you are unprotected
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: A blood test and a vaccination course if needed. A US self-pay example is about 100 USD per adult dose including administration; a German example lists the vaccine at about 66 EUR per dose, with medical fees extra. A three-dose course takes about six months; some adult vaccines use a two-dose schedule. Laboratory charges are separate. [Prices and conditions](cost_examples.md).
- In plain language: Vaccination against hepatitis B can prevent an infection that causes liver cancer. In a trial in Qidong, China, with 30 years of follow-up, people vaccinated at birth had 84% less primary liver cancer and 72% less HBsAg positivity, a marker of infection. If you are an unvaccinated adult, ask about a catch-up course. A common schedule is three doses over six months. A low antibody result alone does not tell you whether you need another course.

### 25. Reach out when you are depressed or thinking about suicide; keep dangerous medicines and pesticides out of reach
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->

- Notes: Disputed. In the same Cochrane review, interventions without equipment provision had an IRR of 0.78, 95% CI 0.66–0.92; interventions supplying equipment did not have a better injury outcome in that comparison. This does not isolate the effect of equipment or prove it is useless: the programs and populations differed. The benefit is therefore rated medium rather than large. CCC marking is the Chinese requirement discussed in the Chinese consumer guidance, not a universal certification rule. For a stored-pressure extinguisher with a gauge, follow the instructions and check that the needle is in its operating zone, usually green. See recommendation 3 for smoke and carbon monoxide alarms, chapter 13, recommendation 12 for tourniquets, and the [household emergency equipment guide, currently in Chinese](../../docs/家庭应急装备清单.md) for selection, placement and replacement intervals.

> Editorial clarification: The Cochrane review concerns prevention of injuries in children and young people aged 19 or younger; it does not establish a mortality benefit for this kit. Use an extinguisher only on a small, contained fire when others have been alerted, the fire service has been called, you can use it safely, smoke is not threatening you, and you have a clear escape route. Otherwise leave and call from safety. [US Fire Administration](https://www.usfa.fema.gov/prevention/home-fires/prepare-for-fire/fire-extinguishers/). A filtering escape hood does not supply oxygen; its protection and limitations depend on the model. Neither a hood nor a blanket justifies delaying evacuation or entering smoke. [Manufacturer’s instructions and limitations](https://www.draeger.com/Content/Documents/Products/parat-ifu-9300800-de-me.pdf).

### 27. Get visible blood in your urine checked, even if it does not hurt or disappears the next day
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=小 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: One US self-pay urinalysis example is 46 USD including the physician service fee. This is only the laboratory test, not an assessment of bleeding. A clinician may recommend imaging and, if appropriate, cystoscopy. A comparable total in EUR has not been verified; local insurance and referral rules affect the amount you pay. [Price scope](cost_examples.md).
- In plain language: In a UK study, visible blood in the urine had a predictive value for bladder cancer of about 2.8% in people aged 60 or older and 1.2% at ages 40–59. Most people with this symptom will not have bladder cancer, but the risk is high enough to get it checked. Bleeding can stop by itself; that does not show that the cause has gone away.
- Benefit: A case-control study using UK primary-care electronic records compared 4,915 bladder cancer cases with 21,718 controls. For visible blood in urine, the positive predictive value for bladder cancer was 2.8% at age 60 or older (95% CI 2.5–3.1) and 1.2% at ages 40–59 (0.6–2.3). Non-visible blood was also associated with bladder cancer: OR 20, 95% CI 12–33; its positive predictive value at age 60 or older was 1.6% (1.2–2.1).
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Price SJ, Shephard EA, Stapley SA, Barraclough K, Hamilton WT (2014). Non-visible versus visible haematuria and bladder cancer risk: a study of electronic records in primary care. British Journal of General Practice, 64(626), e584-e589. <https://doi.org/10.3399/bjgp14X681409>
- Notes: The benefit is labeled small because the study estimates diagnostic risk, not how much earlier investigation reduces mortality. Lack of pain is not reassuring: stones or infection may cause pain, frequent urination or burning, while a tumor may cause painless, intermittent bleeding. Symptoms alone do not identify the cause. Taking anticoagulants is not a reason to skip assessment. Tell the clinician about strenuous exercise or possible contamination from menstruation; they may arrange a repeat sample, but do not use either explanation to postpone seeking advice about visible blood.

> Editorial clarification: The percentages are estimates from a particular UK primary-care population, not a diagnosis or a universal personal probability. Seek prompt medical advice even after the first episode, with a small amount of blood or no other symptoms. [NHS advice](https://www.nhs.uk/symptoms/blood-in-urine/).

### 28. Use a condom from start to finish every time; never share needles
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: About 0.80 USD per condom in one US pack or 1.10 EUR in one German pack. The main challenge is using one every time and putting it on before sexual contact. [Pack sizes and prices](cost_examples.md).
- In plain language: Consistent condom use was associated with a reduction of more than 70% in heterosexual HIV transmission. Using condoms only sometimes leaves important gaps in protection. Condoms also reduce the risk of other sexually transmitted infections, including gonorrhea and chlamydia; protection is less complete for infections spread through skin or sores outside the covered area, such as syphilis.
- Benefit: A systematic review and meta-analysis of 25 studies involving 10,676 heterosexual couples in which one partner had HIV found an HIV transmission RR of 0.29 (95% CI 0.20–0.43) for consistent condom use compared with never using condoms, and 0.23 (95% CI 0.13–0.40) compared with inconsistent use. The review concluded that consistent use reduced transmission by more than 70%, while acknowledging that protection was not complete. Condoms also help prevent other sexually transmitted infections, including syphilis, gonorrhea and chlamydia, with effectiveness depending on the infection and exposure.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Giannou FK, Tsiara CG, Nikolopoulos GK, et al. (2016). Condom effectiveness in reducing heterosexual HIV transmission: a systematic review and meta-analysis of studies on HIV serodiscordant couples. Expert Review of Pharmacoeconomics & Outcomes Research, 16(4), 489-499. <https://doi.org/10.1586/14737167.2016.1102635>
- Notes: Consistency matters, but these two comparisons do not establish that inconsistent use and never using condoms have identical effects. Shared needles or other blood-contaminated equipment used for injections, tattoos or piercings create a separate route of transmission. People at ongoing risk can ask a clinician about pre-exposure prophylaxis (PrEP). In a meta-analysis of 18 studies, trials with adherence above 70% found an HIV RR of 0.30 versus placebo (95% CI 0.21–0.45); low-adherence trials did not show clear protection (Fonner VA et al., 2016, AIDS, <https://doi.org/10.1097/QAD.0000000000001145>). That study grouping is not permission to miss 30% of doses. PrEP requires an appropriate prescription, testing and follow-up; do not buy and take it without medical supervision. For care after a possible exposure, see chapter 13; seek urgent medical advice after a possible exposure.

> Editorial clarification: Post-exposure prophylaxis (PEP) is different from PrEP. Seek urgent assessment after a possible HIV exposure: PEP must start within 72 hours, and sooner is better. [CDC PEP guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/hiv/prevention/pep.html). Protection against syphilis depends on whether the condom covers the affected area. [CDC on syphilis](https://www.cdc.gov/syphilis/about/).

### 29. Get tested after a possible HIV exposure; check local free and anonymous testing options
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: US programs offer free or reduced-cost testing; look for a suitable local service rather than assuming every test costs 0 USD. Berlin’s public service lists an HIV test at 10 EUR, with no charge for people with little or no income; counseling is free and anonymous testing is available. Allow time for a visit and a blood sample, depending on the test. [Services and conditions](cost_examples.md).
- In plain language: Find out your status so you can get treatment if you need it. HIV is treatable. A person taking antiretroviral therapy who achieves and maintains an undetectable viral load does not transmit HIV through sex. In a study following more than 700 couples and over 70,000 condomless sex acts, no infections were transmitted between the study partners. A negative test too soon after exposure cannot rule out infection.
- Benefit: In China, Article 23 of the 2006 Regulations on AIDS Prevention and Treatment requires designated health institutions to provide voluntary counseling and initial HIV screening free of charge. That is a Chinese rule, not a guarantee of free care worldwide. WHO states that people taking antiretroviral therapy with an undetectable viral load do not transmit HIV sexually. PARTNER2 followed 782 gay male couples with different HIV statuses for 1,593 eligible couple-years, covering 76,088 condomless sex acts. There were 15 new infections, but genetic analysis linked none to the study partner: within-couple transmission was zero.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: State Council of China (2006). Regulations on AIDS Prevention and Treatment, Articles 3, 23 and 38, republished by the Beijing Municipal Health Commission. <https://wjw.beijing.gov.cn/zwgk_20040/zcwj2022/flfg/202304/t20230408_2992986.html> ; Rodger AJ, Cambiano V, Bruun T, et al. (2019). Risk of HIV transmission through condomless sex in serodifferent gay couples with the HIV-positive partner taking suppressive antiretroviral therapy (PARTNER): final results of a multicentre, prospective, observational study. The Lancet, 393(10189), 2428-2438. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S0140-6736(19)30418-0>
- Notes: Test timing matters. A Guangdong CDC explanation gives early possible detection times of about 1 week for nucleic acid tests, 2 weeks for fourth-generation antigen/antibody tests, and 3 weeks for third-generation antibody tests; it suggests testing after at least 2 weeks, repeating after 2–4 weeks, and describes exclusion after 3 months as greater than 99.99% (<https://cdcp.gd.gov.cn/jkjy/kpydjwjxz/content/post_3441820.html>). Early possible detection is not the end of the window period, and that percentage is not a universal guarantee for every test or situation. Use the test-specific windows below and a clinician’s follow-up plan. A reactive self-test requires confirmatory testing. Separately, China’s Article 3 prohibits discrimination and protects rights including marriage, employment, medical care and education; Article 38 requires disclosure to sexual partners. These provisions concern China, not US or European law. Pregnancy screening for three infections and prevention of transmission to the baby are covered in chapter 27.

> Editorial clarification: CDC gives usual detection windows of 10–33 days for a nucleic acid test, 18–45 days for a laboratory antigen/antibody test using blood from a vein, 18–90 days for a rapid finger-stick antigen/antibody test, and 23–90 days for antibody tests. These ranges do not replace medical follow-up after PEP or during PrEP. Seek PEP assessment immediately after a possible exposure rather than waiting for a testing window. [CDC testing guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/hiv/testing/index.html) and [PEP guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/hiv/prevention/pep.html). PARTNER2 used a viral-load threshold below 200 copies/mL, which is not identical to the detection limit of every laboratory assay. [WHO guidance](https://www.who.int/news-room/fact-sheets/detail/hiv-aids). Its zero-transmission result concerns sex while viral suppression is maintained; it does not establish safety for sharing needles or protection from other infections.
