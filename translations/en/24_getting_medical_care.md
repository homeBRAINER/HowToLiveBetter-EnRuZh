# 24. Getting medical care with less expense and hassle

This chapter is about the money and time involved in getting care: where to start for an ordinary illness, how referrals work, which records to keep, where to go in an emergency, and what assessments or documents may be needed after a serious injury. For income support and benefits, see [chapter 7](07_when_money_is_tight.md); for continuing management of chronic conditions, see [chapter 16](16_living_with_a_chronic_condition.md); and for what to do before emergency help arrives, see [chapter 13](13_emergencies.md). Seek emergency care promptly for potentially life-threatening symptoms; cost-saving steps and routine referrals must not delay it.

The Chinese rules on referrals and payment below chiefly draw on the State Council's **April 2026** measures to develop a tiered healthcare system. They link primary care to the cost of treatment, but implementation depends on the region and its insurance fund. US and European arrangements are described separately: Chinese reimbursement percentages and patient deductibles do not apply elsewhere.

The introduction and entries 1–2 of 11 are translated so far. The [Chinese chapter](../../book/24-看病.md) is available in full.

### 1. For routine care, check the referral route before a planned hospital admission
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Checking referral rules costs $0 or €0 and a little time; a primary-care visit and treatment may have separate costs under your country's health system or your insurance plan.
- In plain language: If it is not an emergency, ask your primary-care service and insurer how to reach the right hospital and how transfers affect the part of the bill you pay yourself. In China, when an insured patient is referred step by step from primary care to a higher-level hospital **within the same insurance-pooling area**, the inpatient spending threshold before reimbursement **may be counted continuously**. If the hospital later transfers the patient back to primary care during the same course of illness, no new inpatient threshold is set there. Do not assume this works without the required referral and local rules.
- Benefit: China's **2026** measures allow continuous calculation of the inpatient reimbursement threshold for insured patients referred upward in stages from a primary healthcare facility **within one insurance-pooling area**. When an inpatient is transferred back down from a higher-level hospital to a primary facility **during the same episode of the same illness**, another inpatient threshold is not set. This may prevent a patient from having to reach a second threshold while continuing treatment; the policy does not promise the same saving for everyone.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: General Office of China's State Council (2026), measures to accelerate the development of a tiered healthcare system, section 9. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202604/content_7065031.htm>; [publication by China's National Health Commission](https://www.nhc.gov.cn/wjw/mtbd/202604/7f64a7d2f54c4f7197692ff18869b02a.shtml). [US Medicare: referrals are generally required for specialist care in an HMO Medicare Advantage plan](https://www.medicare.gov/health-drug-plans/health-plans/your-health-plan-options/HMO). [Your Europe: planned treatment and reimbursement in another EU country depend on national insurance conditions](https://europa.eu/youreurope/citizens/health/planned-healthcare/expenses-reimbursements/index_en.htm).
- Notes: The Chinese threshold is the amount the patient pays before insurance reimbursement starts; the exact rule depends on the pooling area and a proper referral. Going straight to a tertiary hospital and later transferring down **may** produce a different calculation, but the claim of “two deductibles” is not universal without the local policy and care details. Ask the clinic, hospital or insurer whether the first payment is carried over. In the US, an HMO Medicare Advantage plan commonly requires referrals for specialists and has network rules; other insurance arrangements differ. In Europe, national and insurer rules vary, and planned care in another EU country can have separate prior-authorisation requirements. Neither setting uses China's national **2026** rule. For an emergency, seek appropriate emergency care immediately rather than waiting for a routine referral; see [chapter 13](13_emergencies.md) for first actions.

### 2. Check the local reimbursement rate before comparing hospital levels
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Checking your plan's coverage and rates costs $0 or €0 and some time; the treatment itself may still have a patient cost.
- In plain language: In China, planned inpatient care at a lower-level facility may be reimbursed at a higher rate than at a tertiary hospital. A **2026** policy asks regions to aim for a gap of about **10 percentage points** between successive levels, but actual rates are set locally. This is no reason to treat every illness at a community clinic: first make sure the facility can provide the care you need and check what your insurance will pay. In the US or Europe, use your own plan and local rules instead of the Chinese percentage.
- Benefit: The Chinese policy calls for different inpatient reimbursement shares at healthcare institutions of different levels, taking local conditions into account. **Within a single insurance-pooling area**, the intended gap is **about 10 percentage points between successive levels in principle**. Annual new health-insurance funds are also to favour primary-care facilities. Where conditions permit, regions may introduce level-based differences for outpatient care too. This is a policy direction, not an already universal rate for every area or patient.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: General Office of China's State Council (2026), measures to accelerate the development of a tiered healthcare system, section 11 (a policy target of about 10 percentage points between levels). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202604/content_7065031.htm>; [publication by China's National Health Commission](https://www.nhc.gov.cn/wjw/mtbd/202604/7f64a7d2f54c4f7197692ff18869b02a.shtml). [HealthCare.gov: how deductibles, coinsurance and out-of-pocket costs differ by plan](https://www.healthcare.gov/choose-a-plan/your-total-costs/). [Your Europe: cross-border planned-care reimbursement varies with coverage and circumstances](https://europa.eu/youreurope/citizens/health/planned-healthcare/expenses-reimbursements/index_en.htm).
- Notes: **10 percentage points** is a difference between reimbursement rates, not 10% of the bill and not a guaranteed personal saving. Without a covered-services list, the bill and the local rate, no honest USD or EUR saving can be calculated. In the US, check your plan's network, covered services, deductible and coinsurance before choosing a provider. In Europe, ask your national public insurer or the relevant provider; no single EU-wide percentage applies to ordinary domestic care. A lower hospital level or a better rate never substitutes for the appropriate clinical assessment when symptoms are serious.
