# 24. Getting medical care with less expense and hassle

This chapter is about the money and time involved in getting care: where to start for an ordinary illness, how referrals work, which records to keep, where to go in an emergency, and what assessments or documents may be needed after a serious injury. For income support and benefits, see [chapter 7](07_when_money_is_tight.md); for continuing management of chronic conditions, see [chapter 16](16_living_with_a_chronic_condition.md); and for what to do before emergency help arrives, see [chapter 13](13_emergencies.md). Seek emergency care promptly for potentially life-threatening symptoms; cost-saving steps and routine referrals must not delay it.

The Chinese rules on referrals and payment below chiefly draw on the State Council's **April 2026** measures to develop a tiered healthcare system. They link primary care to the cost of treatment, but implementation depends on the region and its insurance fund. US and European arrangements are described separately: Chinese reimbursement percentages and patient deductibles do not apply elsewhere.

The introduction and all 11 entries are translated. The [Chinese chapter](../../book/24-看病.md) is available in full.

### 1. For routine care, check the referral route before a planned hospital admission
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Checking referral rules costs $0 or €0 and a little time; a primary-care visit and treatment may have separate costs under your country's health system or your insurance plan.
- In plain language: If it is not an emergency, ask your primary-care service and insurer how to reach the right hospital and how transfers affect the part of the bill you pay yourself. In China, when an insured patient is referred step by step from primary care to a higher-level hospital **within the same insurance-pooling area**, the inpatient spending threshold before reimbursement **may be counted continuously**. If the hospital later transfers the patient back to primary care during the same course of illness, no new inpatient threshold is set there. Do not assume this works without the required referral and local rules.
- Benefit: China's **2026** measures allow continuous calculation of the inpatient reimbursement threshold for insured patients referred upward in stages from a primary healthcare facility **within one insurance-pooling area**. When an inpatient is transferred back down from a higher-level hospital to a primary facility **during the same episode of the same illness**, another inpatient threshold is not set. This may prevent a patient from having to reach a second threshold while continuing treatment; the policy does not promise the same saving for everyone.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: General Office of China's State Council (2026), measures to accelerate the development of a tiered healthcare system, section 9. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202604/content_7065031.htm>; [publication by China's National Health Commission](https://www.nhc.gov.cn/wjw/mtbd/202604/7f64a7d2f54c4f7197692ff18869b02a.shtml). [US Medicare: referrals are generally required for specialist care in an HMO Medicare Advantage plan](https://www.medicare.gov/health-drug-plans/health-plans/your-health-plan-options/HMO). [Your Europe: planned treatment and reimbursement in another EU country depend on national insurance conditions](https://europa.eu/youreurope/citizens/health/planned-healthcare/expenses-reimbursements/index_en.htm).
- Notes: The Chinese threshold is the amount the patient pays before insurance reimbursement starts; the exact rule depends on the pooling area and a proper referral. Going straight to a tertiary hospital and later transferring down **may** produce a different calculation, but the claim of “two deductibles” is not universal without the local policy and care details. Ask the clinic, hospital or insurer whether the first payment is carried over. In the US, an HMO Medicare Advantage plan commonly requires referrals for specialists and has network rules; other insurance arrangements differ. In Europe, national and insurer rules vary, and planned care in another EU country can have separate prior-authorisation requirements. Neither setting uses China's national **2026** rule. For an emergency, seek appropriate emergency care immediately rather than waiting for a routine referral; see [chapter 13](13_emergencies.md) for first actions.

### 2. Check the local reimbursement rate before comparing hospital levels
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Checking your plan's coverage and rates costs $0 or €0 and some time; the treatment itself may still have a patient cost.
- In plain language: In China, planned inpatient care at a lower-level facility may be reimbursed at a higher rate than at a tertiary hospital. A **2026** policy asks regions to aim for a gap of about **10 percentage points** between successive levels, but actual rates are set locally. This is no reason to treat every illness at a community clinic: first make sure the facility can provide the care you need and check what your insurance will pay. In the US or Europe, use your own plan and local rules instead of the Chinese percentage.
- Benefit: The Chinese policy calls for different inpatient reimbursement shares at healthcare institutions of different levels, taking local conditions into account. **Within a single insurance-pooling area**, the intended gap is **about 10 percentage points between successive levels in principle**. Annual new health-insurance funds are also to favour primary-care facilities. Where conditions permit, regions may introduce level-based differences for outpatient care too. This is a policy direction, not an already universal rate for every area or patient.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: General Office of China's State Council (2026), measures to accelerate the development of a tiered healthcare system, section 11 (a policy target of about 10 percentage points between levels). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202604/content_7065031.htm>; [publication by China's National Health Commission](https://www.nhc.gov.cn/wjw/mtbd/202604/7f64a7d2f54c4f7197692ff18869b02a.shtml). [HealthCare.gov: how deductibles, coinsurance and out-of-pocket costs differ by plan](https://www.healthcare.gov/choose-a-plan/your-total-costs/). [Your Europe: cross-border planned-care reimbursement varies with coverage and circumstances](https://europa.eu/youreurope/citizens/health/planned-healthcare/expenses-reimbursements/index_en.htm).
- Notes: **10 percentage points** is a difference between reimbursement rates, not 10% of the bill and not a guaranteed personal saving. Without a covered-services list, the bill and the local rate, no honest USD or EUR saving can be calculated. In the US, check your plan's network, covered services, deductible and coinsurance before choosing a provider. In Europe, ask your national public insurer or the relevant provider; no single EU-wide percentage applies to ordinary domestic care. A lower hospital level or a better rate never substitutes for the appropriate clinical assessment when symptoms are serious.

### 3. Use a referral or the hospital's referral service to reach a larger hospital
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: Asking about the referral route costs $0 or €0 but takes time; a consultation or treatment may have a separate charge under your health system or plan.
- In plain language: If an appointment at a larger hospital seems out of reach, ask your primary-care clinician about an official referral and call the hospital's referral service. Do not buy an appointment from a broker: that will not determine the right specialty or replace a clinical assessment. In China, leading hospitals in closely integrated healthcare alliances are to reserve some appointments and beds for people referred from primary care. A reserved allocation does not promise an immediate appointment in every place.
- Benefit: China's **2026** measures require the leading hospital of a closely integrated healthcare alliance to reserve **a proportion of outpatient slots and inpatient beds** for referrals from primary-care facilities and to receive referred patients promptly. Healthcare institutions are to establish a referral centre or designate a standing department for this work, with full coverage targeted **by 2027**.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: General Office of China's State Council (2026), measures to accelerate the development of a tiered healthcare system, section 8 (reserved slots and the **2027** referral-service target). <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202604/content_7065031.htm>; [full text published by China's National Healthcare Security Administration](https://www.nhsa.gov.cn/art/2026/4/10/art_14_20158.html). [US Medicare: specialist referrals and network rules in an HMO Medicare Advantage plan](https://www.medicare.gov/health-drug-plans/health-plans/your-health-plan-options/HMO).
- Notes: The Chinese reserved-slot rule applies to **leading hospitals in closely integrated healthcare alliances**; the **2027** date is a policy target, not proof that every institution already has a referral centre. If you cannot find its referral service, use the hospital's official contact number. In the US, check whether your own plan requires a referral and whether the hospital is in network; HMO Medicare Advantage rules are not universal. In Europe, ask your primary-care provider or national insurer how specialist referrals work in your country. Seek emergency care without waiting for a routine booking when the condition is urgent.

### 4. Check referral and payment rules before planned care away from home
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Asking your clinician or insurer about the rules costs $0 or €0; travel and treatment may carry charges under your local system or plan.
- In plain language: Before travelling for planned treatment, find out who must assess the need for the trip, how to document it and how much your insurer will cover. In China, the need for treatment outside the insurance-pooling area or province should **in principle** be assessed by a doctor with the professional rank of **associate chief physician or above** at a secondary or tertiary hospital. For people temporarily seeking care in another province, the reimbursement level should differ reasonably from that at a comparable institution where they are insured. This is a Chinese policy, not a universal requirement for every traveller.
- Benefit: China's **2026** measures set an **in-principle** assessment of the need for cross-region or cross-province care by a secondary- or tertiary-hospital doctor ranked **副主任医师 (associate chief physician) or higher**. They also call for direct settlement across regions and for a **reasonable difference** in reimbursement between temporary out-of-province care and same-level care in the insured person's home area. Local rules determine the practical process and rates.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: General Office of China's State Council (2026), measures to accelerate the development of a tiered healthcare system, sections 7 and 9. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202604/content_7065031.htm>; [full text published by China's National Healthcare Security Administration](https://www.nhsa.gov.cn/art/2026/4/10/art_14_20158.html). [Your Europe: planned treatment in another EU country and possible prior authorisation](https://europa.eu/youreurope/citizens/health/planned-healthcare/right-to-treatment/index_en.htm).
- Notes: In Chinese insurance, **temporary travel** and **long-term residence away from the home region** are distinct registration categories; [chapter 16](16_living_with_a_chronic_condition.md) covers categories and direct settlement. The policy sets no single discount or patient surcharge. In the US, ask your insurer about network, referral and prior-authorisation rules before planned out-of-area treatment. In Europe, domestic rules vary by country, while planned treatment **in another EU country** may involve separate authorisation and reimbursement conditions. Do not delay emergency care to arrange a planned-care referral.

### 5. For a stable chronic condition, ask about follow-up in primary care
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: Asking where to arrange follow-up or repeat prescriptions costs $0 or €0; visits and medicines follow local coverage rules. Changing a familiar care routine can take effort.
- In plain language: If your diagnosis is clear, treatment is established and your condition is stable, ask your clinician whether you can have monitoring and repeat prescriptions closer to home. A specialist hospital is still important when symptoms worsen, new problems appear or the case is complex. Chinese policy gradually shifts routine follow-ups for stable chronic illness toward primary care and extends specialist teams' ordinary clinics there. It does not bar a patient from returning to a specialist when needed.
- Benefit: China's **2026** measures direct tertiary hospitals to focus on emergencies, severe illness and complex conditions, while **gradually and as appropriate** reducing ordinary outpatient follow-ups for common diseases and diagnosed, stable chronic conditions. Within closely integrated healthcare alliances, higher-level hospitals are to run primary-care clinics for hypertension, diabetes and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, provide ongoing management and extend their expert teams' ordinary outpatient sessions into primary care.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: General Office of China's State Council (2026), measures to accelerate the development of a tiered healthcare system, sections 1 and 4. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202604/content_7065031.htm>; [full text published by China's National Healthcare Security Administration](https://www.nhsa.gov.cn/art/2026/4/10/art_14_20158.html).
- Notes: For **eligible** patients, the Chinese policy permits a long-term prescription from a primary-care facility for **no more than 12 weeks at a time**; see [chapter 16](16_living_with_a_chronic_condition.md). This is not a US- or Europe-wide prescription limit. Ask your own clinician or pharmacist about local repeat-prescription and coverage rules. Do not change or stop prescribed treatment on your own; request reassessment if your condition changes, and seek emergency care for severe symptoms.

### 6. Keep copies of your medical records, test results and images
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: You can ask how to access your records for $0 or €0; making copies may carry a charge under local rules. In China, any copying charge must be published.
- In plain language: Keep discharge summaries, laboratory reports, written imaging results and the images themselves together. You may need them when moving to another hospital, seeking a second opinion, claiming on insurance or resolving a dispute. In China, patients have a legal right to inspect and copy the medical records about them: outpatient and inpatient charts, temperature and orders sheets, test and imaging records, consent forms, surgery and anaesthesia records, pathology, nursing notes and bills. Ask the hospital's records service for copies rather than relying on your treating doctor to hand them over during an appointment.
- Benefit: Article 16 of China's **2018** regulation gives patients the right to **inspect and copy** those records. The institution must offer copies and mark them as certified; the patient or a close relative is present during copying, and any cost-based copying charge must be published. After a patient's death, close relatives can access and copy records under the same regulation. The **2013** medical-records rules require a designated department or staff member to handle copy requests and confirmation in the applicant's presence.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: China's State Council (2018), [Regulation on the Prevention and Handling of Medical Disputes, Decree No. 701, articles 15–16](https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2018-08/31/content_5318057.htm); [readable publication by the Beijing government](https://www.beijing.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengcefagui/201905/t20190522_61432.html). National Health Commission of China (2013), [Medical Records Management Rules, 2013 edition, articles 19–22](http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2014/content_2600084.htm); [National Health Commission publication](https://www.nhc.gov.cn/zwgk/wtwj/201312/a84f3666d1be49f7a959d7912a978db7.shtml). [US HHS: HIPAA right of access, scope of records and permitted copying fees](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-professionals/privacy/guidance/access/index.html). [European Commission: GDPR right of access and copies of personal data](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection/information-individuals_en).
- Notes: File records by date. Give particular attention to the discharge summary, operation report, pathology report and key images; keep a timeline of long-term measurements as described in [chapter 16](16_living_with_a_chronic_condition.md). Chinese law prohibits altering, falsifying, concealing, destroying or taking medical records. In the US, HIPAA-covered providers and plans generally must provide access to records in the designated record set, including images, with limited exceptions; permitted copying fees are restricted to reasonable, cost-based charges. In the EU, the GDPR gives a right to access and a copy of personal data, generally free for the first copy, while national health-record procedures can add detail. Ask the provider how to request the actual medical file and images in your country.

### 7. If you dispute the care, promptly ask for records and physical evidence to be preserved
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Asking how to preserve evidence costs $0 or €0; expert review or legal help can carry separate costs under local rules.
- In plain language: If a procedure leads to a dispute about what happened, contact the hospital's official complaints service promptly and ask it to preserve the chart and any relevant physical items. **In China**, records are sealed or unsealed with both sides present. The hospital lists what was sealed, both sides sign or stamp the list and each keeps a copy. If the chart is unfinished, the completed portion is sealed first and the properly completed remainder later. For a suspected problem with an infusion, transfusion, injection or medicine, preserve the items involved as well.
- Benefit: China's **2018** medical-dispute regulation allows the **original or a copy** of the chart to be sealed, with the institution keeping it. Completed records and later-completed records are sealed in stages; both sides sign or stamp the inventory and each receive a copy. The parties jointly seal physical items implicated in a suspected adverse outcome from an infusion, blood transfusion, injection or medicine. If testing is needed, they jointly commission a qualified laboratory; if they cannot agree, the county-level health authority where the hospital is located designates one. When transfused blood is involved, the blood station must send a representative to attend its preservation. Falsifying or destroying records is prohibited. The institution may unseal records once the dispute is resolved or after **3 years** without a further request to resolve it. The regulation took effect on **1 October 2018**.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: China's State Council (2018), [Regulation on the Prevention and Handling of Medical Disputes, Decree No. 701, articles 15, 18 and 24–25](https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2018-08/31/content_5318057.htm); [readable publication by the Beijing government](https://www.beijing.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengcefagui/201905/t20190522_61432.html). [US HHS: HIPAA access to records and limited correction rights](https://www.hhs.gov/hipaa/for-individuals/medical-records/index.html). [European Commission: access to and copies of personal data under GDPR](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection/information-individuals_en).
- Notes: Dispute-related sealing differs from routine copying in [entry 6](#6-keep-copies-of-your-medical-records-test-results-and-images). In China, hospitals must display the dispute procedure and complaints contact details. Sealing **does not** mean an unfinished chart can never be completed: a later-completed section is sealed separately. Once the dispute is resolved, or after **3 years** without a further resolution request from the patient, the institution may unseal the records. US and European readers should not assume their jurisdiction has China's two-party sealing procedure; request copies, put the complaint in writing and ask about local evidence-preservation rules. Do not try to seize materials or interrupt treatment.

### 8. For a serious condition, tell emergency triage staff immediately
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: Reporting serious symptoms at the emergency entrance costs $0 or €0 as a step in seeking help; the care itself may still be billed under local rules. Call an ambulance if the person cannot safely reach hospital.
- In plain language: With heavy bleeding, trouble breathing, confusion, major injury or crushing chest pain, do not silently wait in the routine registration queue. Tell staff at the emergency entrance what is happening, or call an ambulance. Patients are seen according to how seriously ill they are, not simply who arrived first. If you deteriorate while waiting, ask for reassessment immediately. Clinical staff assign triage levels; do not try to decide yours from one measurement.
- Benefit: China's **2024** national emergency-quality indicators describe **4 triage levels**: **I** means immediately life-threatening and needs immediate care; **II** is severe and assessment often happens alongside treatment; **III** needs assessment and treatment soon; **IV** is non-emergency or subacute and is seen in the ordinary queue. A **2019 Beijing** standard requires immediate assessment and a resuscitation area for level **1**, treatment within **10 minutes** for level **2**, priority over level **4** and reassessment after a wait of more than **30 minutes** for level **3**, and possible reassessment after more than **4 hours** for level **4**. These response times are **Beijing's**, not a single national timetable.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: China's National Health Commission (2024), notice No. 150 on quality indicators for 6 specialties (2024 edition), [Emergency Medicine Quality-Control Indicators (2024 edition), appendix 1, indicator 4](https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202405/content_6951260.htm); [government publication with attachment](https://app.www.gov.cn/govdata/gov/202405/17/515069/article.html). Beijing Municipal Health Commission (2019), [emergency pre-triage plan and draft grading standard](http://wjw.beijing.gov.cn/zwgk_20040/ylws/201912/t20191216_1242338.html). [US 911 programme: call 911 for an emergency](https://www.911.gov/calling-911/). [European Commission: 112 reaches emergency services across the EU](https://digital-strategy.ec.europa.eu/en/policies/112).
- Notes: For **adults**, Beijing lists among level **1** examples a heart rate **above 180** or **below 40** per minute, systolic blood pressure **below 70 mmHg**, oxygen saturation **below 80% with breathing difficulty that does not improve with oxygen**, shock, a confirmed heart attack and an acute change in consciousness. These are clinical triage examples, **not thresholds to wait for at home**; children have different criteria. For a life-threatening emergency, call **120 in China**, **911 in the US**, or **112 in the EU**. Ambulance staff can alert the receiving hospital about a critically ill patient; [chapter 13](13_emergencies.md) covers what to do before help arrives. A repeat prescription, removal of stitches or routine follow-up for a stable condition may have low priority; staff decide the level.

### 9. Seek emergency help even without money or identity papers
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Do not wait to find cash or documents before seeking help; **$0 or €0 upfront is not a promise that all subsequent care will be free**. Billing depends on the jurisdiction, insurance and the care provided.
- In plain language: If your condition could be life-threatening, do not stay home because you cannot pay, lack identification or cannot say who you are. In China, hospitals must promptly treat critically ill or injured patients. A special fund can cover the **emergency-care costs** when a patient's identity cannot be established or the person genuinely cannot pay. The hospital applies for this reimbursement after care; patients do not collect money from the fund. The **120** ambulance service may not refuse or delay service solely over payment.
- Benefit: China's **2013** emergency medical assistance system covers people in China with severe, urgent illness or injury who need emergency care but whose identity is unknown or who cannot pay. The fund can cover emergency costs of unidentified people and unpaid emergency bills of identified people unable to pay, after other applicable channels such as the responsible person, work-injury or basic medical insurance and assistance have been checked. Medical institutions must not refuse, deflect or delay emergency rescue. National ambulance rules forbid refusal or delay on payment grounds; the call number is **120**. Transport priorities are proximity, urgency, appropriate clinical capacity and the patient's wishes, in that order.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: General Office of China's State Council (2013), 2013 Directive No. 15, [Guidance on Establishing an Emergency Medical Assistance System](https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2013-03/01/content_6069.htm); [publication by China's National Health Commission](https://www.nhc.gov.cn/tigs/c100053/201302/262df861c35b4f3e8fea9058132da23c.shtml). China's National Health Commission (2014), Order No. 3, [Pre-hospital Emergency Care Regulations, articles 13, 22, 23 and 25](http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2014/content_2580977.htm); [current agency publication](https://www.nhc.gov.cn/wjw/c100221/202201/26ea3c97e82d466f9aa2b4a9901ae187.shtml). [US Centers for Medicare & Medicaid Services: emergency-department rights under EMTALA](https://www.cms.gov/priorities/your-patient-rights/emergency-room-rights). [Your Europe: medically necessary care on a temporary stay and EHIC costs](https://europa.eu/youreurope/citizens/health/unplanned-healthcare/temporary-stays/index_en.htm).
- Notes: The Chinese fund covers the **emergency phase**, not every later inpatient stay or the entire bill. It does not pay for someone able but unwilling to pay; local rules define eligibility and amounts. For later financial help, see [chapter 7](07_when_money_is_tight.md). An ambulance may take the patient to the nearest appropriate hospital rather than their preferred one. In the US, **EMTALA** requires a covered hospital emergency department to offer an appropriate medical screening exam and, when an emergency condition exists, stabilizing care or an appropriate transfer **regardless of ability to pay**; it does **not** erase a later bill. In the EU, an EHIC can cover medically necessary public care during a temporary stay at the host country's terms and cost, which may include a charge. For a life-threatening emergency call **120 in China**, **911 in the US**, or **112 in the EU**.

### 10. Assess lasting effects of an injury after treatment ends or the outcome stabilises
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: An assessment may carry a fee. Ask the authorised body for a quote in USD or EUR and who must pay it upfront in your case. Timing depends on treatment and a stable outcome; a **3–6 month** rule of thumb after injury is not a mandatory period and may be too short.
- In plain language: If an injury has left lasting functional problems, gather your medical records and images and identify the kind of assessment your claim needs. For personal-injury compensation in China, residual impairment is assessed **after treatment of the original injury and related complications is completed, or after the clinical result is stable**. An assessment too early can misstate the eventual condition in either direction. The lasting impairment matters; a diagnosis of “fracture” alone does not establish a grade.
- Benefit: China's bodily-injury impairment standard, effective from **1 January 2017**, calls for assessment **after treatment or clinical stabilisation**. It has **10 grades**: grade **1** corresponds to a **100%** impairment rate and grade **10** to **10%**, with **10 percentage points** between successive grades. The assessment considers damage to organs and tissues, functional loss, dependence on medical or nursing care, and relevant social and psychological effects. Examples of criteria for **grade 10** include compression of **1 vertebral body** by at least **1/3**; **2 or more pelvic fractures** healed with deformity; or loss of **more than 25%** of function in a major limb joint other than the ankle. An ordinary fracture that heals without qualifying lasting effects does not automatically meet a grade.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Supreme People's Court, Supreme People's Procuratorate and the ministries of public security, state security and justice of China (2016), announcement of 18 April 2016 (effective 2017-01-01), [Standard for Grading the Degree of Disability Caused by Bodily Injury, sections 4.2, 4.4, 4.5, 5.10.6 and 6.2](https://www.moj.gov.cn/pub/sfbgw/zwxxgk/fdzdgknr/fdzdgknrtzwj/201908/P020210316701681025917.pdf); [alternate Ministry of Justice publication](https://www.moj.gov.cn/pub/sfbgw/zwxxgk/fdzdgknr/fdzdgknrtzwj/202103/P020210316701681025917.pdf). [US Social Security Administration: disability-benefit decisions use medical evidence and their own legal standard](https://www.ssa.gov/disability/determination.htm). [European Commission: invalidity-assessment criteria vary by country](https://employment-social-affairs.ec.europa.eu/policies-and-activities/moving-working-europe/eu-social-security-coordination/frequently-asked-questions/faq-social-security-invalidity_en).
- Notes: Keep **3 different processes** apart: forensic assessment of lasting bodily injury after assault, a road crash or another personal-injury claim for compensation ([chapter 9](09_legal_pitfalls.md)); work-injury or work-capacity assessment ([chapter 19](19_work_exit_and_injury.md)); and eligibility for a disability-status document and social support ([entry 11](#11-apply-for-disability-status-after-treatment-if-impairment-persists)). The Chinese standard bars counting the same site and type of impairment twice but requires separate grades for qualifying impairments at multiple sites. An assessor must also explain how much an earlier illness contributed to the outcome. Bring charts, operation reports and follow-up images ([entry 6](#6-keep-copies-of-your-medical-records-test-results-and-images)). Fees and timing in the US and Europe depend on the particular process; Chinese impairment percentages **are not** a local compensation scale. Missing images make a finding harder to substantiate but do not allow anyone to predict a grade in advance.

### 11. Apply for disability status after treatment if impairment persists
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: In China, the certificate itself has no issue fee; applicants generally pay for the assessment and photos, with possible local subsidy or hardship relief. In the US or Europe, ask the relevant authority about its own fees in USD or EUR. For a first application under China's national procedure, bring an identity card, household-registration document and **3 recent two-inch colour photographs on a white background**; local services may offer other ways to submit them.
- In plain language: China's disability certificate establishes a person's disability category and grade for social measures; it is not simply a record of an injury. A person applies voluntarily through the county-level Disabled Persons' Federation where their household is registered, then an institution designated by provincial authorities assesses them. There are **7 categories** — vision, hearing, speech, physical, intellectual, mental and multiple disabilities — and **4 grades**. The process includes application, acceptance, assessment, a public notice where required, review and issue. If the criteria are not met, no certificate is issued.
- Benefit: China's rules adopted in **2017** and effective from **1 January 2018** use national standard **GB/T 26341-2010**. The county federation receives applications and issues certificates. A qualifying assessment is displayed in the applicant's village or community for **5 working days** (generally without public notice for minors); the federation then reviews the complete materials within **10 working days**. The certificate is free to issue, while assessment and photos are normally paid by the applicant unless local assistance applies. It is valid for **10 years** and can be renewed free of charge. A change in condition can prompt reassessment. An applicant who disputes the assessment may seek review from the city-level federation within **10 working days**, followed, if necessary, by a final provincial expert review.
- Evidence grade: A

- Notes: The Chinese certificate does **not replace** an injury-impairment finding or a work-injury assessment ([entry 10](#10-assess-lasting-effects-of-an-injury-after-treatment-ends-or-the-outcome-stabilises)). It does not automatically pay a cash benefit. Depending on local eligibility, support may include a living allowance for low-income people with disabilities, a care allowance for people with severe disabilities who need sustained care, rehabilitation, assistive devices, employment measures and local concessions; see [chapter 7, entry 9](07_when_money_is_tight.md) and the local federation. For multiple disabilities, the most severe category sets the grade and other categories are recorded. The certificate cannot be lent. If someone no longer meets the criteria, the certificate is cancelled; reapplication after cancellation must wait **1 year**. In the US, Social Security disability benefits require their own legal and medical assessment; European countries apply their own national criteria. China's **7 categories and 4 grades** do not carry over to them.
