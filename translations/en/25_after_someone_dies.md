# 25. What to do after someone dies

This chapter covers paperwork and money: what to do immediately, which funeral charges have an official service list, where to recover money left in accounts, and which rights close relatives can exercise. The first five entries follow the sequence of events: actions at the scene, death documents, transport and cremation, a dispute over the cause of death, and registration. The remaining entries are ordered by benefit relative to cost. For arrangements to make while an older person is alive — agreed guardianship, a will and accounts — see [chapter 17](17_caring_for_older_family_members.md). For the three Chinese benefits following a death caused by a work injury, see [chapter 19](19_work_exit_and_injury.md).

The Chinese funeral rules below draw on the revised Regulation on Funeral Administration, effective **30 March 2026**. The labour and social-security authorities set pension-insurance funeral grants and survivor payments separately. Verified primary text establishing their amounts is not yet available here, so no amounts are given. US and European procedures are described separately: Chinese certificates, deadlines and police powers do not apply to other jurisdictions.

### 1. For a sudden, unexplained or apparently accidental death, call for help and preserve the scene
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: Calling to report an emergency is a separate step from paying for subsequent care or funeral services. Asking about the procedure costs $0 or €0; phone charges depend on the service used.
- In plain language: If you find someone unresponsive and do not know whether they have died, call emergency services immediately and follow the dispatcher's instructions. Preserving a scene must not interfere with saving a life. Once death is established, report a sudden death, injury, unexplained circumstances or the discovery of someone who has been alone for a long time. Do not move or wash the body, change clothing, tidy up medicine containers or remove notes before the responsible officials permit it. **In China**, police issue death documentation for unnatural-death cases they lawfully handle **when the person did not receive treatment from a healthcare institution**. A case involving medical treatment follows a different route; “only the police can issue it” is not a rule for every unnatural death.
- Benefit: **Article 8** of China's Household Registration Regulation requires the head of the household or the person who finds the body to report an accidental death or an unknown cause of death immediately to the local police station or township administration. **Article 23** of the Funeral Administration Regulation, State Council Decree **No. 824**, effective **30 March 2026**, distinguishes ordinary deaths documented by healthcare institutions from police-handled unnatural deaths **without medical treatment**, documented by police. An undisturbed scene helps establish what happened; the appropriate death document is needed for subsequent cremation, registration and insurance procedures.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Standing Committee of China's National People's Congress (1958), Household Registration Regulation, article 8. <http://www.gd.gov.cn/zwgk/wjk/zcfgk/content/mpost_2531969.html>; [National Health Commission publication](https://www.nhc.gov.cn/wjw/flfg/202505/4ff7613b24f542d48f98fa5b540fd7f2.shtml). China's State Council (2026), Funeral Administration Regulation, Decree No. 824, effective 30 March 2026, article 23. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202601/content_7054169.htm>; [official publication by the State Administration for Market Regulation](https://www.samr.gov.cn/zw/zfxxgk/fdzdgknr/bgt/art/2023/art_1201c90551fb45889d041109ac329290.html). [US CDC: medical examiners, coroners and deaths requiring investigation](https://www.cdc.gov/phlp/php/coroner/). [GOV.UK: deaths reported to a coroner](https://www.gov.uk/after-a-death/when-a-death-is-reported-to-a-coroner).
- Notes: For an expected death at home after a long illness in China, contact the treating healthcare institution or the local primary-care service; see entry 2. If the circumstances are unclear, call **110 in China** and explain them. Police will determine the next steps; a call does not guarantee a certificate without investigation. For an immediate emergency, use **911 in the US**, **112 in the EU**, or **999 or 112 in the UK**. US states determine when a medical examiner or coroner investigates a death; there is no single nationwide rule assigning all certificates to police. In England and Wales, violent, unnatural or unexplained deaths may require a coroner's investigation. Elsewhere in Europe, ask the local emergency service or healthcare institution which authority must be informed. Do not arrange cleaning or removal of the body before the necessary scene work is complete.

### 2. Obtain the death documents and check names, dates and identification details immediately
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: Asking how to obtain the documents costs $0 or €0. Official copies, travel and other services may cost money; an ordinary photocopy does not necessarily replace a certified copy.
- In plain language: A medical document records death and its cause; a civil-registration certificate records the death in the public register. They may be separate documents. **In China**, an ordinary death during treatment or on the way to a healthcare institution is documented by the treating institution. For an ordinary death at home or elsewhere, a local community health centre or township hospital investigates and issues the document. If the cause of an ordinary death is clear, it must be issued **within one day of the death or the close relatives' report**. Paper and electronic versions have equal legal force. Check the details and find out which documents are required for cremation, registration, benefits, insurance and inheritance.
- Benefit: China's **2014** death-information rules, notice **No. 68**, **article 9**, described the treating-institution and local primary-care routes. Those rules **ceased to apply on 1 July 2026**; the current joint notice from five authorities is **2026 No. 5**. It gives paper and electronic documents equal legal force, requires issue within **one day of the death or relatives' report** when the cause of an ordinary death is clear, allows **one** replacement of a lost paper document and **one** reissue following a substantiated error. Correct documents enable applications concerning household registration, cremation, housing provident-fund savings, social benefits, insurance and inheritance; each service's requirements still need to be checked.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: General Office of China's National Health and Family Planning Commission (2014), interim death-information registration rules, notice No. 68 of 2014, article 9, **no longer in force**. <https://mzj.sz.gov.cn/szmz/pc/zwgk/jcxxgk/zcfg/byfw/content/post_2952215.html>. Five Chinese authorities (2026), notice No. 5 of 2026 on death-document information and electronic credentials. <https://wjw.fujian.gov.cn/xxgk/fgwj/gjwj/202602/t20260228_7102810.htm>. [USAGov: how to get certified death certificates and when a photocopy is sufficient](https://www.usa.gov/death-certificate). [Berlin service portal: reporting a death and obtaining certificates](https://service.berlin.de/dienstleistung/318987/). [GOV.UK: medical review and death registration](https://www.gov.uk/after-a-death).
- Notes: In China, keep the paper document and request its electronic counterpart where available. Check names and identification numbers at collection; correcting them later can mean returning to several organisations. A lost paper document can be replaced once and a substantiated error corrected through one reissue. Electronic issue is not yet guaranteed at every institution: gradual parallel issue is planned from **1 January 2027**. In the US, contact the vital-records office of the **state where the death occurred** for eligibility, fees and certified copies. Banks, insurers and pension providers may require certified copies, while some subscriptions need only a photocopy. In Berlin, report the death to the registry office for the place of death; in England and Wales, wait for the medical examiner's office to confirm that registration can proceed, or follow the coroner's instructions. Keep copies in your own file and ask each recipient what form it accepts. China's one-replacement limit is not a US or European rule.

### 3. Check who may transport and hold the body, and settle the paperwork before cremation
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Checking the provider and documents costs $0 or €0. Transport, storage, preparation, cremation and the funeral may be charged separately; request an itemised written quote in the local currency.
- In plain language: **In China**, a funeral institution, 殡仪馆, is responsible for transport, storage, embalming, presentation of the body and cremation. A hospital mortuary may provide temporary storage, but may not be outsourced or run funeral services. A funeral institution normally holds a body for no more than **3 days**; extending storage requires paperwork and the applicable fee. Cremation requires death documentation and cremation confirmation, and a cremation certificate is issued afterwards. Keep it and check which later procedures require it.
- Benefit: China's Funeral Administration Regulation, Decree **No. 824**, effective **30 March 2026**, provides: **article 24**, no outsourcing of hospital mortuaries or funeral services there; **article 25**, the listed services involving the body are provided by funeral institutions; **article 27**, ordinary storage for up to **3 days**, with an extension arranged by the family or transferring party; **article 28**, death documentation and cremation confirmation before cremation and a certificate afterwards; **article 29**, cremation locally or nearby in areas where it is required. Under **articles 8–9**, provincial authorities designate cremation and permitted burial areas, and ethnic-minority funeral customs are respected within the applicable rules.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: China's State Council (2026), Funeral Administration Regulation, Decree No. 824, effective 30 March 2026, articles 8, 9, 24, 25, 27, 28 and 29. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202601/content_7054169.htm>; [official text](https://www.samr.gov.cn/zw/zfxxgk/fdzdgknr/bgt/art/2023/art_1201c90551fb45889d041109ac329290.html). [US Federal Trade Commission: funeral price lists and choosing services](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/shopping-funeral-services). [GOV.UK: documents needed before the funeral](https://www.gov.uk/after-a-death). [GOV.UK: release of a body after a coroner's examination](https://www.gov.uk/after-a-death/when-a-death-is-reported-to-a-coroner).
- Notes: Someone offering an all-inclusive package at a hospital does not necessarily represent its official funeral institution. Check the organisation's identity, the person's authority and the contract. In China, transport is a basic service subject to the applicable pricing rules; see entry 6. The cremation certificate may be needed to move ashes and for other procedures; check inheritance and insurance requirements separately rather than assuming every organisation demands it. Chinese civil-affairs authorities may order a breach to be corrected when a body is buried despite mandatory cremation or a grave is created outside a permitted cemetery or ecological-burial area. These rules do not establish mandatory cremation or a **3-day** storage limit in the US or Europe. Check local transport, release and permit requirements before booking services. In England and Wales, give the burial or cremation certificate to the funeral director, crematorium or burial authority before the funeral; a coroner releases the body when no further examinations are needed. Resolve any question about investigating the cause of death before cremation; see entry 4.

### 4. If you question the cause of death, promptly discuss an examination and preservation of the medical records
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Raising your concerns and asking about the procedure costs $0 or €0. An examination, independent opinion or legal advice may have a separate charge; remembering to act promptly can be hard while grieving.
- In plain language: Do not postpone questions about the cause of death until after the funeral. **In China, when a healthcare institution and the family dispute the cause, the autopsy must take place within 48 hours of death**, extendable to **7 days** when facilities for freezing the body are available. This is the deadline for conducting the examination, not merely requesting one. Close relatives must give signed consent. At the same time, request preservation and sealing of the medical records through the applicable procedure. Cremation prevents a later examination of the body; discuss any necessary investigation with the responsible officials beforehand.
- Benefit: **Article 26** of China's Regulation on the Prevention and Handling of Medical Disputes, **2018 Decree No. 701**, sets the **48-hour** period, possible extension to **7 days** with freezing facilities, and signed consent from close relatives. Refusing to sign is treated as refusal of the autopsy. If refusal or delay exceeds the period **and prevents determination of the cause of death**, responsibility under that provision falls on the party that refused or delayed. Delay alone is not automatically proof of fault. **Article 24** requires both sides to be present when medical records are sealed or unsealed.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: China's State Council (2018), Regulation on the Prevention and Handling of Medical Disputes, Decree No. 701, articles 24 and 26. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/content/2018-08/31/content_5318057.htm>; [official Beijing government publication](https://www.beijing.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengcefagui/201905/t20190522_61432.html). [US CDC: state-specific coroner and medical-examiner laws](https://www.cdc.gov/phlp/php/coroner/). [GOV.UK: post-mortems ordered by a coroner](https://www.gov.uk/after-a-death/when-a-death-is-reported-to-a-coroner).
- Notes: For China's records-copying and sealing procedures, see [chapter 24, entries 6–7](24_getting_medical_care.md). Qualified institutions and specialists conduct the autopsy; families should not arrange an examination themselves. In the US, investigation and authorisation depend on state law and whether the medical examiner or coroner has jurisdiction. In England and Wales, a family cannot veto a coroner's post-mortem, although it can ask to be told when and where it will take place. Other European jurisdictions have their own rules. Contact the healthcare institution promptly and report suspected crime to the police; ask who decides on an examination, how the body is held and how to obtain records. China's **48-hour and 7-day** periods do not apply abroad. Do not authorise cremation before the need for further examination has been resolved.

### 5. Register the death promptly and check which benefits and records need updating
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=小 口径=时间 -->
- Cost: Asking about the process costs $0 or €0; allow time to gather documents and contact the offices involved. Copies, travel and additional services may cost money.
- In plain language: **In China**, the deceased person's entry in the household register, hukou, must be cancelled **before burial in an urban area**, or **within one month in a rural area**. Contact the registration authority with death documentation and the household register, following the local document list. Delayed registration or notification can complicate inheritance, property transfers and applications for family benefits. Do not assume money credited after death is available to spend; first establish the period and entitlement it covers.
- Benefit: **Article 8** of China's **1958** Household Registration Regulation requires the household head, a relative, a person who supported the deceased or a neighbour to report the death and cancel the registration: **before burial in cities and within one month in rural areas**. If death occurs at a temporary residence, its registration authority notifies the authority for the permanent residence. This is a Chinese hukou procedure; US states and European jurisdictions use their own death-registration systems and deadlines.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Standing Committee of China's National People's Congress (1958), Household Registration Regulation, article 8. <http://www.gd.gov.cn/zwgk/wjk/zcfgk/content/mpost_2531969.html>; [official National Health Commission publication](https://www.nhc.gov.cn/wjw/flfg/202505/4ff7613b24f542d48f98fa5b540fd7f2.shtml). [US Social Security Administration: reporting a death](https://www.ssa.gov/personal-record/when-someone-dies). [Berlin: report a death within 3 working days](https://service.berlin.de/dienstleistung/318987/). [GOV.UK: registering a death and notifying organisations](https://www.gov.uk/after-a-death).
- Notes: In the US, funeral homes generally report a death to Social Security, but confirm that this happened; if they do not, contact Social Security yourself. Ask the state vital-records office or funeral director about local registration requirements. In **Berlin**, report the death to the registry office for the place of death within **3 working days**. In **England and Wales**, registration normally follows confirmation from the medical examiner's office and must be completed within **5 days** of that confirmation; a coroner's case follows its own route. These are jurisdiction-specific examples, not one European deadline. Ask which agencies receive the information automatically and which banks, insurers, employers or pension providers you must tell separately. In China, ask about stopping social-insurance and medical-insurance benefits and applying for funeral support when cancelling hukou; entry 9 lists possible payments. If money arrives after the death, inform the payer and request a calculation: entitlement depends on the benefit and the period covered, not just the date it reached the account. Do not conceal a death to keep receiving payments.

### 6. Check the basic funeral-service list and local charges before buying extras
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Asking for the list costs $0 or €0; take a few minutes to read it before you go. The services themselves may have charges.
- In plain language: **In China**, the national basic-service list includes transport and storage of the body, a farewell ceremony, cremation, storage of ashes, ecological burial and placement of ashes in a niche provided by a government-run funeral institution. Charges must be set under the applicable rules. Funeral services are managed through approved lists; providers may not invent a service outside those lists and charge for it. First ask which services are basic and what the local charges are, then discuss optional purchases.
- Benefit: Article **5** of China's Funeral Administration Regulation places transport, storage, farewell ceremonies, cremation, ash storage, ecological burial and government-run ash-niche burial within the national basic-service list. Charges must be established lawfully. Civil-affairs authorities at the level of a city divided into districts or above, together with the relevant authorities, determine the non-basic-service list. Article **50** prohibits creating and charging for services outside the applicable lists.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: China's State Council (**2026**), Funeral Administration Regulation, Decree **No. 824**, revised at the **72nd** executive meeting on **14 November 2025**, effective **30 March 2026**, articles **5** and **50**. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202601/content_7054169.htm>; [official Shanghai civil-affairs publication](https://mzj.sh.gov.cn/MZ_BZ12_0-2-10-11/20260108/1a59585eeaca4affbc264e8489e311d0.html). [US FTC: choosing funeral services and comparing prices](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/shopping-funeral-services). [UK CMA: funeral price lists](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/things-to-know-when-arranging-a-funeral/things-to-know-when-arranging-a-funeral).
- Notes: Chinese provinces, autonomous regions and directly administered municipalities may add basic services to the national list. Ask for the local list and charges; “basic” does not mean every service is free. **In the US**, the FTC Funeral Rule lets you choose individual goods and services, subject to a non-declinable basic-services fee and applicable legal requirements. Ask for an itemized price list and a written statement of what you selected. **In the UK**, funeral directors must display a Standardised Price List in their window, premises and website; compare it with their additional options and the crematorium's charges. These are separate national rules. Elsewhere in Europe, check local requirements and request a written breakdown before accepting a package.

### 7. Keep evidence and complain about forced purchases, hidden charges or duplicate billing
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Keeping evidence costs $0 or €0. Finding the energy to challenge a bill while grieving can be difficult; paid legal help, if needed, is a separate expense.
- In plain language: **In China**, funeral providers may not hide prices, add undisclosed charges, force you to buy funeral goods or services through bundles or unreasonable conditions, or make you pay a third party against your wishes. Splitting one service into several charges or expanding what is billed to raise the price is also prohibited. Ask for the price list and keep receipts, the contract and messages. If you cannot deal with a complaint immediately, preserve the evidence and check the applicable deadline promptly. Pricing violations go to the market-regulation authority; funeral-service complaints go to civil affairs.
- Benefit: Article **42** of China's Funeral Administration Regulation prohibits unauthorized charge categories and disguised increases through splitting services or expanding the scope of charges. Article **43** lists **five** categories of pricing violations: missing or misleading price disclosure and extra undisclosed fees; deceptive pricing; forced or effectively forced sales through bundling or unreasonable restrictions; forced third-party services or repeated charges through service outlets; and other pricing violations prohibited by law. Article **51** requires civil-affairs authorities to coordinate with government service hotlines and other departments, address reported problems and tell the family the result.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: China's State Council (**2026**), Funeral Administration Regulation, Decree **No. 824**, revised at the **72nd** executive meeting on **14 November 2025**, effective **30 March 2026**, articles **42**, **43** and **51**. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202601/content_7054169.htm>; [official Shanghai civil-affairs publication](https://mzj.sh.gov.cn/MZ_BZ12_0-2-10-11/20260108/1a59585eeaca4affbc264e8489e311d0.html). [US FTC: Funeral Rule requirements and enforcement](https://www.ftc.gov/consumer-protection/funeral-rule). [UK CMA: required price information](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/things-to-know-when-arranging-a-funeral/things-to-know-when-arranging-a-funeral).
- Notes: In China, market regulators investigate unlawful prices and charges; civil-affairs authorities oversee funeral services. Request the price list at the time and keep payment records and messages even if you submit the complaint later. “Later” does not mean there is no deadline. **In the US**, a funeral home may not refuse a casket bought elsewhere or charge a handling fee for it under the FTC Funeral Rule. That rule does not cover every standalone cemetery or third-party seller; check the provider and the applicable state rules before relying on it. **In the UK**, compare the bill with the displayed lists and written terms. In other European countries, use the local consumer-protection and funeral-service complaint procedures rather than China's civil-affairs route.

### 8. In China, check that a funeral intermediary has filed with the county civil-affairs authority
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Asking about the filing costs $0 or €0 and takes a short conversation.
- In plain language: **In China**, organizations and individuals offering funeral agency work, purchasing goods on your behalf, planning or hosting a ceremony, or information services outside a funeral institution must file with the county-level civil-affairs authority. Ask for the filing details and verify them with that authority or its funeral-service information system. Intermediaries who use deception to charge unlawfully, push extravagant funerals, force services on families or sell information about the deceased can face legal action; the violation is also recorded in the system and made public. A sudden wave of sales calls after the paperwork may indicate a leak, but does not establish who disclosed the information or whether it was sold.
- Benefit: Article **41** of China's Funeral Administration Regulation requires the listed intermediaries to file with county civil affairs and provides for their inclusion in the funeral-service information system. Conduct harming bereaved families, including deceptive unlawful charges, pressure for extravagant funerals, forced services and selling the deceased person's information, is handled under the law, marked in the system and disclosed publicly. An intermediary that has not completed the required filing cannot be relied on as a properly filed provider in that system.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: China's State Council (**2026**), Funeral Administration Regulation, Decree **No. 824**, revised at the **72nd** executive meeting on **14 November 2025**, effective **30 March 2026**, article **41**. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202601/content_7054169.htm>; [official Shanghai civil-affairs publication, including article 45](https://mzj.sh.gov.cn/MZ_BZ12_0-2-10-11/20260108/1a59585eeaca4affbc264e8489e311d0.html). [UK CMA: disclosure of funeral directors' business and financial interests](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/things-to-know-when-arranging-a-funeral/things-to-know-when-arranging-a-funeral).
- Notes: Selling information about the deceased is one risk to watch for. If unsolicited sellers contact you after registration, save the numbers, dates and messages and ask the responsible organization to investigate how they obtained the information. Do not identify a seller or a source of the leak without evidence. China's article **45** also covers online memorial platforms: they must protect information about the deceased and must not induce consumption or unlawfully process that information. In the **US and Europe**, verify the provider's legal identity, any locally required license or registration, the contract and authority to act on your behalf. China's county filing requirement does not apply there. In the UK, published terms and business or financial connections can help you identify links between funeral providers and other organizations.

### 9. Claim money from each source: housing savings, social insurance and work-related death benefits
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Allow time for several applications and gather death and relationship documents. Travel, copies, notarization or other services may cost money; ask each office what is actually required.
- In plain language: **In China**, heirs and legatees can withdraw the deceased employee's housing provident-fund balance. This money is easy to overlook because it is held separately from an ordinary bank account; it requires a claim and does not automatically arrive with other estate funds. A work-related death can give rise to three separate benefits: a funeral grant, a pension for qualifying dependent relatives and a lump-sum work-related death benefit. Ask the social-insurance office where the person was insured about the pension-insurance personal-account balance and survivor benefits; document requirements vary. Keep a list of each account, benefit, office and application.
- Benefit: The final paragraph of article **24** of China's Housing Provident Fund Administration Regulation allows heirs and legatees to withdraw the balance when an employee dies or is declared dead. If there are neither heirs nor legatees, the balance becomes part of the fund's investment income under that provision. Article **39** of the Work Injury Insurance Regulation provides for the three benefits following a work-related death; see [chapter 19](19_work_exit_and_injury.md) for their separate conditions.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: China's State Council (**2026**), Housing Provident Fund Administration Regulation, amended by Decree **No. 844**, effective **20 September 2026**, final paragraph of article **24**. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2026/issue_12946/202608/content_7079363.html>; [official Ministry of Ecology and Environment publication](https://www.mee.gov.cn/zcwj/gwywj/202608/t20260820_1164286.shtml). Work Injury Insurance Regulation, article **39**; see [chapter 19](19_work_exit_and_injury.md). <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2011/content_1778064.htm>; [official human-resources publication](https://www.dhms.gov.cn/rsj/Web/_F0_0_05LB9M0Y4Z4F8JGUYXIKP1BN38.htm). **TODO — verification pending:** inheritance rules for the basic pension-insurance personal-account balance and the specific funeral-grant and survivor-payment standards; the relevant human-resources documents have not been checked here. [US Social Security: lump-sum death payment](https://www.ssa.gov/personal-record/when-someone-dies/lump-sum-death-payment). [GOV.UK: Funeral Expenses Payment eligibility](https://www.gov.uk/funeral-payments/eligibility) and [claim deadline](https://www.gov.uk/funeral-payments/how-to-claim).
- Notes: Include the Chinese housing fund in the asset search and make the separate withdrawal application; a separate account is not a reason to exclude it from the estate. Ask the social-insurance office for its document list and written explanation of any pension or survivor entitlement. Wills and inheritance formalities are discussed in [chapter 17](17_caring_for_older_family_members.md). **In the US**, separately check banks, employer benefits, insurance and Social Security. Its **$255** lump-sum death payment is available only to an eligible spouse or certain children; apply within **2 years of death**. It does not promise reimbursement of the full funeral bill. **In England and Wales**, Funeral Expenses Payment depends on qualifying benefits, responsibility for the funeral and the deceased person's circumstances; apply within **6 months of the funeral**. Scotland has a separate Funeral Support Payment. Elsewhere in Europe, ask the relevant pension, social-insurance and employer schemes about local survivor benefits. Chinese housing-fund and work-injury rules are not entitlements in these jurisdictions.

### 10. In China, close relatives may request access to, copies of, correction or deletion of the deceased person's data
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: Asking the platform about its procedure costs $0 or €0. A disputed request may require a complaint or court action, with separate costs and effort.
- In plain language: **Under China's Personal Information Protection Law**, close relatives may exercise rights to access, copy, correct or delete relevant personal information about the deceased to protect their own lawful, legitimate interests. If the platform refuses, it must explain why; the law provides for bringing a case before a people's court. The exception is an arrangement made by the person while alive. Check for an appointed account successor or clear instructions and identify what they authorize. Rights concerning data do not automatically transfer every account, password or piece of correspondence to a relative.
- Benefit: Article **49** of China's Personal Information Protection Law expressly grants close relatives these rights for their own lawful, legitimate interests, unless the deceased made other arrangements while alive. Article **50** requires a convenient mechanism for receiving and handling rights requests, an explanation for refusal and the possibility of court proceedings under the law.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Standing Committee of China's National People's Congress (**2021**), Personal Information Protection Law, articles **49** and **50**. <http://www.npc.gov.cn/npc/c2/c30834/202108/t20210820_313088.html>; [official National Bureau of Statistics publication](https://www.stats.gov.cn/gk/tjfg/xgfxfg/202503/t20250310_1958923.html). - Notes: Make account and data arrangements while you can express your wishes; see [chapter 17](17_caring_for_older_family_members.md). Use the platform's designated contact or legacy mechanism and record where the authorized person can find the access information securely. Closing an account and deleting personal information are different actions; see [chapter 14](14_account_and_information_security.md). **In the US and Europe**, use the provider's deceased-user procedure and the applicable local law rather than treating China's article 49 as universal. Apple checks legal documents; a Legacy Contact can request access with the access key and death documentation, while other routes depend on the country and may require a court order. Google may release content in certain circumstances; if the account is closed first, it cannot process a later request for its contents. **In France**, the GDPR does not cover a deceased person's data, but national law permits instructions about its fate and gives heirs certain rights where needed to settle the estate or record the death. This is not unrestricted access to every account. Preserve needed information before requesting irreversible deletion.
