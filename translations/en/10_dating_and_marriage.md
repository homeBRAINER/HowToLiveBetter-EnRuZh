# 10. Dating and marriage: costs and benefits

> Partial translation: the introduction and entries 1–9 of 17. Entries 10–17 remain in [Chinese](../../book/10-恋爱和结婚划不划算.md). Legal figures refer to China. Evidence and legal clarifications are labeled; regional legal adaptation is not yet complete. References checked September 21, 2026.

Whether marriage is worth it involves several separate questions: money, time, health, legal risks, and relationship quality. Each needs its own figures, without moral judgments or a ready-made verdict for you. Health figures use mortality risk, time figures use minutes per day, and legal figures follow the relevant provisions; these measures are not converted into one another. See [the marriage costs and benefits guide](../../docs/结婚划不划算.md) (Chinese) for the method, a checklist to fill in, and common mistakes.

**Evidence clarification:** the chapter draws on official statistics and meta-analyses, but also on individual studies. Not every claim has the same evidentiary basis; each entry identifies its sources and limitations.

### 1. Meet more people instead of fixating on one: don’t count on a questionnaire to tell you who you will click with
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=时间 -->

- Cost: No purchase is needed. Spend time getting to know more people instead of repeatedly analyzing the same person.
- In plain language: In two speed-dating studies, people completed more than 100 measures of traits and partner preferences before meeting. The models could predict who tended to like others and who tended to be liked, but not the attraction unique to a particular pair. Even stated preferences for looks or earning potential did not predict interest in a specific person during an earlier speed-dating study. Give yourself a chance to meet people instead of investing all your time in one imagined match.
- Benefit: In the two studies, participants completed more than 100 self-report measures relevant to partner selection. Random forest models predicted 4%–18% of the variation in a person's general tendency to desire others and 7%–27% of the variation in being desired. They could not predict the additional attraction specific to a pair from any combination of the traits and preferences measured before the dates. An earlier speed-dating study by researchers on the same team found that stated ideal-partner preferences for physical attractiveness and earning prospects did not predict participants' interest in particular people they met.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Joel S, Eastwick PW, Finkel EJ (2017). Is romantic desire predictable? Machine learning applied to initial romantic attraction. Psychological Science, 28(10), 1478–1489. <https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797617714580>; Eastwick PW, Finkel EJ (2008). Sex differences in mate preferences revisited: do people know what they initially desire in a romantic partner? Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 94(2), 245–264. <https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.94.2.245>
- Notes: Failure to predict attraction before a meeting does not mean that making an effort is pointless. The practical suggestion is to spread your time across genuine opportunities to meet people instead of putting everything into one person. These university-student samples do not represent every dating population; applying their findings to China's different dating and marriage context requires caution, as does applying them elsewhere. **Evidence clarification:** the findings concern the measures, models, and brief encounters studied. They do not prove that compatibility can never be predicted, or that meeting more people is always the best strategy.

### 2. Stop after a clear refusal: harassment is not a way to show sincerity
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=是 收益=中 口径=自由 -->

- Cost: No purchase is needed. Stop sending unwanted messages or waiting for the person outside places they visit.
- In plain language: In China, pestering or stalking someone in a way that interferes with their normal life can lead to administrative detention or a fine. Under the law effective from January 2026, the basic penalty is up to 5 days' detention or a fine of up to CNY 1,000; more serious cases carry 5–10 days' detention. Police can also impose a temporary no-contact restriction. Breaching it carries another 5–10 days' detention. Records may matter for civil-service recruitment, public-institution jobs, vetting, or regulated occupations, subject to the specific rules and lawful access to those records.
- Benefit: China's Public Security Administration Punishments Law, effective 2026-01-01, expressly covers repeatedly sending obscene, insulting, threatening, or similar messages, or using nuisance, pestering, stalking, or similar methods to interfere with another person's normal life. Article 50(1)(5) provides up to 5 days' administrative detention or a fine of up to CNY 1,000; more serious circumstances carry 5–10 days' detention and an optional additional fine of up to CNY 1,000. For the specified nuisance, pestering, or stalking, approval from the head of the public security organ allows a no-contact restriction for a defined period, in addition to the penalty. Breaching that restriction carries 5–10 days' detention. **Legal clarification:** Article 50(2) also allows an additional fine of up to CNY 1,000 for the breach.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2025 revision). Public Security Administration Punishments Law, Article 50(1)(5) and Article 50(2). <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202506/t20250627_699863.shtml> (reproduced by the Supreme People's Procuratorate).
- Notes: These references provide no research basis for the idea that relentless pursuit will eventually win someone over. Detention and no-contact restrictions, however, are explicit legal consequences. Respect a clear refusal regardless of your intentions. **Legal clarification:** refusal is a personal boundary, not a complete legal test: the provision requires interference with normal life and does not require a prior explicit refusal. It does not make every message after rejection an automatic offense. Article 136 requires these administrative records to be sealed, with exceptions for state bodies handling cases and organizations authorized to inquire under national rules. A record therefore does not automatically bar every public-sector or regulated job; access and eligibility need a separate legal basis. Administrative detention is not a criminal conviction. See the [official statutory text](https://www.npc.gov.cn/npc/c2/c30834/202506/t20250627_446254.html). The figures above are Chinese statutory penalties, not US or European law; this entry does not establish local penalties or employment restrictions.

### 3. Look at what the other person does: who initiates, makes plans, and follows up
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=时间 -->

- Cost: No purchase is needed. Shift your attention from interpreting facial expressions and tone to noticing what actually happens.
- In plain language: After 52 pairs of male and female strangers talked for roughly ten minutes, they were better at recognizing the absence of flirting than its presence. The same pattern appeared when 261 observers watched one-minute clips. Instead of trying to read minds, notice who starts conversations, who arranges another meeting, and whether the other person follows up when you are not moving things forward.
- Benefit: Two studies tested flirting detection. In the first, 52 opposite-sex pairs of strangers talked for 10–12 minutes, then reported whether they had flirted and whether they thought their partner had flirted. Interactions without flirting were identified more accurately than interactions with flirting. In the second, 261 observers judged 26 one-minute video clips, with the same general result: the absence of flirting was easier to recognize than its presence.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Hall JA, Xing C, Brooks S (2015). Accurately detecting flirting: error management theory, the traditional sexual script, and flirting base rate. Communication Research, 42(7), 939–958. <https://doi.org/10.1177/0093650214534972>
- Notes: This area is debated. Perilloux and Kurzban proposed an alternative to the claim that men overestimate women's sexual interest: women might underreport their interest, so a discrepancy alone does not establish whose judgment is more accurate (Perilloux C, Kurzban R (2015). Do men overperceive women's sexual interest? Psychological Science, 26(1), 70–77. <https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797614555727>). **Evidence clarification:** this interpretation is not proof that women conceal interest, and the cited studies do not establish a consensus that reading expressions and tone is always inaccurate. Nor do they directly show that tracking initiation, plans, and follow-up is more accurate. Treat those behaviors as useful observations, not a reliable test of attraction. You can ask directly; flirting or initiative is not consent, and a refusal must be respected.

### 4. Judge a relationship by your experience together, not just a partner's credentials
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=时间 -->

- Cost: No purchase is needed. Replace a partner checklist with attention to how you actually get along.
- In plain language: An analysis of 43 longitudinal studies of couples from 29 labs found that the strongest predictors of relationship quality were people's own perceptions of their relationships: their partner's commitment, appreciation for their partner, sexual satisfaction, their partner's satisfaction, and conflict. Their own reports explained two to four times as much variation as their partner's reports. Yet the same study largely failed to predict whether a relationship would improve or deteriorate over time from self-report questionnaires.
- Benefit: The machine-learning analysis combined 43 longitudinal datasets from 29 labs. The strongest relationship-specific predictors were perceived partner commitment, appreciation, sexual satisfaction, perceived partner satisfaction, and conflict. The strongest individual predictors were life satisfaction, negative affect, depression, attachment avoidance, and attachment anxiety. Relationship-specific measures explained up to 45% of the variation in quality at baseline and 18% at the end of the studies. A person's own reports explained two to four times as much variation as their partner's reports. Individual differences and partner reports added no predictive value beyond that person's own relationship-specific reports.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Joel S, Eastwick PW, Allison CJ, et al. (2020). Machine learning uncovers the most robust self-report predictors of relationship quality across 43 longitudinal couples studies. Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, 117(32), 19061–19071. <https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1917036117>
- Notes: The same study found that changes in relationship quality—whether things improved or deteriorated during follow-up—were largely unpredictable from any combination of self-report variables. Use your experience to assess the relationship you have now rather than relying only on a checklist of partner attributes; do not treat it as a forecast. This connects to the later entry about emotional support and relationship quality, specifying which aspects of the relationship matter. **Evidence clarification:** “appreciation” here includes appreciation you feel toward your partner, not simply feeling appreciated by them. Explained variation is not the percentage of couples whose future was correctly predicted; two to four times as much explained variation does not mean you are two to four times more accurate than your partner. The study assessed subjective relationship quality, not causation or a guarantee about any individual couple.

### 5. To get closer, take turns answering increasingly personal questions
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=小 口径=时间 -->

- Cost: No purchase is needed; allow 45 minutes.
- In plain language: Pairs of strangers took turns working through three sets of questions and tasks that became more personal, 36 in all. After 45 minutes, they felt closer than a comparison group who spent the same time making small talk. The useful idea is mutual, gradual openness, not a clever line or a performance. The study measured closeness after a short conversation, not whether a lasting relationship would follow. **Evidence clarification:** the ratings did not exceed the average rating of students' closest existing relationships; the comparison is explained below.
- Benefit: Strangers were paired and took turns answering three increasingly personal sets of questions and tasks, 36 in total, ranging from choosing an ideal dinner guest to recalling the last time they cried in front of another person. After 45 minutes, closeness was significantly higher than in the small-talk condition. **Evidence clarification:** across the three studies, excluding the small-talk group, the mean Inclusion of Other in the Self score was 3.82. A separate, comparable student sample rated their closest existing relationship at 4.65 on average. The experimental mean was around the 30th percentile of that comparison distribution—not above its average.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Aron A, Melinat E, Aron EN, Vallone RD, Bator RJ (1997). The experimental generation of interpersonal closeness: a procedure and some preliminary findings. Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin, 23(4), 363–377. <https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167297234003>
- Notes: This is evidence about a brief laboratory interaction with university students, not a guarantee of a lasting friendship or romance. The paper reports three studies; the direct comparison with small talk comes from Study 1. A practical approach is to share something real, invite the other person to answer at a similar level of depth, and take turns. The procedure combines reciprocal self-disclosure and relationship-building tasks; it does not isolate each element's causal contribution. Either person can decline a question, and the conversation creates no obligation to continue the relationship. See the [paper, discussion and appendix](https://pdf.stafforini.com/aron-1997-experimental-generation-interpersonal.pdf).

### 6. Distance does not doom a relationship; prepare for the first three months after moving closer
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=多 时间=多 毅力=是 收益=中 口径=时间 -->

- Cost: Travel expenses and time; fewer opportunities to meet in person. Costs depend on the route and frequency of visits.
- In plain language: In the studies cited here, long-distance relationships were more stable while the partners remained apart, and idealization was more pronounced when they met less often. Moving closer brought new difficulties. In the reunion study, about half of the long-distance partners reached that stage; the other half broke up while apart. Among the reunited relationships, one-third ended within 3 months. Longer gaps between visits and stronger idealization were associated with less stability after reunion. Prepare for the adjustment: less autonomy, more knowledge of each other, and possible increases in conflict and jealousy.
- Benefit: The research found greater idealization in long-distance than geographically close relationships: idealistic distortion, romantic love, memories of the relationship, and perceived agreement. Idealization was associated with less frequent face-to-face contact. Long-distance relationships were more stable while partners remained apart, but that advantage did not persist upon reunion. In the study of the transition to living nearby, about half of the long-distance partners made the transition, while the other half ended their relationships during separation; one-third of the reunited relationships ended within 3 months. Longer periods without face-to-face visits and more extreme idealization predicted instability after reunion. **Evidence clarification:** these associations do not establish that distance or idealization caused either stability or a breakup. Becoming geographically close does not necessarily mean moving into the same home.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Stafford L, Merolla AJ, Castle JD (2006). When long-distance dating partners become geographically close. Journal of Social and Personal Relationships, 23(6), 901–919. <https://doi.org/10.1177/0265407506070472>; Stafford L, Merolla AJ (2007). Idealization, reunions, and stability in long-distance dating relationships. Journal of Social and Personal Relationships, 24(1), 37–54. <https://doi.org/10.1177/0265407507072578>
- Notes: These are older studies of dating relationships among US university students, not marriages. Their percentages are not a personal breakup forecast. Another study of 717 people in long-distance relationships and 425 in geographically close relationships found few differences in relationship quality; individual and relationship characteristics were predictive (Dargie E, et al. (2015). Journal of Sex & Marital Therapy, 41(2), 181–202. <https://doi.org/10.1080/0092623X.2013.864367>). Do not reject a relationship solely because “distance always ends badly.” Discuss the first months after moving closer: autonomy, what you will learn about each other, conflict, and jealousy. The evidence does not make the first three months the only difficult period or prove that every long-distance relationship is as good as every nearby one.

### 7. Check the registration figures before accepting family advice: what marriage and divorce rates actually measure
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=小 口径=时间 -->

- Cost: No purchase is needed; spend a few minutes reading the marriage-registration section of China's annual civil affairs bulletin.
- In plain language: China recorded 6.106 million marriages in 2024, about one-fifth fewer than the year before, and 3.513 million divorces. Dividing those figures produces roughly 57.5%, sometimes presented online as a “divorce rate above half.” But the marriages and divorces involve different groups of couples. This is not a measure published as a divorce rate in the bulletin, and it does not mean that half of marriages will end in divorce. The reported marriage rate of 4.3‰ and divorce rate of 2.5‰ use the total population as their denominator, not the married population.
- Benefit: In 2024, China registered 6.106 million marriages, down 20.5% from the previous year, with a crude marriage rate of 4.3‰. There were 3.513 million divorces: 2.622 million registered by civil affairs departments and 891,000 through court judgments or mediation. The crude divorce rate was 2.5‰. The bulletin's note 5 defines the rates using the number of marriages or divorces during the year divided by the average total population during that year, expressed per 1,000 people. These are crude population rates. **Calculation clarification:** multiply the ratio by 1,000 to get the numerical rate per thousand; appending ‰ then identifies the unit. Multiplying by the quantity 1,000‰ would instead multiply by one.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Ministry of Civil Affairs of China (2025). Statistical Bulletin on the Development of Civil Affairs in 2024, section V(II)(1), marriage registration services, and note 5. <https://www.mca.gov.cn/n1288/n1294/n1554/c1662004999980006190/content.html> (PDF: <https://www.mca.gov.cn/gdnps/n2445/n2451/n2458/n2681/c1662004999980006189/attr/400985.pdf>).
- Notes: The often-repeated “divorce rate above 50%” divides divorces registered in a year by marriages registered in that same year: 3.513 / 6.106 ≈ 57.5% (equivalently, 351.3 / 610.6 when both are expressed in units of ten thousand). This divorce-to-marriage ratio is not a cohort probability. Estimating the share of marriages that eventually end in divorce requires following marriage cohorts or an explicitly defined demographic model; the cited bulletin does not provide that estimate. **Verification note:** these are the dated figures from the cited 2024 bulletin. Its official page and PDF could not be retrieved for this edition's fresh check, so independent verification against the full bulletin remains pending. They are not current US or European rates.

### 8. Consider the health association, but do not treat observational findings as a promise
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=小 口径=死亡率 -->

- Cost: No purchase is needed. Distinguish “married people had lower mortality” from “getting married will make you live longer.”
- In plain language: One review of older adults found a roughly 12% lower risk of death among married people than among people who were not married. A different review found a roughly 24% higher mortality hazard among never-married people than among married people. The latter association weakened with age; another review found stronger associations for some outcomes in men. These are associations, not proof of cause and effect. Health, income, and social connections can influence both marriage and survival. More rigorous analyses sometimes find smaller differences. The samples were largely from Europe and North America, so the figures are not universal predictions.
- Benefit: A meta-analysis of older adults combined 53 comparisons involving more than 250,000 people. The all-cause mortality relative risk for married versus non-married people was RR 0.88 (95% CI 0.85–0.91). Relative to married people, the estimates were 1.11 for widowed people, 1.16 for divorced or separated people, and 1.11 for never-married people, without a detected difference by sex. An especially conservative analysis gave RR 0.94. A separate review of never-married versus married people covered 95 publications reporting data on more than 500 million people; the adjusted hazard ratio was HR 1.24 (1.19–1.30), with a smaller association at older ages. A sex-specific meta-analysis of 21 studies and 7,881,040 people found associations between non-married status and all-cause, cancer, and cardiovascular mortality in both sexes, with stronger all-cause and cardiovascular associations in men. **Statistical clarification:** for never-married status, the women-to-men ratios of relative risks were 0.69 for stroke mortality and 0.91 for all-cause mortality—31% and 9% lower, respectively, in that direction. Each sex's relative risk compares never-married with married people of that sex. These figures are not a direct comparison of death probabilities between never-married men and women; reversing a percentage comparison requires taking the reciprocal, not keeping the same percentage.
- Evidence grade: A (disputed)
- Sources: Manzoli L, et al. (2007). Marital status and mortality in the elderly: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Social Science & Medicine, 64, 77–94. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.socscimed.2006.08.031>; Roelfs DJ, et al. (2011). The rising relative risk of mortality for singles: meta-analysis and meta-regression. American Journal of Epidemiology, 174(4), 379–389. <https://doi.org/10.1093/aje/kwr111>; Wang Y, et al. (2020). Sex differences in the association between marital status and the risk of cardiovascular, cancer, and all-cause mortality: a systematic review and meta-analysis of 7,881,040 individuals. Global Health Research and Policy, 5, 4. <https://doi.org/10.1186/s41256-020-00133-8>
- Notes: Selection is a central limitation: healthier people, people with higher incomes, and people with stronger social networks may be more likely both to marry and to live longer. All three reviews synthesize observational research and discuss publication bias or methodological limitations. Roelfs also found smaller associations with higher study quality; that does not mean every review showed the same pattern. Samples were predominantly Western, limiting direct transfer to China and other populations. **Evidence clarification:** RR and HR are different measures, and these estimates compare different groups. The stroke ratio's 95% CI was 0.47–1.00, making that sex contrast borderline. None of the percentages gives years of life gained, an absolute risk reduction, or a reason to stay in a harmful relationship.

### 9. Count unpaid work as a time cost, and agree on responsibilities before marriage
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=时间 -->

- Cost: No purchase is needed. Spend an hour or two before marriage agreeing on housework, care, and whose paid work may need to give way; then follow the agreement.
- In plain language: In China's 2024 time-use survey, men who did unpaid work averaged 1 hour 52 minutes a day on it, while women who did it averaged 3 hours 29 minutes—a gap of about an hour and a half. Participation differed by about 16 percentage points as well. The cited tables do not separate people by marital status, so they cannot tell you how much extra housework getting married adds. You can agree on a different division of work before marrying. **Statistical clarification:** a population gender gap is not an upper bound on marriage's effect, nor an estimate for your household.
- Benefit: In China's 2024 national time-use survey, unpaid work, including housework and family care, averaged 2 hours 45 minutes per day among participants in that activity: 1 hour 52 minutes for men and 3 hours 29 minutes for women. Participation rates were 67.5% and 83.9%, respectively. Housework averaged 1 hour 17 minutes across all respondents, or 1 hour 59 minutes among those doing it—the latter was 28 minutes less than in 2018. Family companionship and care averaged 30 minutes across all respondents and 1 hour 46 minutes among participants. In 2018, average housework time was 45 minutes for men and 2 hours 6 minutes for women; time caring for children was 17 and 53 minutes, respectively. **Statistical clarification:** the 2024 male/female unpaid-work gap is 97 minutes, and the participation gap is 16.4 percentage points. Participant averages exclude those who did none of the activity; they must not be presented as averages for every man or woman.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: National Bureau of Statistics of China (2024). Communiqués on the Third National Time Use Survey, Nos. 2 and 3. <https://www.stats.gov.cn/sj/zxfb/202410/t20241031_1957216.html>; <https://www.stats.gov.cn/sj/zxfb/202410/t20241031_1957215.html>. National Bureau of Statistics of China (2019). Communiqué on the 2018 National Time Use Survey. <https://www.stats.gov.cn/sj/zxfb/202302/t20230203_1900224.html>
- Notes: The cited releases break results down by sex, urban/rural residence, and age, not marital status. They therefore do not establish how much a person's housework increases after marriage, and the gender gap cannot serve as a statistically justified ceiling. These are population averages; your own division of work can be negotiated. **Comparability clarification:** the 2024 survey expanded its geographical coverage, added children aged 6–14, and changed activity categories and collection methods. It is not a matched before-and-after sample of the same married couples. Official alternative copies: [2024 activity totals and definitions](https://www.stats.gov.cn/zt_18555/zthd/lhfw/2025/2025_qgsjlydc/202501/t20250127_1958535.html), [2024 participant figures by sex](https://www.stats.gov.cn/english/PressRelease/202411/t20241115_1957437.html), and [the bureau's explanation of the 28-minute change](https://www.stats.gov.cn/zwfwck/sjfb/202410/t20241031_1957218.html). These Chinese figures should not be presented as US or European household averages.
