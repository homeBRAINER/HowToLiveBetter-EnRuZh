# 10. Dating and marriage: costs and benefits

> Complete translation: the introduction and all 17 entries. Legal figures refer to China. Evidence and legal clarifications are labeled; regional legal adaptation is not yet complete. References checked September 21, 2026.

Whether marriage is worth it involves several separate questions: money, time, health, legal risks, and relationship quality. Each needs its own figures, without moral judgments or a ready-made verdict for you. Health figures use mortality risk, time figures use minutes per day, and legal figures follow the relevant provisions; these measures are not converted into one another. See [the marriage costs and benefits guide](../../docs/结婚划不划算.md) (Chinese) for the method, a checklist to fill in, and common mistakes.

**Evidence clarification:** the chapter draws on official statistics and meta-analyses, but also on individual studies. Not every claim has the same evidentiary basis; each entry identifies its sources and limitations.

### 1. Meet more people instead of fixating on one: don’t count on a questionnaire to tell you who you will click with
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=时间 -->

- Cost: No purchase is needed. Spend time getting to know more people instead of repeatedly analyzing the same person.
- In plain language: In two speed-dating studies, people completed more than 100 measures of traits and partner preferences before meeting. The models could predict who tended to like others and who tended to be liked, but not the attraction unique to a particular pair. Even stated preferences for looks or earning potential did not predict interest in a specific person during an earlier speed-dating study. Give yourself a chance to meet people instead of investing all your time in one imagined match.
- Benefit: In the two studies, participants completed more than 100 self-report measures relevant to partner selection. Random forest models predicted 4%–18% of the variation in a person's general tendency to desire others and 7%–27% of the variation in being desired. They could not predict the additional attraction specific to a pair from any combination of the traits and preferences measured before the dates. An earlier speed-dating study by researchers on the same team found that stated ideal-partner preferences for physical attractiveness and earning prospects did not predict participants' interest in particular people they met.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Joel S, Eastwick PW, Finkel EJ (2017). Is romantic desire predictable? Machine learning applied to initial romantic attraction. Psychological Science, 28(10), 1478–1489. <https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797617714580>; Eastwick PW, Finkel EJ (2008). Sex differences in mate preferences revisited: do people know what they initially desire in a romantic partner? Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 94(2), 245–264. <https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.94.2.245>
- Notes: Failure to predict attraction before a meeting does not mean that making an effort is pointless. The practical suggestion is to spread your time across genuine opportunities to meet people instead of putting everything into one person. These university-student samples do not represent every dating population; applying their findings to China's different dating and marriage context requires caution, as does applying them elsewhere. **Evidence clarification:** the findings concern the measures, models, and brief encounters studied. They do not prove that compatibility can never be predicted, or that meeting more people is always the best strategy.

### 2. Stop after a clear refusal: harassment is not a way to show sincerity
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=是 收益=中 口径=自由 -->

- Cost: No purchase is needed. Stop sending unwanted messages or waiting for the person outside places they visit.
- In plain language: In China, pestering or stalking someone in a way that interferes with their normal life can lead to administrative detention or a fine. Under the law effective from January 2026, the basic penalty is up to 5 days' detention or a fine of up to CNY 1,000; more serious cases carry 5–10 days' detention. Police can also impose a temporary no-contact restriction. Breaching it carries another 5–10 days' detention. Records may matter for civil-service recruitment, public-institution jobs, vetting, or regulated occupations, subject to the specific rules and lawful access to those records.
- Benefit: China's Public Security Administration Punishments Law, effective 2026-01-01, expressly covers repeatedly sending obscene, insulting, threatening, or similar messages, or using nuisance, pestering, stalking, or similar methods to interfere with another person's normal life. Article 50(1)(5) provides up to 5 days' administrative detention or a fine of up to CNY 1,000; more serious circumstances carry 5–10 days' detention and an optional additional fine of up to CNY 1,000. For the specified nuisance, pestering, or stalking, approval from the head of the public security organ allows a no-contact restriction for a defined period, in addition to the penalty. Breaching that restriction carries 5–10 days' detention. **Legal clarification:** Article 50(2) also allows an additional fine of up to CNY 1,000 for the breach.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2025 revision). Public Security Administration Punishments Law, Article 50(1)(5) and Article 50(2). <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202506/t20250627_699863.shtml> (reproduced by the Supreme People's Procuratorate).
- Notes: These references provide no research basis for the idea that relentless pursuit will eventually win someone over. Detention and no-contact restrictions, however, are explicit legal consequences. Respect a clear refusal regardless of your intentions. **Legal clarification:** refusal is a personal boundary, not a complete legal test: the provision requires interference with normal life and does not require a prior explicit refusal. It does not make every message after rejection an automatic offense. Article 136 requires these administrative records to be sealed, with exceptions for state bodies handling cases and organizations authorized to inquire under national rules. A record therefore does not automatically bar every public-sector or regulated job; access and eligibility need a separate legal basis. Administrative detention is not a criminal conviction. See the [official statutory text](https://www.npc.gov.cn/npc/c2/c30834/202506/t20250627_446254.html). The figures above are Chinese statutory penalties, not US or European law; this entry does not establish local penalties or employment restrictions.

### 3. Look at what the other person does: who initiates, makes plans, and follows up
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=时间 -->

- Cost: No purchase is needed. Shift your attention from interpreting facial expressions and tone to noticing what actually happens.
- In plain language: After 52 pairs of male and female strangers talked for roughly ten minutes, they were better at recognizing the absence of flirting than its presence. The same pattern appeared when 261 observers watched one-minute clips. Instead of trying to read minds, notice who starts conversations, who arranges another meeting, and whether the other person follows up when you are not moving things forward.
- Benefit: Two studies tested flirting detection. In the first, 52 opposite-sex pairs of strangers talked for 10–12 minutes, then reported whether they had flirted and whether they thought their partner had flirted. Interactions without flirting were identified more accurately than interactions with flirting. In the second, 261 observers judged 26 one-minute video clips, with the same general result: the absence of flirting was easier to recognize than its presence.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Hall JA, Xing C, Brooks S (2015). Accurately detecting flirting: error management theory, the traditional sexual script, and flirting base rate. Communication Research, 42(7), 939–958. <https://doi.org/10.1177/0093650214534972>
- Notes: This area is debated. Perilloux and Kurzban proposed an alternative to the claim that men overestimate women's sexual interest: women might underreport their interest, so a discrepancy alone does not establish whose judgment is more accurate (Perilloux C, Kurzban R (2015). Do men overperceive women's sexual interest? Psychological Science, 26(1), 70–77. <https://doi.org/10.1177/0956797614555727>). **Evidence clarification:** this interpretation is not proof that women conceal interest, and the cited studies do not establish a consensus that reading expressions and tone is always inaccurate. Nor do they directly show that tracking initiation, plans, and follow-up is more accurate. Treat those behaviors as useful observations, not a reliable test of attraction. You can ask directly; flirting or initiative is not consent, and a refusal must be respected.

### 4. Judge a relationship by your experience together, not just a partner's credentials
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=时间 -->

- Cost: No purchase is needed. Replace a partner checklist with attention to how you actually get along.
- In plain language: An analysis of 43 longitudinal studies of couples from 29 labs found that the strongest predictors of relationship quality were people's own perceptions of their relationships: their partner's commitment, appreciation for their partner, sexual satisfaction, their partner's satisfaction, and conflict. Their own reports explained two to four times as much variation as their partner's reports. Yet the same study largely failed to predict whether a relationship would improve or deteriorate over time from self-report questionnaires.
- Benefit: The machine-learning analysis combined 43 longitudinal datasets from 29 labs. The strongest relationship-specific predictors were perceived partner commitment, appreciation, sexual satisfaction, perceived partner satisfaction, and conflict. The strongest individual predictors were life satisfaction, negative affect, depression, attachment avoidance, and attachment anxiety. Relationship-specific measures explained up to 45% of the variation in quality at baseline and 18% at the end of the studies. A person's own reports explained two to four times as much variation as their partner's reports. Individual differences and partner reports added no predictive value beyond that person's own relationship-specific reports.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Joel S, Eastwick PW, Allison CJ, et al. (2020). Machine learning uncovers the most robust self-report predictors of relationship quality across 43 longitudinal couples studies. Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences, 117(32), 19061–19071. <https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1917036117>
- Notes: The same study found that changes in relationship quality—whether things improved or deteriorated during follow-up—were largely unpredictable from any combination of self-report variables. Use your experience to assess the relationship you have now rather than relying only on a checklist of partner attributes; do not treat it as a forecast. This connects to the later entry about emotional support and relationship quality, specifying which aspects of the relationship matter. **Evidence clarification:** “appreciation” here includes appreciation you feel toward your partner, not simply feeling appreciated by them. Explained variation is not the percentage of couples whose future was correctly predicted; two to four times as much explained variation does not mean you are two to four times more accurate than your partner. The study assessed subjective relationship quality, not causation or a guarantee about any individual couple.

### 5. To get closer, take turns answering increasingly personal questions
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=小 口径=时间 -->

- Cost: No purchase is needed; allow 45 minutes.
- In plain language: Pairs of strangers took turns working through three sets of questions and tasks that became more personal, 36 in all. After 45 minutes, they felt closer than a comparison group who spent the same time making small talk. The useful idea is mutual, gradual openness, not a clever line or a performance. The study measured closeness after a short conversation, not whether a lasting relationship would follow. **Evidence clarification:** the ratings did not exceed the average rating of students' closest existing relationships; the comparison is explained below.
- Benefit: Strangers were paired and took turns answering three increasingly personal sets of questions and tasks, 36 in total, ranging from choosing an ideal dinner guest to recalling the last time they cried in front of another person. After 45 minutes, closeness was significantly higher than in the small-talk condition. **Evidence clarification:** across the three studies, excluding the small-talk group, the mean Inclusion of Other in the Self score was 3.82. A separate, comparable student sample rated their closest existing relationship at 4.65 on average. The experimental mean was around the 30th percentile of that comparison distribution—not above its average.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Aron A, Melinat E, Aron EN, Vallone RD, Bator RJ (1997). The experimental generation of interpersonal closeness: a procedure and some preliminary findings. Personality and Social Psychology Bulletin, 23(4), 363–377. <https://doi.org/10.1177/0146167297234003>
- Notes: This is evidence about a brief laboratory interaction with university students, not a guarantee of a lasting friendship or romance. The paper reports three studies; the direct comparison with small talk comes from Study 1. A practical approach is to share something real, invite the other person to answer at a similar level of depth, and take turns. The procedure combines reciprocal self-disclosure and relationship-building tasks; it does not isolate each element's causal contribution. Either person can decline a question, and the conversation creates no obligation to continue the relationship. See the [paper, discussion and appendix](https://pdf.stafforini.com/aron-1997-experimental-generation-interpersonal.pdf).

### 6. Distance does not doom a relationship; prepare for the first three months after moving closer
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=多 时间=多 毅力=是 收益=中 口径=时间 -->

- Cost: Travel expenses and time; fewer opportunities to meet in person. Costs depend on the route and frequency of visits.
- In plain language: In the studies cited here, long-distance relationships were more stable while the partners remained apart, and idealization was more pronounced when they met less often. Moving closer brought new difficulties. In the reunion study, about half of the long-distance partners reached that stage; the other half broke up while apart. Among the reunited relationships, one-third ended within 3 months. Longer gaps between visits and stronger idealization were associated with less stability after reunion. Prepare for the adjustment: less autonomy, more knowledge of each other, and possible increases in conflict and jealousy.
- Benefit: The research found greater idealization in long-distance than geographically close relationships: idealistic distortion, romantic love, memories of the relationship, and perceived agreement. Idealization was associated with less frequent face-to-face contact. Long-distance relationships were more stable while partners remained apart, but that advantage did not persist upon reunion. In the study of the transition to living nearby, about half of the long-distance partners made the transition, while the other half ended their relationships during separation; one-third of the reunited relationships ended within 3 months. Longer periods without face-to-face visits and more extreme idealization predicted instability after reunion. **Evidence clarification:** these associations do not establish that distance or idealization caused either stability or a breakup. Becoming geographically close does not necessarily mean moving into the same home.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Stafford L, Merolla AJ, Castle JD (2006). When long-distance dating partners become geographically close. Journal of Social and Personal Relationships, 23(6), 901–919. <https://doi.org/10.1177/0265407506070472>; Stafford L, Merolla AJ (2007). Idealization, reunions, and stability in long-distance dating relationships. Journal of Social and Personal Relationships, 24(1), 37–54. <https://doi.org/10.1177/0265407507072578>
- Notes: These are older studies of dating relationships among US university students, not marriages. Their percentages are not a personal breakup forecast. Another study of 717 people in long-distance relationships and 425 in geographically close relationships found few differences in relationship quality; individual and relationship characteristics were predictive (Dargie E, et al. (2015). Journal of Sex & Marital Therapy, 41(2), 181–202. <https://doi.org/10.1080/0092623X.2013.864367>). Do not reject a relationship solely because “distance always ends badly.” Discuss the first months after moving closer: autonomy, what you will learn about each other, conflict, and jealousy. The evidence does not make the first three months the only difficult period or prove that every long-distance relationship is as good as every nearby one.

### 7. Check the registration figures before accepting family advice: what marriage and divorce rates actually measure
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=小 口径=时间 -->

- Cost: No purchase is needed; spend a few minutes reading the marriage-registration section of China's annual civil affairs bulletin.
- In plain language: China recorded 6.106 million marriages in 2024, about one-fifth fewer than the year before, and 3.513 million divorces. Dividing those figures produces roughly 57.5%, sometimes presented online as a “divorce rate above half.” But the marriages and divorces involve different groups of couples. This is not a measure published as a divorce rate in the bulletin, and it does not mean that half of marriages will end in divorce. The reported marriage rate of 4.3‰ and divorce rate of 2.5‰ use the total population as their denominator, not the married population.
- Benefit: In 2024, China registered 6.106 million marriages, down 20.5% from the previous year, with a crude marriage rate of 4.3‰. There were 3.513 million divorces: 2.622 million registered by civil affairs departments and 891,000 through court judgments or mediation. The crude divorce rate was 2.5‰. The bulletin's note 5 defines the rates using the number of marriages or divorces during the year divided by the average total population during that year, expressed per 1,000 people. These are crude population rates. **Calculation clarification:** multiply the ratio by 1,000 to get the numerical rate per thousand; appending ‰ then identifies the unit. Multiplying by the quantity 1,000‰ would instead multiply by one.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Ministry of Civil Affairs of China (2025). Statistical Bulletin on the Development of Civil Affairs in 2024, section V(II)(1), marriage registration services, and note 5. <https://www.mca.gov.cn/n1288/n1294/n1554/c1662004999980006190/content.html> (PDF: <https://www.mca.gov.cn/gdnps/n2445/n2451/n2458/n2681/c1662004999980006189/attr/400985.pdf>).
- Notes: The often-repeated “divorce rate above 50%” divides divorces registered in a year by marriages registered in that same year: 3.513 / 6.106 ≈ 57.5% (equivalently, 351.3 / 610.6 when both are expressed in units of ten thousand). This divorce-to-marriage ratio is not a cohort probability. Estimating the share of marriages that eventually end in divorce requires following marriage cohorts or an explicitly defined demographic model; the cited bulletin does not provide that estimate. **Verification note:** these are the dated figures from the cited 2024 bulletin. Its official page and PDF could not be retrieved for this edition's fresh check, so independent verification against the full bulletin remains pending. They are not current US or European rates.

### 8. Consider the health association, but do not treat observational findings as a promise
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=小 口径=死亡率 -->

- Cost: No purchase is needed. Distinguish “married people had lower mortality” from “getting married will make you live longer.”
- In plain language: One review of older adults found a roughly 12% lower risk of death among married people than among people who were not married. A different review found a roughly 24% higher mortality hazard among never-married people than among married people. The latter association weakened with age; another review found stronger associations for some outcomes in men. These are associations, not proof of cause and effect. Health, income, and social connections can influence both marriage and survival. More rigorous analyses sometimes find smaller differences. The samples were largely from Europe and North America, so the figures are not universal predictions.
- Benefit: A meta-analysis of older adults combined 53 comparisons involving more than 250,000 people. The all-cause mortality relative risk for married versus non-married people was RR 0.88 (95% CI 0.85–0.91). Relative to married people, the estimates were 1.11 for widowed people, 1.16 for divorced or separated people, and 1.11 for never-married people, without a detected difference by sex. An especially conservative analysis gave RR 0.94. A separate review of never-married versus married people covered 95 publications reporting data on more than 500 million people; the adjusted hazard ratio was HR 1.24 (1.19–1.30), with a smaller association at older ages. A sex-specific meta-analysis of 21 studies and 7,881,040 people found associations between non-married status and all-cause, cancer, and cardiovascular mortality in both sexes, with stronger all-cause and cardiovascular associations in men. **Statistical clarification:** for never-married status, the women-to-men ratios of relative risks were 0.69 for stroke mortality and 0.91 for all-cause mortality—31% and 9% lower, respectively, in that direction. Each sex's relative risk compares never-married with married people of that sex. These figures are not a direct comparison of death probabilities between never-married men and women; reversing a percentage comparison requires taking the reciprocal, not keeping the same percentage.
- Evidence grade: A (disputed)
- Sources: Manzoli L, et al. (2007). Marital status and mortality in the elderly: a systematic review and meta-analysis. Social Science & Medicine, 64, 77–94. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.socscimed.2006.08.031>; Roelfs DJ, et al. (2011). The rising relative risk of mortality for singles: meta-analysis and meta-regression. American Journal of Epidemiology, 174(4), 379–389. <https://doi.org/10.1093/aje/kwr111>; Wang Y, et al. (2020). Sex differences in the association between marital status and the risk of cardiovascular, cancer, and all-cause mortality: a systematic review and meta-analysis of 7,881,040 individuals. Global Health Research and Policy, 5, 4. <https://doi.org/10.1186/s41256-020-00133-8>
- Notes: Selection is a central limitation: healthier people, people with higher incomes, and people with stronger social networks may be more likely both to marry and to live longer. All three reviews synthesize observational research and discuss publication bias or methodological limitations. Roelfs also found smaller associations with higher study quality; that does not mean every review showed the same pattern. Samples were predominantly Western, limiting direct transfer to China and other populations. **Evidence clarification:** RR and HR are different measures, and these estimates compare different groups. The stroke ratio's 95% CI was 0.47–1.00, making that sex contrast borderline. None of the percentages gives years of life gained, an absolute risk reduction, or a reason to stay in a harmful relationship.

### 9. Count unpaid work as a time cost, and agree on responsibilities before marriage
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=时间 -->

- Cost: No purchase is needed. Spend an hour or two before marriage agreeing on housework, care, and whose paid work may need to give way; then follow the agreement.
- In plain language: In China's 2024 time-use survey, men who did unpaid work averaged 1 hour 52 minutes a day on it, while women who did it averaged 3 hours 29 minutes—a gap of about an hour and a half. Participation differed by about 16 percentage points as well. The cited tables do not separate people by marital status, so they cannot tell you how much extra housework getting married adds. You can agree on a different division of work before marrying. **Statistical clarification:** a population gender gap is not an upper bound on marriage's effect, nor an estimate for your household.
- Benefit: In China's 2024 national time-use survey, unpaid work, including housework and family care, averaged 2 hours 45 minutes per day among participants in that activity: 1 hour 52 minutes for men and 3 hours 29 minutes for women. Participation rates were 67.5% and 83.9%, respectively. Housework averaged 1 hour 17 minutes across all respondents, or 1 hour 59 minutes among those doing it—the latter was 28 minutes less than in 2018. Family companionship and care averaged 30 minutes across all respondents and 1 hour 46 minutes among participants. In 2018, average housework time was 45 minutes for men and 2 hours 6 minutes for women; time caring for children was 17 and 53 minutes, respectively. **Statistical clarification:** the 2024 male/female unpaid-work gap is 97 minutes, and the participation gap is 16.4 percentage points. Participant averages exclude those who did none of the activity; they must not be presented as averages for every man or woman.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: National Bureau of Statistics of China (2024). Communiqués on the Third National Time Use Survey, Nos. 2 and 3. <https://www.stats.gov.cn/sj/zxfb/202410/t20241031_1957216.html>; <https://www.stats.gov.cn/sj/zxfb/202410/t20241031_1957215.html>. National Bureau of Statistics of China (2019). Communiqué on the 2018 National Time Use Survey. <https://www.stats.gov.cn/sj/zxfb/202302/t20230203_1900224.html>
- Notes: The cited releases break results down by sex, urban/rural residence, and age, not marital status. They therefore do not establish how much a person's housework increases after marriage, and the gender gap cannot serve as a statistically justified ceiling. These are population averages; your own division of work can be negotiated. **Comparability clarification:** the 2024 survey expanded its geographical coverage, added children aged 6–14, and changed activity categories and collection methods. It is not a matched before-and-after sample of the same married couples. Official alternative copies: [2024 activity totals and definitions](https://www.stats.gov.cn/zt_18555/zthd/lhfw/2025/2025_qgsjlydc/202501/t20250127_1958535.html), [2024 participant figures by sex](https://www.stats.gov.cn/english/PressRelease/202411/t20241115_1957437.html), and [the bureau's explanation of the 28-minute change](https://www.stats.gov.cn/zwfwck/sjfb/202410/t20241031_1957218.html). These Chinese figures should not be presented as US or European household averages.

### 10. Check the default property rules before deciding whether to make a written agreement
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->

- Cost: Reading the rules costs nothing. Read the property provisions in China's Civil Code and, if needed, sign a written property agreement together. Article 1065 requires writing, not notarization; professional advice or other formalities may involve separate costs.
- In plain language: Under China's default rules, wages, bonuses, business and investment income, intellectual-property income, and most inheritances and gifts received during marriage belong to both spouses. Premarital property, personal-injury compensation, property expressly left or given to one spouse alone, and items for one spouse's personal use remain separate. You can agree in writing to separate ownership, joint ownership, or a mixture. A valid agreement binds both spouses without a general requirement for notarization. A spouse who takes on more childcare or care for older family members can seek compensation at divorce.
- Benefit: Article 1062 makes wages, bonuses, payment for services, production, business and investment income, intellectual-property income, and inherited or gifted property acquired during marriage joint property, subject to the exception for gifts or bequests to one spouse alone. Article 1063 identifies premarital property, personal-injury damages or compensation, property designated for one spouse in a will or gift contract, and items for one spouse's exclusive personal use as separate property. Article 1065 allows a written agreement covering both premarital property and property acquired during marriage: separate ownership, joint ownership, or a combination, binding on both spouses. Under Article 1088, a spouse who has borne more duties in raising children, caring for older people, or supporting the other spouse's work may claim compensation from the other at divorce.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: National People's Congress (2020). Civil Code of the People's Republic of China, effective 2021-01-01, Articles 1062, 1063, 1065 and 1088. National Laws and Regulations Database: <https://flk.npc.gov.cn/detail?title=%E4%B8%AD%E5%8D%8E%E4%BA%BA%E6%B0%91%E5%85%B1%E5%92%8C%E5%9B%BD%E6%B0%91%E6%B3%95%E5%85%B8&id=ff808081729d1efe01729d50b5c500bf>; full text reproduced in the Supreme People's Court Gazette: <http://gongbao.court.gov.cn/Details/7f184078694d811fb3314f6af9accf.html>.
- Notes: No official statistical basis is supplied here for typical betrothal gifts, wedding expenses, or the cost of a marital home, so this entry gives no estimates. Under Article 1088, the spouses agree on compensation; if they cannot agree, the court decides. There is no fixed statutory formula. Property agreements can help both the higher earner and the person doing more unpaid work. **Legal clarification:** the statutory lists also include other property that should be jointly or separately owned. A signed document is not automatically valid regardless of its contents. Under Article 1065, using a separate-property agreement against an outside creditor requires that creditor to know of it; an agreement between spouses does not simply erase creditors' rights. See the [official Civil Code text](https://tjca.miit.gov.cn/zwgk/zcwj/flfg/art/2020/art_20cf1a2e1b854924b5caa744c8045d1f.html). These are Chinese rules, not US or European agreement or inheritance rules.

### 11. When parents contribute to a home purchase, record whether the money is a gift or a loan at the time of transfer
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->

- Cost: No purchase is needed. On the day the money is provided, write a one-page statement naming who receives the gift or borrows the money. Have the contributing parents and the child sign; for a loan intended for both spouses, have both sign. Use a matching payment reference.
- In plain language: In China, parents' contributions before the wedding are generally treated as gifts to their own child, unless they expressly give to both. For contributions during marriage, the agreement matters; without a clear agreement, the joint-property rules apply. Since February 2025, where one spouse's parents paid the whole purchase price during marriage without clearly giving solely to their own child, a divorce court may award the home to that child. Whether the other spouse receives compensation, and how much, depends on the couple's life together and children, fault in the divorce, contributions to the family, and the home's value. If the money is genuinely a loan to both spouses, document that when it is paid and obtain both signatures.
- Benefit: Article 29 of Interpretation (I) treats parents' contributions toward the couple's home before marriage as gifts to their own child, unless the parents expressly state that the gift is to both. For contributions after marriage, the agreement governs; absent a clear agreement, Article 1062(1)(4)'s joint-property principle applies. From 2025-02-01, Article 8 of Interpretation (II) provides that, where one side's parents fully funded a home bought during marriage without a clear gift solely to their own child, a court dividing joint property at divorce may award the home to that child. It considers cohabitation and having and raising children together, divorce fault, contributions to the family, and the home's market value at divorce when deciding whether compensation is due and its amount. Where one side's parents paid only part, or both sides' parents contributed, the court may use the sources and proportions of funding as a starting point, consider those same factors, and award the home to one spouse with reasonable compensation to the other. These rules apply nationwide in China. **Legal clarification:** in both funding situations, Article 8 first gives effect to a gift contract that clearly assigns the relevant contribution to the parents' own child; the discretionary division described here concerns absent or unclear agreements.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Supreme People's Court (2020). Interpretation (I) on applying the Marriage and Family Book of the Civil Code, Fa Shi [2020] No. 22, Article 29. <https://www.court.gov.cn/fabu/xiangqing/282071.html>; Supreme People's Court (2025). Interpretation (II), Fa Shi [2025] No. 1, Article 8. <https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/452771.html>; National People's Congress (2020). Civil Code, Article 1062(1)(4). <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202006/t20200602_463888.shtml>.
- Notes: Clarity helps both sides. Parents should not rely on producing a new IOU during a divorce to recover money previously given as a gift; the actual source of funding is already relevant to property division under Interpretation (II). The recipient should know from the outset what the contribution means. For a real loan, keep evidence of the agreement to borrow, repayment terms, the transfer reference, and any repayments. Missing evidence may need explaining in court. **Legal clarification:** the name on the title does not by itself resolve every divorce dispute, but registration is not legally irrelevant. Nor is every item in this practical evidence checklist a separate statutory condition for a valid loan. A later document cannot truthfully turn a gift into a loan that never existed. This entry describes Chinese property division, not local law elsewhere.

### 12. A large loan taken out by your spouse does not automatically become your debt because you are married
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->

- Cost: No purchase is needed. Do not sign as a co-borrower or guarantor unless you intend to accept that obligation. Avoid casually writing “we will repay this money” in a chat: it could be evidence that you later agreed to the debt.
- In plain language: Under Chinese law, if your spouse borrows well beyond the family's ordinary daily needs without your signature or later agreement, the debt does not automatically become joint. The creditor must establish a basis such as use for the couple's shared life or joint business. Money used to buy a home you both live in or own may provide that evidence. If your spouse's parents produce an IOU signed only by your spouse during divorce proceedings, first examine whether a loan actually existed. A transfer record alone does not settle whether the payment was a loan rather than a gift or another payment.
- Benefit: Article 1064 covers debts based on both spouses' common intention, including joint signatures or later ratification by one spouse, and debts taken out in one spouse's name during marriage for the family's ordinary daily needs. Borrowing in one spouse's name beyond those needs is not joint debt unless the creditor proves use for the couple's shared life, joint production or business, or both spouses' common intention. A home purchase exceeds ordinary daily household needs, so the creditor bears that burden. But if the money actually financed a jointly occupied or jointly owned home, it may support the shared-life ground. This is why recording the nature of the payment at the outset, as in entry 11, matters. These are nationwide Chinese rules under the Civil Code effective in 2021; lack of a signature alone is not a complete defense.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: National People's Congress (2020). Civil Code, Article 1064. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202006/t20200602_463888.shtml>; Supreme People's Court (second amendment, 2020). Provisions on several issues concerning the application of law in trials of private lending cases, Articles 15 and 16. <http://gongbao.court.gov.cn/Details/94b6623974526df7d2430a3c73f050.html> (Supreme People's Court Gazette).
- Notes: If a spouse's parents produce a loan agreement signed only by that spouse when property is being divided, examine two questions separately. First, did the loan exist? When a defendant reasonably explains why the claimed lending never actually occurred, the court considers the amount, delivery of funds, the parties' financial means, local or customary transaction methods, changes in their property, and witness evidence. A claimant relying on a transfer record still has to establish a loan relationship. Second, if a loan existed, was it joint marital debt? See chapter 8, entry 16, for the general rules on IOUs and guarantees. **Legal clarification:** Article 16 specifically addresses a defendant who says the transfer repaid an earlier loan or another debt: the defendant must provide supporting evidence, after which the claimant still bears the burden of proving the loan relationship. Simply denying a loan is not always enough. The provisions do not establish how often creditors succeed in home-purchase disputes. The [Supreme People's Court's amended text](https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/282621.html) and [official Civil Code text](https://tjca.miit.gov.cn/zwgk/zcwj/flfg/art/2020/art_20cf1a2e1b854924b5caa744c8045d1f.html) provide the legal basis. US and European debt rules need a separate local assessment.

### 13. In mainland China, marriage registration is free and no longer requires a household registration booklet
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=小 口径=金钱 -->

- Cost: No registration fee in China. Both people must attend in person. A straightforward visit may take tens of minutes, but the regulations do not guarantee a particular number of minutes or include travel, waiting, or optional services.
- In plain language: Since May 2025, the regulations list two documents for mainland residents: each person's resident identity card and a signed declaration that they have no spouse and are not related to the other person in a prohibited degree. You no longer need a household registration booklet or to return to the place where one person's household is registered. Registration itself is free, and eligible applicants must be registered and issued a marriage certificate on the spot. Do not pay a supposed certificate-production or expedited-registration fee; challenge it and complain. **Practical clarification:** the two statutory documents are not a complete packing list: the marriage registration procedures also require certificate photographs. Check the office's current instructions before attending.
- Benefit: The revised Marriage Registration Regulations took effect on 2025-05-10. Article 8 requires mainland residents to provide a resident identity card and a signed declaration that they have no spouse and are neither direct blood relatives nor collateral blood relatives within three generations. A household registration booklet is no longer required. Article 2 designates county-level civil affairs departments or township governments authorized by provincial, autonomous-region, or directly administered municipal governments for convenient access. The former restriction to one party's permanent household-registration location has been removed. Article 6 prohibits charging for marriage registration. Article 10 requires the office to check documents and relevant information, including identity and marital status online, and register eligible applicants and issue their certificates on the spot.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: State Council (2025). Marriage Registration Regulations, Order No. 804, Articles 2, 6, 8 and 10. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202504/content_7017752.htm>.
- Notes: Article 9 lists four grounds for refusal: being below the legal marriage age, lack of both parties' fully voluntary consent, an existing spouse, or a direct blood relationship or collateral blood relationship within three generations. Photography or copying may involve separate service charges; registration itself is free. Refuse and report purported certificate-production or expedited-registration charges. The 30-day cooling-off period for divorce by agreement is covered in this chapter's entry on the costs of leaving a marriage. **Legal and practical clarification:** these identity-document rules cover mainland residents; foreign nationals and residents of Hong Kong, Macao or Taiwan, and overseas Chinese have separate requirements. The [current regulations in the Ministry of Justice database](https://xzfg.moj.gov.cn/mobile/law/detail?LawID=1766) confirm the scope and free registration. Under the 2025 registration procedures, applicants also submit 3 identical recent joint half-length, bareheaded ID photographs in the Chinese 2-cun format; see the [civil affairs office's instructions](https://www.szns.gov.cn/main/ywzsk/zjbl/content/post_12410610.html). “On the spot” follows eligibility checks and does not promise that every visit, queue included, takes only tens of minutes. These are Chinese public-service rules, not a claim that registration is free in the US or Europe.

### 14. Arrange a health consultation before marriage; in China, disclose a serious illness before registering
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->

- Cost: Allow roughly half a day for a visit, travel and waiting; tests or follow-up may take longer. **Local price examples, checked September 21, 2026:** about USD 150 for a preconception/family-planning visit at [Morning Glory Birth & Wellness](https://www.morningglorybirthandwellness.com/our-services) in northwest Iowa; about EUR 180 for an initial pre-pregnancy consultation at [Clarity Menopause Clinic](https://www.claritymenopause.ie/fees) in Mullingar, Ireland. These are individual consultation prices, not national averages or the cost of examining both partners. Ask what tests are included and whether public services or insurance cover the care you need.
- In plain language: In China, concealing a serious illness before registering a marriage can give the other spouse grounds to ask a court to annul it. The application must be made within one year of learning, or when they should have learned, of the grounds for annulment. A premarital examination has been voluntary, rather than a registration prerequisite, since 2003. Free or subsidized examinations are available under local programs for eligible people; check the current terms. Allow roughly half a day, and check charges before booking if you pay yourself. The local consultation examples above are not equivalent to a complete Chinese premarital examination package.
- Benefit: Article 12 of China's revised Marriage Registration Regulations requires a person with a serious illness to tell the other person truthfully before registration. Failure to do so allows the other spouse to seek annulment under Civil Code Article 1053. China's birth-defect prevention plan treats premarital care as an early opportunity for prevention. Its targets for 2027 include maintaining premarital examination uptake at 70% or more and preconception health-check coverage at 80% or more of the target population. It calls for examination facilities close to marriage registration offices and combined services for registration, premarital examinations and reproductive guidance.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: State Council (2025). Marriage Registration Regulations, Order No. 804, Article 12. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202504/content_7017752.htm>; General Office of the National Health Commission (2023). Birth Defect Prevention Capacity Improvement Plan (2023–2027), Guo Wei Ban Fu You Fa [2023] No. 9. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202308/content_6900320.htm>.
- Notes: Since 2003, an examination has not been a prerequisite for marriage registration in China, but annulment for nondisclosure of a serious illness is a real legal remedy. The one-year deadline runs from when the person knew or should have known the grounds, under [Article 1053](https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/233181.html). Local rules determine eligibility for free examinations and the tests offered; ask the maternal and child health service or the combined service desk at the registration office. **Coverage clarification:** a [2022 National Health Commission response](https://www.nhc.gov.cn/wjw/jiany/202207/30117c0884534d5f94acbff23ac8c1e9.shtml) reported free-examination policies in more than 20 provinces. This does not establish current nationwide eligibility. Preconception care is relevant if you plan a pregnancy, regardless of marriage plans. Choose care with a clinician rather than buying every test in a commercial package. ### 15. Look at the quality of the relationship, not just whether your partner provides emotional support
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=时间 -->

- Cost: No purchase is needed. Before marriage, observe how you handle several disagreements and several months together instead of only checking a list of desired qualities.
- In plain language: A review of 126 papers involving more than 72,000 people linked better marital quality to better health. The association was small, on a scale comparable to associations involving health behaviors such as diet. Studies that directly tested differences between men and women generally found few. The practical suggestion is to observe how the relationship works over several disagreements and months together. A checklist alone cannot do that. The claim that a bad marriage is worse than remaining unmarried is an inference here, not a result of a direct mortality comparison in these reviews.
- Benefit: The meta-analysis of 126 papers and more than 72,000 participants found associations between higher marital quality and better health, with effect sizes r = 0.07–0.21. The mortality association was r = 0.11, and the association with cardiovascular reactivity during conflict was r = −0.13. Their size was comparable to associations between health behaviors, such as diet, and health. Studies explicitly testing gender differences generally found few. A review of cardiovascular disease also linked marital dissatisfaction and marital quality to cardiovascular risk. **Evidence clarification:** these are associations, not proof that improving a marriage causes better health. The positive mortality coefficient reflects the review's coding of better health with higher marital quality; it does not mean higher mortality. Neither r = 0.11 nor r = −0.13 means an 11% or 13% change in risk.
- Evidence grade: B (disputed)
- Sources: Robles TF, et al. (2014). Marital quality and health: a meta-analytic review. Psychological Bulletin, 140(1), 140–187. <https://doi.org/10.1037/a0031859>; Dhindsa DS, et al. (2020). Marital status and outcomes in patients with cardiovascular disease. Trends in Cardiovascular Medicine, 30(4), 215–220. <https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tcm.2019.05.012>.
- Notes: The uncertainty concerns small effect sizes, publication bias for some outcomes and study designs that do not establish causation. Robles and colleagues discuss these limitations. The evidence includes cross-sectional and longitudinal studies; a cross-sectional association alone cannot establish which came first or rule out other causes. These references do not supply a meta-analysis directly comparing mortality in low-quality marriages with mortality among unmarried people. “A bad marriage is worse than no marriage” is therefore an inference here, not a quantified conclusion. **Evidence clarification:** “few gender differences” has exceptions, including some surrogate health endpoints; it does not establish identical effects for everyone. Observing several disagreements or months together is practical advice, not a validated diagnostic test for a marriage.

### 16. Understand the cost of leaving: China has a 30-day cooling-off period for divorce by agreement and legal grounds for court divorce
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->

- Cost: No purchase is needed. Before marrying, read how long separation and divorce might take and what procedures you would have to follow.
- In plain language: In China, signing an agreement does not finish a divorce. Either spouse can withdraw the application within 30 days after the registration office receives it. After that period, both must attend in person to apply for the divorce certificates within another 30 days; otherwise the application is treated as withdrawn. If one spouse refuses, the other can go to court. The court must attempt mediation. Grounds for granting divorce when mediation fails include bigamy or cohabitation with another person, domestic violence, abuse or abandonment, persistent gambling or drug use despite attempts at correction, and two years of separation due to a breakdown in the relationship. These are not the only grounds. If a court refuses divorce and the spouses then live apart for a further year, divorce must be granted on a renewed application.
- Benefit: Article 1076 requires a written divorce agreement and a personal application to the marriage registration office. Under Article 1077, either spouse may withdraw within 30 days after the office receives the application; both must then apply for the certificates in person within the following 30 days, or the application is treated as withdrawn. Article 1079 provides for court proceedings when one spouse seeks divorce and requires mediation. If mediation fails, divorce must be granted for grounds including bigamy or cohabitation with another person; domestic violence, abuse or abandonment; persistent gambling or drug use despite attempts at correction; or two years of separation because of relationship breakdown. It also requires divorce to be granted on a renewed application after a further year of separation following a judgment refusing divorce. China's reported 2024 total of 3,513,000 divorces included 891,000 through court judgments or mediation; the outstanding verification limit for these figures is stated in entry 7.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: National People's Congress (2020). Civil Code, Articles 1076, 1077 and 1079. <http://gongbao.court.gov.cn/Details/7f184078694d811fb3314f6af9accf.html>; Ministry of Civil Affairs (2020). Notice on implementing the Civil Code's marriage registration provisions, Min Fa [2020] No. 116. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2020-12/04/content_5567010.htm>; Ministry of Civil Affairs (2025). 2024 Statistical Bulletin on the Development of Civil Affairs; see entry 7.
- Notes: The statutory cooling-off period applies to administrative divorce by agreement, not court divorce. Court proceedings can nevertheless take longer. Court and lawyer fees depend on the case and location; no prices are estimated here. The cost of leaving is not by itself a reason against marriage, but you should know it before marrying. **Legal clarification:** Article 1079 also covers other circumstances causing relationship breakdown and requires divorce to be granted if one spouse has been declared missing and the other applies. Two years of separation is one ground, not a mandatory wait for every court divorce. A second application alone does not replace the further-year-of-separation condition after a refusal. See the [official Civil Code text](https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/233181.html). **Reference clarification:** the statistical bulletin is cited in entry 7, not entry 1; a fresh check of its full official text remains pending. All procedures in this entry concern China, not US or European divorce law.

### 17. Keep the benefits to older relatives separate from your own reasons for marrying
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=时间 -->

- Cost: No purchase is needed. Make two columns on paper: what you gain from this marriage, using the earlier entries where figures help, and what older relatives gain—peace of mind, social standing or an end to pressure to marry.
- In plain language: Those benefits to your relatives are real, but they do not automatically improve your health or the quality of your relationship. You are the one entering the marriage and facing its demands on your time, finances and ability to leave. Write the two sets of benefits separately so you can judge whether the tradeoff works for you.
- Benefit: Your relatives' gains do not automatically become health benefits for you (entry 8) or a better relationship (entry 15). The demands on time (entry 9), financial consequences (entries 10–12) and costs of leaving (entry 16) fall on the people marrying. Keeping the two accounts separate helps you judge whether marrying to fulfill a family expectation is worth it. Nobody else needs to make that decision for you.
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources: Practical advice based on the author's experience; the figures are covered in the earlier entries in this chapter.
- Notes: This entry does not decide for you. Your relatives' happiness can carry a lot of weight in your decision. The fill-in checklist is in [the marriage costs and benefits guide](../../docs/结婚划不划算.md) (Chinese). **Scope clarification:** this exercise does not assume that relatives never contribute money or practical help. Record that support too, without treating it as proof that the marriage will benefit your health or suit you.
