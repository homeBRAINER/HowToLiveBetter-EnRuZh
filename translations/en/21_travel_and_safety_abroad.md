# 21. Travel and safety abroad

This chapter tracks money and personal liberty; a few entries also concern physical safety. The country you visit sets the rules there. Your embassy or consulate can help within defined limits, so know those limits before you leave.

The introduction and entries 1–4 of 10 are translated below. [Entries 5–10 are available in Chinese](../../book/21-出国旅行与境外安全.md).

### 1. Check official travel advice before you leave, and avoid destinations under a warning against travel
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Reading a government travel notice carries no fee: $0 in the United States or €0 in Europe, apart from any internet or mobile-data charge. Allow a few minutes, and check again just before departure.
- In plain language: Read the safety notice for your destination on the foreign ministry website that serves your citizenship. If it warns against travel, postpone the trip or choose another place. In China, rules effective 15 September 2026 tell citizens to heed overseas safety notices and avoid high-risk countries or regions. Border officials may warn a traveler and, in the most severe cases, try to dissuade them from leaving. This is not an automatic legal ban on every trip.
- Benefit: China's foreign ministry and overseas missions publish notices about safety risks abroad. Under article 2 of State Council Decree 841, effective 2026-09-15, Chinese citizens should follow those notices and avoid travel to or stays in high-risk countries or regions. Immigration officials are to warn people headed for risky destinations; where the warning is at its highest level or severe threats to personal safety suddenly become frequent, they may dissuade travel when necessary. The earlier Consular Protection and Assistance Regulation requires the ministry to publish safety notices and travelers to pay attention to them. These are duties and risk warnings, not a measured reduction in deaths.
- Evidence grade: A

- Notes: China's 2023 consular guide describes **three** formal travel-advice levels: “pay attention to safety,” “travel with caution,” and “do not travel for now.” Individual notices also say “strengthen safety precautions”; that phrase is not listed as a fourth level in the guide. On 2026-09-07, the linked Chinese notice archive still showed “do not travel for now” notices for Eswatini (2026-08-25), the Palestinian territories (2023-10-10), Sudan (2023-04-17), Afghanistan (2023-02-17), Syria (2023-01-04), Peru (2022-12-29), and Somalia (2022-11-24), plus specific warnings for northern Myanmar and parts of the Democratic Republic of the Congo. This dated snapshot is not a current destination list. In China, travel agencies also have a duty to disclose destination risks; ask for those risks in writing before a group tour. For a US passport, consult the State Department's destination advisory; its Level 4 means “Do Not Travel.” For a German passport, consult the Federal Foreign Office's travel warnings; for a British passport, consult FCDO advice, including any warning against all travel. Warnings and insurance exclusions differ by country and policy. Check your own government's current notice and the destination's entry rules before buying tickets and again before departure.

### 2. Save your consulate's emergency number and a backup contact before something happens
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: Saving two numbers is free: $0 or €0. Calling from abroad may incur phone or roaming charges; keep a written copy as well.
- In plain language: Put your nearest embassy or consulate's emergency number and your government's central consular contact in your phone. Write both on paper or give them to someone you trust, because a lost phone can take the saved numbers with it. For Chinese citizens, the central 24-hour line is 12308 from China and +86-10-12308 or +86-10-65612308 from abroad. If someone is in immediate danger, contact local police or emergency medical services first.
- Benefit: China's 12308 consular-protection hotline operates 24 hours a day. From abroad, call +86-10-12308 or +86-10-65612308. The foreign ministry reported in July 2023 that it had answered more than 3 million calls since opening in 2014; over the same broad period, the ministry and overseas missions had handled more than 500,000 consular-protection cases. Those are historical service volumes, not a measured effect of saving a phone number. Having the right numbers ready removes the need to search for them during an emergency.
- Evidence grade: A

Chinese foreign ministry, current 12308 dialing formats. <https://cs.mfa.gov.cn/lgk/202212/t20221228_10997066.shtml>. - Notes: For Chinese citizens, the mainland dialing options are 12308 or 010-12308, and the overseas options are +86-10-12308 or +86-10-65612308; the latter is the current backup number. Find the emergency number for the Chinese mission at your destination on the consular-service website and save it too. For a US passport, the Department of State lists +1-202-501-4444 from outside the US and Canada. Germany's foreign-ministry emergency switchboard is +49 30 5000 0, staffed around the clock; its similarly named +49 30 5000 2000 citizens' service has limited weekday hours. The UK FCDO consular line is +44 20 7008 5000, available 24/7. These national lines serve their own citizens; neither Chinese 12308 nor any one European number serves everyone. Keep a paper copy in your wallet or with a travel companion and leave a copy with family.

### 3. Know the limits of consular help: officials may visit you, but cannot get you out of jail or pay your bills
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Reading your government's consular guidance is free: $0 or €0, apart from any internet charge. Legal representation, medical care, transport and accommodation can cost money; their prices depend on the country and case.
- In plain language: If you are detained abroad, ask the local authorities to contact your embassy or consulate and find a local lawyer. Consular staff may check what happened, contact or visit you where local law and treaties allow, observe a hearing and raise concerns about fair treatment. They cannot overturn a court decision or simply take you home. Plan to pay your own lawyer and other bills, and arrange suitable travel insurance and access to emergency funds before leaving.
- Benefit: Under China's Consular Protection and Assistance Regulation, article 9 allows a Chinese mission to check the circumstances of a detention, seek fair treatment and, subject to local law and treaties, visit or contact the citizen. Article 10 allows observation of proceedings and requests that procedural rights be protected. Article 14 covers help with urgent treatment after an injury and with arrangements after a death. Article 26 requires the recipient to pay the costs that are their responsibility when third parties provide accommodation, transport, medical care or other services. Consular help has defined powers and financial limits; these provisions do not quantify a saving.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: State Council of China (2023), Consular Protection and Assistance Regulation, Decree 763, articles 9, 10, 12, 14, 16 and 26. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202307/content_6891761.htm>; official legal database. <https://xzfg.moj.gov.cn/front/law/detail?LawID=1674>. US Department of State, arrest or detention abroad. <https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/help-abroad/arrest-detention.html>. German Federal Foreign Office, assistance from overseas missions and EU consular cooperation. <https://www.auswaertiges-amt.de/de/service/konsularinfo/hilfe-av>; <https://www.auswaertiges-amt.de/de/service/konsularinfo/eu-konsularhilfe-kooperation-2083486>. UK FCDO, arrested or in prison abroad. <https://www.gov.uk/guidance/arrested-or-detained-abroad>.
- Notes: A Chinese mission can give contact information for local lawyers, interpreters, doctors and funeral services, help reach family and seek available relief, but it cannot change a foreign judgment. For US citizens, the State Department says consuls may visit a detainee and provide a list of lawyers, but cannot secure release, act as counsel or pay legal or medical fees. UK guidance likewise says a consulate cannot get a citizen out of prison, pay fines or represent them. Germany may provide limited, repayable emergency financial help in exceptional cases; this is not payment of hotel, hospital or legal bills as a matter of course. If your EU country has no mission in a non-EU destination, another EU country's mission may be able to help. Check the rules of the government that serves your citizenship and the law of the place where you are detained.

### 4. Choose travel insurance that covers overseas medical care and medical transport home, not just flight delays
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Prices vary by age, destination, trip length and cover. As examples rather than a universal rate, US policies bought for Mexico trips averaged about $74 in August 2026, while one insurer listed €21.95–€54.95 for a seven-day Schengen visitor policy, depending on the plan. Check a live quote in USD or EUR for your own trip and verify the medical and evacuation limits before paying.
- In plain language: Treatment abroad and a medically escorted trip home can be expensive, and your usual health cover may not pay for either. Flight-delay cover does not solve that problem. Before you travel, check whether your existing plan covers overseas hospital bills and emergency medical transport home. If it does not, choose a policy with both benefits and enough cover for the destination.
- Benefit: China's Consular Protection and Assistance Regulation, article 26, says citizens must pay costs that are their responsibility when third parties provide accommodation, transport or medical care during consular help. The US State Department says the government does not pay medical bills abroad and that an air-ambulance evacuation back to the US can cost $20,000–$200,000, depending on location and condition. EU health-card rules cover medically necessary public care in participating countries under local conditions, but do not pay for private care or repatriation. These sources explain the size and nature of the possible bill; they do not measure a typical saving from any one policy.
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources: State Council of China (2023), Consular Protection and Assistance Regulation, Decree 763, article 26. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202307/content_6891761.htm>; official legal database. <https://xzfg.moj.gov.cn/front/law/detail?LawID=1674>. US Department of State, travel insurance and health abroad. <https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/planning/guidance/insurance.html>; <https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/planning/guidance/medicine-health.html>. US Medicare, travel outside the US. <https://www.medicare.gov/coverage/travel-outside-the-u.s/>. European Commission, European Health Insurance Card. <https://employment-social-affairs.ec.europa.eu/policies-and-activities/moving-working-europe/eu-social-security-coordination/european-health-insurance-card_en>. InsureMyTrip, US-market Mexico policy purchase data (August 2026). <https://www.insuremytrip.com/destinations/mexico-travel-insurance/>. AXA Schengen, seven-day visitor-plan prices (2026). <https://www.axa-schengen.com/en/travel-insurance/benefits/price-travel-insurance>.
- Notes: The $74 figure is a purchase average for one destination and includes different kinds of policies; the euro figures are for visitors to Schengen, not a quote for every European resident traveling abroad. Neither example proves that a particular policy will pay for a medical evacuation. If you have US private health insurance, ask the insurer what it covers overseas; Original Medicare usually does not cover care outside the US, with limited exceptions. The European Health Insurance Card is not travel insurance and does not pay to bring you home. Check three things in the contract: whether every destination is covered, whether activities such as diving, skiing or riding a motorcycle are excluded, and whether the insurer pays providers directly or reimburses you later. Check separate limits for hospital care and emergency medical evacuation or repatriation. Typical costs of all destinations and evacuation routes are not established here; compare policies for your actual trip.
