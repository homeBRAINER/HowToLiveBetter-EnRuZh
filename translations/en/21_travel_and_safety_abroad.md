# 21. Travel and safety abroad

This chapter tracks money and personal liberty; a few entries also concern physical safety. The country you visit sets the rules there. Your embassy or consulate can help within defined limits, so know those limits before you leave.

The introduction and all 10 entries are available in English. The [Chinese text](../../book/21-出国旅行与境外安全.md) and [Russian version](../ru/21_travel_and_safety_abroad.md) are also available.

### 1. Check official travel advice before you leave, and avoid destinations under a warning against travel
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Reading a government travel notice carries no fee: $0 in the United States or €0 in Europe, apart from any internet or mobile-data charge. Allow a few minutes, and check again just before departure.
- In plain language: Read the safety notice for your destination on the foreign ministry website that serves your citizenship. If it warns against travel, postpone the trip or choose another place. In China, rules effective 15 September 2026 tell citizens to heed overseas safety notices and avoid high-risk countries or regions. Border officials may warn a traveler and, in the most severe cases, try to dissuade them from leaving. This is not an automatic legal ban on every trip.
- Benefit: China's foreign ministry and overseas missions publish notices about safety risks abroad. Under article 2 of State Council Decree 841, effective 2026-09-15, Chinese citizens should follow those notices and avoid travel to or stays in high-risk countries or regions. Immigration officials are to warn people headed for risky destinations; where the warning is at its highest level or severe threats to personal safety suddenly become frequent, they may dissuade travel when necessary. The earlier Consular Protection and Assistance Regulation requires the ministry to publish safety notices and travelers to pay attention to them. These are duties and risk warnings, not a measured reduction in deaths.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: State Council of China (2026), Decree 841, article 2. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202607/content_7077173.htm>; accessible official publication. <https://big5.www.gov.cn/gate/big5/www.gov.cn/zhengce/content/202607/content_7077172.htm>. Chinese foreign ministry, safety notices. <https://cs.mfa.gov.cn/zggmzhw/lsbh/aqtx/>; its 2023 consular guide, travel-advice levels. <https://cs.mfa.gov.cn/zggmzhw/lsbh/lbsc_660514/202311/P020231114392262052796.pdf>. - Notes: China's 2023 consular guide describes **three** formal travel-advice levels: “pay attention to safety,” “travel with caution,” and “do not travel for now.” Individual notices also say “strengthen safety precautions”; that phrase is not listed as a fourth level in the guide. On 2026-09-07, the linked Chinese notice archive still showed “do not travel for now” notices for Eswatini (2026-08-25), the Palestinian territories (2023-10-10), Sudan (2023-04-17), Afghanistan (2023-02-17), Syria (2023-01-04), Peru (2022-12-29), and Somalia (2022-11-24), plus specific warnings for northern Myanmar and parts of the Democratic Republic of the Congo. This dated snapshot is not a current destination list. In China, travel agencies also have a duty to disclose destination risks; ask for those risks in writing before a group tour. For a US passport, consult the State Department's destination advisory; its Level 4 means “Do Not Travel.” For a German passport, consult the Federal Foreign Office's travel warnings; for a British passport, consult FCDO advice, including any warning against all travel. Warnings and insurance exclusions differ by country and policy. Check your own government's current notice and the destination's entry rules before buying tickets and again before departure.

### 2. Save your consulate's emergency number and a backup contact before something happens
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: Saving two numbers is free: $0 or €0. Calling from abroad may incur phone or roaming charges; keep a written copy as well.
- In plain language: Put your nearest embassy or consulate's emergency number and your government's central consular contact in your phone. Write both on paper or give them to someone you trust, because a lost phone can take the saved numbers with it. For Chinese citizens, the central 24-hour line is 12308 from China and +86-10-12308 or +86-10-65612308 from abroad. If someone is in immediate danger, contact local police or emergency medical services first.
- Benefit: China's 12308 consular-protection hotline operates 24 hours a day. From abroad, call +86-10-12308 or +86-10-65612308. The foreign ministry reported in July 2023 that it had answered more than 3 million calls since opening in 2014; over the same broad period, the ministry and overseas missions had handled more than 500,000 consular-protection cases. Those are historical service volumes, not a measured effect of saving a phone number. Having the right numbers ready removes the need to search for them during an emergency.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Chinese foreign ministry and its missions in Myanmar (2023), consular safety notice. <https://cs.mfa.gov.cn/zggmzhw/lsbh/aqtx/202311/t20231109_11176682.shtml>. State Council Information Office of China (2023), briefing on consular protection and assistance. <https://www.gov.cn/xinwen/2023zccfh/11/index.htm>. Chinese foreign ministry, current 12308 dialing formats. <https://cs.mfa.gov.cn/lgk/202212/t20221228_10997066.shtml>. US Department of State, crisis contacts. <https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/help-abroad/crisis-response.html>. German Federal Foreign Office, emergency switchboard. <https://www.auswaertiges-amt.de/de/service/fragenkatalog-node/15-notrufnummer/606090>. UK FCDO, consular contact. <https://www.gov.uk/guidance/consular-assistance-how-the-foreign-commonwealth-development-office-provides-support>.
- Notes: For Chinese citizens, the mainland dialing options are 12308 or 010-12308, and the overseas options are +86-10-12308 or +86-10-65612308; the latter is the current backup number. Find the emergency number for the Chinese mission at your destination on the consular-service website and save it too. For a US passport, the Department of State lists +1-202-501-4444 from outside the US and Canada. Germany's foreign-ministry emergency switchboard is +49 30 5000 0, staffed around the clock; its similarly named +49 30 5000 2000 citizens' service has limited weekday hours. The UK FCDO consular line is +44 20 7008 5000, available 24/7. These national lines serve their own citizens; neither Chinese 12308 nor any one European number serves everyone. Keep a paper copy in your wallet or with a travel companion and leave a copy with family.

### 3. Know the limits of consular help: officials may visit you, but cannot get you out of jail or pay your bills
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Reading your government's consular guidance is free: $0 or €0, apart from any internet charge. Legal representation, medical care, transport and accommodation can cost money; their prices depend on the country and case.
- In plain language: If you are detained abroad, ask the local authorities to contact your embassy or consulate and find a local lawyer. Consular staff may check what happened, contact or visit you where local law and treaties allow, observe a hearing and raise concerns about fair treatment. They cannot overturn a court decision or simply take you home. Plan to pay your own lawyer and other bills, and arrange suitable travel insurance and access to emergency funds before leaving.
- Benefit: Under China's Consular Protection and Assistance Regulation, article 9 allows a Chinese mission to check the circumstances of a detention, seek fair treatment and, subject to local law and treaties, visit or contact the citizen. Article 10 allows observation of proceedings and requests that procedural rights be protected. Article 14 covers help with urgent treatment after an injury and with arrangements after a death. Article 26 requires the recipient to pay the costs that are their responsibility when third parties provide accommodation, transport, medical care or other services. Consular help has defined powers and financial limits; these provisions do not quantify a saving.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: State Council of China (2023), Consular Protection and Assistance Regulation, Decree 763, articles 9, 10, 12, 14, 16 and 26. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202307/content_6891761.htm>; official legal database. <https://xzfg.moj.gov.cn/front/law/detail?LawID=1674>. US Department of State, arrest or detention abroad. <https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/help-abroad/arrest-detention.html>. German Federal Foreign Office, assistance from overseas missions and EU consular cooperation. <https://www.auswaertiges-amt.de/de/service/konsularinfo/hilfe-av>; <https://www.auswaertiges-amt.de/de/service/konsularinfo/eu-konsularhilfe-kooperation-2083486>. UK FCDO, arrested or in prison abroad. <https://www.gov.uk/guidance/arrested-or-detained-abroad>.
- Notes: A Chinese mission can give contact information for local lawyers, interpreters, doctors and funeral services, help reach family and seek available relief, but it cannot change a foreign judgment. For US citizens, the State Department says consuls may visit a detainee and provide a list of lawyers, but cannot secure release, act as counsel or pay legal or medical fees. UK guidance likewise says a consulate cannot get a citizen out of prison, pay fines or represent them. Germany may provide limited, repayable emergency financial help in exceptional cases; this is not payment of hotel, hospital or legal bills as a matter of course. If your EU country has no mission in a non-EU destination, another EU country's mission may be able to help. Check the rules of the government that serves your citizenship and the law of the place where you are detained.

### 4. Choose travel insurance that covers overseas medical care and medical transport home, not just flight delays
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Prices vary by age, destination, trip length and cover. As examples rather than a universal rate, US policies bought for Mexico trips averaged about $74 in August 2026, while one insurer listed €21.95–€54.95 for a seven-day Schengen visitor policy, depending on the plan. Check a live quote in USD or EUR for your own trip and verify the medical and evacuation limits before paying.
- In plain language: Treatment abroad and a medically escorted trip home can be expensive, and your usual health cover may not pay for either. Flight-delay cover does not solve that problem. Before you travel, check whether your existing plan covers overseas hospital bills and emergency medical transport home. If it does not, choose a policy with both benefits and enough cover for the destination.
- Benefit: China's Consular Protection and Assistance Regulation, article 26, says citizens must pay costs that are their responsibility when third parties provide accommodation, transport or medical care during consular help. The US State Department says the government does not pay medical bills abroad and that an air-ambulance evacuation back to the US can cost $20,000–$200,000, depending on location and condition. EU health-card rules cover medically necessary public care in participating countries under local conditions, but do not pay for private care or repatriation. These sources explain the size and nature of the possible bill; they do not measure a typical saving from any one policy.
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources: State Council of China (2023), Consular Protection and Assistance Regulation, Decree 763, article 26. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202307/content_6891761.htm>; official legal database. <https://xzfg.moj.gov.cn/front/law/detail?LawID=1674>. US Department of State, travel insurance and health abroad. <https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/planning/guidance/insurance.html>; <https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/planning/guidance/medicine-health.html>. US Medicare, travel outside the US. <https://www.medicare.gov/coverage/travel-outside-the-u.s/>. European Commission, European Health Insurance Card. <https://employment-social-affairs.ec.europa.eu/policies-and-activities/moving-working-europe/eu-social-security-coordination/european-health-insurance-card_en>. InsureMyTrip, US-market Mexico policy purchase data (August 2026). <https://www.insuremytrip.com/destinations/mexico-travel-insurance/>. AXA Schengen, seven-day visitor-plan prices (2026). <https://www.axa-schengen.com/en/travel-insurance/benefits/price-travel-insurance>.
- Notes: The $74 figure is a purchase average for one destination and includes different kinds of policies; the euro figures are for visitors to Schengen, not a quote for every European resident traveling abroad. Neither example proves that a particular policy will pay for a medical evacuation. If you have US private health insurance, ask the insurer what it covers overseas; Original Medicare usually does not cover care outside the US, with limited exceptions. The European Health Insurance Card is not travel insurance and does not pay to bring you home. Check three things in the contract: whether every destination is covered, whether activities such as diving, skiing or riding a motorcycle are excluded, and whether the insurer pays providers directly or reimburses you later. Check separate limits for hospital care and emergency medical evacuation or repatriation. Typical costs of all destinations and evacuation routes are not established here; compare policies for your actual trip.

### 5. Turn down a suspicious offer of a high-paying job abroad
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: Checking an employer and declining a suspicious offer need not cost anything — $0 or €0. It takes some time and the resolve to walk away.
- In plain language: Do not travel for a job that promises exceptional pay without relevant experience while hiding the employer's identity, asking you to describe paid work as tourism, or planning to keep your passport. Verify the company and terms independently, ask for a written contract, and check that you can legally work at the destination. A job advert can be a trap: people have been taken abroad and forced to run online scams. If someone is already holding you or your documents, look for a safe way to contact local emergency services and your country's consulate.
- Benefit: The UN Office on Drugs and Crime documents recruitment into scam compounds through adverts for well-paid jobs, including in Southeast Asia. Retained identity documents and restricted movement are warning signs of possible forced labour. Under China's Decree 841, effective 15 September 2026, article 3 allows authorities to refuse a travel document or exit after false application documents or statements. Article 4 permits a 6-month-to-3-year exit ban for a Chinese citizen **after they have served an administrative-detention penalty for obtaining travel documents by deception or illegally crossing a border**. Separately, a 6-month-to-3-year exit ban from the date of return to China may follow a specified finding of unlawful acts abroad that harm the country's national security and interests. Being deceived by a recruiter is not, by itself, a stated ground for this ban. The sources do not quantify how many cases checking an offer prevents.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: State Council of China (2026), Decree 841, articles 3–4. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202607/content_7077173.htm>; official regulations database. <https://xzfg.moj.gov.cn/front/law/detail?LawID=1820>. China's Ministry of Justice, Ministry of Public Security and National Immigration Administration (2026), explanation. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/202608/content_7077270.htm>; immigration authority copy. <https://www.nia.gov.cn/n794014/n1050181/n1050484/c1793235/content.html>. UNODC, policy brief on trafficking for forced participation in online fraud. <https://www.unodc.org/roseap/uploads/documents/Publications/2023/TiP_for_FC_Summary_Policy_Brief.pdf>. International Labour Organization, indicators of forced labour. <https://www.ilo.org/resource/article/questions-and-answers-forced-labour>.
- Notes: Red flags include pay far above the market rate without skill requirements, no verifiable employer or written terms, a meeting point near a border, travel on a tourist permission for paid work, and a recruiter asking to hold your passport. One sign alone does not establish a crime, but several warrant stopping and checking independently. China has also issued a consular warning about northern Myanmar. <https://cs.mfa.gov.cn/zggmzhw/lsbh/aqtx/202311/t20231109_11176682.shtml>. China's exit-ban periods are not US or European rules. For a US or European passport holder, check the destination's work-permit requirements and never misstate your travel purpose. If you have already been trapped, you may be a trafficking victim; do not assume punishment is inevitable, and seek help.

### 6. Keep copies of your passport and visa away from your phone; contact your consulate if a document is lost
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Making digital copies can cost $0 or €0; printing, a new passport photograph or replacing a document may cost money. Set aside a few minutes before travel.
- In plain language: Save an image of your passport's identity page, any visa or entry permission, and your national identity card if you have one, in protected cloud storage you can reach without your missing phone. Keep a paper copy separate from the passport. If the document disappears, contact your country's consulate promptly; report a theft to local police. A police report is not mandatory everywhere for a simple loss. Ask your consulate, local authorities and insurer whether you need one for your own trip.
- Benefit: Article 23 of China's Passport Law directs a Chinese citizen temporarily abroad whose passport is lost to apply to a Chinese mission for a travel document. China's foreign ministry also describes passport replacement and applications through its consular app. A US citizen who loses a passport abroad must obtain a replacement before returning home; the US State Department can issue a limited-validity emergency passport when urgent travel leaves too little time for a regular one. EU citizens abroad can seek their national consulate, and an unrepresented EU citizen outside the Union may seek another EU country's mission for an emergency travel document. Copies can help provide document details or support identity checks, but they are not travel documents and do not guarantee fast replacement. A general amount of time saved has not been established.
- Evidence grade: C
- Sources: State Council of China (2023), Consular Protection and Assistance Regulation, Decree 763, article 16. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202307/content_6891761.htm>; official regulations database. <https://xzfg.moj.gov.cn/front/law/detail?LawID=1674>. China's Passport Law, article 23. <https://cs.mfa.gov.cn/zlbg/flfg/crjxg/200605/t20060512_961854.shtml>. Chinese Ministry of Foreign Affairs, passport and travel-document service abroad. <https://www.mfa.gov.cn/wjbzwfwpt/byw/hzlxz/>. US Department of State, replacing a lost or stolen passport abroad. <https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/help-abroad/lost-stolen-passport.html>. European Union, consular protection and lost travel documents. <https://europa.eu/youreurope/citizens/travel/security-and-emergencies/consular-protection/index_en.htm>; <https://europa.eu/youreurope/citizens/travel/entry-exit/expired-lost-passports/>.
- Notes: Article 16 of the cited Chinese regulation concerns information about local services; **it does not set the passport-replacement procedure**. Article 23 of the Passport Law provides the relevant rule. Chinese consular guidance advises reporting a lost passport to local police because a record may be needed for exit formalities. <https://cs.mfa.gov.cn/zggmzhw/lsbh/lbsc_660514/P020181207363467982595.pdf>. The US State Department says a police report is **not mandatory** for a US passport replacement abroad, though it can document loss or theft. Rules for European nationals vary by citizenship and destination; EU guidance says to check exit and transit requirements. A replacement document may not preserve the visa in the old passport. Store two protected backups in different places, including a printed copy if practical; safeguard the account and do not post identity documents publicly. Save entry records, card details and necessary prescriptions separately too. If you share a copy with a trusted person, use a secure method. Ask the consulate and authorities about visas, exit requirements and transit before buying a new ticket.

### 7. Check whether your licence is valid at your destination before you drive
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: Checking the rules can cost $0 or €0, apart from any internet charges. A certified translation, international driving permit or local licence may have a separate fee if required.
- In plain language: Do not buy an “international driver's licence” from an online seller before checking who issued it and whether your destination accepts it. Ask the destination's transport authority what it requires alongside your national licence, including a translation or international permit. Check the vehicle category and insurance too. Get the rental company's document requirements in writing, but remember that a company handing over the keys does not make driving lawful.
- Benefit: The Geneva Convention on Road Traffic was concluded on 19 September 1949 and entered into force on 26 March 1952; the UN listed 103 parties on 25 September 2026. An international driving permit under that convention accompanies a valid national licence rather than replacing it. The convention does not extend to mainland China, so a purported convention permit sold there on the basis of a mainland Chinese licence does not by itself authorise driving abroad. Each destination decides whether it recognises the national licence, a certified translation or an international permit, or requires a local licence. The separate 1968 Vienna Convention is another framework: not all international permits come from the 1949 treaty. Russia and Belarus are parties to the 1968 convention. Some countries accept a US licence while others require an IDP; EU licences are generally recognised within the EU, while recognition of non-EU licences varies by member state.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: United Nations Treaty Collection, status of the Convention on Road Traffic, Geneva, 19 September 1949. <https://treaties.un.org/pages/ViewDetailsV.aspx?src=TREATY&mtdsg_no=XI-B-1&chapter=11&clang=_en>. United Nations Treaty Collection, status of the 1968 Vienna Convention. <https://treaties.un.org/pages/ViewDetailsIII.aspx?src=TREATY&mtdsg_no=XI-B-19&chapter=11&clang=_en>. Chinese Ministry of Foreign Affairs, overseas driving reminder. <https://cs.mfa.gov.cn/gyls/lsgz/fwxx/202604/t20260430_11903029.shtml>. Chinese Embassy in Indonesia, mainland Chinese licences and IDPs. <https://id.china-embassy.gov.cn/lsfw/zytz_0/202407/P020240715641567842915.pdf>. US Department of State, driving and transportation safety abroad. <https://travel.state.gov/en/international-travel/planning/guidance/driving-transportation.html>. European Union, driving-licence recognition. <https://europa.eu/youreurope/citizens/vehicles/driving-licence/driving-licence-exchange-recognition/index_en.htm>.
- Notes: The UN records separate application of the 1949 convention in Hong Kong and Macao; do not assume their rules are the same as mainland China's. Taiwan and individual destinations also have separate arrangements. Mainland Chinese licence holders may need a notarised translation, another document or a local licence depending on the destination; check the country's authority or the destination guidance on China's consular website. For a US licence, the State Department identifies AAA and AATA as authorised US IDP issuers and says to carry the original licence as well. A European traveller should also check any destination outside the EU; an EU-issued permit and a non-EU licence exchanged for an EU one can raise different questions. Driving without a recognised licence can bring fines or criminal consequences depending on local law. Insurance may exclude an unlicensed driver, but automatic denial in every country is not established. Confirm the licence and coverage before paying for a rental.

### 8. Check a visa or immigration intermediary's status and terms before you pay
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: Checking official instructions and asking for written terms can cost $0 or €0, apart from internet access. The actual visa application and an adviser’s services may have fees; get the full price and refund terms first.
- In plain language: If someone promises a guaranteed visa, study place or move abroad, ask who actually decides the application and what will be submitted in your name. Compare the agency's claims with the destination government's instructions, verify the business and read every form before it goes in. A consultant's promise will not make invented employment, bank or invitation documents genuine.
- Benefit: China removed its admission licence for private exit-and-entry intermediaries in 2018 and later introduced a filing system as the sector grew. Under Decree 841, effective 15 September 2026, a Chinese intermediary must file with its local immigration authority within 15 days of establishment; existing operators have 90 days from the regulation's start. Article 10 bars false information, exaggerated or misleading advertising, fabricated documents, improper assistance with immigration procedures, unlawful disclosure of personal information and help with cross-border crime. Article 8 sets eligibility requirements and bars overseas organisations from providing such services within China. These Chinese rules are not a US or European adviser-registration scheme, and legitimate help completing truthful forms is not prohibited just because it is help.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: State Council of China (2026), Decree 841, articles 7, 8, 10, 12 and 13. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202607/content_7077173.htm>; official regulations database. <https://xzfg.moj.gov.cn/front/law/detail?LawID=1820>. Chinese Ministry of Justice, Ministry of Public Security and National Immigration Administration (2026), explanation of the filing system and 2018 licence change. <https://www.nia.gov.cn/n794014/n1050181/n1050484/c1793235/content.html>. US Department of State, visa fraud warning. <https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/visa-information-resources/fraud.html>. European External Action Service, Schengen visa guidance on intermediaries and false documents. <https://www.eeas.europa.eu/delegations/t%C3%BCrkiye/visa_en>.
- Notes: In China, the filing requirement covers intermediaries engaged to give exit-and-entry policy advice or handle documents and procedures commercially; non-profit policy advice and information lookups are outside this definition. Chinese authorities also say a foreign-invested company lawfully established in China can offer services there, even though an overseas organisation cannot do so directly inside China. The phrases “guaranteed visa” and “we will prepare your documents” are not themselves enumerated offences in Article 10; deceptive promises, false material and illegal processing are the problem. Treat a guarantee as a warning sign because a government, not an agent, makes the decision. For a US visa, use the State Department's official instructions; false facts or documents can affect later eligibility. For Schengen applications, the EU says an unofficial intermediary does not improve approval chances and you should personally check every document. Adviser regulation varies by place and service; China's register is not a substitute for the rules where you apply. Mistakes and deliberate deception have different possible consequences. Keep a written contract and share personal data only as necessary.

### 9. Do not sign a false invitation or let someone use your name for immigration papers
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: Declining a dubious request can cost $0 or €0. Any real invitation procedure and legal responsibility depend on the country receiving it.
- In plain language: If a stranger asks you to confirm a job, address or visit that is not real, refuse. Before signing a genuine invitation, check the person's details, reason for travel and any obligations you take on. In China, the person or organisation that supplies a false invitation can be fined.
- Benefit: Articles 3 and 11 of China's Decree 841 require a person or organisation issuing an invitation or other application material for another person's entry, exit or stay to stand behind its truth. An individual who issues a false invitation or other false supporting material can be fined 5,000–10,000 CNY, with any unlawful gains confiscated. An organisation can be fined 10,000–50,000 CNY, also with unlawful gains confiscated; its directly responsible managers and other directly responsible staff face an additional 5,000–10,000 CNY each. Those are Chinese-law amounts. Penalties in the US and European countries depend on their own law.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: State Council of China (2026), Decree 841, articles 3 and 11. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202607/content_7077173.htm>; official regulations database. <https://xzfg.moj.gov.cn/front/law/detail?LawID=1820>.
- Notes: As with the warning in [chapter 8 against carrying a stranger's property across a border](08_protect_your_rights_and_property.md), what you sign, stamp or guarantee in your name can have consequences for you. Do not invent facts or lend your signature to an application you cannot verify. China has another rule for intermediaries: if a public servant or member of the armed forces asks one to arrange foreign nationality or overseas residence in violation of the rules, the intermediary must refuse and report it to the supervisory authority (Decree 841, article 10). That is not a blanket ban on foreign nationality for every reader and not a US or European rule.

### 10. Share your itinerary with someone you trust and agree when you will check in
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: Sending a message can cost $0 or €0 when you already have connectivity; mobile data or roaming may cost extra.
- In plain language: Give one trusted person your flight number, accommodation address, travel companion's name and phone number, and the next time you expect to make contact. Agree when a missed check-in should prompt calls to local emergency services or your consulate. Update that person when your plans change. They will then know where to start looking if you disappear.
- Benefit: Under Article 13 of China's Consular Protection and Assistance Regulation, relatives of a Chinese citizen missing abroad may ask a Chinese mission for help. The mission must explain how to contact local police and find other assistance; if police open a case, the mission must urge them to handle it promptly. A shared itinerary and check-in time help relatives know where and when to look and what to tell police or consular staff. No numerical reduction in mortality has been established for this step.
- Evidence grade: C

- Notes: Four items are enough to begin: the flight number, accommodation address, companion's name and number, and an agreed contact schedule. If you are going somewhere with poor reception or a higher safety risk, say how long a silence should last before your contact starts calling for help. For an immediate emergency, contact local services at once; do not wait for the check-in deadline. Article 13 states what Chinese missions do for Chinese citizens. US nationals should contact a US mission, and European travellers should contact their own country's mission; an unrepresented EU citizen outside the Union may have other EU consular options. The US State Department advises relatives to keep a missing traveller's itinerary and contact details. Do not publish your room address or full route openly.
