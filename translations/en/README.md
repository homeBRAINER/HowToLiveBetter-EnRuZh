## Live better. Spend your resources wisely.

A guide to longevity and disease prevention, emergencies and first aid, saving money, avoiding scams and legal trouble, unemployment, business, relationships, parenting, travel and practical skills.

The original contains **498 recommendations in 31 chapters**. Each explains what it costs, what you may gain, how strong the evidence is, and where the claim comes from. Sources are journal articles and official documents.

> **Translation in progress.** The introduction and interface are available in English and Russian. The chapters and source-verification records are still in Chinese. Chapter links below open that original text. Laws, benefits, prices and emergency services refer to China unless a passage explicitly says otherwise.

## How to read this guide

- **Browse by topic:** start with a chapter below. Recommendations within each chapter are ordered by the author's assessment of benefit relative to cost.
- **Use filters:** choose a chapter, evidence grade, expected benefit and how much money, time or willpower you want to spend. Multiple choices within one group mean “or”; different groups are combined with “and”. Until the chapters are translated, searching their text requires Chinese terms or the original scientific abbreviations.
- **Read the plain-language explanation:** each recommendation has a short explanation alongside the detailed results, numbers and confidence intervals.
- **Check the caveats:** disputed claims and claims awaiting verification are marked. Translating a claim does not verify its source.

## Four resources

| Resource | What the recommendations cover |
| --- | --- |
| Lifespan | More years of life; fewer avoidable deaths. |
| Time and energy | Less wasted time, attention and physical effort. |
| Money | Fewer unnecessary expenses and better use of limited funds. |
| Personal freedom | Avoiding legal consequences caused by not knowing the rules. |

Each recommendation explains **what it costs and what you may gain**. Mortality, time, money and legal consequences are measured separately, not converted into a single score.

The original also distinguishes who benefits: yourself; your spouse and direct relatives; friends, colleagues and other relatives; strangers. For each group, the author considers what you might gain, including help in return, and what risks you might face. Benefits for different groups are not added together. Whether to help a stranger is a personal decision; the guide describes possible benefits and risks.

## Evidence grades

These are the author's categories, not the GRADE framework.

| Grade | Meaning in this guide |
| --- | --- |
| A | Quantifiable findings from a meta-analysis, large cohort or randomized controlled trial; in legal and benefits chapters, verified official figures. |
| B | Research supports the claim, but the size of the effect is unclear, or there is only one study or a small sample. This category also includes some official rules under which benefit amounts vary by region. |
| C | The author’s experience or a commonly held view without direct supporting research. |

The original reports **323 A-grade, 126 B-grade and 49 C-grade recommendations**, **45 disputed entries** and **39 TODO markers**. Translations retain the evidence grades, caveats and links. Claims awaiting verification keep that label.

## Benefit relative to cost

The evidence grade indicates how well a claim is supported. Benefit relative to cost is **the author’s assessment** of whether the result is worth the resources it takes. The author assesses this separately from the evidence grade.

| Category | Rule used in the original |
| --- | --- |
| Very high | Large benefit with no cost in money, time or willpower. |
| High | Large benefit with low costs, or medium benefit with zero costs. |
| Moderate | Other combinations; this does not mean the action is not worth taking. |

The author assesses the size of each benefit separately: relative mortality reduction; money saved; legal consequences avoided; or time saved. Do not compare a 12% reduction in mortality with saving 500 yuan a year as though they were the same measure.
