# 8. Protect your freedom and property

> Complete translation: the introduction and all 35 entries. The numbered laws and penalties describe China; regional notes are separate. Clarifications are labeled. New reference checks: September 21, 2026.

This chapter deals with money and personal liberty: legal liability, losses you can avoid, and your procedural rights. It does not convert these outcomes into years of life. Article references point to the official texts used for verification, with qualifications where needed. The order puts potentially costly incidents that are easy to prevent first, measures needing advance preparation in the middle, and experience-based advice last.

### 1. After a road crash, stop, help, and call the police—do not flee
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: No application fee. Stop, switch on your hazard lights, help injured people, and call the police. Allow time for traffic officers to arrive; 1–2 hours is a rough estimate, not a maximum. For a minor crash in China with no injuries and no disagreement about the facts, document the scene and record names, phone numbers, license plates, driver-license numbers, insurance-certificate numbers, and points of impact. Where the self-settlement rules apply, both parties sign the record before clearing the road and discussing compensation.
- In plain language: Fleeing a crash in China normally leaves you responsible for the whole accident unless evidence of the other party's fault reduces that responsibility. A criminal hit-and-run can lead to license revocation and a lifetime ban on obtaining another license. Where the traffic-accident offense applies, fleeing carries 3–7 years in prison; causing a death through flight carries more than 7 years. Commercial insurance can exclude the loss, leaving you with a large bill. Stay, arrange help, and report the crash.
- Benefit: Under Chinese rules, the fleeing party bears full accident responsibility, subject to reduction when the other party's fault is proved. Criminal hit-and-run can bring license revocation and a lifetime driving ban. The traffic-accident offense carries 3–7 years for flight and more than 7 years where flight causes death. The 2020 commercial motor-insurance model terms exclude vehicle-damage and third-party claims involving flight or intentional destruction or falsification of the scene or destruction of evidence. An uninsured compensation bill could reach hundreds of thousands of CNY; this is an illustrative Chinese loss, not a fixed penalty or a measured average.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2021). Road Traffic Safety Law, 2021 revision, Articles 70 and 101, reproduced by Beijing traffic police. <https://jtgl.beijing.gov.cn/jgj/jgxx/flfg/fl/205308/index.html>; State Council (2017). Implementing Regulations for the Road Traffic Safety Law, Order No. 405, 2017 revision, Articles 86 and 92. <http://xzfg.moj.gov.cn/front/law/detail?LawID=75>; National People's Congress (1997). Criminal Law, Article 133. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/201802/t20180206_364975.shtml>; Insurance Association of China (2020). Commercial Motor Insurance Model Terms, 2020 edition, Articles 9 and 22. <https://www.iachina.cn/art/2020/9/4/art_24_104621.html>
- Notes: When someone is injured, arrange urgent help and notify the police; if rescuing someone requires moving objects at the scene, mark their positions. Saying you did not notice the impact does not automatically settle whether your departure was flight; if unsure, report it. **Legal clarification:** [Beijing police explain](https://gaj.beijing.gov.cn/xxfb/fjjx/202204/t20220422_2686215.html) the distinction between noncriminal flight and criminal hit-and-run; a lifetime ban is not the automatic consequence of every minor incident. The insurance text is an industry model: check your own policy and applicable law. Compulsory third-party insurance is a separate scheme. The wait can exceed the planning estimate. **US/Europe:** reporting, moving vehicles, exchanging details, and insurance duties depend on the state or country. For injuries or immediate danger, call the local emergency service; follow local police and insurer instructions rather than applying Chinese deadlines or penalties.

### 2. If you have sent money to a scammer, contact the bank and police immediately
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Reporting the scam does not require paying a recovery intermediary. Give the transfer time, amount, and recipient account; preserve transfer screenshots and messages. In China, report a completed fraud to 110 and contact your bank or payment provider immediately.
- In plain language: Act as soon as you notice the fraud. Chinese police can use emergency payment stops and rapid freezing procedures, and banks and payment providers must cooperate. Recovery becomes harder when money moves through several accounts; do not spend the first minutes investigating or confronting the caller yourself. Report even if more time has passed. The Chinese 96110 line provides fraud warnings and advice; take a warning seriously, but do not treat a displayed number as proof of identity or transfer money on a caller's instructions.
- Benefit: China's Anti-Telecom and Online Fraud Law provides for prompt inquiries into implicated funds, emergency payment stops, and rapid freezing, with cooperation from banks and payment institutions. Frozen funds can be returned through the prescribed process. Earlier reporting can help prevent further transfers; no recovery percentage is established here. The national 96110 line supports warnings and intervention. The law took effect in December 2022.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2022). Anti-Telecom and Online Fraud Law, Articles 20 and 34. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202209/t20220902_575631.shtml>; Fujian Public Security Department (2023). Answer calls from 96110; provincial police guidance. <http://gat.fujian.gov.cn/ztzl/fjjffpzxrx/spjq/202303/t20230306_6126156.htm>
- Notes: The law establishes payment-stop and freezing mechanisms, not a guaranteed refund. Recovery depends in part on whether funds remain available when the report is made. The National Anti-Fraud Center app and 96110 guidance come from Fujian police because the central Ministry of Public Security page was unavailable. Requests to move money to a “safe account” from supposed police, prosecutors, or court staff are scams. **Correction:** [official Guangdong guidance](https://www.gd.gov.cn/gdywdt/bmdt/content/post_2859267.html) distinguishes 96110 warnings/advice from 110 crime reports. There is no established universal deadline of a few dozen minutes after which reporting is pointless. **US/Europe:** contact the bank or payment provider through a trusted channel and ask whether the transfer can be stopped or reversed. The [FTC guide](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/what-do-if-you-were-scammed) covers payment-specific steps and US reporting. [Europol lists national cybercrime reporting routes](https://www.europol.europa.eu/report-a-crime/report-cybercrime-online); reports go to the relevant national or local authority, not directly to Europol. Refund rights depend on the payment and jurisdiction.

### 3. Do not trust an unexpected caller: protect your details and verify every payment request
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No purchase is needed. Remember four habits: question unexpected calls, keep sensitive information private, avoid unsolicited links, and verify transfers. If someone asks you to transfer money, share your screen, install an unfamiliar app, open a link, or read out a verification code, end the call and contact the organization through an independently checked official number. In China, the National Anti-Fraud Center app is another official prevention tool.
- In plain language: Hang up before acting on a financial request, then verify it yourself. Scammers pose as police, prosecutors, court staff, customer support, managers, or a platform's fraud team. Their stories include “cooperate with an investigation,” “cancel a membership,” paid fake-order tasks, or inside investment information. Recognizing the setup before paying costs less than trying to recover the money afterward.
- Benefit: China's law requires public education about telecom and online fraud and warning systems involving police, finance, telecom, and internet authorities. People identified as potential victims should receive timely warnings. The national 96110 line is used for this intervention; do not dismiss a genuine warning, and independently verify any accompanying request. Prevention avoids the need to seek recovery, whose outcome depends partly on whether money is still available when reported; see entry 2. The national law has applied since December 2022.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2022). Anti-Telecom and Online Fraud Law, Articles 8, 20, and 34. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202209/t20220902_575631.shtml>; Fujian Public Security Department (2023). Answer calls from 96110; includes the four-habit prevention slogan and a warning about scam calls beginning with 192. <http://gat.fujian.gov.cn/ztzl/fjjffpzxrx/spjq/202303/t20230306_6126156.htm>
- Notes: Grade B reflects official prevention guidance, not a measured reduction in fraud among people who memorize it. The Ministry of Public Security website, mps.gov.cn, was unavailable during the source check; the provincial reproduction is used. TODO: verify the ministry or National Anti-Fraud Center original page. Impersonated officials, support teams, managers, and fraud teams use different stories to push the same unsafe actions. **Clarification:** a phone prefix, caller ID, or an official-looking app is not identity proof; a 192 prefix alone does not establish fraud. Outside China, use your bank's and national authorities' official channels; the Chinese app and 96110 are not foreign reporting services.

### 4. A familiar face or voice is not identity proof; hang up and call a number you already trust
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: One extra call and a few minutes. Limit public close-up face videos and long voice recordings that could be copied.
- In plain language: A convincing face or voice can be faked. Chinese police guidance suggests asking for a continuous head turn or for a hand to cover the whole face and move away quickly; artifacts may appear, but passing that test proves nothing. The stronger check is to hang up and call the person's old number already saved in your contacts, or confirm through someone you both know. If the caller refuses and says the signal is bad or they are in a meeting, do not send money until you have independent confirmation. AI-content labels also cannot authenticate the caller: a scammer may omit them.
- Benefit: The Ministry of Public Security's cyber police describe three checks: movement, voice details, and independent contact. Head turns or brief full-face occlusion may expose stuttering, blur, or distortion; unusual breathing pauses, emotional inflection, mechanical sound, or background noise may also raise suspicion. The central step is to call a familiar number independently—not the suspicious caller's number—or ask a trusted mutual contact. The practical rule is to question video and voice and verify through another channel. Chinese deep-synthesis rules prohibit fraudulent use and require prominent labels for covered face-generation, face-replacement, synthetic-voice, and voice-imitation services. The 2025 labeling rules prohibit malicious removal, alteration, forgery, or concealment of labels; absence of a label does not prove authenticity. In one reported investigation, police uncovered a 14-person group, more than 50,000 synthesized moving-face videos, more than 500,000 personal-information records, and seized 60 cloud servers. The 2025 labeling measures took effect on September 1, 2025.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Ministry of Public Security Cyber Security Bureau (2025). Seeing is not believing: beware new AI scams, reproduced by Fujian police. <http://gat.fujian.gov.cn/ztzl/fjjffpzxrx/spjq/202509/t20250922_7013008.htm>; Cyberspace Administration of China, Ministry of Industry and Information Technology, and Ministry of Public Security (2022). Deep Synthesis Internet Information Service Provisions, Articles 6, 16, and 17; effective January 10, 2023. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2022-12/12/content_5731431.htm>; Cyberspace Administration of China and three other authorities (2025). Measures for Labeling AI-Generated and Synthetic Content, Article 10; effective September 1, 2025. <https://www.cac.gov.cn/2025-03/14/c_1743654684782215.htm>; Ministry of Public Security Criminal Investigation Bureau and CCTV News (2026). Police uncover more than 50,000 synthesized moving-face videos: who is copying your face?, reproduced by Fujian police. <http://gat.fujian.gov.cn/ztzl/fjjffpzxrx/spjq/202603/t20260330_7117146.htm>
- Notes: Grade B reflects official advice and a case report, not a measured reduction in fraud from these checks. Use the old number in your own contacts, not caller ID or a new number sent in chat. Refusal to perform a movement or join a video call, including excuses about a meeting or poor signal, is a reason to stop the transaction and verify independently; it is not proof of a crime. **Technical clarification:** movement and voice artifacts are clues, not reliable authentication tests. Models change, and even independent contact must use a channel you actually trust. Chinese deep-synthesis duties began under the 2023 rules; September 2025 is the start of the additional labeling measures, not the first labeling obligation. For money already sent, see entry 2; for protecting face and voice data, see chapter 14, currently in Chinese. The central ministry website returned 521 during the earlier check, so the cyber and criminal-investigation guidance is cited through Fujian's official reproductions. These Chinese labeling laws do not establish US or European duties.

### 5. If you are accused or summoned for questioning, get a lawyer and keep your records
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=多 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: Ask about the fee before hiring counsel. Local examples checked September 21, 2026: the [New York City Bar referral service](https://portal.311.nyc.gov/article/?kanumber=KA-01040) offers an initial consultation of up to 30 minutes for 35 USD or free, depending on the case. In Germany, the default fee ceiling for a consumer's first consultation without a separate fee agreement is 190 EUR, plus VAT and necessary expenses ([German Federal Bar](https://www.brak.de/service/verbraucherinformationen/was-kostet-das/)). These are consultation figures, not the cost of defending a whole case. If you cannot afford a lawyer, ask a legal-aid provider about eligibility. During questioning in China, you may refuse questions unrelated to the case; get legal advice about the questions and your procedural position.
- In plain language: In a Chinese criminal investigation, you can appoint a lawyer from your first interrogation or when a compulsory measure is imposed. A detention center generally must arrange a properly requested lawyer visit within 48 hours, and the conversation must not be monitored; certain national-security and terrorism investigations require permission first. No one may force you to incriminate yourself. You may refuse unrelated questions, but Chinese law also requires truthful answers to questions about the case. A summons or compulsory appearance normally lasts no more than 12 hours; the 24-hour exception requires both an especially serious, complex case and a need for detention or arrest. Repeated summonses must not be used to disguise detention. Avoid private contact with the complainant or their family, and discuss any settlement with your lawyer: what you say or agree to could be interpreted as an admission. Preserve messages, call records, and travel records that may support your account.
- Benefit: In China, the right to appoint defense counsel begins with the first interrogation or a compulsory measure; during the investigation, only a lawyer may act as appointed defense counsel. The ordinary limit for arranging a lawyer visit is 48 hours after a request with the required documents, and visits must not be monitored. The permission exception for national-security and terrorism cases still applies. Compelled self-incrimination is prohibited, and questions unrelated to the case may be refused. A summons or compulsory appearance is limited to 12 hours, or 24 hours in the serious, complex cases requiring detention or arrest described above; repeated summonses cannot substitute for detention. Fabricating accusations to have someone criminally prosecuted, in serious circumstances, carries up to 3 years in prison, criminal detention, or noncustodial public supervision; serious consequences carry 3–10 years. State compensation may be available after detention or arrest followed by withdrawal of the case, a decision not to prosecute, or acquittal, provided the statutory conditions are met. Compensation for loss of liberty is calculated per day using the previous year's national average daily wage for employees.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2018). Criminal Procedure Law, 2018 amendment, Articles 34, 35, 39, 52, 119, and 120. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/zdgz/201810/t20181027_396818.shtml>; National People's Congress (1997). Criminal Law, Article 243. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/201802/t20180206_364975.shtml>; Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2012). State Compensation Law, 2012 amendment, Articles 17 and 33, reproduced by the National Bureau of Statistics. <https://www.stats.gov.cn/gk/tjfg/xgfxfg/202503/t20250306_1958899.html>
- Notes: Keep messages, call records, and travel records intact; deleting them can destroy evidence in your favor. Do not approach the other party or their family to negotiate privately. A settlement is not automatically a confession, but legal advice helps you avoid damaging statements and unlawful contact. The false-accusation offense does not cover an honest mistake or an inaccurate report without the required intent. These protections apply regardless of the accused person's gender. Entries 31, 32, and 33 of [this chapter in Chinese](../../book/08-别把自己搭进去.md) cover fabricated accusations, acquittal for insufficient evidence and review proceedings, and daily compensation after wrongful detention. **Legal clarifications:** the [2018 Criminal Procedure Law reproduced by Shenzhen police](https://www.szns.gov.cn/nsnsgafj/gkmlpt/content/11/11403/mpost_11403806.html) sets the visit exception, the additional condition for 24 hours, and the duty to answer case-related questions truthfully; it does not create a general right to have counsel answer every question for you. Under the [Supreme People's Court's compensation filing rules](https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/3663.html), an initial detention and a formal arrest have different compensation conditions: for detention, illegality or exceeding the lawful time limit matters. Release or acquittal alone does not guarantee compensation; statutory exclusions also apply. **US/Europe:** rights during questioning, appointed counsel, and compensation depend on the jurisdiction and procedural status. Use a local criminal-defense lawyer; [chapter 7, entry 3](07_when_money_is_tight.md) lists legal-aid starting points.

### 6. If you have been drinking, do not drive—even an electric vehicle or “just to move the car”
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: Arrange a sober driver before a gathering, or pay for a taxi or a service that drives your car home. Taxi illustrations checked September 21, 2026: in New York City, a 3-mile metered ride at normal speed works out to about 15 USD before time-of-day or Manhattan surcharges, tolls, waiting, and tip (3 USD start + 15 distance units at 0.70 USD + 0.50 USD MTA charge + 1 USD improvement charge; [TLC rates](https://home4.nyc.gov/site/tlc/passengers/taxi-fare.page)). In Berlin, 5 km works out to about 17.90 EUR before waiting and other applicable extras (4.30 + 3 × 2.80 + 2 × 2.60; [city rates](https://www.berlin.de/en/public-transportation/1756978-2913840-taxi-phone-numbers-fares-rules.en.html)). These are calculations for specified trips, not live quotes or designated-driver prices. Check the actual fare before booking.
- In plain language: In China, driving a motor vehicle after drinking can mean a 6-month license suspension and a 1,000–2,000 CNY fine. A repeat offense after a previous penalty can bring administrative detention and license revocation. Driving while legally intoxicated can bring revocation, a 5-year ban on getting another license, and criminal liability. A conviction can affect official background checks and some occupations. Rules for commercial transport vehicles are stricter: intoxicated driving brings a 10-year license ban and a permanent ban on driving those vehicles. Commercial motor policies can exclude losses involving alcohol or drugs. Moving a car only a short distance is still driving, although criminal liability depends on the legal conditions and circumstances; see the clarification below.
- Benefit: Chinese motor-vehicle rules provide for a 6-month suspension and a 1,000–2,000 CNY fine for driving after drinking. Repeating it after a previous penalty brings up to 10 days of administrative detention, another 1,000–2,000 CNY fine, and license revocation. Driving while legally intoxicated brings revocation and a 5-year ban on obtaining a new license; where the dangerous-driving offense is established, the penalty is criminal detention plus a fine. Driving a commercial transport vehicle after drinking brings 15 days of administrative detention, a 5,000 CNY fine, revocation, and a 5-year ban; intoxicated driving brings a 10-year ban and permanent exclusion from commercial transport driving, as well as criminal liability where the offense is established. An alcohol-related major crash that constitutes a crime brings a lifetime driving ban. The 2020 commercial motor-insurance model terms exclude vehicle-damage and third-party claims involving driving after alcohol or illegal drugs.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2021). Road Traffic Safety Law, 2021 revision, Article 91, reproduced by Beijing traffic police. <https://jtgl.beijing.gov.cn/jgj/jgxx/flfg/fl/205308/index.html>; Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2015). Criminal Law Amendment IX, Article 8, inserting the relevant provisions of Article 133-1. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/201802/t20180205_364562.shtml>; Insurance Association of China (2020). Commercial Motor Insurance Model Terms, 2020 edition, Articles 9 and 22. <https://www.iachina.cn/art/2020/9/4/art_24_104621.html>
- Notes: A criminal conviction for intoxicated driving can affect background checks and eligibility for some occupations. The legal distinction between driving after drinking and driving while intoxicated uses blood-alcohol thresholds; the full applicable national standard and its numerical boundaries have not been checked for this entry. Do not estimate your legal status from how sober you feel. **Legal clarification:** the [2023 joint guidance on intoxicated driving](https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/xwfbh/wsfbt/202312/t20231218_637161.shtml) requires the legal elements of a motor vehicle, a road, and intoxication. An electric car or electric motorcycle is not legally interchangeable with every electric bicycle. The guidance allows some negligible cases, including qualifying short vehicle moves without aggravating circumstances, to be treated as noncriminal; minor cases can also receive other lenient dispositions. “Just moving the car” is therefore neither a universal exemption nor automatic proof of a criminal conviction. The insurance wording is an industry model, not every policy or the separate compulsory-insurance scheme. **US/Europe:** alcohol limits, vehicle classifications, penalties, and insurance rules vary by state or country. The practical decision is to arrange a sober ride, not to apply Chinese thresholds or penalties abroad.

### 7. Do not lend your bank card, SIM, or payment account for someone else's transfers: money-mule work is not a safe side job
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: No purchase or fee. Refuse offers to pay you for letting money pass through your account or for handing over a card or SIM.
- In plain language: In China, unlawfully selling, renting out, or lending bank accounts, phone cards, payment accounts, or internet accounts is itself punishable. Illegal proceeds can be confiscated, with a fine of 1–10 times those proceeds. If there are no proceeds or they are below 20,000 CNY, the fine can instead be up to 200,000 CNY. Serious cases can also bring up to 15 days of administrative detention. Other measures may include credit-record entries and account restrictions. Knowingly helping online crime through payment processing, in serious circumstances, can lead to up to 3 years in prison. Investigators can consider circumstantial evidence of knowledge, including a fee you received or an implausible transaction volume; those facts must be assessed in context.
- Benefit: China's Anti-Telecom and Online Fraud Law prohibits unlawful sale, rental, or lending of phone cards, bank accounts, payment accounts, and internet accounts. It provides for confiscation of illegal proceeds and fines of 1–10 times those proceeds; where proceeds are absent or below 20,000 CNY, the maximum fine is 200,000 CNY. Serious cases can also bring up to 15 days of administrative detention. Additional measures may include credit-record entries, limits on account functions, and suspension of transactions that do not take place at a bank counter, under the applicable conditions. Knowingly providing payment-settlement or other assistance for online crime, in serious circumstances, is a separate criminal offense punishable by up to 3 years in prison or criminal detention, with a fine, or a fine alone.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2022). Anti-Telecom and Online Fraud Law, Articles 31 and 44. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202209/t20220902_575631.shtml>; Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2015). Criminal Law Amendment IX, Article 29, inserting Article 287-2. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/201802/t20180205_364562.shtml>
- Notes: Receiving a fee and handling an obviously abnormal volume of transfers can support a finding that you knew what was happening; neither is an automatic substitute for examining the evidence. If your card receives criminal proceeds, it may be frozen and other restrictions may follow even without a criminal conviction. **Legal clarification:** [Articles 31 and 44](https://www.samr.gov.cn/zw/zfxxgk/fdzdgknr/bgt/art/2023/art_4d2f14016988480e979f4cd49251e07a.html) concern unlawful transfers or use of accounts; they should not be read as banning every properly authorized family or business arrangement. The alternative fine ceiling also covers proceeds below 20,000 CNY, not only cases with no proceeds. Whether criminal knowledge and serious circumstances are established is a separate question. **US/Europe:** the Chinese offenses and fine amounts do not carry over to other jurisdictions. If someone offers a job that requires receiving and forwarding strangers' money through your personal account, decline it and contact your bank if transfers have already occurred.

### 8. Check your credit report for loans or cards you did not open; China provides two free reports a year
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No fee within the applicable free-report entitlement. Allow about 10 minutes for a straightforward online request; registration, identity checks, and delivery can take longer. Free-report rules for the US and Europe are described below.
- In plain language: In China, you are entitled to two free copies of your credit report each year. Checking can reveal loans or cards opened with stolen identity details before a rejected mortgage application exposes a problem that has been building for years. Negative information is generally retained for 5 years after the underlying adverse conduct or event ends, then must be deleted. If you dispute an error, the credit bureau or information provider must investigate, deal with it, and send a written response within 20 days. Use the People's Bank of China's official Credit Reference Center or an authorized bank channel. Avoid unfamiliar “credit-check” apps that ask for sensitive data.
- Benefit: China's Credit Reporting Industry Regulations give individuals two free personal credit reports annually. Negative information is retained for 5 years from the end of the adverse conduct or event and must be deleted after those 5 years. If information is wrong or missing, you can challenge it. The credit bureau or information provider must mark it as disputed, investigate and handle the dispute, and respond in writing within 20 days of receiving it.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: State Council (2013). Credit Reporting Industry Regulations, Order No. 631, Articles 16, 17, and 25. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2013-01/29/content_2303.htm>
- Notes: Identity misuse can remain unnoticed for years, until you apply for a mortgage or another loan. In China, go through the official Credit Reference Center or an authorized mobile-banking service. Unfamiliar third-party apps can collect the very information you are trying to protect; not every third-party service is fraudulent, but check who operates it and what authorization it has. **Clarification:** the [regulations](https://www.samr.gov.cn/zw/zfxxgk/fdzdgknr/bgt/art/2023/art_f06d5be45fcf464abe3fbdb9b2b3b37c.html) start the 5-year clock when the adverse conduct or event ends, not simply when a loan was opened. The 20-day deadline is for investigation and a written answer, not a guarantee that any disputed debt will be erased. **US:** the [FTC](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/free-credit-reports) directs consumers to [AnnualCreditReport.com](https://www.annualcreditreport.com/) for free reports from Equifax, Experian, and TransUnion. Each bureau currently offers a free weekly report through that site; this is more frequent than the statutory annual entitlement. **EU:** the [European Commission](https://commission.europa.eu/law/law-topic/data-protection/information-business-and-organisations/dealing-requests-individuals_en) explains the right to a free copy of personal data held by an organization. This can help you inspect data held by a credit bureau, but it is not a uniform Europe-wide entitlement to two commercial credit reports per year. Excessive or manifestly unfounded requests can have different rules; check the bureau and your country's procedure. Regional information checked September 21, 2026.

### 9. If a confrontation escalates, get to safety and call the police; do not start a fight
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: No purchase needed. Resist insults and shoving, move toward other people if you can do so safely, and call the police. Nearby security cameras may help preserve evidence.
- In plain language: In China, assault ordinarily brings 5–10 days of administrative detention and a 500–1,000 CNY fine. Group assaults, attacks on certain vulnerable people, and repeated or multiple-victim assaults carry 10–15 days and a 1,000–2,000 CNY fine. Intentional injury that reaches the criminal threshold can bring up to 3 years in prison; serious injury carries 3–10 years. Joining a fight can make a self-defense claim much harder. Move to safety, call the police, and preserve footage rather than escalating. You may defend yourself against an ongoing unlawful attack, but once the danger has actually ended, chasing and hitting someone is retaliation. Who struck first is not the sole legal test.
- Benefit: Under Chinese law, the ordinary administrative penalty for assault or intentional injury is 5–10 days of detention plus 500–1,000 CNY. The aggravated range is 10–15 days plus 1,000–2,000 CNY for a group assault; an assault on a disabled person, a pregnant person, someone under 14, or someone aged 70 or older; repeated assaults; or one assault against multiple people. Group brawling that falls under the public-order provision can bring up to 15 days of detention, with an additional fine of up to 2,000 CNY. Criminal intentional injury ordinarily carries up to 3 years in prison, criminal detention, or noncustodial public supervision; serious injury carries 3–10 years. A mutual fight is distinguished from self-defense by its circumstances and intent. A response can generally qualify as defense if the other person initiates clearly excessive violence, or continues attacking while you try to avoid the conflict. Excessive defense requires both clearly exceeding what is necessary and causing serious harm; even then, punishment must be reduced or waived. These are nationwide Chinese rules; the revised Public Security Administration Punishments Law took effect on January 1, 2026.

### 16. Put a loan in writing; guarantee someone’s debt only if you are prepared to repay it
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No special purchase needed. Write down the lender, borrower, amount, interest rate, term, and repayment method, and have both parties sign. Use a traceable transfer rather than handing over cash; any transfer fee depends on the payment service.
- In plain language: A complete written debt acknowledgment makes it easier to prove what was agreed: name the lender and borrower, record the amount, interest rate, term, and repayment method, obtain both signatures, and keep the transfer record. Under an ordinary general guarantee, the creditor normally has to sue or arbitrate against the borrower and try enforcement before requiring you to pay. With a joint and several guarantee, the creditor can pursue you directly when the covered debt falls due and is not paid, or another agreed trigger occurs. Before signing, decide whether you are willing and able to repay that person’s debt yourself.
- Benefit: China’s Civil Code generally requires a loan agreement to be in writing, while allowing individuals lending to each other to agree otherwise. If a guarantee agreement does not specify the guarantee type, or is unclear, the default is a general guarantee. Its guarantor normally may refuse payment until the main dispute has been heard in court or arbitration and lawful enforcement against the debtor’s property has failed to satisfy the debt. A joint and several guarantee does not provide that sequence of protection: the creditor can claim directly against the guarantor within the guarantee’s scope when the statutory or agreed trigger occurs. These rules apply in China and have exceptions described below.

### 17. Check the deadline for your claim: China generally allows 3 years for civil claims and 1 year for labor arbitration

- Cost: No special purchase needed to keep a digital record. Request payment through a traceable channel such as WeChat, text message, or email; registered mail is another option, with postage charged locally. Keep the request, the recipient’s identity, and evidence of delivery.
- In plain language: A documented demand can be one of the least costly ways to protect a claim. In China, the general civil limitation period is 3 years from when you knew or should have known that your rights were harmed and who owed the obligation. If it expires, the court does not apply the time bar on its own, but the other side can raise it as a defense. The debt is not erased; compulsory recovery may become unavailable. A qualifying payment demand received, or deemed received, while the period is still running can interrupt it and start a fresh period. A message sent after expiry does not by itself revive the claim. Labor disputes have a separate general period of 1 year. A wage-arrears claim made while the employment relationship continues is exempt from that one-year limit; after employment ends, apply within 1 year of its end.
- Benefit: Article 188 of China’s Civil Code sets a general civil limitation period of 3 years, unless another law provides otherwise, running from actual or expected knowledge of both the infringement and the liable party. The general outer limit is 20 years from the infringement; a court may extend it on request in exceptional circumstances. After expiry, the obligor may raise a limitation defense, but the court may not apply it of its own initiative. The underlying obligation remains. Four events can interrupt the running period: a demand for performance; the obligor’s agreement to perform; filing a lawsuit or applying for arbitration; and another event with equivalent legal effect. The period starts afresh from interruption or the conclusion of the relevant proceedings. Where the same debt is payable in agreed installments, the period starts when the final installment falls due. Four categories are exempt: claims to stop an infringement, remove an obstruction, or eliminate danger; claims by holders of rights in immovable property or registered movable property for its return; claims for child, elder, or other family maintenance; and other claims exempted by law. Labor arbitration generally has a 1-year period from when the claimant knew or should have known of the infringement. Wage arrears during an ongoing employment relationship are exempt from that limit, but a claim after termination must be brought within 1 year of termination. These rules concern China.

### 18. If you are sued or face enforcement, disclose your assets and pay what you can; do not hide property with relatives, friends, or a company
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: No purchase needed to prepare an asset report. Follow the court’s requirements: in China, the report covers your current assets and those held during the year before you received the enforcement notice. Pay what you can; if you cannot pay in full, discuss a settlement or installments with the creditor seeking enforcement.
- In plain language: Moving your home into a company’s name or sending your money to relatives is not a way around a judgment. In China, hiding assets to prevent enforcement can amount to criminal refusal to comply with a judgment or order. The ordinary maximum prison term is 3 years; particularly serious cases carry 3–7 years. A relative, friend, or company that knowingly conspires to help can face liability too, and unlawfully disposed-of assets can still be recovered. Refusing to report assets or reporting them falsely can bring an individual a fine of up to 100,000 CNY or detention of up to 15 days. Genuine inability to comply is different from deliberate refusal: necessary living expenses for you and your dependents must be excluded when assessing your ability to comply. In a minor case, full or partial performance before public prosecution may lead to a decision not to prosecute; performance before the first-instance judgment may support a lighter penalty or exemption from punishment.
- Benefit: China’s 2002 interpretation of Criminal Law Article 313 expressly covers hiding, transferring, deliberately destroying, giving away, or selling assets at a clearly unreasonable low price, where this prevents enforcement and meets the test of a serious refusal despite an ability to comply. The 2024 judicial interpretation adds ten specified circumstances, including sham settlements or transfers and malicious depletion of assets by buying property at a clearly unreasonable high price or guaranteeing another person’s debt. The enforcement-prevention and seriousness requirements still matter. The ordinary penalty is up to 3 years in prison, criminal detention, or a fine; particularly serious cases carry 3–7 years plus a fine. Examples include sham litigation, arbitration, or notarization that prevents enforcement; organizing a crowd to storm an enforcement site; surrounding, detaining, or beating enforcement officers and preventing enforcement; or refusal that causes the creditor to die by suicide, injure themselves, or suffer another serious consequence. A third party who knowingly conspires with the debtor and helps prevent enforcement can be an accomplice. Unlawfully disposed-of assets must be recovered, or restitution ordered, as required by law. Hiding or transferring assets after receiving notice of the lawsuit but before the judgment takes effect can also qualify if, after the judgment becomes effective, the person still refuses to comply and the remaining criminal conditions are met. On the civil side, a creditor may ask a court to set aside gifts or clearly underpriced transfers that impair recovery, subject to the statutory conditions. Refusing to disclose assets or making a false disclosure can bring an individual a fine of up to 100,000 CNY and detention of up to 15 days. These are Chinese rules; the 2024 criminal interpretation took effect nationwide on December 1, 2024.

### 19. If you face spending restrictions or an enforcement listing, check the exact grounds and apply to correct mistakes
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No purchase needed to gather records. Ask the enforcement court which ground it used and prepare proof of payment or adequate security. Apply separately for each otherwise prohibited expense that is necessary for living or business, and obtain approval before incurring it.
- In plain language: In China, the list of judgment defaulters and court-ordered spending restrictions are different measures, and neither is the same as a credit report. A listed debtor must generally also face spending restrictions, but a debtor can face restrictions without being listed. There are six grounds for listing. The 2-year term applies to grounds 2–6, not automatically to every listing. Removal within 3 working days is required in specified cases, with a separate rule for fixed-term listings; active compliance or correction may support early removal. Spending restrictions cover flights, soft-sleeper train travel, expensive hotels, property purchases, vacations, costly private schooling for children, and other specified expenses. If a prohibited expense is necessary for living or business, seek the court’s permission for that expense. Effective security or the enforcing creditor’s consent may allow the court to lift restrictions.
- Benefit: The six Chinese listing grounds are: ability to comply but refusal to do so; obstruction or resistance through fabricated evidence, violence, or threats; evasion through sham litigation, sham arbitration, or hidden or transferred assets; breach of asset-reporting duties; breach of a spending-restriction order; and unjustified failure to perform an enforcement settlement. Adequate effective security, or sufficient assets already seized, detained, or frozen to cover the debt, prevents listing under the first ground; minors must not be listed. For grounds 2–6, the term is 2 years. Serious violent or threatening obstruction, or multiple breaches of trust, can extend it by 1–3 years. Article 10 provides removal within 3 working days in seven circumstances: full performance or completed enforcement; full performance of an enforcement settlement; the creditor’s written removal request approved by the court; closure of the current enforcement procedure followed by at least two unsuccessful asset searches and no effective asset leads; a stay because of adjudicatory review or bankruptcy; an order refusing enforcement; or an order terminating enforcement. **Fixed-term exception:** that seven-ground paragraph does not govern fixed-term listings; they must be removed within 3 working days after expiry, while Article 2 allows discretionary early removal for active performance or correction. Spending restrictions cover the eight expressly listed categories: flights, soft sleepers, and second-class or better ship cabins; high spending at star-rated hotels, nightclubs, or golf courses; buying real estate or building, extending, or luxuriously renovating a home; renting high-end offices, hotels, or apartments for business; buying vehicles unnecessary for business; travel and vacations; children attending high-fee private schools; and high-premium insurance or investment products. The amended ninth category also includes all seats on G-series high-speed trains, first-class or better seats on other electric multiple-unit trains, and other spending unnecessary for living or work. A necessary expense requires advance court approval. The court may lift restrictions after effective security or the creditor’s consent; after full performance of the effective legal instrument, it must lift them promptly. These rules apply in China.

### 21. Think before making a large gift to a partner or spouse; you usually cannot demand it back afterward
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No purchase needed. Before transferring a large amount, decide whether it is a loan or a gift. If it is a loan, record the agreement in writing and label the transfer “loan.”
- In plain language: Giving someone money is not the same as lending it. In China, an ordinary gift can generally be revoked before ownership passes; afterward, changing your mind is not enough. Article 663 provides three grounds for revocation: the recipient seriously infringes your or a close relative’s lawful rights, fails to perform a duty to support you, or fails to meet an obligation in the gift agreement. Other legal grounds for challenging a transaction may still apply. Before sending a large amount, make your intention clear. A written loan acknowledgment, a transfer labeled “loan,” and a message such as “I’m lending you this money” are more useful than arguing afterward.
- Benefit: Chinese Civil Code Article 658 generally permits revocation before rights to the gifted property pass, with exceptions for notarized gifts and certain public-interest or moral-duty gifts that cannot lawfully be revoked at will. Article 663 permits revocation on the three grounds of serious infringement of the donor’s or a close relative’s lawful rights, failure to perform a duty of support toward the donor, or failure to perform an agreed gift obligation. Small gifts or payments for holidays, birthdays, and other meaningful occasions, and ordinary spending to express or strengthen affection, are not treated as marriage-related customary gifts called caili. They are not returned under the caili rules merely because the relationship ends. These are Chinese legal distinctions.

### 22. Keep a clear transfer record for marriage-related gifts; demands for property through marriage can be challenged
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No special purchase needed; transfer fees depend on the payment service. Record the purpose of caili, the customary marriage payment; sanjin, the traditional three gold jewelry items; and gaikoufei, the ceremonial payment when addressing the other family’s parents as “Mom” and “Dad.” For money, use a traceable transfer; for jewelry, keep receipts and evidence of delivery. Confirm spoken agreements in messages or with family members present.
- In plain language: China prohibits demanding property through marriage, and courts support repayment where someone uses caili as a pretext for doing so. Repayment may also be sought if the marriage was never registered, if it was registered but the couple never actually lived together, or if a premarital payment caused the payer financial hardship. The latter two grounds require divorce. If the marriage was registered and the couple lived together, repayment is generally not ordered on divorce; however, a short period together and an excessively large payment can justify a different result. The court considers how the money was used, any dowry, pregnancy or childbirth, each party’s fault, and the other relevant circumstances. Keep records of every payment and what it was for, whichever partner or family paid it.
- Benefit: Chinese law prohibits using marriage to demand property. If one party does so in the name of caili, a court supports the other party’s claim for repayment. The marriage-law interpretation recognizes nonregistration, registration without actually living together, and hardship caused by premarital payment as grounds for seeking repayment; the latter two depend on divorce. Where a registered couple has lived together, repayment is generally refused on divorce. If they lived together only briefly and the caili was excessive, the court decides whether repayment is appropriate and, if so, what proportion, considering actual use, dowry, the amount, time together, pregnancy or childbirth, fault, and local custom. If the couple lived together without registering the marriage, the court likewise assesses the amount to return rather than automatically ordering full repayment. The specific caili provisions took effect nationwide in China on February 1, 2024.

### 23. Put property agreements and parental contributions in writing before changing a home’s ownership
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=少 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: You can write down your intentions without buying anything. Allow a few hours to collect documents and discuss them; legal advice, notarization, and property registration may cost extra. When parents contribute, record whether the money is a loan or a gift and who receives it. In China, a written marital-property agreement does not generally require notarization to be valid. Formal requirements elsewhere differ; see the local examples below.
- In plain language: In China, property owned before marriage does not become jointly owned just because the marriage lasts a long time. Spouses can make a binding written agreement about separate and shared property. Parents’ premarital contribution toward a home is normally a gift to their own child unless they expressly give it to both partners. After marriage, the agreement matters; without a clear agreement, joint-property rules apply, with specific rules for division on divorce. Adding a spouse’s name to a home’s title is a serious transaction. Do not assume either “I can always take it back before registration” or “registration settles everything forever”: the 2025 judicial interpretation provides more specific rules for disputed transfers on divorce. A spouse who hides, transfers, sells off, or squanders joint property, or fabricates joint debts to take the other spouse’s property, may receive less or none of the joint property in the division.
- Benefit: Under Chinese Civil Code Articles 1063 and 1065, premarital property is separate property unless the spouses agree otherwise. A written agreement may make premarital assets and assets acquired during marriage separate, joint, or partly each, and binds both spouses. Under the 2020 marriage interpretation, parents’ premarital home contributions are gifts to their own child unless expressly given to both; postmarital contributions follow the agreement, or the Civil Code’s joint-property principles when it is absent or unclear. That interpretation also refers proposed interspousal home gifts revoked before registration to the general gift rule in Article 658, including its exceptions. **Current divorce rules:** Article 5 of the [2025 interpretation](https://www.hncourt.gov.cn/public/detail.php?id=199761) addresses disputed promises to transfer a home before or during marriage, even when registration has not occurred. The court may award the home to either spouse and decide whether compensation is due, considering the purpose of the gift, marriage duration, shared life and children, divorce-related fault, family contributions, and the home’s value at divorce. If registration has occurred, a short marriage and no serious fault by the giver can support awarding the home back to the giver, with any compensation determined from the circumstances. Fraud, coercion, serious infringement of the giver’s or a close relative’s rights, and failure to perform a support duty can also justify revocation under the law. Article 8 gives specific division rules for parental funding: where one side’s parents paid in full and no clear gift agreement exists, a court may award the home to their child and decide whether and how much to compensate the spouse. With partial funding from one side or funding from both sides, the court considers contribution sources and proportions alongside the other family circumstances and awards reasonable compensation to the other spouse. A clear agreement gifting the relevant contribution only to the parents’ own child is respected. Hiding, transferring, selling off, or squandering joint assets, or fabricating joint debts, can reduce or eliminate a spouse’s share under Article 1092. These rules apply in China; the 2025 interpretation took effect on February 1, 2025.

### 32. Challenge insufficient evidence and coerced confessions; a final conviction can still be reviewed
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=自由 -->

- Cost: No purchase is needed to know these safeguards. A petition for review and a retrial take time and often require a lawyer; see entry 5 for costs.
- In plain language: Chinese criminal procedure sets a threshold for conviction: the evidence used to decide the case must have been verified through lawful procedures, and the evidence as a whole must exclude reasonable doubt. If the evidence cannot establish guilt, the court must acquit. Confessions obtained through torture or other unlawful methods, and witness or victim statements obtained through violence, threats, or other unlawful methods, must be excluded. This applies during investigation, prosecution review, and trial: excluded evidence cannot support a recommendation to prosecute, a prosecution decision, or a judgment. After a judgment becomes final, a party, their legal representative, or a close relative may still petition a court or procuratorate for review. The court must retry the case where the statutory grounds are met.
- Benefit: Article 55 of China's Criminal Procedure Law requires reliable and sufficient evidence: the facts supporting conviction and sentencing must be proved, evidence relied on must have been lawfully verified, and the evidence as a whole must exclude reasonable doubt. Article 56 requires exclusion of confessions obtained through torture or other unlawful methods and witness or victim statements obtained through violence, threats, or other unlawful methods. Evidence requiring exclusion must be excluded at the investigation, prosecution-review, or trial stage and cannot support a recommendation to prosecute, a prosecution decision, or a judgment. Article 200(3) requires acquittal when the evidence is insufficient to establish the charged offense. Under Article 252, parties, their legal representatives, and close relatives may petition a court or procuratorate against a final judgment or ruling; the petition itself does not suspend enforcement. Article 253 specifies the grounds on which the court must retry the case. These provisions apply nationally in China.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2018). Criminal Procedure Law, 2018 revision, Articles 55, 56, 200, 252, and 253. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/zdgz/201810/t20181027_396818.shtml>
- Notes: These are legal safeguards, not a guarantee of the outcome. No verified official Chinese acquittal rate or rate of convictions overturned after review is established here, so no percentage or prediction about an individual case is given. Take three practical steps: retain a lawyer as early as possible (entry 5), read every word of the record before signing, and do not admit to something you did not do just to “get out sooner.” Admitting guilt and accepting punishment can support leniency; it does not itself exempt you from liability. **Legal clarification:** Article 34 gives a suspect the right to retain defense counsel from the first interrogation or the imposition of a compulsory measure; there is no need to wait until the interrogation is over. During investigation, that counsel must be a lawyer. The [officially reproduced law](https://www.samr.gov.cn/zw/zfxxgk/fdzdgknr/bgt/art/2025/art_48b3b0c3f954448e996602a71fc6f878.html) also distinguishes a petition against a final decision from an ordinary appeal. **US/Europe:** counsel, exclusion of evidence, appeals, and reopening a final case follow the law of the particular country and, where relevant, state. Have a local criminal-defense lawyer check the available procedure and deadline; Chinese article numbers do not establish those rights abroad.

### 33. If your case ends after you were held in custody, check your right to state compensation
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->

- Cost: No application fee under China's State Compensation Law. Apply first to the legally responsible authority. Which police authority, procuratorate, or court must pay depends on the detention, arrest, or judgment involved; it is not simply any body that handled the case.
- In plain language: In China, withdrawal of a case, a decision not to prosecute, or an acquittal after custody can give you a right to state compensation, subject to the statutory conditions. **Legal clarification:** ordinary criminal detention must have been unlawful or have exceeded its legal time limit; ending a case after a lawful detention within the time limit does not by itself create this entitlement. The law separately covers a case ending after formal arrest and an acquittal on retrial after the original sentence has been carried out. Compensation for loss of liberty is calculated by the day. For procuratorial compensation decisions made from 2026-05-18, the rate is CNY 495.94 per day, up from CNY 475.52—roughly CNY 180,000 for a year. Where the infringement caused mental harm, remedies can also include removing its effects, restoring your reputation, and an apology within the scope of the harm; serious consequences can justify an additional monetary award. One exclusion is custody or a sentence caused by your own deliberately false confession or fabricated evidence of guilt. Other statutory exclusions also apply.
- Benefit: Article 17 of China's State Compensation Law covers unlawful criminal detention, or otherwise lawful detention beyond the legal time limit, followed by withdrawal, non-prosecution, or acquittal ending criminal proceedings; formal arrest followed by one of those outcomes; and acquittal through retrial where the original punishment has already been carried out. Article 33 bases the daily payment for loss of liberty on the previous year's national average daily wage. Article 35 provides remedies for mental harm, including removal of the effects, restoration of reputation, and an apology within the affected sphere; serious consequences require an appropriate monetary award. Article 19 excludes liability where the person's own deliberately false confession or fabricated incriminating evidence caused the custody or sentence. The Supreme People's Procuratorate's May 2026 notice uses the 2025 national average annual wage for urban non-private-sector employees, CNY 129,441, published by the National Bureau of Statistics on 2026-05-15. The resulting average daily wage is CNY 495.94. Procuratorates apply a daily compensation rate of CNY 495.94 to decisions made from 2026-05-18; the rate applicable from May 2025 was CNY 475.52 per day. These are Chinese statutory amounts, not local living-cost estimates.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2012 revision). State Compensation Law, Articles 17, 19, 33, and 35, reproduced by the National Bureau of Statistics. <https://www.stats.gov.cn/gk/tjfg/xgfxfg/202503/t20250306_1958899.html>; Supreme People's Procuratorate (2026). Latest compensation rate for violations of personal liberty. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/xwfbh/wsfbh/202605/t20260519_727675.shtml>
- Notes: The daily rate is generally updated in May following publication of the previous year's wage data. Check the rate applicable when the compensation decision is made. Mental-harm awards depend on the consequences; no personal award estimate is offered here. **Legal clarification:** there are judicial guidelines, not unrestricted discretion: the [2021 Supreme People's Court interpretation, Article 8](https://www.court.gov.cn/shenpan/xiangqing/292671.html), generally places the award at no more than 50% of the combined liberty and life/health compensation, with higher awards possible for especially serious consequences or evidence that the usual standard is inadequate. Article 21 of the State Compensation Law identifies the responsible body: for compensable detention, the body that decided it; after formal arrest followed by termination, the body that decided the arrest; after acquittal on retrial, the court that made the original final conviction. Specific rules also cover acquittal on appeal or after remand. Apply there first, then use the statutory review or court-compensation-committee procedure if you dispute the decision; check its deadlines. Guo Li's case in entry 34 shows the calculation: 1,826 days at CNY 258.89, based on the 2016 wage standard applicable to that decision, produced CNY 472,733.14 for loss of liberty, plus CNY 165,450 for mental harm. The rate came from the compensation decision's applicable standard, not the years of detention. He had sought CNY 88.88 million for ten years of mental harm; the excess was refused. Claims for lost earnings, lawyer fees, and family payments toward living expenses in custody were also refused as outside the statutory scope. **Case-specific clarification:** the life/health and property claims were rejected because the court found no qualifying infringement by the liable court, not because those categories can never be compensated. **US/Europe:** entitlement, responsible authority, limitation periods, and award rules vary by jurisdiction. An acquittal is not a universal promise of a daily payment; have the local legal grounds checked.

### 34. Make a lawful compensation claim through documented channels; do not offer silence in exchange for money
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=自由 -->

- Cost: A complaint to China's 12315 consumer service is a free starting point. A lawsuit or a lawyer may involve costs; see entries 5 and 20. Preserve negotiations in writing and, where recording is lawful, record the whole meeting. Check local recording and consent rules before making a recording.
- In plain language: If you have suffered harm, claiming compensation from the responsible party has a legal basis. Asking for a large amount or taking the matter to the media does not automatically turn a legitimate claim into extortion. In Guo Li's case, prosecutors concluded that the evidence did not establish an intent to unlawfully appropriate property: as his daughter's guardian, he could seek compensation from the milk-powder manufacturer, and consumers could expose and criticize wrongdoing through the media. Yet the case also shows the possible cost: he was first sentenced to five years for extortion, spent 1,826 days in custody, and was acquitted on retrial by the Guangdong High People's Court only on 2017-04-07. Use channels that leave a record, do not go alone to negotiate at a place chosen by the other party, and do not make “pay me” conditional on “I won't expose or report you.” These precautions do not mean that every disputed demand is a crime.
- Benefit: In this official Chinese case, authorities reported melamine in some batches of Shi'en milk powder in 2008. Guo Li's daughter's examination found several small, bright echoes in the central collecting systems of both kidneys. Guo repeatedly sought compensation from the retailer and Shi'en and contacted the media. In June 2009, an agreement provided CNY 400,000 in compensation, and he signed a statement agreeing not to pursue the matter further and waiving compensation claims. The company later reported him to police, and the Chao'an County People's Court sentenced him to five years for extortion. During retrial, Guangdong prosecutors concluded that the evidence did not establish an intent to unlawfully appropriate property. As his daughter's legal guardian, he had a lawful basis to claim compensation from the manufacturer; consumers were also entitled to expose and criticize harm to their rights through the media. On 2017-04-07, the Guangdong High People's Court acquitted him: the facts and evidence did not establish that he had used threats or coercion to obtain property with an unlawful appropriation purpose, and his conduct remained within a civil dispute. He was in custody from 2009-07-23 to 2014-07-22, a total of 1,826 days. This was an individual Guangdong case, overturned on retrial in 2017.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Guangdong Provincial People's Procuratorate (2017). Recommendation to acquit Guo Li on retrial, reproduced by the Heyuan People's Procuratorate. <https://jcy.heyuan.gov.cn/node/142>; Compensation Committee of the Guangdong High People's Court (2018). Guo Li's state-compensation claim after acquittal on retrial against the Chaozhou Intermediate People's Court; decision (2018) Yue Wei Pei No. 11. <https://www.gdcourts.gov.cn/caipanwenshuxuandeng/caipanwenshuxuandeng/content/post_1045709.html>
- Notes: Grade B reflects a single case, not a generalizable probability. It shows both that this compensation claim had a lawful basis and that criminal proceedings nevertheless occurred. Having a lawyer handle the claim or bringing a civil action can help keep the dispute documented and avoid unsupported pressure; this is practical advice, not a measured ranking of the safest routes. Be especially cautious about an invitation to “settle things in person” in the other party's area. Where a report is filed and which authority has jurisdiction depend on the facts; no frequency estimate is established here. Honor a settlement, and get legal advice before making a new, larger demand after signing one: its basis and presentation can become disputed. The [prosecutorial account cited above](https://jcy.heyuan.gov.cn/node/142) specifically considered the later CNY 3 million demand a disputed civil claim, not automatic proof of extortion. For what to do after a criminal case is opened, see entries 5, 32, and 33. Entry 30 covers the reverse situation—someone threatening you to obtain money. **US/Europe:** use the relevant consumer body, lawyer, or civil court; 12315 is a Chinese service. Settlement, publicity, extortion, and recording rules require a local legal assessment.

### 35. If you face online harassment, protect your account and preserve evidence; use platform reports, injunctions, or police as needed
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=中 毅力=些 收益=中 口径=自由 -->

- Cost: No purchase is needed to start. Allow roughly ten to nineteen minutes for initial protection settings and evidence preservation, not as a guaranteed completion time. An injunction or private prosecution takes further work with the court and may require a lawyer.
- In plain language: Start with three immediate steps. Turn on the protections you need: block strangers or particular accounts, restrict who can see your posts, disable reposting or comments, and accept messages only from friends or disable them altogether. Use the platform's quick evidence-preservation feature where available, then submit a report through its dedicated reporting channel. In China, three routes may help. If an unlawful infringement of your personal rights is happening or imminent and delay would cause harm that is difficult to repair, ask the court for an injunction. Criminal insult and defamation cases generally require the victim to bring a private prosecution. If collecting evidence is genuinely difficult, the court may request police assistance to identify the perpetrator and document dissemination and harm; once the filing requirements are met, the court must accept the case. Cases meeting the public-prosecution exception follow that route, with the prosecution bearing the burden of proving guilt. This does not make preserving evidence or cooperating with the investigation unnecessary. Unlawfully collecting and publishing personal information to an unrestricted audience through doxxing can constitute the offense of infringing citizens' personal information when the circumstances are serious.
- Benefit: Paragraph 15 of Chinese guidance Fa Fa [2023] No. 14 permits a court injunction under Civil Code Article 997 where evidence shows an ongoing or imminent unlawful infringement of personal rights and delay would cause irreparable harm. Under paragraph 11, a court may request police assistance where a private prosecutor genuinely struggles to obtain evidence. Police should promptly identify the perpetrator and collect evidence of dissemination and impact; service providers must supply necessary technical support. If police-assisted collection produces enough evidence to meet the requirements for accepting a private prosecution, the court must accept it; if evidence cannot be obtained, police must explain that in writing. Paragraph 12 lists five categories of serious harm to social order under Criminal Law Article 246(2): severe consequences such as a mental breakdown or suicide of the victim or a close relative, together with a seriously adverse social impact; arbitrary targeting of ordinary members of the public, wide dissemination, and large volumes of vulgar or malicious comments, seriously disrupting online order and causing a seriously adverse social impact; insulting or defaming multiple people, or repeatedly spreading such material, with a seriously adverse social impact; organizing or directing people to spread large amounts across multiple platforms, with a seriously adverse social impact; and other circumstances seriously harming social order. Paragraph 4 covers organized doxxing that unlawfully collects and releases personal information to an unrestricted audience, where the circumstances are serious. Article 23 of the 2024 Provisions on the Governance of Online Violence Information requires platform controls for blocking strangers or specified users, limiting the visibility of one's posts, disabling reposts or comments, and restricting private messages to friends or refusing all private messages. Article 25 requires quick evidence-preservation tools; Article 26 requires a prominent, dedicated complaint and reporting channel; Article 15 requires prompt police reports when suspected unlawful or criminal conduct is discovered.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Supreme People's Court, Supreme People's Procuratorate, and Ministry of Public Security (2023). Guidance on Punishing Online Violence Offenses, Fa Fa [2023] No. 14, paragraphs 4, 11, 12, and 15. <https://www.court.gov.cn/zixun/xiangqing/412992.html>; National People's Congress (2020). Civil Code, Article 997; full text reproduced by the Supreme People's Procuratorate. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202006/t20200602_463888.shtml>; Cyberspace Administration of China, Ministry of Public Security, Ministry of Culture and Tourism, and National Radio and Television Administration (2024). Provisions on the Governance of Online Violence Information, Articles 15, 23, 25, and 26; effective 2024-08-01; State Council Gazette. <https://www.gov.cn/gongbao/2024/issue_11526/202408/content_6969181.html>
- Notes: Platform controls and reports are practical first steps you can take yourself; preserve evidence before requesting removal. Court proceedings can take time and incur costs, so consider legal advice about an injunction or private prosecution. **Legal clarification:** these are not three mandatory consecutive stages. Do not delay a police report or an urgent injunction application while waiting for the platform. Under paragraph 12, the listed serious-public-order circumstances support public prosecution of insult or defamation; falling below that threshold does not rule out civil relief, administrative action, or prosecution for a different offense. Nor must every victim necessarily pay all legal costs: paragraph 17 requires information about representation and legal aid. Avoid retaliatory abuse, which can expose you to liability (entry 14), and do not erase the evidence in your account before preserving it. You can still restrict access and seek removal once it is safely recorded. If you feel unable to cope, China's 12356 mental-health line is discussed in chapter 1, entry 25; use an appropriate local support or emergency service outside China. Requests to remove personal information are covered in chapter 14, entry 8. If harassment followed your helping a stranger, see [Should you stop when a stranger is in trouble? (Chinese)](../../docs/遇到陌生人出事该不该停.md). **US/Europe:** use the platform's local reporting route and seek local legal or police help for threats, stalking, or unlawful disclosure. Injunctions, privacy rights, and criminal procedures differ by country or state; the Chinese private-prosecution rules and hotline are not international services.
