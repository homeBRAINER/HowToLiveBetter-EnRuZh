# 8. Protect your freedom and property

> Partial translation: the introduction and entries 1–4 of 35. Entries 5–35 remain in [Chinese](../../book/08-别把自己搭进去.md). The numbered laws and penalties describe China; regional notes are separate. Clarifications are labeled. New reference checks: September 21, 2026.

This chapter deals with money and personal liberty: legal liability, losses you can avoid, and your procedural rights. It does not convert these outcomes into years of life. Article references point to the official texts used for verification, with qualifications where needed. The order puts potentially costly incidents that are easy to prevent first, measures needing advance preparation in the middle, and experience-based advice last.

### 1. After a road crash, stop, help, and call the police—do not flee
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: No application fee. Stop, switch on your hazard lights, help injured people, and call the police. Allow time for traffic officers to arrive; 1–2 hours is a rough estimate, not a maximum. For a minor crash in China with no injuries and no disagreement about the facts, document the scene and record names, phone numbers, license plates, driver-license numbers, insurance-certificate numbers, and points of impact. Where the self-settlement rules apply, both parties sign the record before clearing the road and discussing compensation.
- In plain language: Fleeing a crash in China normally leaves you responsible for the whole accident unless evidence of the other party's fault reduces that responsibility. A criminal hit-and-run can lead to license revocation and a lifetime ban on obtaining another license. Where the traffic-accident offense applies, fleeing carries 3–7 years in prison; causing a death through flight carries more than 7 years. Commercial insurance can exclude the loss, leaving you with a large bill. Stay, arrange help, and report the crash.
- Benefit: Under Chinese rules, the fleeing party bears full accident responsibility, subject to reduction when the other party's fault is proved. Criminal hit-and-run can bring license revocation and a lifetime driving ban. The traffic-accident offense carries 3–7 years for flight and more than 7 years where flight causes death. The 2020 commercial motor-insurance model terms exclude vehicle-damage and third-party claims involving flight or intentional destruction or falsification of the scene or destruction of evidence. An uninsured compensation bill could reach hundreds of thousands of CNY; this is an illustrative Chinese loss, not a fixed penalty or a measured average.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2021). Road Traffic Safety Law, 2021 revision, Articles 70 and 101, reproduced by Beijing traffic police. <https://jtgl.beijing.gov.cn/jgj/jgxx/flfg/fl/205308/index.html>; State Council (2017). Implementing Regulations for the Road Traffic Safety Law, Order No. 405, 2017 revision, Articles 86 and 92. <http://xzfg.moj.gov.cn/front/law/detail?LawID=75>; National People's Congress (1997). Criminal Law, Article 133. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/201802/t20180206_364975.shtml>; Insurance Association of China (2020). Commercial Motor Insurance Model Terms, 2020 edition, Articles 9 and 22. <https://www.iachina.cn/art/2020/9/4/art_24_104621.html>
- Notes: When someone is injured, arrange urgent help and notify the police; if rescuing someone requires moving objects at the scene, mark their positions. Saying you did not notice the impact does not automatically settle whether your departure was flight; if unsure, report it. **Legal clarification:** [Beijing police explain](https://gaj.beijing.gov.cn/xxfb/fjjx/202204/t20220422_2686215.html) the distinction between noncriminal flight and criminal hit-and-run; a lifetime ban is not the automatic consequence of every minor incident. The insurance text is an industry model: check your own policy and applicable law. Compulsory third-party insurance is a separate scheme. The wait can exceed the planning estimate. **US/Europe:** reporting, moving vehicles, exchanging details, and insurance duties depend on the state or country. For injuries or immediate danger, call the local emergency service; follow local police and insurer instructions rather than applying Chinese deadlines or penalties.

### 2. If you have sent money to a scammer, contact the bank and police immediately
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Reporting the scam does not require paying a recovery intermediary. Give the transfer time, amount, and recipient account; preserve transfer screenshots and messages. In China, report a completed fraud to 110 and contact your bank or payment provider immediately.
- In plain language: Act as soon as you notice the fraud. Chinese police can use emergency payment stops and rapid freezing procedures, and banks and payment providers must cooperate. Recovery becomes harder when money moves through several accounts; do not spend the first minutes investigating or confronting the caller yourself. Report even if more time has passed. The Chinese 96110 line provides fraud warnings and advice; take a warning seriously, but do not treat a displayed number as proof of identity or transfer money on a caller's instructions.
- Benefit: China's Anti-Telecom and Online Fraud Law provides for prompt inquiries into implicated funds, emergency payment stops, and rapid freezing, with cooperation from banks and payment institutions. Frozen funds can be returned through the prescribed process. Earlier reporting can help prevent further transfers; no recovery percentage is established here. The national 96110 line supports warnings and intervention. The law took effect in December 2022.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2022). Anti-Telecom and Online Fraud Law, Articles 20 and 34. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202209/t20220902_575631.shtml>; Fujian Public Security Department (2023). Answer calls from 96110; provincial police guidance. <http://gat.fujian.gov.cn/ztzl/fjjffpzxrx/spjq/202303/t20230306_6126156.htm>
- Notes: The law establishes payment-stop and freezing mechanisms, not a guaranteed refund. Recovery depends in part on whether funds remain available when the report is made. The National Anti-Fraud Center app and 96110 guidance come from Fujian police because the central Ministry of Public Security page was unavailable. Requests to move money to a “safe account” from supposed police, prosecutors, or court staff are scams. **Correction:** [official Guangdong guidance](https://www.gd.gov.cn/gdywdt/bmdt/content/post_2859267.html) distinguishes 96110 warnings/advice from 110 crime reports. There is no established universal deadline of a few dozen minutes after which reporting is pointless. **US/Europe:** contact the bank or payment provider through a trusted channel and ask whether the transfer can be stopped or reversed. The [FTC guide](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/what-do-if-you-were-scammed) covers payment-specific steps and US reporting. [Europol lists national cybercrime reporting routes](https://www.europol.europa.eu/report-a-crime/report-cybercrime-online); reports go to the relevant national or local authority, not directly to Europol. Refund rights depend on the payment and jurisdiction.

### 3. Do not trust an unexpected caller: protect your details and verify every payment request
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: No purchase is needed. Remember four habits: question unexpected calls, keep sensitive information private, avoid unsolicited links, and verify transfers. If someone asks you to transfer money, share your screen, install an unfamiliar app, open a link, or read out a verification code, end the call and contact the organization through an independently checked official number. In China, the National Anti-Fraud Center app is another official prevention tool.
- In plain language: Hang up before acting on a financial request, then verify it yourself. Scammers pose as police, prosecutors, court staff, customer support, managers, or a platform's fraud team. Their stories include “cooperate with an investigation,” “cancel a membership,” paid fake-order tasks, or inside investment information. Recognizing the setup before paying costs less than trying to recover the money afterward.
- Benefit: China's law requires public education about telecom and online fraud and warning systems involving police, finance, telecom, and internet authorities. People identified as potential victims should receive timely warnings. The national 96110 line is used for this intervention; do not dismiss a genuine warning, and independently verify any accompanying request. Prevention avoids the need to seek recovery, whose outcome depends partly on whether money is still available when reported; see entry 2. The national law has applied since December 2022.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Standing Committee of the National People's Congress (2022). Anti-Telecom and Online Fraud Law, Articles 8, 20, and 34. <https://www.spp.gov.cn/spp/fl/202209/t20220902_575631.shtml>; Fujian Public Security Department (2023). Answer calls from 96110; includes the four-habit prevention slogan and a warning about scam calls beginning with 192. <http://gat.fujian.gov.cn/ztzl/fjjffpzxrx/spjq/202303/t20230306_6126156.htm>
- Notes: Grade B reflects official prevention guidance, not a measured reduction in fraud among people who memorize it. The Ministry of Public Security website, mps.gov.cn, was unavailable during the source check; the provincial reproduction is used. Impersonated officials, support teams, managers, and fraud teams use different stories to push the same unsafe actions. **Clarification:** a phone prefix, caller ID, or an official-looking app is not identity proof; a 192 prefix alone does not establish fraud. Outside China, use your bank's and national authorities' official channels; the Chinese app and 96110 are not foreign reporting services.

### 4. A familiar face or voice is not identity proof; hang up and call a number you already trust
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=些 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: One extra call and a few minutes. Limit public close-up face videos and long voice recordings that could be copied.
- In plain language: A convincing face or voice can be faked. Chinese police guidance suggests asking for a continuous head turn or for a hand to cover the whole face and move away quickly; artifacts may appear, but passing that test proves nothing. The stronger check is to hang up and call the person's old number already saved in your contacts, or confirm through someone you both know. If the caller refuses and says the signal is bad or they are in a meeting, do not send money until you have independent confirmation. AI-content labels also cannot authenticate the caller: a scammer may omit them.
- Benefit: The Ministry of Public Security's cyber police describe three checks: movement, voice details, and independent contact. Head turns or brief full-face occlusion may expose stuttering, blur, or distortion; unusual breathing pauses, emotional inflection, mechanical sound, or background noise may also raise suspicion. The central step is to call a familiar number independently—not the suspicious caller's number—or ask a trusted mutual contact. The practical rule is to question video and voice and verify through another channel. Chinese deep-synthesis rules prohibit fraudulent use and require prominent labels for covered face-generation, face-replacement, synthetic-voice, and voice-imitation services. The 2025 labeling rules prohibit malicious removal, alteration, forgery, or concealment of labels; absence of a label does not prove authenticity. In one reported investigation, police uncovered a 14-person group, more than 50,000 synthesized moving-face videos, more than 500,000 personal-information records, and seized 60 cloud servers. The 2025 labeling measures took effect on September 1, 2025.
- Evidence grade: B
- Sources: Ministry of Public Security Cyber Security Bureau (2025). Seeing is not believing: beware new AI scams, reproduced by Fujian police. <http://gat.fujian.gov.cn/ztzl/fjjffpzxrx/spjq/202509/t20250922_7013008.htm>; Cyberspace Administration of China, Ministry of Industry and Information Technology, and Ministry of Public Security (2022). Deep Synthesis Internet Information Service Provisions, Articles 6, 16, and 17; effective January 10, 2023. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/2022-12/12/content_5731431.htm>; Cyberspace Administration of China and three other authorities (2025). Measures for Labeling AI-Generated and Synthetic Content, Article 10; effective September 1, 2025. <https://www.cac.gov.cn/2025-03/14/c_1743654684782215.htm>; Ministry of Public Security Criminal Investigation Bureau and CCTV News (2026). Police uncover more than 50,000 synthesized moving-face videos: who is copying your face?, reproduced by Fujian police. <http://gat.fujian.gov.cn/ztzl/fjjffpzxrx/spjq/202603/t20260330_7117146.htm>
- Notes: Grade B reflects official advice and a case report, not a measured reduction in fraud from these checks. Use the old number in your own contacts, not caller ID or a new number sent in chat. Refusal to perform a movement or join a video call, including excuses about a meeting or poor signal, is a reason to stop the transaction and verify independently; it is not proof of a crime. **Technical clarification:** movement and voice artifacts are clues, not reliable authentication tests. Models change, and even independent contact must use a channel you actually trust. Chinese deep-synthesis duties began under the 2023 rules; September 2025 is the start of the additional labeling measures, not the first labeling obligation. For money already sent, see entry 2; for protecting face and voice data, see chapter 14, currently in Chinese. The central ministry website returned 521 during the earlier check, so the cyber and criminal-investigation guidance is cited through Fujian's official reproductions. These Chinese labeling laws do not establish US or European duties.
