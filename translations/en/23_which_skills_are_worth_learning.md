# 23. Which skills are worth learning?

This chapter weighs education and skills mainly against their monetary return and the time they take. Entry 2 also looks at adult mortality. The first six entries help you decide whether to keep studying: legal limits on work at a young age, evidence about health, how common different qualifications are, help when education seems unaffordable, routes onward from vocational school, and a way to do the calculation for yourself. The remaining seven entries concern the choice of a particular skill. Reliable official Chinese earnings figures for each occupation are difficult to verify, so this chapter gives no promised monthly wage for a specific trade. It concentrates on evidence about returns to education, ways to check whether a certificate or training subsidy is legitimate, and features that may make a skill harder to replace.

The introduction and entries 1–4 of 13 are translated so far. The [Chinese chapter](../../book/23-学什么技能划算.md) is available in full.

### 1. Before taking a job as a teenager, check the age rules and get your employment documented
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: Checking your age and the applicable rules costs $0 or €0 and takes a little time. This is not the cost of a job or of education.
- In plain language: Someone leaving lower secondary school may be 15 or 16. In China, employers generally cannot hire anyone under 16. An employer who does so faces penalties, while undocumented work may make it harder for the young worker to prove what happened and claim protection. Do not accept a job with unclear terms or no record of the working relationship. Lawful light work may be possible at 14 in Russia and Belarus; the US and EU have their own age rules. Check the law where you live before choosing work over further study.
- Benefit: China's Compulsory Education Law requires a parent or other guardian to send a child to school at age **six** and ensure that the child completes compulsory education (Article 11). It prohibits hiring a child who should still be receiving compulsory education (Article 14), and provides for instruction and an order to correct an unjustified failure to send the child to school (Article 58). China's Provisions on the Prohibition of Child Labour ban government bodies, associations, enterprises, institutions, private non-enterprise organizations and individual businesses from hiring children **under 16** (Article 2). The employer is fined **5,000 yuan per child per month**. If it does not return the child to a parent or guardian after an order within the prescribed time, the rate becomes **10,000 yuan per child per month** (Article 6). Introducing a child under 16 to an employer brings a **5,000-yuan fine per child** (Article 7). If an illegally employed child is sick or injured, the employer must arrange treatment and pay all medical and living costs during it; disability or death also calls for a lump-sum payment calculated under occupational-injury insurance rules (Article 10). Professional arts and sports organizations may employ under-16 performers and athletes with guardian consent while protecting their health and schooling. Regulated, safe educational practice and vocational training are not classed as child labour (Article 13).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Standing Committee of the National People's Congress of China (2018), Compulsory Education Law, second 2018 amendment, Articles 11, 14 and 58, published by the Ministry of Education. <http://www.moe.gov.cn/jyb_sjzl/sjzl_zcfg/zcfg_jyfl/202110/t20211029_575949.html>. State Council of China (2002), Provisions on the Prohibition of Child Labour, Decree 364, Articles 2, 6, 7, 10 and 13. <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2002/content_61798.htm>; [current Ministry of Justice text](https://xzfg.moj.gov.cn/front/law/detail?LawID=604). US Department of Labor, [child-labor rules for non-agricultural jobs](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fact-sheets/43-child-labor-non-agriculture). European Union, [Directive 94/33/EC on the protection of young people at work](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/1994/33/oj/eng). Russian Labour Ministry, [guidance on employment contracts](https://mintrud.gov.ru/ministry/programms/trudotn/dogovor). Belarusian Tax Ministry, [explanation of Labour Code Article 21](https://nalog.gov.by/clarifications/clarifications/13891/).
- Notes: The fines in yuan are **Chinese statutory amounts**, not local costs or penalties in the US or Europe. The Chinese ban must not be presented as a universal ban on all under-16 work. Under US federal rules for *covered non-agricultural jobs*, 14- and 15-year-olds may work outside school in specified nonhazardous jobs with limits on hours; 16- and 17-year-olds may work longer hours but remain barred from federally hazardous occupations. State law can be stricter. In the EU, the baseline minimum age is generally tied to the end of compulsory full-time schooling and at least 15, with nationally defined exceptions for light work, training, and cultural or artistic activity; European countries outside the EU have their own laws. Russia generally allows employment at 16 and, with conditions, light work at 15 or 14; Belarus generally allows it at 16 and certain light work at 14 with a parent's or guardian's written consent. An unlawfully employed child in China is **not** without legal protection: Article 10 expressly obliges the employer to cover illness and injury costs. Records of work, agreements, and pay can matter when claiming rights; see [chapter 19](19_work_exit_and_injury.md). Reaching 16 does not guarantee safe or well-paid work; entry 10 considers how easily routine tasks can be replaced.

### 2. Consider the link between education and longevity without treating 1.9% as a personal promise
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=多 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: More years of education take time and effort. Tuition, if any, and available aid depend on the country, program, and your circumstances; entry 4 describes Chinese support measures. There is no one honest USD or EUR price for everyone.
- In plain language: Across the studies, adults with more years of education tended to live longer. A review of **603 studies in 59 countries** associated each extra year of education with about **1.9% lower relative risk of death** in adulthood. The estimate was **2.9%** at ages 18–49 and **0.8%** at age 70 or older. Education may affect health and income, but family resources, where you live, and working conditions can also matter. The association belongs in the decision; it cannot predict your personal lifespan.
- Benefit: A systematic review and meta-analysis searched publications from **January 1980 to 31 May 2023**, with the search updated on **16 June 2023**. It included **603 studies**, **70 locations in 59 countries**, and **10,355 observations**. Years of education had a dose–response association with all-cause adult mortality: each additional year was associated on average with **1.9% lower relative risk** (**95% uncertainty interval: 1.8–2.0%**). The age-specific estimates were **2.9%** (**2.8–3.0%**) for adults aged **18–49** and **0.8%** (**0.6–1.0%**) for those aged **70 or older**. The model found no differential association by sex or country Socio-demographic Index level. These are not percentage-point changes in a person's absolute chance of death.
- Evidence grade: A (disputed)
- Sources: IHME-CHAIN Collaborators (2024), “Effects of education on adult mortality: a global systematic review and meta-analysis”, *The Lancet Public Health*, 9(3), e155–e165. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S2468-2667(23)00306-7>; [PubMed abstract and reference](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38278172/); [full article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10901745/).
- Notes: The included evidence was observational. The authors reported evidence of publication bias (**p < 0.0001**) and substantial variation between studies; **85.9%** of the observations came from high-income countries. Statistical adjustment cannot fully separate schooling from family resources, prior health, place of residence, and occupation. The group-level association between more education and lower mortality is strong, but it does **not** prove that your next year in school will *cause* your own risk to fall by 1.9%. You are the potential beneficiary; calculate the financial side separately.

### 3. Before calling education worthless, look at how educational levels are distributed
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=中 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Looking up the published figures costs $0 or €0 and takes a little time.
- In plain language: China's **2020** census counted **15,467 people per 100,000** at the college or university education level (about 15%), **15,088** at senior secondary level, including vocational secondary education, **34,507** at junior secondary level, and **24,767** at primary level. Among people aged 15 or older, estimated average schooling was **9.91 years**, just over the length of junior secondary schooling in China. These figures show how widespread each level of education was across China's population; college or university education accounted for a smaller share. Stories about a graduate of a **Project 985** university delivering food or a junior-secondary leaver owning a business cannot tell you how often either outcome occurs. Compare yourself with people your own age and in your intended field before drawing conclusions.
- Benefit: In China's seventh population census, whose reference date was **1 November 2020**, **218,360,767 people** were counted at college or university education level, **213,005,258** at senior secondary level, including vocational secondary school, **487,163,489** at junior secondary level, and **349,658,828** at primary level. Between **2010** and **2020**, the counts per **100,000** inhabitants changed as follows: college or university, **8,930 to 15,467**; senior secondary, **14,032 to 15,088**; junior secondary, **38,788 to 34,507**; and primary, **26,779 to 24,767**. For people **aged 15 or older**, estimated average schooling rose from **9.08 to 9.91 years**, while the illiteracy rate fell from **4.08% to 2.67%**.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: National Bureau of Statistics of China and Office of the Leading Group of the State Council for the Seventh National Population Census (2021), *Communiqué of the Seventh National Population Census, No. 6: Education of the population*. <https://www.stats.gov.cn/sj/tjgb/rkpcgb/qgrkpcgb/202302/t20230206_1902006.html>.
- Notes: The denominator is the **whole population of mainland China's 31 provincial-level areas plus active military personnel**, not its workforce or job applicants. It excludes residents of Hong Kong, Macao, and Taiwan living in mainland China and foreign nationals. Each education category includes **graduates, current students, and people who left before graduating**; thus 15,467 per 100,000 is **not** the proportion holding a completed degree. Average years of schooling is calculated from education-level categories, rather than each person's exact time in school. The share with college or university experience can be higher among young people than across all ages, so the national figure cannot establish your standing among peers. Neither can it show that a junior-secondary leaver is easier to replace at work. It does not answer whether a specific course will pay off; use the calculation in entry 6. These Chinese figures must not be treated as US or European statistics. The comparison is for your own decision.

### 4. Before giving up on education because of cost, calculate the aid you can actually receive
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=金钱 -->
- Cost: Asking a school about support and checking eligibility costs $0 or €0, although preparing an application takes time. Tuition, housing, and living expenses may still be substantial.
- In plain language: Compulsory education in China is free of tuition and standard school fees. Since **2012**, specified groups of students in public secondary vocational schools have had tuition waived. The national *average* annual grant for eligible vocational secondary and general senior-secondary students increased from **2,000 to 2,300 yuan** starting in the spring **2025** term. For college and university students, the average grant rose from **3,300 to 3,700 yuan a year**, and the National Encouragement Scholarship from **5,000 to 6,000 yuan** in **2024**. Annual maximum national student-loan applications rose from **16,000 to 20,000 yuan** for undergraduates and from **20,000 to 25,000 yuan** for graduate students, with interest at the corresponding loan prime rate (LPR) minus **70 basis points**. These are **Chinese** programs and amounts, not US or European aid. Find out which grants, fee waivers, funded places, or loans you personally qualify for. Then compare the remaining cost of different courses, including earnings you would give up while studying. You may need to delay or change your route; aid is not automatic.
- Benefit: From the autumn **2012** term, tuition waivers in Chinese public secondary vocational schools covered full-time students in **years 1, 2 and 3** from rural areas, including county towns, students of urban agriculture-related programs, and students from low-income families. Related performing-arts programs were excluded. A **2024** policy raised the average national college and undergraduate grant from **3,300 to 3,700 yuan per student per year** from the autumn **2024** term, and the National Encouragement Scholarship from **5,000 to 6,000 yuan** in **2024**. The average national grant for vocational secondary and general senior-secondary education rose from **2,000 to 2,300 yuan per student per year** from the spring **2025** term. From autumn **2024**, the annual ceiling on national student-loan applications rose from **16,000 to 20,000 yuan** for college and undergraduate students and from **20,000 to 25,000 yuan** for graduate students; the interest rate became the matching-term LPR minus **70 basis points**. Neither a loan ceiling nor an average grant guarantees that amount to any one student.
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: China's Ministry of Finance, National Development and Reform Commission, Ministry of Education, and Ministry of Human Resources and Social Security (2012), **财教〔2012〕376号**. <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2013/content_2332779.htm>; [Ministry of Education publication](https://www.moe.gov.cn/jyb_xxgk/moe_1777/moe_1779/201210/t20121030_143848.html). China's Ministry of Finance, Ministry of Education, and Ministry of Human Resources and Social Security (2024), explanation of student-aid changes. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/202410/content_6983672.htm>; [Ministry of Education policy notice](https://www.moe.gov.cn/jyb_xxgk/moe_1777/moe_1779/202410/t20241029_1159726.html). China's Ministry of Finance, Ministry of Education, People's Bank, and financial regulator (2024), **财教〔2024〕188号**. <https://www.gov.cn/zhengce/zhengceku/202411/content_6984812.htm>; [Ministry of Education publication](https://www.moe.gov.cn/jyb_xxgk/moe_1777/moe_1779/202411/t20241104_1160686.html). [US Federal Student Aid: free FAFSA application](https://studentaid.gov/articles/fafsa-student-steps/) and [how to compare aid offers](https://studentaid.gov/articles/evaluating-financial-aid-offers/). [European Commission: country profiles for studying in Europe](https://education.ec.europa.eu/eu/study-in-europe/countries).
- Notes: The possible beneficiaries are you and your children. In China, the exact coverage of waivers and grant tiers depends on province and school. The National Encouragement Scholarship is for academically strong students in financial need; [China's Ministry of Education sets out the eligibility rules](https://www.moe.gov.cn/jyb_xxgk/moe_1777/moe_1779/201410/t20141021_178435.html). A private vocational school's subsidy may cover only the local public-school standard; the student pays any difference. **Loans must be repaid**: check the interest rate, repayment schedule, and total repayment. In the US, submit the free **FAFSA** by the relevant deadlines to be considered for federal, state, and school grants, work-study, and loans; compare the actual offers and remember that a work-study job is not guaranteed. In Europe, fees and aid depend on the **country, institution, course, and student status**; consult the country's current rules and the school's aid office, not a single “European” rate. Record tuition, housing, travel, foregone earnings, and confirmed aid for each course. If household income has stopped, also see [chapter 7](07_when_money_is_tight.md) for support and training while unemployed. The yuan amounts describe Chinese policies and are not converted into dollars or euros.
