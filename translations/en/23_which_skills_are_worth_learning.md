# 23. Which skills are worth learning?

This chapter weighs education and skills mainly against their monetary return and the time they take. Entry 2 also looks at adult mortality. The first six entries help you decide whether to keep studying: legal limits on work at a young age, evidence about health, how common different qualifications are, help when education seems unaffordable, routes onward from vocational school, and a way to do the calculation for yourself. The remaining seven entries concern the choice of a particular skill. Reliable official Chinese earnings figures for each occupation are difficult to verify, so this chapter gives no promised monthly wage for a specific trade. It concentrates on evidence about returns to education, ways to check whether a certificate or training subsidy is legitimate, and features that may make a skill harder to replace.

The introduction and entries 1–2 of 13 are translated so far. The [Chinese chapter](../../book/23-学什么技能划算.md) is available in full.

### 1. Before taking a job as a teenager, check the age rules and get your employment documented
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=少 毅力=否 收益=大 口径=自由 -->
- Cost: Checking your age and the applicable rules costs $0 or €0 and takes a little time. This is not the cost of a job or of education.
- In plain language: Someone leaving lower secondary school may be 15 or 16. In China, employers generally cannot hire anyone under 16. An employer who does so faces penalties, while undocumented work may make it harder for the young worker to prove what happened and claim protection. Do not accept a job with unclear terms or no record of the working relationship. Lawful light work may be possible at 14 in Russia and Belarus; the US and EU have their own age rules. Check the law where you live before choosing work over further study.
- Benefit: China's Compulsory Education Law requires a parent or other guardian to send a child to school at age **six** and ensure that the child completes compulsory education (Article 11). It prohibits hiring a child who should still be receiving compulsory education (Article 14), and provides for instruction and an order to correct an unjustified failure to send the child to school (Article 58). China's Provisions on the Prohibition of Child Labour ban government bodies, associations, enterprises, institutions, private non-enterprise organizations and individual businesses from hiring children **under 16** (Article 2). The employer is fined **5,000 yuan per child per month**. If it does not return the child to a parent or guardian after an order within the prescribed time, the rate becomes **10,000 yuan per child per month** (Article 6). Introducing a child under 16 to an employer brings a **5,000-yuan fine per child** (Article 7). If an illegally employed child is sick or injured, the employer must arrange treatment and pay all medical and living costs during it; disability or death also calls for a lump-sum payment calculated under occupational-injury insurance rules (Article 10). Professional arts and sports organizations may employ under-16 performers and athletes with guardian consent while protecting their health and schooling. Regulated, safe educational practice and vocational training are not classed as child labour (Article 13).
- Evidence grade: A
- Sources: Standing Committee of the National People's Congress of China (2018), Compulsory Education Law, second 2018 amendment, Articles 11, 14 and 58, published by the Ministry of Education. <http://www.moe.gov.cn/jyb_sjzl/sjzl_zcfg/zcfg_jyfl/202110/t20211029_575949.html>. State Council of China (2002), Provisions on the Prohibition of Child Labour, Decree 364, Articles 2, 6, 7, 10 and 13. <http://www.gov.cn/gongbao/content/2002/content_61798.htm>; [current Ministry of Justice text](https://xzfg.moj.gov.cn/front/law/detail?LawID=604). US Department of Labor, [child-labor rules for non-agricultural jobs](https://www.dol.gov/agencies/whd/fact-sheets/43-child-labor-non-agriculture). European Union, [Directive 94/33/EC on the protection of young people at work](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/1994/33/oj/eng). Russian Labour Ministry, [guidance on employment contracts](https://mintrud.gov.ru/ministry/programms/trudotn/dogovor). Belarusian Tax Ministry, [explanation of Labour Code Article 21](https://nalog.gov.by/clarifications/clarifications/13891/).
- Notes: The fines in yuan are **Chinese statutory amounts**, not local costs or penalties in the US or Europe. The Chinese ban must not be presented as a universal ban on all under-16 work. Under US federal rules for *covered non-agricultural jobs*, 14- and 15-year-olds may work outside school in specified nonhazardous jobs with limits on hours; 16- and 17-year-olds may work longer hours but remain barred from federally hazardous occupations. State law can be stricter. In the EU, the baseline minimum age is generally tied to the end of compulsory full-time schooling and at least 15, with nationally defined exceptions for light work, training, and cultural or artistic activity; European countries outside the EU have their own laws. Russia generally allows employment at 16 and, with conditions, light work at 15 or 14; Belarus generally allows it at 16 and certain light work at 14 with a parent's or guardian's written consent. An unlawfully employed child in China is **not** without legal protection: Article 10 expressly obliges the employer to cover illness and injury costs. Records of work, agreements, and pay can matter when claiming rights; see [chapter 19](19_work_exit_and_injury.md). Reaching 16 does not guarantee safe or well-paid work; entry 10 considers how easily routine tasks can be replaced.

### 2. Consider the link between education and longevity without treating 1.9% as a personal promise
<!-- 成本标签: 钱=0 时间=多 毅力=是 收益=大 口径=死亡率 -->
- Cost: More years of education take time and effort. Tuition, if any, and available aid depend on the country, program, and your circumstances; entry 4 describes Chinese support measures. There is no one honest USD or EUR price for everyone.
- In plain language: Across the studies, adults with more years of education tended to live longer. A review of **603 studies in 59 countries** associated each extra year of education with about **1.9% lower relative risk of death** in adulthood. The estimate was **2.9%** at ages 18–49 and **0.8%** at age 70 or older. Education may affect health and income, but family resources, where you live, and working conditions can also matter. The association belongs in the decision; it cannot predict your personal lifespan.
- Benefit: A systematic review and meta-analysis searched publications from **January 1980 to 31 May 2023**, with the search updated on **16 June 2023**. It included **603 studies**, **70 locations in 59 countries**, and **10,355 observations**. Years of education had a dose–response association with all-cause adult mortality: each additional year was associated on average with **1.9% lower relative risk** (**95% uncertainty interval: 1.8–2.0%**). The age-specific estimates were **2.9%** (**2.8–3.0%**) for adults aged **18–49** and **0.8%** (**0.6–1.0%**) for those aged **70 or older**. The model found no differential association by sex or country Socio-demographic Index level. These are not percentage-point changes in a person's absolute chance of death.
- Evidence grade: A (disputed)
- Sources: IHME-CHAIN Collaborators (2024), “Effects of education on adult mortality: a global systematic review and meta-analysis”, *The Lancet Public Health*, 9(3), e155–e165. <https://doi.org/10.1016/S2468-2667(23)00306-7>; [PubMed abstract and reference](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38278172/); [full article](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10901745/).
- Notes: The included evidence was observational. The authors reported evidence of publication bias (**p < 0.0001**) and substantial variation between studies; **85.9%** of the observations came from high-income countries. Statistical adjustment cannot fully separate schooling from family resources, prior health, place of residence, and occupation. The group-level association between more education and lower mortality is strong, but it does **not** prove that your next year in school will *cause* your own risk to fall by 1.9%. You are the potential beneficiary; calculate the financial side separately.
